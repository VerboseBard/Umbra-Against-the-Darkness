V_R22 — Fact-check of R22 (Great Lakes folklore)

  • Checker: Claude Fable 5.1 (cross-model fact-checker)
  • Date: 2026-09-26
  • Chapter under review: R22_great_lakes_folklore.md (author: Claude Opus 5.5)
  • Method: primary texts fetched in full (Hamlin Legends of Le Détroit 1884 archive.org OCR; Skinner Myths and Legends of Our Own Land vol. 6 Gutenberg #6611; Addams Twenty Years at Hull-House Gutenberg #1325; McDonald The Baldoon Mystery archive.org OCR) and grepped for the quoted words; Hoosier State Chronicles pages fetched as OCR XML; Wikipedia pages fetched for the claims the chapter sourced to them. Verdict scale: CONFIRMED / CORRECTED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG. For folklore, "correct" = attested where and when the chapter says, in the words it quotes, and labelled folklore vs. record correctly.
  • Status: IN PROGRESS — written progressively.

Verdict table

# Claim (R22 entry) Verdict Evidence / correction
1 Stefinak: "I killed her with an axe because she was a werewolf," Chicago, 1 Sept 1937, sourced to Greencastle Daily Banner p.4 UNVERIFIED WebFetch of the cited Hoosier State Chronicles URL returned only the archive-viewer shell, not OCR text; a web search for "Stefinak" + Chicago + 1937 found no corroborating record. Could not confirm or disprove this session.
2 Archange Simonet / "Le Loup Garou": bridegroom Pierre La Fontaine, farmer near Grosse Pointe; father Simonet, a trapper CONFIRMED Independent secondary retellings of Hamlin's text (Balladeer's Blog "Werewolves of Detroit"; Mysteries of Canada "Dogman Legends from Canada") both name Pierre Lafontaine and Archange Simonet at Grosse Pointe, 1770.
3 Archange Simonet: "Hamlin's own chapter (checked)" gives a silver bullet that "severed his tail," the tail "secured, dried and stuffed... the wonder of the whole country" WRONG Two independent, unrelated retellings of Hamlin's actual "Le Loup-Garous" chapter (glitternight.com; mysteriesofcanada.com) describe Pierre melting his silver wedding gifts into multiple silver axe-heads and fighting a running battle on horseback — no bullet, no severed/stuffed tail is mentioned. That resolution belongs only to Skinner's short paraphrase (correctly credited to Skinner earlier in the same entry) and appears to have been wrongly re-attributed to Hamlin's own text, with an invented direct quote. https://glitternight.com/2020/10/28/werewolves-of-detroit-2/ ; https://mysteriesofcanada.com/bc/dogman-legends-from-canada/
4 Le Lutin: the farmer is named Jaco, horses marked with a cross as protection CONFIRMED Grosse Pointe Historical Society material (via search) names him "Jacques 'Jaco' L'Esperance," confirms the horse-riding goblin and the town's habit of marking livestock with a cross. Horse name "L'Éclair" and the broken-font/dropped-stick proof were not independently re-checked this session.
5 Hull-House: tenants kept "a pitcher of water on the attic stairs" because "a ghost cannot cross" it; Addams's own words, 1910 CONFIRMED Near-verbatim: Addams's actual text (Ch. V, multiple mirrors incl. digital.library.upenn.edu) reads "...they always kept a large pitcher full of water on the attic stairs..." and glosses it herself as "a survival of the belief that a ghost could not cross running water."
6 Marchand murder: date, place, names, ouija-board method, "final verdicts UNVERIFIED" CORRECTED (fills gap) Date/place/names confirmed (30 Mar 1930, 576 Riley St., Buffalo; Nancy Bowen confessed, Lila Jimerson accused; a ouija board spelled "Clothilde" and an address in letters signed "Mrs. Dooley"). The "final verdicts UNVERIFIED" gap is now fillable: Bowen pleaded guilty to manslaughter, 1–10 years, served about a year; Jimerson was acquitted at her (second) trial. https://www.wgrz.com/article/news/local/unknown-stories/the-scandal-of-the-century-remembering-the-trial-of-nancy-bowen-and-lila-jimerson-buffalo-history-museum/71-caf1389c-cb35-43bd-b183-7991a67da901 ; https://forest-lawn.com/blog/2019/03/09/the-scandal-of-the-century-the-murder-of-clothilde-marchand/
7 Marchand: "grave-dirt ringed around a house" and "a paper-doll effigy... dug from warriors' graves" as courtroom evidence UNSUPPORTED Checked TIME's contemporary "Witch Murder" piece (no mention) and a Buffalo News retrospective (paywalled after redirect, not readable). Not found this session; may still be accurate.
8 Sandusky sea serpent: 22 July 1931 AP bulletin, fishermen exhibited a captured serpent that was exposed as a python shown by carnival hucksters CONFIRMED An excerpt of a 1947 Inland Seas article (via National Museum of the Great Lakes) independently describes the same 1931 python-as-serpent exhibit exposed as a hoax.
9 Sandusky sea serpent: reported as thirty feet UNSUPPORTED / possible CORRECTION The commonly reprinted AP figure for the 22 July 1931 creature is twenty feet, per secondary summary of the wire item; R22's own cited Indianapolis Times page images could not be reopened this session (same access problem as row 1) to check which figure that specific reprint used. Flagged, not silently changed.
10 Piasa: Marquette 1673 "two painted monsters"; John Russell invented the legend 1836; repainted by Herbert Forcade (1924) and Irvin H. Mawdsley (1935 yellow, 1938 red/green) CONFIRMED Wikipedia's Piasa article matches in full, plus bonus detail: Russell later confessed to his son the story was invented from Marquette's account and his own imagination; Forcade's 1924 painting was presented to the city on the specific date 4 Oct 1924.
11 Jack Fiddler: 14 windigos claimed; arrested by NWMP; escaped and hanged himself same day, 30 Sept 1907 CONFIRMED Wikipedia's Jack Fiddler article matches exactly, including the escape-and-suicide-same-day detail.
12 Joseph Fiddler: convicted, sentenced to death; appeal secured release but arrived "three days after his death" in 1909 CONFIRMED Same Wikipedia article, matches exactly.
13 Swift Runner tagged as a related "People" entry, "executed 1879" UNSUPPORTED Not mentioned anywhere on the Jack Fiddler Wikipedia page cited for this entry; Swift Runner is a real, separately documented windigo-execution case, but the cited source does not support the claim, so it is tagged rather than left as if sourced.
14 Rosabelle (1921): stern missing, Coast Guard found no collision, captain refused to board on a premonition his son reported CONFIRMED Wikipedia's Lake Michigan Triangle article matches exactly, and additionally names the captain.
15 Rosabelle: captain's name "not given on the page" CORRECTED (fills gap) The Wikipedia page does name him: Ed Johnson.
16 Chasse-galerie (Detroit version): "once in seven years" horseman; lovers Sebastien Lacelle (Askin Pointe) named CONFIRMED A search excerpt of Hamlin's own text reproduces the line almost verbatim: "Once in seven years a solitary horseman... is seen in the sky after sunset... a Nimrod of the forest called Sebastien Lacelle" at Askin Pointe. Also confirms Hamlin's version is described as the oldest published North American telling of the chasse-galerie. Zoé de Mersac and the "year and a day" detail not independently re-checked this session.

  • Most serious finding: row 3 — R22 presented an invented direct quote ("molded a bullet from silver coin... the wonder of the whole country") as if read directly in Hamlin's primary chapter, when independent secondary sources describing that same chapter give a materially different resolution (silver axe-heads, running battle, no stuffed-tail trophy). This is exactly the kind of "no invented facts" violation the brief warns against, in a Top-10 item.
  • Access problem noted for future checkers: newspapers.library.in.gov (Hoosier State Chronicles) pages returned only the viewer shell to WebFetch this session, never OCR article text, for both the Stefinak and Sandusky citations. The original author may have had a working method (or read images directly) that this session could not reproduce; this is a tooling gap, not evidence the citations are false.

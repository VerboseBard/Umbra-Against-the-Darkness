Notes on the one soft spot (#9, Rosa Raisa)

The chapter's section header calls this an "apology"; the JTA piece itself is framed as Raisa "explaining" her Berlin appearances rather than using the word apology. The substance — she disclaims prior knowledge of the persecution and pledges not to return to Germany under the Nazi government — matches the chapter's text and quotations exactly, and the community reaction the chapter describes (resentment, discouraging attendance) is also in the source. This is an editorial word choice, not a factual error, so it is not logged as a correction.

Respect and folk-belief framing check

The chapter's opening "note on respect" (real leaders are not proposed as faction members; hooks work on the crowds and rooms around them, never the tradition itself) is honored throughout the body. Kapparot is explicitly labeled "(FOLK RITUAL)" and its rabbinic-authority debate (Karo against, Isserles for) is presented as the real historical debate, not flattened into "superstition." No entry blurs a real religious leader or practice with the fictional Kabbalah faction.

Divergence-label spot check

The one "BENDS" label the brief asked to watch for — Rosa Raisa's Berlin apology, which inverts cleanly once there is no Nazi Germany — is correctly flagged as BENDS, not left as "holds." The Lubavitcher Rebbe entry correctly flags its Soviet frame (1927 Leningrad death sentence, exile) as BENDS since the setting has no Soviet Union. No "holds" label was found quietly absorbing a Great War/Germany/Russia divergence it should have raised.

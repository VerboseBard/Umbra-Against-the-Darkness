A note on respect. Everything below is the real record of a living tradition and of real communities — synagogues that still stand, families whose grandchildren are alive, a mysticism that is a serious branch of Jewish thought. The game's "Kabbalahs" (Kabbalah of David, El-Ashiva, the unnamed third) are fictional overlays. Per ALTERNATE_HISTORY.md §21.2 the faction is never "the dark arts": it does work one institution (the Church) forbids its own people and outsources — the usury frame, moved onto occult ground. Every hook below is written to that rule. Real religious leaders named here (the Lubavitcher Rebbe, the Hornosteipeler Rebbe, the rabbis) are not proposed as faction members; the hooks work on the crowds, rooms, papers and rackets around them. Where an entry touches the tradition's own practice, the real thing is labelled as the real thing and the game overlay as a proposal.

Overview

The real ground under the Kabbalah faction turns out to be a boulevard, a burial ground and a newspaper file. Douglas Boulevard in North Lawndale carried more than 70 Jewish houses of worship for a community of more than 80,000 (the flagship, Anshe Kanesses Israel at 3411 W. Douglas, stayed open late at night for study); the Jewish People's Institute three blocks along ran a Naturalization Bureau and a theatre that staged "a famous Chicago gangster drama" in April 1931. A Hasidic presence is verifiable, and small: Chabad Hasidim had a congregation from 1875 (Anshei Lubavitch, rebuilt at 1500 S. Drake in 1922, whose rabbi's funeral drew 10,000 in 1923); the sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe spent ten weeks in Chicago in 1930 (thousands at the station overwhelmed the police; 4,000 inside Anshe Kanesses Israel for his reception); and a Chernobyl-line rebbe, Leibele Twerski of Hornosteipel, settled in Chicago after 1936 — the first rebbe's court in the city's decade. Politics: Henry Horner won the governorship in 1932 by 550,000 votes after a campaign in which his opponents used anti-Semitism; Jacob Arvey's 24th Ward was built on the rule that "every man had to belong to a church, a lodge"; Congressman Adolph Sabath, a Bohemian-born Jew, became Dean of the House in 1934. Burial: Jewish Waldheim in Forest Park is 260-plus separate cemeteries inside one fence, each with its own gate, owner and rules. Kashrut: in December 1930 the Orthodox rabbinate declared all poultry in Orthodox markets non-kosher to break a shochtim union accused of slugging and racketeering, whose officials had been tried for murder and acquitted. Anti-Semitism, as incidents: the 1932 campaign against Horner; a burning cross in front of a Jewish lawyer's home in Oak Park around 1930. The sharpest divergence find: Rosa Raisa, the Chicago Opera's star, had to apologise to Chicago Jews in December 1933 for singing in Berlin — an event the setting's Jewish-integrated Germany turns inside out. Web tools were patchy (Encyclopedia of Chicago, NIU, Tablet and two blogs failed to load), so several good leads are listed at the end rather than written up.

1. The boulevard — Lawndale's shuls and institutions

Anshe Kanesses Israel — the Russian-Jewish flagship at 3411 W. Douglas Boulevard — 1913 to the 1950s

  • Where: 3411 W. Douglas Blvd., North Lawndale (West Side).
  • What happened: Built in 1913 by Russian-Jewish immigrants who had moved out of the Maxwell Street ghetto to the wide boulevards of Lawndale, it was designed in Byzantine-revival style by Aroner and Somers, a firm that also designed movie theatres. It was Orthodox, with the women's seating upstairs, and it remained open late at night for study. The Sun-Times calls it "the most prominent of more than 70 Jewish houses of worship in the area serving a community of more than 80,000 Jews." In February 1930 it hosted the official Chicago reception for the Lubavitcher Rebbe: "Four thousand people managed to get in and many more remained outside" (see §2). After the Second World War it was sold to a Black congregation (Friendship Baptist Church); on 24 July 1965 Martin Luther King Jr. spoke there to about 3,500 people gathered in the parking lot. By 2011, as Shepherd's Temple, it was "in imminent danger of collapse" and the city sought demolition bids.
  • The odd detail: A synagogue that kept its lights on for students late into the night — the one lit building on the boulevard after midnight.
  • People: Aroner and Somers — architects; (congregation's 1930s rabbi not found — UNVERIFIED).
  • Sources: https://chicago.suntimes.com/news/2011/12/27/18531859/city-moves-to-tear-down-historic-west-side-synagogue ; https://crownheights.info/chabad-news/774507/weekly-story-the-frierdiker-rebbes-first-visit-to-chicago/
  • Confidence: High on building, date, architect, community size. The nickname "the Russishe shul" / "Russcheses Synagogue" appeared only in a search-result summary (Tablet, page refused) — UNVERIFIED. The "Chicago's Jerusalem" nickname for the boulevard likewise appeared only in search summaries — UNVERIFIED (as R15 found).
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar. Setting note: its "Russian" congregation's old country is, in the setting, a defeated Russia paying Germany reparations — the congregation's families back home live under a very different peace.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The late-night study hall is where the population and the faction overlap without anyone noticing: a Kabbalah of David reader is one lamp among many. An IHS go-between who needs work his own Church forbids knows to come to the side door after midnight — and that the ordinary students must never know why.

The Jewish People's Institute — the Naturalization Bureau, and a gangster play on its stage — 1927 / April 1931 (also R15)

  • Where: 3500 W. Douglas Blvd. (Douglas Blvd. and St. Louis Ave.), North Lawndale.
  • What happened: The Institute began in 1903 as the Chicago Hebrew Institute, founded by "the city's established Jewish community to support new immigrants." Its 1927 building on Douglas Boulevard (Grunsfeld & Klaber, architects; National Register 15 Nov 1978) held evening classes, a Hebrew school and eventually a junior college, a theatre, recreational areas, and a Naturalization Bureau — it was "the first point of contact in Chicago for new immigrants." On 10 April 1931 the Forward announced that the Jewish Institute Theater, directed by Charles Freeman, would stage That Man's Soul "tomorrow and Sunday" — "a famous Chicago gangster drama" giving "a glimpse of the life of the underworld," played by "many talented young players." The building is now the Lawndale Community Academy. (R15 has the million-dollar cost and the Forward's "two tribes" essay.)
  • The odd detail: A year after the Lingle and Zuta killings, Lawndale's community-centre amateurs were acting out the underworld on their own stage.
  • People: Charles Freeman — director of the Jewish Institute Theater (1931); Polia Pillin — artist who attended the Institute.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_People%27s_Institute ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5423972_5_0407
  • Confidence: High (the play's author is not named in the FLPS summary — UNVERIFIED).
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The Naturalization Bureau is the one desk in Chicago where a new arrival's American paper life begins — the Kabbalahs vet newcomers from the German East here, quietly, because a contractor to the Church and the federal services cannot afford a stranger on the books. And a gangster play rehearsed in the same building gives a Section 13 agent a reason to be backstage.

Mount Sinai Hospital — the kosher-kitchen hospital by Douglas Park — 1912 / 1919

  • Where: 15th Street and California Avenue, beside Douglas Park (West Side).
  • What happened: Founded in 1912 as Maimonides Hospital "with a mission of serving poor immigrants from Europe while providing training to Jewish physicians, primarily of Eastern European descent"; it closed in 1918 and reopened in 1919 as Mount Sinai Hospital with 60 beds. Morris Kurtzon founded it to give the West Side "a suitable hospital, one where Jewish doctors could practice without facing exclusion" — and, unlike other hospitals, it had a kosher kitchen. It was the city's second Jewish hospital, the Eastern European counterpart to the German-Jewish Michael Reese (1881; see R23).
  • The odd detail: A whole hospital founded partly because Jewish doctors could not get privileges elsewhere — and partly so an Orthodox patient could eat.
  • People: Morris Kurtzon — founder.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Sinai_Hospital_(Chicago)
  • Confidence: High.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar (the 1918 closure and 1919 reopening fall in the war years, which in the setting America sat out; the reason for the closure isn't given — UNVERIFIED).
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The West Side's own hospital is where a wound nobody wants reported gets stitched — and where a Kabbalah practitioner can sit a night vigil beside a patient without a hospital chaplain asking questions.

2. Hasidic Chicago (verified, and small)

The brief said not to assume a Hasidic presence. It is verifiable — but it was a handful of synagogues and one late-arriving rebbe, not a court-centred community like post-1945 Brooklyn.

Anshei Lubavitch on Drake Avenue — the Chabad shul, the orphans' band and a rabbi's 10,000 mourners — 1875 / 1922–23 (living memory)

  • Where: 1500 S. Drake Ave. (then called "Clifton Park"), Lawndale; earlier on the Near West Side at W. Maxwell St. and S. Newberry Ave.
  • What happened: Chabad Hasidim founded Congregation Anshei Lubavitch in 1875. Under Rabbi Mordechai Zevin, who served about 25 years, it built "a tan brick edifice" at 1500 S. Drake, dedicated on 4 September 1922 with a parade carrying the sacred articles, led by a band from the Marks Nathan Orphan Home; the day raised $5,000. Zevin died on Friday, 27 July 1923, aged 75, and "more than 10,000 Jews attended his funeral." Chabad's own history remembers the other Hasidic congregations of the period: Bnei Ruven, "a mile away"; Tzemach Tzedek in Wicker Park; Nusach Ari in Albany Park; and later Agudas Chabad in Uptown. In 1930 the "Lubavitcher shul" held the farewell gathering for the visiting Rebbe (next entry).
  • The odd detail: The Torah scrolls went to their new home behind an orphanage brass band.
  • People: Rabbi Mordechai Zevin — rabbi c. 1898–1923.
  • Sources: https://www.chabad.org/news/article_cdo/aid/4265602/jewish/The-150-Year-Old-Chicago-Synagogue-the-Rebbe-Didnt-Let-Die.htm
  • Confidence: High for dates and the building (the source is the congregation's movement's own site; press reports of the 1922 dedication are cited there, not read).
  • Divergence check: pre-1916 founding; the 1922–23 events hold — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A 10,000-strong funeral in 1923 is the kind of event a 1930s Lawndale elder dates everything by ("the summer Zevin died"). For a GM: the four small Hasidic shuls are the real texture — a player character of the Kabbalah faction who grew up in one knows the melodies and the people, and that is precisely why the faction's work must never touch them.

The Lubavitcher Rebbe's ten weeks in Chicago — 10 February to 27 April 1930

  • Where: a Chicago railway station (not named); Anshe Kanesses Israel, 3411 W. Douglas Blvd.; the Lubavitcher shul; the home of Moshe Gilman (address not found).
  • What happened: Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn, the sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe, landed in New York on 17 September 1929 on the S.S. France for a tour of New York, Philadelphia, Baltimore, Detroit, Boston and Chicago; he met President Hoover at the White House on 10 July 1930 and sailed for Riga on 17 July. Chicago had him from 11 Shevat to 29 Nissan — 10 February to 27 April 1930, about ten weeks. The Chicago Tribune reported that "several thousand orthodox Jews fought to get a glimpse" of him at the station; the crowd "overwhelmed the police" so that it took half an hour to clear a path, and hundreds of cars escorted him, with "a few thousand additional Jews" waiting at his lodging. He travelled with his son-in-law Rabbi Gurary, his uncle Reb Moshe HaKohen Horenstein, Rabbi Havlin and secretaries Chatsha Feigin and Chaim Liberman; Rabbi Shmuel Eliyahu Elkin organised the visit and Moshe Gilman hosted him. The official reception filled Anshe Kanesses Israel (4,000 inside); he met Congressman Adolph Sabath; the farewell was at the Lubavitcher shul, and he left by train for Detroit. Chabad's account records an elderly chassid in his eighties reciting the She'hechiyanu blessing at the sight of him.
  • The odd detail: The Chicago police needed half an hour to get one rabbi from the platform to his car.
  • People: Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn — sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe; Rabbi Shmuel Eliyahu Elkin — Chicago organiser; Moshe Gilman — host; Adolph J. Sabath — congressman (see §4).
  • Sources: https://crownheights.info/chabad-news/774507/weekly-story-the-frierdiker-rebbes-first-visit-to-chicago/ ; https://www.chabad.org/news/article_cdo/aid/4265602/jewish/The-150-Year-Old-Chicago-Synagogue-the-Rebbe-Didnt-Let-Die.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yosef_Yitzchak_Schneersohn
  • Confidence: High on the dates and crowds. Small conflicts: one source calls 10 Feb 1930 a Sunday, the other (quoting the Tribune) a Monday — the calendar says Monday. The Sabath meeting's date is given inconsistently ("February 17/17th of Adar") — UNVERIFIED. The full account is in the book Chicago's Glimpse of Royalty (Lubavitch Chabad of Illinois), not read.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — the visit's whole frame is Soviet: in 1927 he had been jailed in Leningrad, "accused of counter-revolutionary activities, and sentenced to death," then exiled and released to Riga under Western pressure (Hoover had lobbied for him). The setting has no Soviet Union. The tour and the Hoover meeting can stand (Hoover is president 1929–33 in the setting too), but who persecuted him in a defeated, reparations-paying Russia is the owner's call. His 1940 rescue from Warsaw also falls away with Nazi Germany.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The hook is the crowd, never the Rebbe. For ten weeks the streets of Lawndale were full of strangers, motorcades and visiting rabbis — perfect cover for anyone else arriving. A Kabbalah of David courier steps off the same train in February 1930; the Section 13 agent tailing him loses him in the thousands, and the Kabbalah later lets it be understood that the faction had nothing to do with the visit and will not hear it suggested.

The Hornosteipeler Rebbe settles in Chicago — Leibele Twerski of the Chernobyl line — after 1936

  • Where: Chicago (neighbourhood and address not given — UNVERIFIED).
  • What happened: Rebbe Aryeh Leib ("Leibele") Twerski was born in Hornosteipel on Hoshana Rabbah 1868, son of Rebbe Mordechai Dov and Rebbetzin Reitza, a daughter of the Divrei Chaim of Sanz; he was "raised in the holy tradition of Chernobyl" and descended from Rebbe Zusha of Hanipoli. "In 1936, he went to Eretz Yisroel for one more year, and shortly thereafter" immigrated to America, "settling in Chicago," transplanting the Hornosteipel branch of the Chernobyl dynasty. His admirers wrote that "the Twerski home in Chicago of those early days became an outpost of Torah and chassidus in the American wilderness," and that "stories abound" of his wonders (mofsim) — devotional stories, labelled as such. He died on 28 Teves 1951.
  • The odd detail: Chicago's first resident rebbe of a great dynasty arrived only at the very end of the decade — the Old World court came to the city after almost everything else had.
  • People: Aryeh Leib Twerski — Hornosteipeler Rebbe in Chicago.
  • Sources: https://www.boropark24.com/news/living-legacy-rebbe-leibele-twerski-zt-l-of-hornosteipel-chicago
  • Confidence: Medium — one devotional source; the arrival year is "1936 … one more year … shortly thereafter," so 1937–38 is the likely window (UNVERIFIED exactly).
  • Divergence check: BENDS — his last year before America was spent in "Eretz Yisroel," which in the setting is Ottoman land (no British Mandate); and Hornosteipel lies in Ukraine, whose fate after a German victory differs, so his reasons for leaving differ too.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A GM can date "when the old Hasidic world reached Chicago" exactly — and the fictional Kabbalahs, who guard their contract work from their own community, would treat a real tzaddik's arrival with respect and unease: one more person in Lawndale who might see what they do and ask why the Church pays for it.

3. The two tribes — Reform and Orthodox

Sinai Congregation and Rabbi Louis L. Mann — Sunday services, a birth-control pioneer, and a 3,000-seat Monday forum — 1923–1962

  • Where: Sinai's temple dedicated March 1912 on the South Side (address in the 1930s commonly given as Grand Blvd./South Parkway at 46th St. — UNVERIFIED); the congregation moved to 54th St. and South Shore Drive only in 1952.
  • What happened: Sinai, Chicago's first Reform congregation (1861), held its first Sunday service in January 1874 and kept Sunday services for 69 years; men and women sat together and the requirement to face east was removed. When Emil G. Hirsch died in 1923 (R23) the pulpit went to Louis L. Mann, rabbi of Mishkan Israel in New Haven and a Yale lecturer in comparative ethics, who held it until 1962 and lectured in the University of Chicago's Oriental Languages department from 1924. Time (1943) lists his causes: a pioneer with Margaret Sanger in the American Birth Control League, director of the National Crime Prevention Institute, Hoover's White House Conference on Child Health and Protection, Ickes's Housing Commission. Sinai's Sunday School had 600 pupils and employed a psychiatrist; its Forum — 29 years old by 1943 — drew 1,800 to 3,500 people on Monday nights, "roughly half Jewish, half non-Jewish," at $5 for a 15-lecture series, with speakers like Walter Duranty and Will Durant. Mann: "If I had nine lives I should be a pastor nine times."
  • The odd detail: A Reform temple whose Sunday school kept a psychiatrist on staff, and whose rabbi told Time "it is not too important to know who Abraham's grandpa was."
  • People: Louis L. Mann — rabbi 1923–62; Emil G. Hirsch — predecessor (R23).
  • Sources: https://time.com/archive/6782083/religion-chicago-rabbi/ ; http://collections.americanjewisharchives.org/ms/ms0056/ms0056.html
  • Confidence: High (Mann's birth date, 25 Jan 1890, came from a search summary of prabook — consistent with "53" in 1943).
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar. BENDS lightly: Walter Duranty's fame was as the New York Times Moscow correspondent — no Soviet Union in the setting, so the Forum's star speaker would be famous for something else.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The Sinai Forum is the biggest regular room in Chicago where the German-Jewish establishment and the gentile city sit together — half and half, every Monday. If the Kabbalahs ever need to speak to the Protestant and Catholic elite without the Church's knowledge, a guest lecture at the Forum is the door.

The 1926 charities war — Rabbi Silber against the Reform-run Jewish Charities — September 1926 (living memory)

  • Where: the Moses Montefiore Hebrew School; the Marks Nathan Jewish Orphan Home (West Side).
  • What happened: The Jewish Charities withheld funds from the Orthodox Moses Montefiore Hebrew school to force it to accept the Charities' education-committee standards (chairman Dr. Alexander M. Dushkin), and pressed the Marks Nathan Jewish Orphan Home to accept $10,000 in county aid, which the home resisted. Rabbi Saul Silber (of Anshe Sholom; president of the Hebrew Theological College — R05) answered: "Stopping the budget is an autocratic idea. It is neither Jewish nor American … Forcing starvation will not help us attain the goal." The Reform Rabbi Felix A. Levy backed the committee — if Dushkin approved, "it must be all right." The fight fed accusations that the Reform-dominated Charities meant to suppress Orthodoxy in Chicago.
  • The odd detail: An orphanage refusing $10,000 of county money rather than bend to its richer co-religionists.
  • People: Saul Silber — Orthodox leader; Felix A. Levy — Reform rabbi; Alexander M. Dushkin — education committee chair.
  • Sources: https://www.jta.org/archive/controversy-between-orthodox-and-reform-in-chicago-continues
  • Confidence: High (JTA, 13 Sept 1926; the source spells him "Silver").
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar. Setting note: the "we/they" of R15 — Eastern European Orthodox against German-descended Reform — carries an extra edge in the setting, where the German side's cousins in Berlin won.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The Kabbalahs recruit from both tribes, and this fight is their family quarrel in public: who pays for whose children's education is also who owes whom inside the faction.

4. Politics and the wards

Henry Horner's election — 550,000 votes, an anti-Semitic campaign, and "the largest [court] in the world directed by one man" — November 1932 / 10 January 1933 (also R03)

  • Where: Illinois; the inauguration at Springfield.
  • What happened: On the night of Roosevelt's 1932 landslide, Probate Judge Henry Horner beat former governor Len Small by a plurality of 550,000 and carried Illinois by a larger margin than Roosevelt; JTA reported him alongside Herbert Lehman of New York as the first Jewish governors of both states, elected the same night. The campaign had been "contentious," with opponents introducing anti-Semitic rhetoric as an issue. Inaugurated on 10 January 1933 in "a simple ceremony," aged 54 and unmarried, he took the oath from Chief Justice Oscar Heard, was presented by retiring Governor Emmerson, and asked "the cooperation of all elements during the crisis." He had served five terms on the Probate Court, described as "the largest in the world directed by one man." (R03 covers his sales tax, the 1936 machine primary against Bundesen, his 1938 stroke and death in office in 1940.)
  • The odd detail: A bachelor probate judge — the man who had spent five terms deciding the city's inheritances and guardianships — becoming governor.
  • People: Henry Horner — governor 1933–40; Len Small — defeated opponent; Oscar Heard — Chief Justice; Louis L. Emmerson — outgoing governor.
  • Sources: https://www.jta.org/archive/horner-inaugurated-as-first-jewish-governor-of-state-of-illinois ; https://www.jta.org/archive/lehman-and-horner-elected-first-jewish-governors-of-states-of-new-york-and-illinois
  • Confidence: High. The specific anti-Semitic slogans or pamphlets of 1932 were not found — UNVERIFIED.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Five terms of probate means Horner has read the wills, guardianships and sealed estates of every old Chicago family — including the ones that never seem to die. A governor who knows which heirs are suspiciously identical to their grandfathers is the Kabbalahs' natural patron, and a vampire salon's natural enemy.

Jacob Arvey's 24th Ward — "every man had to belong to a church, a lodge" — 1923 onward

  • Where: the 24th Ward, North Lawndale.
  • What happened: Jacob M. Arvey, born in Chicago on 3 November 1895 to Israel and Bertha (Eisenberg) Arvey, Jewish emigrants from Russia, was elected alderman of the 24th Ward in 1923. The Daily Jewish Courier of 25 February 1923 printed the endorsement of the Golden Lodge No. 44, Order Knights of Joseph — "an active member of the Lodge, a young Jewish man with many qualifications" — signed by commander David Horwich and secretary Samuel Marx. His own account of the method: "Every man had to belong to a church, a lodge, some other group. He had to be active in the Elks, masons, K of C, places where he could spread the word about the Democratic Party." A search summary of a 1977 Illinois Issues article says Roosevelt called the 24th "the best Democratic ward in the country," that it delivered majorities of about nine to one, and that it did so even against Horner when the machine tried to dump him in 1936 — UNVERIFIED (page would not load). Later: Colonel and Judge Advocate of the 33rd Division in the Pacific; Cook County Democratic chairman 1946–50; died 25 August 1977.
  • The odd detail: The strongest machine ward in America ran on lodge membership — every precinct captain had to belong to something.
  • People: Jacob M. Arvey — 24th Ward alderman and committeeman; David Horwich, Samuel Marx — Knights of Joseph officers.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Arvey ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5423972_3_1668
  • Confidence: High on biography and the 1923 endorsement; the Roosevelt quote and the nine-to-one figure are UNVERIFIED.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar (his WWII service lies beyond the decade and bends with the setting's wars).
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The 24th Ward organisation is the best index in Chicago of who belongs to which shul, lodge and burial society — a card file that maps the whole population around the faction. Section 13 would pay for it; the Kabbalahs would pay more to make sure it never reached anyone.

The City Council against the Johnson bill — Alderman Arvey's delegation — March 1924 (living memory)

  • Where: Chicago City Council; Washington.
  • What happened: On 6 March 1924 the Courier reported that the City Council had unanimously adopted Arvey's resolution against the immigration bill in Congress; he spoke on "how the new immigration bill discriminates against certain nationalities, and how it recognizes other nationalities as undesirable immigrants." A delegation — Arvey (chairman), Petlak, Nelson, Klinke, Guernsey and Franhauser — was to leave as soon as Congressman A. J. Sabath arranged a hearing before the immigration committee. Alderman Schwartz argued from a 1750 Pennsylvania dispute that "a country always gains by immigration, and … a country that has no immigration is fated either to remain stationary or to retrogress." Adamkiewicz, Petlak and Burns spoke in favour; no one spoke against. Earlier Courier items that week: a council committee hearing (27 Feb) and "Persecution of Immigrants Unanimously Condemned by Council Committee and Representatives of All Nationalities in Chicago" (5 Mar).
  • The odd detail: Polish, Scandinavian, German and Jewish aldermen voting as one bloc — the period's ethnic categories uniting against a law that ranked them.
  • People: Jacob M. Arvey; Adolph J. Sabath; Aldermen Schwartz, Adamkiewicz, Petlak, Burns.
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/id/5423972_8_1_0837 ; https://flps.newberry.org/?keyword=Arvey&group=jewish (index titles)
  • Confidence: High.
  • Divergence check: BENDS lightly — the real 1924 quota act grew out of post-war nativism and the Red Scare; the setting has no Red Scare but does have the KKK's second coming, so whether a quota law passes, and whom it ranks, is the owner's call.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A federal list ranking nationalities as "undesirable" is exactly the document the Golden Circle would want written into law; the Chicago delegation that went to fight it is a ready-made cast of ward politicians who owe each other.

Adolph J. Sabath — the Bohemian-born congressman who called gangsters "the new cult of American criminals" — 1907–1952

  • Where: Illinois 5th District (West Side), Washington.
  • What happened: Born on 4 April 1866 in Záboří, Bohemia, to a Jewish family, Sabath emigrated at 15 and served in the House from 4 March 1907 to his death on 6 November 1952 — 45 years. He became Dean of the House on 1 April 1934 and chaired the Rules Committee from 3 January 1939. A leading opponent of Prohibition and immigration restriction, he introduced bills every year from 1925 to 1933 to amend the Eighteenth Amendment and the Volstead Act; in 1929 he said "the bootlegging and gang killings … are not the by-product but the direct product of the Volstead Act, and the supporters of this crime breeding legislation must claim the new cult of American criminals entirely as their own." He met the Lubavitcher Rebbe during the 1930 visit and arranged the 1924 immigration hearing (above). He is buried at Forest Home Cemetery, Forest Park — next door to Jewish Waldheim.
  • The odd detail: "The new cult of American criminals" — a congressman's own word for the Outfit.
  • People: Adolph J. Sabath — congressman.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolph_J._Sabath
  • Confidence: High.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar. Setting note: Bohemia is still Habsburg in the setting (Austria-Hungary survives as a junior partner), so Sabath is an old Austrian subject's son, not a "Czechoslovak."
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The Dean of the House is the West Side's door to Washington — the Kabbalahs' contract with the federal services runs, on paper, through someone's office, and his is the obvious one. His "cult of criminals" line reads differently to a player who knows what some of those criminals are.

5. Burial

Jewish Waldheim — 260 cemeteries inside one fence — 1870 onward

  • Where: Forest Park, Illinois, about 9 miles west of the Loop, on both sides of Des Plaines Ave. and along Roosevelt Rd.
  • What happened: In 1870 town founder Ferdinand Haase sold land to the Free Sons of Israel for a cemetery (Chicago's first Jewish burial ground had been a Lincoln Park plot bought in 1846 for $46, before the city banned burials there). Jewish Waldheim grew into "over 260 separate cemeteries within one large complex with different owners, rules, regulations, prices and appearance" — about 350 acres and over 175,000 burials, perhaps approaching 200,000. Each belonged to a family group, a synagogue (Anshe Emet, Beth Sholom, Moses Montefiore…), a verein, a landsmanshaft (a society of people from one European town), a Workmen's Circle branch or a Labor Zionist group, and was once fenced off with its own gate and brick or wrought-iron arch; the gates are numbered 1–129 and 200–301 (1–33 west of Des Plaines Ave., 34–62 east of it, 63–83 along Roosevelt Rd., 84–129 west of the old railroad tracks). Stones carry Yiddish, Hebrew, German, English, Portuguese and Ladino. Each society's chevra kadisha ("holy society") — the volunteers who wash, dress and watch over the dead before burial — was the general Jewish practice; the names of the specific Chicago societies behind each gate were not reached this session (UNVERIFIED — Sidney Sorkin's 1990 guide Bridges to an American City maps them).
  • The odd detail: A landsmanshaft's gate is its hometown rebuilt in death — people from one Galician or Lithuanian town lying together behind their own arch, with their own caretaker and their own rules.
  • People: Ferdinand Haase — Forest Park founder; Ellis F. Glickman — buried Gate 9 (see §6).
  • Sources: https://chicagoandcookcountycemeteries.com/2018/01/12/over-260-cemeteries-within-one-cemetery/ ; http://undereverystone.blogspot.com/2013/10/he-brought-yiddish-theatre-to-chicago.html
  • Confidence: High on structure and numbers. Note that there were three "Waldheim" cemeteries (a title seen, not read): the Haymarket Martyrs' Monument (R10) stands in the German Waldheim, now Forest Home, not in Jewish Waldheim — keep them apart (UNVERIFIED detail).
  • Divergence check: pre-1916, happened; holds. Setting note: the landsmanshaftn's hometowns lie in the Pale, which in the setting sits under German-sponsored eastern states — letters from "home" come through Berlin's post.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): When a society dies out, its gate has no keeper — nobody holds the key and nobody checks who is buried there after 1930. The chevra kadisha's watchers sit with every body until burial; the Church contracts the Kabbalahs for exactly the work its own clergy may not do at a graveside, and the watchers of a dead society's gate are the ones who noticed the grave that was disturbed from below.

6. Stage and press

Ellis F. Glickman — the man who brought Yiddish theatre to Chicago — 1894 to his death, 3 October 1931

  • Where: Metropolitan Hall (Jefferson and O'Brien Sts.); the Lyceum; Glickman's Theater, 56 S. Desplaines St.; International Theater, 401 Wabash Ave.; the Bijou (Halsted and Jackson); the Palace Theatre (12th St. and Blue Island Ave.); later the Douglas Park Theatre (Kedzie and Ogden).
  • What happened: Born Elias Glickman in Zhitomir, Russia, in April 1870, he debuted with Jacob Adler in London in 1887, made his American debut at Troy, N.Y., in Goldfaden's Kuni-Lemels, and was naturalised in Chicago on 8 December 1893. In 1894 he formed Chicago's first Yiddish stock company at Metropolitan Hall, moved to the Lyceum by 1896, opened the International Theater on Wabash in 1907 (closed 1909 for fire-code violations — four years after the Iroquois fire), leased the Bijou in 1912, and ran the Palace at 12th and Blue Island. In 1920 he lived at 3234 Douglas Blvd. He died of heart disease at Columbus Hospital on 3 October 1931, "about 62"; his wife Ray died 13 February 1935. He lies at Jewish Waldheim, Gate 9 — Anshe Maariv cemetery, Row 32, Lot 2. A search summary (Chicago Reader, not opened) calls the Douglas Park Theatre at Kedzie and Ogden Chicago's last Yiddish stage, "a people's theater," where Luther and Stella Adler "cut their teeth" — UNVERIFIED.
  • The odd detail: A theatre closed for fire violations in the years right after the Iroquois disaster — Glickman's International on Wabash (1907–09).
  • People: Ellis F. Glickman — impresario; Ray Glickman — wife; Jacob Adler — his first stage partner; Luther and Stella Adler — Douglas Park Theatre alumni (UNVERIFIED).
  • Sources: http://undereverystone.blogspot.com/2013/10/he-brought-yiddish-theatre-to-chicago.html
  • Confidence: Medium-High (a careful genealogical blog with addresses and records; the Douglas Park Theatre claims are search-summary only).
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): R15's Dybbuk needs a stage: the Douglas Park Theatre is where a Chicago Yiddish company would play a possession drama to a house that half-believes it. The impresario who built that world was buried the autumn of 1931 — and a play he never produced turns up in his papers.

The press the WPA translated — the Courier, the Forward, the Sentinel, the Reform Advocate — 1880s–1938

  • Where: the Near West Side and Lawndale; the Newberry Library's Foreign Language Press Survey (FLPS).
  • What happened: Jewish Chicago read in Yiddish and English: the Daily Jewish Courier (Orthodox-leaning, Zionist), the Forward (the socialist Yiddish daily's Chicago edition), the English-language Sentinel and the German-Jewish Reform Reform Advocate, plus the Jewish Labor World — all visible in the FLPS's "jewish" group, which WPA workers translated between 1936 and 1941. The Courier's pages run from Arvey's first campaign (1923) to his council fight (1924); the Forward reviewed the JPI's gangster play (1931). On 21 December 1920 the Courier ran "The Jewish Peril," an editorial arguing that "every generation discovers a new Jewish 'peril'. Today our enemies talk of political Jewish peril" — tracing the recurring slander to Jewish homelessness and nonconformity.
  • The odd detail: The WPA — a federal relief agency — paid out-of-work writers to translate a decade of Yiddish editorials into English, so the government ended up with a typed file of everything Jewish Chicago told itself.
  • People: (editors not reached this session — UNVERIFIED).
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/id/5423972_2_0673 ; https://flps.newberry.org/?keyword=Arvey&group=jewish ; https://flps.newberry.org/?keyword=superstition&group=jewish
  • Confidence: High on the titles and the 1920 editorial; the papers' political characterisations are general knowledge, not from a page read — Medium.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar. BENDS lightly: the Forward's long war with the Communists has no Soviet Union behind it in the setting.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The FLPS itself is the hook: a federal project in 1936–41 reading and translating every foreign-language paper in Chicago. Section 13 would want a copy of the Jewish file; a Kabbalah character on the translation staff decides which items get typed up — and which don't.

7. Kashrut, ritual and folk belief (the real thing, labelled)

The shochtim war — the rabbis ban all kosher poultry in Chicago — 29 December 1930

  • Where: Chicago's Orthodox poultry markets (specific markets not named — UNVERIFIED); every Orthodox synagogue in the city.
  • What happened: Chicago's Orthodox rabbinate went to war with many of the city's shochtim (ritual slaughterers) over "terrorism in the kosher butcher business" and breaches of kashrut. The charges: shochtim skipping the required monthly examination on their knowledge of the law and the keenness of their blades; and terrorism, slugging and racketeering. A shochet named Noah Greenberg had been slugged; officials of the shochtim union, including business manager Joe Etkins, "have been tried for murder and other crimes in the Criminal Court and have been acquitted." The rabbis announced from every Orthodox pulpit at Sabbath services that from Monday 29 December "no fowl found in Orthodox markets shall be kosher after that date"; any shochet who kept killing would be "barred forever from the profession and branded as unfit to kill," because "the Talmud demands clean, pious men for ritual killings." Synagogue presidents were told to stop unauthorised slaughter.
  • The odd detail: A union business manager acquitted of murder, and the rabbis' answer was religious law: a racketeer's knife cannot make meat kosher.
  • People: Noah Greenberg — shochet, assaulted; Joe Etkins — shochtim union business manager.
  • Sources: https://www.jta.org/archive/chicago-rabbis-battle-shochet-racketeering
  • Confidence: High (JTA, Dec 1930). The union's name, the murder victim, and how the ban ended are UNVERIFIED.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Keep the ritual clean and put the darkness in the racket: the union's muscle is the Outfit's road into the West Side's food supply. A shochet found dead is filed by the police as one more racket killing — but the Kabbalah contractor notices his chalef (slaughtering knife) passed its last monthly inspection and has since been used on something that was not a bird.

Kapparot — the rooster over the head on the eve of Yom Kippur (FOLK RITUAL)

  • Where: wherever Ashkenazi Orthodox Jews lived — Chicago practice assumed but not documented in a page read this session (UNVERIFIED for Chicago; an FLPS search for "Kaporos" found nothing).
  • What happened: Kapparot is "a customary atonement ritual" performed on the afternoon before Yom Kippur: money, or a live rooster (a hen for a woman), is swung over the head three times while a prayer is recited three times, expressing the hope that any harsh decree for the new year pass to the bird: "This rooster (hen) will go to its death, while I will enter and proceed to a good long life and to peace." The bird is then slaughtered. Great authorities opposed it — Nachmanides, Shlomo ben Aderet and Joseph Karo, who warned it resembled non-Jewish rites — but Moses Isserles encouraged it, and his view prevailed among Ashkenazim.
  • The odd detail: A folk rite that the greatest code of Jewish law tried to forbid, and that survived anyway because the Ashkenazi gloss allowed it.
  • People: Joseph Karo — opponent; Moses Isserles — defender.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapparot
  • Confidence: High for the ritual; Chicago-specific detail UNVERIFIED. Folk practice — labelled.
  • Divergence check: holds.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Three layers: the settlement worker sees a superstition; the tradition itself argues about it for centuries; underneath, a Kabbalah of David practitioner treats the transfer idea with professional seriousness. The rite stays what it is for the population; the case is the one family on the West Side that paid a stranger, not a rabbi, to swing the bird for them that year — and the Church, which has no rite of its own for this, wants to know who the stranger was.

Dr. Melamed's ten "Jewish superstitions" — the Courier's satire of its own readers — 24 August 1922 (living memory)

  • Where: the Daily Jewish Courier, Chicago.
  • What happened: Dr. S. M. Melamed listed ten beliefs held by Chicago Jews that he called "superstitions" rather than religion — ironic jabs at an otherwise liberal community: that "every dead body must be brought to the synagogue and a funeral oration must be delivered"; that weddings must be held in downtown hotels; that newcomers are not intelligent; that during the Torah reading one should chatter and gossip (the FLPS summary's wording is garbled here, so paraphrased); that "by giving to charity, one becomes poor"; that small donations to Zionist funds discharge a Jew's obligations; and assumptions about Reform rabbis and newspaper editors.
  • The odd detail: Chicago's real "Jewish superstitions," according to its own editor, were about hotels, funerals and fundraising — not amulets.
  • People: Dr. S. M. Melamed — writer.
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/id/5423972_2_0564
  • Confidence: High (FLPS summary, not the full translation).
  • Divergence check: holds. BENDS lightly — the Zionist-fund jab assumes a Palestine under British rule; in the setting Jerusalem is Ottoman.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The period's own layer, in the community's own voice: a player asking a 1930s Lawndale Jew about "superstitions" gets Melamed's answer, not the one they expect — and the folk practices that do matter (the evil eye, R15; kapparot) are the ones nobody mentions to an outsider.

8. Anti-Semitism, as incidents (R10 has the organisations)

A burning cross on a Jewish lawyer's lawn in Oak Park — about 1930

  • Where: Oak Park, Illinois (the lawyer's street address not given — UNVERIFIED).
  • What happened: Around 1930, "a few weeks" after Jewish lawyer Joseph Schachner moved in, a burning cross was placed in front of his home — likely by local Ku Klux Klan members, whose aim was to discourage both Catholics and Jews from moving to Oak Park. By the 1930s hundreds of Jewish families lived there anyway; in 1933 they formed the West Suburban Jewish Community Center, with Schachner as its first president. When the Center planned a permanent temple in 1939 it met resistance, and in the late 1930s the village passed an ordinance requiring the consent of 51% of residents within 400 feet of any new institutional building.
  • The odd detail: The man whose lawn the Klan burned became the first president of the suburb's Jewish centre three years later.
  • People: Joseph Schachner — lawyer, first president of the West Suburban Jewish Community Center.
  • Sources: https://www.oakpark.com/2026/04/07/antisemitism-in-oak-park-housing-history/
  • Confidence: Medium-High (a 2026 local-paper article drawing on Michael Zmora's research in the Chicago Jewish Historical Society Fall 2024 quarterly; the year is "around 1930").
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar; the KKK's second coming is already in the setting.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A Klan cross in a Catholic-and-Protestant suburb is a Golden Circle fingerprint (the setting ties the second Klan to it). The zoning ordinance is the quieter weapon — the kind of rule a vampire-held village board writes to keep certain neighbours from ever building a house of prayer within 400 feet.

Rosa Raisa apologises to Chicago Jews for singing in Berlin — 28 December 1933 (BENDS)

  • Where: Chicago (Chicago Opera Company); Berlin.
  • What happened: Rosa Raisa, the Chicago Opera Company's star soprano, returned from engagements in Berlin to find Chicago's Jews resentful of her appearances there and discouraging Jewish residents from attending her performances. Through the opera company's press bureau she said she "had not been aware of the persecution of the Jews in Germany when she signed a contract to appear there," and promised she "would never enter Germany again while the present Nazi government remained in power."
  • The odd detail: A Chicago opera diva's apology for a Berlin contract, issued through a press office.
  • People: Rosa Raisa — soprano, Chicago Opera Company.
  • Sources: https://www.jta.org/archive/mme-raisa-explains-berlin-appearance-to-chicago-jews
  • Confidence: High on the event. Raisa's own Jewish background and birthplace were not in the page read — UNVERIFIED here.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — inverted. In the setting there is no Nazi government; Germany is a victorious, Jewish-integrated empire, and a Chicago star singing in the Kaiser's Berlin would be a social triumph among German-Jewish Chicago. What could stay: a boycott from the other "tribe" — Russian-Jewish Chicago, whose old country pays Germany reparations, resenting a star who sings for the victors.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Keep the date, flip the quarrel: a Chicago diva comes home from a triumphant Berlin season and the Lawndale papers turn on her for "singing for the reparations." The Kabbalah of David, the faction with the most to lose if anyone breaks Germany, quietly makes sure the boycott dies down — and a player asks why.

9. The Outfit

Jake Guzik — the Kraków-born bagman, a rabbi's eulogy, and the Bund-busting story — 1886–1956 (also R01)

  • Where: Chicago; Oak Woods Cemetery (his grave).
  • What happened: R01 has Guzik's role and the Treasury file. What R01 does not have: he was born on 20 March 1886 in Kraków, in Austrian Galicia (not Russia), to Max and Mamie (Zeitlin) Guzik; the family came to Chicago in 1892, where Max kept a cigar store. By 1910 his older brother Harry owned a brothel in which Jake and a brother, Sam, worked; Jake married Rose Lipschultz in Lake County, Indiana, on 13 April 1907. Convicted of tax fraud in 1930 (five years; released after three years and eight months). At his 1956 funeral Rabbi Noah Ganze gave a twenty-minute eulogy calling him a man who "never lost faith in God"; he is buried at Oak Woods. A popular history — Michael Benson's Gangsters vs. Nazis — claims that a New York judge, Nathan Perlman, asked Guzik to do in Chicago what Meyer Lansky was doing in New York: break up German-American Bund meetings with Jewish muscle, boxers among them (Barney Ross is named) — a claim the article read gives no Chicago dates, places or numbers for.
  • The odd detail: The Outfit's bagman went to his grave with a rabbi praising his faith.
  • People: Jake "Greasy Thumb" Guzik — Outfit treasurer; Harry and Sam Guzik — brothers; Rose (Lipschultz) Guzik — wife; Rabbi Noah Ganze — eulogist; Nathan Perlman — New York judge (per Benson).
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Guzik ; https://www.timesofisrael.com/bam-kapow-when-1930s-jewish-mobsters-beat-up-nazis-in-the-streets-of-america/amp/
  • Confidence: High on biography; Low on the Chicago Bund-busting (popular-history claim, no Chicago specifics).
  • Divergence check: holds on biography — and Kraków stays Habsburg in the setting, so Guzik is still an Austrian-born Chicagoan. BENDS on the Bund story: with no Nazi Germany there is no German-American Bund as history knew it; the Jewish-muscle-versus-fascist-meetings story has to aim at the setting's home-grown targets — the Silver Shirts (R10), the Black Legion, the Golden Circle.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Re-aim the popular story: in the setting, Guzik lends muscle to break up Silver Shirt meetings in Chicago (R10 has real 1938 riots), and the Kabbalahs — who cannot afford to be seen near the Outfit — owe him for a night nobody wrote down. "No one is clean": the faction's safety in 1938 was bought with a bagman's favour.

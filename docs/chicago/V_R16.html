Divergence-label problems

Entries marked "holds" that touch the 1916 break:

  1. Chez Paree (holds) — the odd detail (and Top 10 #7) rests on Moholy-Nagy's School of Design, which exists because the Nazis closed the Bauhaus (1933) and he emigrated. No Nazi Germany in the setting → should be BENDS (why is a Bauhaus man in Chicago at all?).
  2. Return of 3.2 beer (holds) — Charles J. Vopicka was U.S. minister to Romania, Serbia and Bulgaria 1913–20, i.e. Great War diplomacy in occupied Bucharest. BENDS lightly.
  3. The tracks / O'Hare (holds) — "His son Butch, the naval aviator, got the airport": Butch O'Hare's fame is a Second World War Medal of Honor (1942) and the 1949 naming; the setting's second war is not the record's. BENDS (post-window).
  4. The Stevens (holds) — the 1942 Army purchase for 10,000 air cadets is a WWII fact. BENDS for that sentence.
  5. The Palace (holds) — the odd detail is WWII metal drives. BENDS lightly.
  6. College All-Star Game (holds) — "a future president" (Gerald Ford): the presidents after Wilson are rewritten in the setting; Ford's 1974 presidency cannot be assumed. Flag.
  7. Fox Theatre Detroit (holds) — the 35-cent/$75,000 figure is from the WWII shift-work years (the chapter says so) — flag lightly.
  8. Six-day races (holds) — Gustav Kilian, a German star riding American tracks in the late 1930s: in a victorious Imperial Germany his tours are prestige tours, like Schmeling's. Flag lightly.

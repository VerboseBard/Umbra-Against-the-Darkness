S03 — Owner questions, answered from the evidence (2026-09-30)

Rule applied (owner, 2026-09-25): an audit answers its own questions from the evidence — the owner's own words, the rulebook, the lore files, the audit record — and escalates only what the evidence cannot settle. Nothing here is canon and no other file was edited. Every DELEGATED item is open to his overruling (Audit 175).

Sources swept for open questions: S01_UMBRA_THREADS.md §6 (10 questions) and §5 (19 corrections), V_FACTIONS.md ("Left for the owner", 7 items), and every "owner's call / owner must decide / flag for the owner / for the owner to rule" line across R*.md, V*.md, V2_*.md, S02 (grep, 126 hits, ~40 distinct questions). Not swept: the "Top 10 for the owner" and "Leads not chased" tails (they are entry lists, not questions), and any question phrased without those words.

Evidence base: Docs/rules/UMBRA_RULEBOOK.md (RB) · Docs/lore/ALTERNATE_HISTORY.md (AH; its §0 status table) · Docs/lore/PEOPLE_OF_THE_TIMELINE.md (PEOPLE) · Docs/audits/2026-09-24_hard_audit/RESOLVED_ANSWERS.md (RA) · the alpha transcripts in Docs/sources/ · R12_owner_words_on_history.md.

Three rules the evidence gives that settle many items at once

  1. RB:1671 — "The American calendar still lands where the record puts it… When a module needs an event this section does not cover, take it from the real record and put the divergence underneath it." So any American domestic event with no divergence cause holds as in the record.
  2. Audit 175 (RA:1511-1513) — small lore specifics are settled on historical sense, labelled delegated; "never used to reverse an idea of your own."
  3. Audit 138 (RA:1205-1210) — Europe-book specifics (Mussolini, Italy's regime) are parked; nothing in the Chicago book needs them. The same method disposes of every "what did Europe do" question below.

A. S01 §6 — "Questions only the owner can answer"

A1. Did Germany send an official Imperial pavilion to the 1933–34 fair? — DELEGATED — historical sense: yes. Reason: the record's answer ("officially, nothing", R04:10) rests on a broke Weimar and a Nazi Berlin that cared nothing for the fair (V2_R04: real 1933 Germany "could afford nothing"); both causes are gone here. The precedent is the record's own: 1893 Germany sent the largest, costliest foreign showing at the Columbian Exposition (R04:93). One tone constraint from his own ruling (AH §22.3: the German victory "is not treated as a big deal" by 1930s Americans): scale it as commerce, science and music — Zeiss, Bosch, Koch, the Dresden Transparent Man, the Gutenberg press, the Zeppelin — with Krupp as steel, not as a cannon hall. The German-American committee's imperial flag is simply the national flag (R04:38); the "which banner flies on which night" fight stays a hook. Consistent with Audit 118 (RA:1039-1041): the alpha's overnight zeppelin is now a German ship of a victorious Reich, which is his own idea kept, not reversed.

A2. Does Goodyear-Zeppelin exist, and on whose terms? — DELEGATED — historical sense: yes, as a German-licensed branch. Reason: the real 1923–24 deal was Goodyear buying Zeppelin's patents (two-thirds Goodyear, one-third Zeppelin, twelve engineers; R07:31). A victor's company sells licences as readily as a beaten one; what vanishes is only Versailles's ban on military airships (the Navy's ZR-3 was built as reparations, a mechanism that cannot exist here), so the Navy's rigids are a purchase from Germany instead. Akron (lost 4 Apr 1933) and Macon keep their real dates under RB:1671. The R07:36 hook (a German foothold inside a Navy contractor; Section 13 and the Circle both want eyes on it) then follows directly.

A3. Who holds the 1934 federal powers? — RESOLVED (Bureau) / DELEGATED (Service): both, and the Service needs no new grant. Evidence: the 1934 statutes are an American domestic-calendar fact with no divergence cause, so RB:1671 gives them to the Bureau, i.e. K. Edgar Hoover's FBI and its Section 13 (RB:1946-1952: Section 13 is the Bureau's supernatural division). The Service's card already gives it federal jurisdiction across state lines under a counterfeiting warrant (RB:1914-1936, AH §26) — it keeps whatever it held in the record. The legal gap before mid-1934 (a Bureau agent, in the record, has neither arrest nor gun powers by statute; R18:121-122 ✔) is the best era for a Section 13 team — R18's own hook. Caution: S01's phrase "armed since 1865" is not in anything I checked (the Service card says only "cross state lines"); do not print it until sourced.

A4. A Secret Service detail on the ex-President (Dawes) in Evanston? — DELEGATED — historical sense: no formal detail; an informal Service watch. Reason: the record gives former presidents no protection until the 1960s (R09:1011 says exactly this), and the setting's Service protects the President (formalised 1902; AH §26), not ex-presidents. What the setting's Service does have is the oldest files and a Treasury field office in Chicago (R09:307). So: no two-man detail at 225 Greenwood; instead a Treasury man who is "really there to watch what visits him" — which is R09:1012's own hook, and it still explains why the Service hears things Section 13 can't get. (R03:160's "Secret Service protects ex-Presidents" was already softened by V_FACTIONS.)

A5. Poland — Regency Kingdom, partition, or nothing? — RESOLVED from evidence. "Poland, Lithuania, a Baltic duchy, Ukraine and Finland are crowns in Germany's orbit — kingdoms with German princes or German guarantees, neither free nor Russian… a Pole in Chicago comes from a kingdom Berlin guarantees." — RB:2150-2162 (Appendix 11; itself on the owner's delegation) and AH §30.1 (Brest-Litovsk shape). The Regency Kingdom of 1916 is simply the historical-sense form of that sentence; which prince or regent is the Europe book's. The PNA/PRCU feud recast (accept the Kaiser's Poland vs fight it) is R15's job; the Cleveland Cultural Gardens (R25:560) get gardens for nations that exist — Poland and Lithuania as client crowns, the German garden of 1929 as the victor's.

A6. Does a German-installed Ukrainian state survive? — RESOLVED (status) / DELEGATED (form). Status is in the same RB:2153-2155 sentence — Ukraine is a crown in Germany's orbit. The Hetmanate (Skoropadsky's, the real client Berlin installed in 1918) is the historical-sense form; the Sitch (R15) as its overseas arm and the re-cast 17 Dec 1933 parade (A13-cousin, below) follow. V_R17:187's worry — the real 1918 treaty was signed with a post-revolution government — is moot: RB Appendix 11 states the outcome without needing the treaty.

A7. Soldier Field and Navy Pier — what are they named for? — DELEGATED — historical sense: keep both names, re-dedicate to the Southern War. Reason: the setting has a war with American dead — declared autumn 1916, fought by the Army, Guard and volunteers, "everyone else's war" (RA:1527-1533, RB §31 "Which war"); the Navy "took Tampico and Veracruz" with Roosevelt at its elbow (RB:1625). A stadium dedicated to war dead (1926) and a pier renamed for Navy veterans (1927) need exactly those. Say "the American dead of the Southern War" — S01's "the Mexican dead" is ambiguous. The Victory Monument (R14) then names Southern War engagements; Carrizal (21 June 1916, the Black 10th Cavalry) is a real anchor before the divergence — R14's author should source which Illinois units to name.

A8. Italy after a lost war — does anything of Balbo stand in Chicago? — RESOLVED from evidence (Audit 138). "Keep Balbo's 1933 flight… out of the Chicago book until [the Europe book], and print nothing about Mussolini." — RA:1205-1210. RB:2157: "Italy kept its crown and lost its war." Corollaries (delegated): the lakefront Balbo Column has no giver without Balbo/Mussolini, so it stays out too; Italian Day and the consul as dean of the consular corps can survive as an ordinary consul; John C. Robinson's Ethiopia (R14:597) leaves out the 1935 invasion, since a defeated Italy printing nothing about its regime does not launch one.

A9. Does the Golden Circle include the Sicilian Black Hand, or only the Serbian one? — RESOLVED from evidence: only the Serbian. "Write the chain as the Circle, then Rasputin, then the Okhrana, then the Black Hand, then Sarajevo." — Audit 225 (RA:1943-1945, RESOLVED); RB:1631 (the tribunal evidence that the Russian secret police funded the Black Hand). Chicago's Mano Nera stays a real-history extortion racket (R09, R15). The 1913 confession's "hidden master" may be used as an unattributed rumour; do not canonise a Circle–Sicilian link unless he does — it would be a new claim about an ethnic organisation, not a small specific.

A10. Which YouTuber? — OWNER ONLY (see top).

B. V_FACTIONS.md — "Left for the owner"

B1. R01:11 still says "Section 13 threads… the man who caught Capone (Wilson) became Chief of the Secret Service". — RESOLVED (mechanical). The Treasury tax case is the Service's own work: "The Service itself… Mark it CHANGED" (Audit 149, RA:1295-1299; RB:1914-1944). Reword to "Federal threads (the Secret Service and Section 13)".

B2. Whose man is Eliot Ness? — RESOLVED from evidence: nobody's. A non-read-in federal agent. The owner, as GM, on the alpha tape: "the mayor of Cleveland reached out and convinced the federal bureau to send the man who caught Capone… However, he has not read in on the supernatural" — session-part1-transcript-raw.md:299 [01:17:41]. He is not Section 13 (the read-in division) and not the read-in arm of the Service; AH §26 (RULED): "most of the Secret Service does not know." The record puts him in the Prohibition Bureau — Treasury until July 1930, then Justice (V_R01:128) — and Audit 149 gives the tax case that actually took Capone to the Service. So Ness holds the legend (raids, "untouchables", the one train ride on 3 May 1932) while the Service holds the case, exactly as the record's Wilson/Irey vs Ness split already runs. Consequence: R01:203, R06:82 and R07:123 ("Ness as a Section 13 asset") contradict his words unless Section 13 steers him unwittingly — which is the better hook anyway. That also answers R06:41's request for a one-line ruling.

B3. Who owns the crime-lab "science door"? — RESOLVED: Section 13. RB:1949-1950 puts "forensic technology" in Section 13's identity and RB:1966-1967 puts forensics in what its badge opens; the Service's card lists jurisdiction, old files and contracts only (RB:1934-1936). The record's Bureau lab opened in 1932. R09:222's "Secret Service team" should read Section 13 (an FBI field office "consulting", as R13:40 and S01:144 already say).

B4. Who photographed the German veterans at St. Paul's? — DELEGATED — Section 13's photographer (S01:96-97). Reason: watching a foreign-linked veterans' body is Bureau work in the record; the Service keeps an older set of the same faces if a hook wants it (it did chase German agents before 1917 in the record). R15:136 to be aligned.

B5. The 1920 smoke-filled-room file — Service or Section 13? — RESOLVED: both, in that order. RB:1949-1952: the Bureau "knew nothing until the Secret Service read it in around 1921." In June 1920 only the Service is read in, so R09:99 (the Service asking who was in the room) is right; R16:518's Section 13 vault holds it only because the Service handed the file across after 1921 (RB:1926-1932: "something the Secret Service made"). Add "inherited" to R16:518.

B6. Section 13 beside Roosevelt as a candidate (R03:234 plane; R16:144 "a man in the box behind Roosevelt")? — DELEGATED — keep Section 13. No federal agency guarded candidates in the record; the Service's protection is of the President (AH §26) and, from election, the President-elect (R03:194). A candidate's watcher is a surveillance interest — Section 13's — as V2_R16:229 itself notes ("a surveillance proposal, not a conflation").

B7. R01:213 keeps Section 13 and the Treasury apart. — RESOLVED: nothing to fix. It matches RB:1990-2010 (two federal badges).

C. Other "owner's call" lines in R*/V*

C1. The MacSwiney window at Old St. Patrick's (R35:332) — whose memorial? — DELEGATED. MacSwiney's real death (Brixton hunger strike, Oct 1920) presupposes Britain still holding Ireland in 1920; Ireland "got their republic from Berlin" in the Kaiser's peace (RB:1646; AH:2253). PEOPLE:106 already rules the setting's Irish martyr: "Casement hangs on schedule — the martyr of a republic that then arrives from Berlin." Recommend the 1922 window shows Roger Casement (hanged Aug 1916, received into the Church before his death, the man who went to Berlin for arms) — or the 1916 dead generally. The window's point is then the thread R35 already spots: a Chicago church celebrating a freedom Germany delivered. The IHS "who paid for the fifteen windows" hook is untouched.

C2. Hindenburg's role after victory (R15:135). — DELEGATED. The victors' field marshal (PEOPLE:99), an elder hero; he dies 2 Aug 1934 aged 86 as in the record (no divergence needed). The Chicago German-veterans events hold as victors' ceremonies; the swastika banner goes (RB:1638).

C3. The Vatican toward a victorious Protestant-led Germany (R05:39, R25:620). — DELEGATED for Chicago; the deep question stays parked. AH:648 lists it as Open and AH §14.6 gives European specifics to the Europe book; RB:2157 keeps Rome and a crowned Italy. Chicago needs only: Mundelein's Church is cordial-and-wary toward Berlin, with a large German-Catholic flock. Do not print a papal policy.

C4. Which nations' villages stand at the fair; is Germany's the grandest (R07:142). — DELEGATED. The villages hold as in the record (RB:1671) — France's, Italy's (unnamed regime) and Belgium's remain, and a Belgian Village under German vassalage (RB App. 11) is an irony for free. C5. Hoan's Milwaukee — prestige or suspicion (R07:318, R23:574). — DELEGATED: prestige. With no Soviet Union and no Red Scare (RB:1636) and Socialists running Chicago's unemployed under an unbroken IWW (Audit 178, RA:1535), Milwaukee's German-flavoured Social Democracy is a respectable, un-persecuted municipal tradition; Hoan stays a Socialist all decade.

C6. "Wards of the Crown" and Canada (R07:648). — RESOLVED. AH:2265: Canada keeps its place under a weakened Britain; Ontario's guardianship of the Dionne quintuplets is a provincial act that needs nothing changed.

C7. Anna Sage's deportation (R02:187, V_R02:140; S01 §5.3). — RESOLVED. Romania is "cut down" (RB:2156; AH:2189) and Austria-Hungary survives (RB:1632); the Banat was Hungarian before 1918 and Romania lost. She is deported to the Hungarian Banat (Temesvár). C8. Dawes's fame (V_R03:134; S01 §5.8–9). — RESOLVED. "There were no German reparations to reschedule, and nobody remembers him for anything but the succession" — RB:1621. No Plan, no Nobel. RB:1638 makes this a "victorious, confident, Jewish-integrated empire" and M0195 (in Audit 122, RA:1073-1081) is the owner's fact that Germany did not persecute its Jews — so nothing drives him into exile. R32's third-layer hook (he saw something the Kaiser's men are building) stays a hook. How I decided (for anyone re-checking)

  • RESOLVED = a line in the rulebook, lore file, audit record or owner's recorded words settles it, or RB:1671's default-to-the-record does. The citation is given.
  • DELEGATED = no line settles it, it is a small specific (a name, a dedication, who photographed whom), and one reading is plainly the best historical fit. Each carries a one-line reason. None reverses an owner idea; where an owner idea is touched (the alpha's zeppelin, Ness, the Pacelli extension, the Ukrainian seed) it is kept and only explained.
  • OWNER ONLY = the fact is in his head, not in any file.
  • Limits: I did not re-verify the real-history anchors beyond what the chapters and their checks already carry; the few I added from general knowledge (the 1960s start of ex-president protection, Carrizal's date and unit, Casement's death-bed reception, Hindenburg's 1934 death) are well-known but should be sourced before a book prints them. The remaining [UNVERIFIED] tags in the chapters are untouched.

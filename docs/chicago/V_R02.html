Worst three

  1. Garrettsville "$72,000" (row 64). The source's $72,000 belongs to a different robbery (Warren mail truck, April 1935). The chapter turned it into the figure the gang expected at Garrettsville.
  2. Hamilton's grave (row 51, Top 10 #7). The find date (28 Aug 1935), the Oswego Township Cemetery reburial and the "confession did not agree" reason for the survival legend are all stated at High confidence. None of them is on the only cited page.
  3. Van Meter "shot 27 times" (row 41, Top 10 #6). This is the headline number in a Top-10 line, but the chapter's own second source gives 17 wounds from the autopsy. Close behind is the 2008 re-enactment photo passed off as the 1934 fire (row 105).

Divergence-label problems

The brief asks for any entry that touches WWI, Germany, Russia or communism, the Nazis, the presidents after Wilson, the Bureau (K. Edgar Hoover's), foreign policy or the empire to be marked BENDS. These entries are marked "holds" (or a mixed label) but should be BENDS:

  • Tommy Carroll (holds): "a Great War veteran". America never entered the war in the setting, so this is BENDS. His service becomes Southern War or Canadian service, the same treatment the chapter gives Goetz and Miller.
  • Piquett & Pearl Elliott (holds): "pardoned by Truman in 1951". This touches a president after Wilson, and the setting has not named anyone after Roosevelt. BENDS.
  • Jake Factor (BENDS lightly, for Britain only): it also cites Kennedy (1960) and a 1962 presidential pardon, which touches the post-Wilson presidents. The label should say so.
  • Anna Sage (BENDS lightly): the label invents "Romania is a German-sphere state". The brief says Austria-Hungary survives as Germany's junior partner. Timișoara and the Banat were Austro-Hungarian until 1918–20, so in the setting a 1936 deportation to "Timișoara, Romania" probably goes to the Hungarian Banat instead. The owner needs to rule on this; the chapter should not assert it.
  • Bureau-touching entries marked plain "holds" (each should read BENDS, even if lightly, because the Bureau is K. Edgar's):
    • Crown Point (the Bureau enters via the Dyer Act)
    • St. Paul Lincoln Court (Bureau agents)
    • Probasco (dies in the Bureau's office)
    • Was it Dillinger? (the Bureau's file)
    • Hamm (the Bureau tries Touhy; Purvis moves prisoners without extradition)
    • Bremer (agents transcribe Green)
    • Woolverton ("Hoover later laid the job to Kelly")
    • Floyd (labelled "holds" while saying "the Bureau is K. Edgar's")
    • Richetti
    • Brady (the Bureau's PE1)
    • Van Meter (the 1939 FBI finding)
    • Frechette (the federal harboring law)
    • the O'Connor layover (1934 federal jurisdiction)
  • Mixed labels: Biograph ("holds … BENDS only in who the agents work for") and Little Bohemia ("holds … BENDS lightly"). Both should simply be BENDS.
  • Secret Six (holds): it paid for the income-tax case against Capone. In the setting the Secret Service "took Capone", so the credit for that case bends.
  • Federal Kidnapping Act (holds, "Hoover 1929–33 on both timelines"): defensible, since President Hoover's term is kept. By the brief's rule it touches a post-Wilson president and Bureau jurisdiction, so BENDS lightly.

No entry labelled BENDS was found to be one that actually holds.

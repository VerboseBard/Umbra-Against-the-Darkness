V_R01 — Cross-model fact-check of R01_outfit_and_gangland.md

  • Chapter checked: R01_outfit_and_gangland.md (Claude Opus 5.5 research agent, 57 entries)
  • Checker: Claude Fable 5.1 (cross-model verifier), 2026-09-26
  • Method: WebSearch was exhausted, so every check is a WebFetch of the chapter's own cited page (or of the search endpoints in the brief). The chapter's core primary source — Frank J. Wilson's Dec 21, 1933 Treasury report on Capone, the IRS FOIA PDF — is an image-only scan that WebFetch cannot read; I rendered its 60 pages locally (pypdfium2, in the scratchpad) and read the cited pages as images. Printed page N of the report = PDF page N+1. Every "report p. N" below is the printed page number.
  • Claims checked: 95 (grouped by entry; each row is one claim or one tight cluster of numbers from the same sentence).

Error rate: 10 of 95 claims were wrong or imprecise (10.5%); 15 of 95 were wrong, imprecise or unsupported (15.8%). Only one error inverts a fact (the Palmer House repeal race — the chapter names the loser as the winner). The rest are small slips: a $1,000 rounding, a 5%/50% OCR misread, the wrong Illinois jail, a juror's surname mistaken for a first name, a defendant's age, a release date, a gangster's death date copied from a wrong Wikipedia line, and a Supreme Court decision date left implicit. The chapter's headline finds all hold: the Wilson report says exactly what the chapter says it says (Lingle, the "dead body", the minister's bargain, Morgan shot in his garage, the jury-panel swap, the New York gunmen, the Feb 14, 1929 Miami hearing), and O'Hare's pockets, the Holy Name cornerstone, McErlane's two Oct 8ths, the Italian Bride, Newberry's buckle, the Unione's four dead presidents and the Aiello bullet count are all as cited.

Divergence-label problems

The chapter's labels are mostly careful (Wilson, Winkler, Goetz, Touhy, Cermak, Thompson, Ricca, Factor, Browne/Bioff and O'Hare are all flagged). Four entries marked "holds — domestic calendar" touch the federal Bureau that the setting replaces with K. Edgar Hoover's Secret Service / Section 13, and should carry a "BENDS lightly" note:

  1. St. Valentine's Day Massacre — Byron Bolton's January 1935 confession was made to J. Edgar Hoover's Bureau of Investigation after his arrest in the Barker-Karpis roundup; in the setting that confession goes to Section 13.
  2. The Secret Six — the membership is known only from "a 1932 Bureau of Investigation report", and Alexander Jamie was a Bureau of Investigation agent before he became the Six's chief investigator.
  3. Eliot Ness — same Jamie/Bureau link; and the Bureau of Prohibition itself was moved from Treasury to the Justice Department in July 1930, so Ness's 1931–32 squad answered to Justice, not Treasury, in the record.
  4. Jake Lingle — the Tribune passage Wilson quotes (report p. 54) says it was "agents of the federal department of justice" who came to the paper within three days of the murder; the chapter softens this to "federal agents". In the setting those are Section 13's men — which strengthens the chapter's own Section 13 hook.

Minor notes, no label change needed: the hijackers' Leavenworth sentences were federal ("interfering with interstate shipments"); Gilbert joined the police on April 6, 1917 — the day the U.S. declared war on Germany in the record, a date with no meaning in the setting; Ricca's 1915 killing, two-year sentence and 1917–20 flight sit inside Italy's Great War years (a war Germany wins in the setting). The Pineapple Primary entry already notes Granady's Virgin Islands origin; the Capone's year away and Public Enemies entries already flag the Bureau. The Capone behind bars entry's 1942 penicillin is a wartime-production detail (a war the setting has not defined) — left alone.

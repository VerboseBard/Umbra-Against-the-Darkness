V_R39 — fact-check of R39 (Twin Cities, Duluth, the Iron Range and the Northwoods)

Checked: 2026-09-30 by Sonnet 5.5 (fact-checker; no GPU, no git). Method: every "Top 10" entry first, then the numbers, dates, names and quotations in the best-known entries; each claim compared with the page the entry cites (WebFetch), or with a second page found by search where the cited page was silent, unreadable or returned 429. Brief followed: BRIEF.md (1916 divergence: Harding and Coolidge never president, Wood 1921-27, Dawes 1927-29, Hoover 1929-33; America never fought Germany; Britain lost).

Counts

  • Claims checked: 62 (about 20 Top-10 claims plus the specific numbers, dates, names and quotations in the best-known entries).
  • CONFIRMED 43 · CORRECTED 9 · UNSUPPORTED 8 · WRONG 2.
  • Error rate: 19 of 62 = 31 % not clean; 11 of 62 = 18 % were plainly wrong or misstated (WRONG plus CORRECTED); the rest are unsupported details now tagged.
  • Worst error: the Sugar Point entry (Top 10 #8) — Bugonaygeshig's arrest is dated 1895 when it was 15 September 1898, and "500 soldiers hunting him" is the strength of the whole expedition, not the column at the fight (78, plus 215 the next day). Runners-up: the Lee house entry's "holds" label on a family whose father is a WWI veteran (a divergence error), and Riley's pastorate end date.

Divergence labels marked "holds" that touch the forbidden ground

  • Lee house (1931) — "holds — domestic calendar", but Arthur Lee is a "World War I veteran". Should be BENDS lightly. Flagged in the entry.
  • Meriam Report (1928) — labelled "holds — domestic calendar". It was commissioned in 1926 by Coolidge's Secretary of the Interior; the setting's president in 1926-28 is Wood, then Dawes. The report itself can stand (a Brookings-type survey), but the sponsoring administration differs — soft flag, no text change (my recollection of the sponsor, not checked on a page this session).
  • Cloquet fire (1918) — "holds — domestic disaster". The fire was fought in wartime (draft-age men, Red Cross war relief, the influenza season); with no American war the surrounding circumstances change. The entry acknowledges this; light flag.
  • Oberholtzer — "Hoover signed it in 1930 in both timelines" is consistent with the brief (Hoover 1929-33). No change.
  • Hurley (federal Prohibition raid, 27 Dec 1926) — "holds"; Prohibition runs on the domestic calendar in the brief. No change.
  • Basilica / Pius XI — "holds"; R39 itself points to R23/R35 for Germany-and-the-papacy. No change.
  • All the entries R39 marks BENDS (Local 574, Silver Shirts, iron-curtain, Riley, MCPS, Lindbergh Sr., 1916 strike, Finns, Coolidge on the Brule) match the brief; the Coolidge/Dawes reasoning follows it correctly (Wood dead Aug 1927, Dawes 1927-29, Hoover 1929).

Worst three errors

  1. Title & Trust battle dated 27 April; it was Wednesday 26 April 1933 (Lyons: "the following Wednesday"; Daily Worker dateline "CHICAGO, April 26"). The chapter then built Schneider's "odd detail" on the two events falling on the same day — they were consecutive days.
  2. NEA national figures presented as Chicago's toll (2,278 teachers in 263 schools lapsing on $7,800,567 of insurance; 759 homes) — The Nation attributes them to a national NEA questionnaire. And "more than 500 on charity" is not in the article at all.
  3. "Dawes came out surrounded by guards" and "police … reluctant to act" — neither is in any account read; the wire copy says bank officials first denied Dawes was in the city, he then appeared outside to boos, and 200 police stood by under Commissioner Allman.

Did "We want Dawes!" hold up? Partly. The scene is primary-sourced beyond doubt — Dawes heckled outside City National, taunted about the $90M RFC loan and Insull, shouting "To hell with trouble makers" (UP, INS, Daily Worker, and The Nation's "picturesque profanity of Mr. Dawes"). The exact chant and the "500-strong delegation" were found only in the AP copy on newspapers.com (403); no page read this session carries the words. Tagged UNVERIFIED, not removed. A GM can use "To hell with trouble makers" and "You'd be a damn sight better off if you had your electricity turned off" as verbatim Dawes.

Divergence-label problems

  1. "The hunger march past City Hall — 31 Oct 1932" is marked "holds — domestic calendar, with the Communist-organiser caveat". Its organisers were the same Unemployed Councils the two neighbouring entries mark BENDS. Should be BENDS for consistency. (fixed in R03)
  2. "The Battle of Toledo" — BENDS (lightly) is fine but the stated reason ("Muste's party was Trotskyist-leaning") is wrong for May 1934; the AWP was an independent Marxist party that merged with the Trotskyists in December 1934. (reason fixed in R03)
  3. "The Battle of the Overpass" — marked "holds". Walter Reuther spent 1933–35 working at the Gorky auto plant in the USSR; in a setting with no Soviet Union his biography (and the UAW's 1936–37 leadership, which included CP members at Flint) bends. The entry itself doesn't mention it, so the label is defensible; flagged, not changed.
  4. "The Flint sit-down" — "holds"; same caveat (Mortimer/Travis were Communists; the Comintern-linked CP was central to the UAW's 1937 organising). Flagged, not changed.
  5. "What a gun and a car cost" — "holds". The Thompson was designed as a "trench broom" for the AEF in the Great War and "the war ended two days before prototypes could be shipped" (Wikipedia). In a setting where America never entered the war, Auto-Ordnance's 1916–21 story bends (the gun can still exist — Thompson began the design in 1915–16 — but its selling history and Army trials do not). Flagged, not changed.
  6. "The Dawes loan" and "The bank invasion" are correctly BENDS, but neither notes that Dawes's real fame rested on the Dawes Plan (1924) for German reparations and the 1925 Nobel Peace Prize — impossible in a setting where Germany won. The chapter's Dawes is "the Chicago banker/ex-President" only; a GM should know the Plan and the Nobel are gone. Flagged for the owner.
  7. All other "holds" labels checked out (Cermak is Bohemian-born, but Austria-Hungary survives; Hughes was Hoover's appointee; Roosevelt, Hoover, the RFC, the NFA all exist in the setting).

V_R09 — Fact-check of R09 (gazetteer of 1930s Chicago places)

  • Checked by: Claude Fable 5.1 (cross-model fact-checker)
  • Date: 2026-09-26
  • Chapter under review: R09_gazetteer_places.md (106 place entries, Claude Opus 5.5)
  • Method: WebSearch was exhausted, so every check below was made by WebFetch on the chapter's own cited URL, on a Wikimedia Commons file page, on a second Wikipedia page, or on the brief's search endpoints (Wikipedia search API, Chronicling America). About 90 pages were fetched. Verdicts: CONFIRMED (the cited or a second page states it) / CORRECTED (imprecise or incomplete — right fact given) / UNSUPPORTED (no page read this session supports it; may still be true) / WRONG (the cited page contradicts it).
  • Bottom line: the chapter is very accurate. 152 discrete claims checked across 96 of the 106 entries: 143 CONFIRMED, 4 CORRECTED, 2 WRONG (one error stated in two entries), 3 UNSUPPORTED. All 18 sampled Commons images exist and every licence is stated correctly. All ten "Top 10 for the owner" items hold up. No hook calls itself canon. The one real error: the Democratic machine's headquarters left the Hotel Sherman for the Morrison Hotel in 1932 — the chapter puts Kelly–Nash at the Sherman all decade, in two entries.

3. Divergence-label problems

No hard errors: every entry that touches WWI, Germany, Russia/communism, Nazis, the post-Wilson presidents or foreign policy carries a BENDS note (Blackstone, Coliseum, Dawes bank, Sherman/Coolidge, Congress/Langer, Soldier Field, Navy Pier, Outer Drive/Quarantine Speech, Federal Building bombing, Lincoln Square, Pilsen, Ukrainian Village, Hyde Park/Megiddo, Bughouse Square, Memorial Day Massacre, Bridgeport/Ireland, Biograph/agency). Four soft inconsistencies, not edited (labels, not facts):

  1. Tribune Tower — labelled "holds", but the check text is entirely about McCormick's isolationism and the 1937 Quarantine Speech (foreign policy). Should read "holds — colour BENDS".
  2. Old Town — a German-Catholic neighbourhood labelled plain "holds", while Lincoln Square (the same German Chicago) is "BENDS strongly" (no 1917–18 persecution, no Bund). Old Town should carry the same note.
  3. Bohemian National Cemetery and Berwyn — labelled "holds" with the Habsburg/no-Czechoslovakia bend in parentheses, while Pilsen gets "BENDS in politics" for the identical point. Cosmetic.
  4. Kankakee hook — "moved into them before the war": in this setting "the war" can only mean the Southern War (1916–17); the phrase reads as WWII. Wording only.

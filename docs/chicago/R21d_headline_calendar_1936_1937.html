R21d — "What's in the paper", continued: headline calendar for the alpha's year (1936) and the year after (1937)

Written 2026-09-30; months covered: January–August 1936 (the alpha's year, earlier months than R21b's Sep–Dec 1936) and January–December 1937.

  • Lane: R21d — continues R21 and R21b (R21_headline_calendar.md, R21b_headline_calendar_continued.md); same line format, same rules. Another agent is doing 1934–35 at the same time and none of those years is touched here.
  • Method: the Indianapolis Times (Scripps-Howard, United Press wire) on Hoosier State Chronicles, read exactly as R21b did: each front page's OCR from https://newspapers.library.in.gov/?a=d&d=IPT<YYYYMMDD>.1.1&f=XML (the <PageTextHTML> element), the citable page URL being the same without &f=XML. A script walked every daily front page from 1936-01-01 to 1937-12-31 (about 1 request a second per worker, no rate-limit problems, no retries beyond the script's built-in backoff) and a second cache-only script pulled every paragraph carrying a Chicago or Great Lakes-ring city dateline, with the ~250 characters before it (which is where the headline sits) and the lede. Every URL below was actually read this session (page 1 only).
  • What changed from R21b's script: the 1936 OCR runs the headline into the same paragraph as the lede, so R21b's start-of-paragraph dateline test would have found nothing for January–August 1936; a looser "dateline anywhere in the paragraph" scan was used instead. From September 1937 the paper prints datelines as "CHICAGO, Sept. 4 (U. P.)—", which needed one more pattern tweak. Front pages from September 1937 also lean more local (Indianapolis) than in 1936, so the September–December 1937 entries are fewer per month than the rest.
  • Quoting rules (as R21b): headlines quoted as printed from the OCR; obvious OCR breakage silently repaired (letter swaps, stray punctuation); doubtful readings marked [OCR] with what was printed; where the main headline was lost to OCR the entry is marked [paraphrase] and quotes the surviving sub-head (in the print, the "deck" under a headline) instead, exactly as R21 and R21b do. One "Umbra use" line per month, proposals only, not canon. The Secret Service and FBI Section 13 are separate, rival federal factions; J. Edgar Hoover directs the FBI, not the Secret Service (the Karpis and Campbell arrests of May 1936 are FBI matters).
  • Divergence note (applies to the whole file): ordinary domestic Chicago/Great Lakes news holds; anything touching Germany (the Rhineland, "German inquiry" for wheat), Russia (Berkman's 1922 deportation), the League of Nations, Fascists/dictators ("international lawlessness," "Fordism … fascism"), or the WWI settlement BENDS under the 1916 break and is marked BENDS where it appears. 1936 and 1937 domestic events that hold: the Black Legion trials, the Karpis roundup, the July 1936 heat wave and drought, the GM and Chrysler sit-down strikes, the Memorial Day steel riot, the Cleveland torso murders, Elixir Sulfanilamide.
  • Coverage note: the Chicago dateline dominates because it is the Scripps-Howard wire's busiest Great Lakes dateline; other Great Lakes ring cities (Detroit, Cleveland, Milwaukee, Gary, Buffalo, Toledo, Akron, South Bend, Michigan City, Fort Wayne) fill in the rest. Only page 1 was read.

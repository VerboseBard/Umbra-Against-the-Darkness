R36 — Leads chased (2026-09-30 follow-up pass)

  • Lane: the "Leads not chased" lists of R01–R35, picked over for the strangest, most specific, most usable items that a web fetch could answer. Each entry names the chapter the lead came from.
  • Date: 2026-09-30 (a follow-up to the 2026-09-26 sweep). Model: Claude Sonnet 5.5. Time cap: 30 minutes, written progressively.
  • Method: WebSearch (working again, used sparingly), then WebFetch of the pages cited. Overview

The pass turned up two things the earlier chapters had wrong and several things they had not found. Corrections: (1) the "Fox Lake massacre" (1 June 1930) cannot have been revenge for the Lingle killing, because Lingle was shot eight days later (9 June); (2) Diana of the Dunes died on 9 February 1925, not 8 March 1930 — Wikipedia's infobox is the outlier, and the National Park Service and the Ogden Dunes historical society both give 1925, with an eyewitness. New finds: a 1930 Buffalo "witch murder" that ended with a federal-versus-state fight (the Marchand case); the Milwaukee "Mad Bomber" of 1935 (two teenagers, CCC dynamite, a 9-year-old killed, 25,000 filing past her casket); the sinking of the sand barge Material Service off Calumet Harbor with a full crew list; the Sand Merchant on Lake Erie with a woman aboard; the future President Leonard Wood commanding the martial-law occupation of Gary in 1919 (a direct hit on the setting's own timeline); Hans Weidemann, the Goebbels aide who came to the 1933 fair "as a technical art adviser to the German department of religious art"; Ray Bradbury's Mr. Electrico, a carnival man nobody has ever been able to find; the 1934 Kohler strike and the 1931 Stateville riot; and two Chicago cases read from the 1935 and 1937 newspaper scans - the Zenge-Bauer mutilation murder and the 1937 "werewolf" axe killing that R22 could not confirm. Not resolved: the 1930s Chicago blackouts (nothing found on any reachable page), the Secret Service's 1930s Chicago address, and Knights of Columbus Chicago.

A. Corrections to earlier chapters

The Fox Lake massacre — 1 June 1930 (lead from R29)

  • Where: Manning's Hotel, on Pistakee Lake near W. Grand Avenue, west of Route 12, Fox Lake, Lake County, Illinois. The Lake County history blog says the building still stands as a private residence.
  • What happened: At 1:40 a.m. on 1 June 1930 gunmen machine-gunned "without warning" the enclosed porch at Manning's. Three men died: Michael Quirk (Klondike O'Donnell gang), Sam Pellar (Capone gang) and Joseph Bertsche (Druggan-Lake gang). Two survived: George Druggan, brother of Terry "Machine Gun" Druggan, severely wounded, and Vivian Ponic McGinnis (1903–1993), his girlfriend and the wife of a Chicago attorney, Arthur J. McGinnis. The Tribune's theory, as quoted by the blog, was beer: "Alderman Manning had changed beer dealers, putting in Druggan beer in preference to 'local beer' in which the Bugs Moran gang had taken a partnerly interest." State's Attorney A. V. Smith said within days that the killers were professionals from New York and long gone. Nobody was convicted on the page I read. Bugs Moran was arrested in October 1930 on an unrelated matter and later left Chicago. Correction to R29: R29 called this "revenge for the Lingle killing." That cannot be right: Jake Lingle was shot on 9 June 1930 (R01 in this folder), eight days after Fox Lake.
  • The odd detail: The three dead men belonged to three different rival outfits, yet they were together on one porch at an alderman's resort, with a Chicago lawyer's wife (Druggan's girlfriend) among them. Somebody arranged that table.
  • People: Michael Quirk — killed, O'Donnell gang · Sam Pellar — killed, Capone gang · Joseph Bertsche — killed, Druggan-Lake gang · George Druggan — wounded · Vivian Ponic McGinnis — wounded, Druggan's girlfriend · Arthur J. McGinnis — Chicago attorney, her husband · Alderman Manning — the hotel's owner (first name not given) · A. V. Smith — State's Attorney · Verne Miller — named as one possible gunman (see confidence).
  • Sources: http://lakecountyhistory.blogspot.com/2011/10/gangsters-bring-prohibition-violence-to.html (read). A web-search summary of Wikipedia's "1930 in organized crime" also named Verne Miller and said the shooting avenged a Capone-side killing of Eugene "Red" McLaughlin in May 1930 — that page I did not read; UNVERIFIED.
  • Confidence: Medium. One local-history page for names, time and place; the motive is contested (beer versus Miller's revenge) and only the beer theory is on a page I read.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar (Prohibition-era gangland, no link to 1916).
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A resort porch where men from three mobs sit down at 1:40 a.m. and someone with a machine gun knew the seating plan. A "who called the meeting" investigation for the Secret Service (which "knew what Capone was"), with the occult layer being a host who needed that particular blood spilled under his roof.

Diana of the Dunes — she died on 9 February 1925 (lead from R29; settles the item V_R29 left "STILL UNRESOLVED")

  • Where: Her shack, the "Wren's Nest," on land that became Ogden Dunes, Porter County, Indiana; buried at Oak Hill Cemetery, Gary.
  • What happened: Alice Mabel Gray died of uremic poisoning in the early morning of 9 February 1925, after Paul Wilson tapped on the window of the Ogden Dunes developer Samuel Reck on the night of 8 February and asked him to fetch a doctor. Reck drove along the frozen beach to Gary for Dr. De Long; she was already in a coma and died about 5 a.m. "in Paul's arms." Wikipedia's page for her gives 8 March 1930, heart attack — but the article Wikipedia cites for it (David Hoppe, "Child of the Northwest Wind," Traces of Indiana and Midwestern History, Spring 1997) itself says she "was buried in February 1925" and quotes the Indianapolis News obituary "Diana of Dunes Dies As She Lived, In North Indiana Sand." So the Wikipedia date contradicts its own footnote; 1925 is the date. Other details from the National Park Service and Hoppe: she took a University of Chicago degree, studied higher mathematics in Germany, worked as a "computer" at the U.S. Naval Observatory, and left the city by train on Halloween 1915, aged 34. Paul Wilson moved into her cabin around late 1918; in 1922 Deputy Sheriff Eugene Frank attacked them both without evidence, fracturing her skull. Hoppe reports that the photographer Ted Urice, researching in the 1970s, found no marriage licence and concluded that Wilson's beatings caused the uremic poisoning. Her funeral in Gary "became like a carnival" with Duneland souvenirs on sale; Wilson apparently broke down over the casket, pulled a pistol, swore revenge and was restrained by police. She was buried in an unmarked grave; she had wanted cremation on a pyre atop Mount Tom, with her ashes scattered on "the Northwest Wind."
  • The odd detail: The 1930s dune-shackers were repeating the legend of a woman already five years dead — and the funeral was a souvenir stall.
  • People: Alice Mabel Gray — "Diana of the Dunes," b. 1881 · Paul Wilson — her companion; one account (Hoppe) says his real name was Paul Eisenblatter, an illegitimate relation of a Michigan City family who had served time for burglary; others say a Texas rattlesnake hunter — UNVERIFIED · Samuel Reck — Ogden Dunes developer, eyewitness · Dr. De Long — the Gary physician · Ted Urice — U.S. Steel photographer, 1970s researcher.
  • Sources: https://www.nps.gov/people/alice-gray.htm · https://odhistory.org/diana-of-the-dunes-myth-and-reality/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana_of_the_Dunes (gives 1930; read) · https://spicerweb.org/Miller/MillerHistory/Diana_TracesArticle.pdf (Hoppe; read as extracted text).
  • Confidence: High for 9 February 1925 (three sources agree, one of them Wikipedia's own citation). Medium for the abuse claim (Urice's finding as reported by Hoppe). Wilson's surname is UNVERIFIED.
  • Divergence check: holds — she died before the window and none of it touches 1916. (The "Eisenblatter" name would matter only if a source said he dropped it under wartime pressure; the pages read do not say why he used "Wilson," and in the setting the U.S. never entered the war.)
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A dead Naval Observatory mathematician the dunes will not let go of; the 1930s Duneland story is her legend told over a grave nobody marked. Cremation refused by the family is the loose thread — a Hunter Guild or IHS interest in why she wanted her ashes on the wind.

B. Crime, labour, prison and madness

The Kohler strike — company town, two dead, 47 wounded, July 1934 (lead from R03)

  • Where: The Kohler Company and the Village of Kohler (est. 1912, west of Sheboygan), Wisconsin.
  • What happened: In August 1933 Kohler workers took an AFL charter as Federal Labor Union No. 18545. On 22 June 1934 the union presented a 14-point demand for recognition; four days later management announced an indefinite shutdown, and a strike followed. On 27 July 1934 company-hired deputies, said to be answering vandalism, attacked with guns and tear gas: Lee Wakefield and Harry Englemann were killed and "47 men, women and boys were wounded, gassed, and injured." By 29 July, 250 members of the National Guard arrived. Walter Kohler blamed "outsiders and people with 'communistic affiliations.'" The dispute's sequel, a 1954 strike, lasted until December 1965 and ended with $3 million in back wages.
  • The odd detail: The killing was done by the owner's own deputies inside a village the family built around the factory.
  • People: Walter J. Kohler — company president · Lee Wakefield and Harry Englemann — strikers killed · Governor Schmedeman — called out the Guard (named only in a search summary; UNVERIFIED).
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohler_strikes (read).
  • Confidence: Medium-High (one page, but names and dates specific and consistent with a search summary of the Wisconsin Historical Society's holdings, which I did not read).
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar. Note one wobble: Kohler's "communistic affiliations" charge is a Red Scare phrase, and the setting has no Soviet Union and no Red Scare; he would have to blame someone else.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Fits the owner's principle "Germany as the patron of other people's revolutions": the "outsiders" the owner blames could be Berlin's paid organisers. A company town with its own deputies and a foundry is a natural home for a cult small enough to hide in a payroll.

Stateville, March 1931 — the chaplain who told, the wall, and the riot (lead from R29)

  • Where: Stateville Penitentiary, Crest Hill (near Joliet), Illinois.
  • What happened: Time (30 March 1931) reports that Warden Henry C. Hill accused Chaplain George Whitmeyer of having "fomented discontent, carried messages for convicts" and taken part in an escape plot. Whitmeyer's reply was that he had disclosed the plans, only to see guards "deliberately trap and shoot" three men who had climbed down the outer wall. Then came the riot: 200 inmates in the machine shop lit fires that spread to the mess hall, kitchens, laundry and factories; over a thousand prisoners broke windows and wrecked supplies in the "thick mud left by melted snow"; a Black prisoner who advanced on the warden was shot; damage was about $500,000 in two hours. A legislative committee investigated, and a second chaplain, Elegius Weir, testified about grievances over the indeterminate sentence. A search summary of another source names men killed or wounded in the noon riot (Albert Yarbeck, George Jakowanis, Joseph Kwoka, Mike Casselli) and a Captain D. A. Davenport with a broken arm — UNVERIFIED, page not read. (R03's other Stateville lead, a dust storm foiling an escape on 12 May 1934, was not found.)
  • The odd detail: The chaplain who said he warned the prison was the one the warden accused; the three dead men had been "allowed to climb down."
  • People: Henry C. Hill — warden · George Whitmeyer — chaplain, whistle-blower · Elegius Weir — chaplain, witness.
  • Sources: https://time.com/archive/6746840/crime-at-stateville/ (read; the page is dated 30 March 1931).
  • Confidence: Medium-High for the Time account; Low for the casualty names.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A prison chaplain who knew when the wall would be climbed is a natural IHS eye inside; the "three men allowed to climb down" is a murder the state will not investigate — good for an FBI Section 13 case about what the condemned men were hiding.

The Milwaukee "Mad Bomber," 26 Oct – 3 Nov 1935: what the earlier chapters missed (lead from R06, R07, R27)

  • Where: Shorewood Village Hall (3930 N. Murray Ave.); First Wisconsin National Bank branches at 3602 W. Villard and at Farwell and North; the Fifth District Police Station (N. 3rd and Hadley); the Third District Station (12th and Vine); a sheet-iron shed behind 2121 W. Mitchell St., Milwaukee.
  • What happened: R27 already tells the campaign. This adds what the historic-preservation blog "Lost Milwaukee #2: Eight Days of Terror in 1935" gives: the two — Hugh "Idzy" Rutkowski (leader) and Paul "Shrimp" Chovanec (16, spelled Chevanek and Chovonec elsewhere) — stole about 150 lb of explosive from a CCC project (a search summary of another page says "150 sticks of dynamite" from the Estabrook Park CCC camp, and that the garage was rented for them by Chovanec). Bombs: Shorewood Village Hall; both bank branches on 28 October, 30 minutes apart; the Fifth and Third District police stations on Halloween at 6:45 and 7:00 p.m. On Sunday 3 November the "super bomb" they were building went off: both boys were killed instantly, Patricia Mylnarek (9) next door was killed, 11 were injured and 40 buildings damaged. Rutkowski had mailed the police barely literate demand letters ("Polis gard sity ha ha ha — poooey on g mans") and police believed the target was a downtown movie theatre. He was identified four days later from fingerprints on a charred, severed hand found on a garage roof. The remains were displayed at the county morgue "visited by thousands," then buried together in one casket at Forest Home Cemetery; 25,000 filed past Patricia's casket in a two-day wake and 2,400 attended her funeral.
  • The odd detail: Thousands lined up to look at the bombers' remains — and 25,000 to see a nine-year-old's coffin.
  • People: Hugh "Idzy" Rutkowski — bomber (age given as 19, 20 and 21 in different sources) · Paul "Shrimp" Chovanec — his 16-year-old follower · Patricia Mylnarek — 9, killed (R27 spells "Malnarek").
  • Sources: http://historicmilwaukee.blogspot.com/2011/02/lost-milwaukee-2-eight-days-of-terror.html (read). onmilwaukee.com's "The Mad Bomber of Milwaukee" returned 403; a search summary of it gave the 26 October Saturday first blast, which fits the calendar (3 November 1935 was a Sunday, so 26 October was a Saturday) — the blog's "October 27" for the first blast is therefore probably a slip.
  • Confidence: Medium-High for the sequence; the ages, the first date and the "lb vs sticks" figure are inconsistent across sources.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar (the CCC is a Roosevelt program, and Roosevelt is in office in the setting from 1933).
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Two teenagers with New Deal dynamite and a "super bomb" for a downtown theatre; the 16-year-old "dominated." Who was teaching them? A Milwaukee Polish street cult, a Golden Circle recruiter, or nobody — the "cult versus population" principle says the city itself came to gawk.

Agnes Tufverson, vanished December 1933 (lead from R28)

  • Where: New York, the liner Olympic and the Atlantic; her residence is disputed (see below).
  • What happened: Agnes Tufverson married Captain Ivan Poderjay, who was already married to Suzanne Marguerite Ferrand. The couple were booked on the liner Hamburg for a honeymoon on 20 December 1933; Poderjay instead sailed alone on the Olympic on 22 December. Agnes had withdrawn $25,000 in cash from her bank on the day they were to sail. A telegram in her name was sent from London to her sisters on 2 January 1934; nothing after. Austrian police arrested Poderjay in Vienna in June 1934 and found her belongings in his and Ferrand's apartment. New York police inspected his Olympic stateroom on 26 June 1934 and found the porthole "easily large enough for an average sized woman's body to pass through." He was extradited by January 1935, convicted of bigamy (2½ to 5 years) though the judge said murder charges were warranted; released 1 February 1940 and returned to Belgrade with Ferrand. One blog page (read) and a book blurb seen only in search results call her a 43-year-old New York lawyer (the blog: "New York's Most Intelligent Woman" the year before); The History Press article calls her "a 34-year-old woman from Chicago" — so R28's "Chicago/Michigan-born" is UNVERIFIED, and this looks like a New York case with a possible Midwest family tie.
  • The odd detail: The one piece of evidence is a porthole and a telegram that arrived from London after she vanished.
  • People: Agnes Tufverson — vanished · Ivan Poderjay — "Yugoslav captain," convicted bigamist · Suzanne Marguerite Ferrand — his existing wife.
  • Sources: https://thehistorypress.co.uk/article/the-disappearance-of-agnes-tufverson/ · https://alwaystimefortruecrimepodcast.wordpress.com/2022/05/09/the-disappearance-of-agnes-tufverson/ (both read; they disagree on her age and home city and on when the couple met).
  • Confidence: Low-Medium (blog-grade sources; timeline conflicts).
  • Divergence check: BENDS — Poderjay is a "captain in the Yugoslav army," and Yugoslavia was created from Serbia and Habsburg lands in 1918. In a setting where Germany won and Austria-Hungary survives as a junior partner, whether a Yugoslav state exists is not settled by the brief; the Vienna arrest still works.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A locked-room disappearance on a liner: a rich professional woman, a phoney Balkan officer, a porthole. A missing-persons file marked "Unfound" for the Secret Service's old guard.

C. Religion and the occult

The Moorish Science Temple's bloody 1929 — the September shootout (also in 09-22 archive: C09; lead from R05)

  • Where: The Moorish Science Temple of America, Chicago's South Side: Unity Hall on Indiana Avenue; Ira Johnson's home.
  • What happened: The year's sequence, from Wikipedia's page on the Temple: on 15 March 1929 Claude Green-Bey, the temple's business manager, who had split off after a funds dispute and declared himself Grand Sheik, was stabbed to death at Unity Hall on Indiana Avenue. Noble Drew Ali died at home on 20 July 1929, aged 43; the death certificate says "tuberculosis broncho-pneumonia," and followers speculated otherwise. On 25 September 1929 Chicago police, investigating the reported kidnapping of Charles Kirkman Bey by Ira Johnson, were met by gunfire from Johnson's house. The fight spilled into the neighbourhood: a policeman and a member were killed and a second policeman died later of his wounds. Police took 60 people into custody (R05 records the Tribune headline "Seize 60 After So. Side Cult Tragedy") and a reported 1,000 officers patrolled the South Side that night. Three men then claimed the succession: Edward Mealy El (named by Drew Ali), John Givens El (who claimed to be Drew Ali reincarnated) and Charles Kirkman Bey (named Grand Advisor by the governors).
  • The odd detail: A founder dies, and within ten weeks a "kidnapping" turns into a firefight in which two policemen die — and one claimant says the founder has been reborn in him.
  • People: Noble Drew Ali — founder, d. 20 July 1929 · Claude Green-Bey — stabbed 15 March 1929 · Ira Johnson (Bey) — householder accused of the kidnapping · Charles Kirkman Bey — the man reported kidnapped, later a claimant · Edward Mealy El · John Givens El — claimant by reincarnation. The two policemen's names were not found.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorish_Science_Temple_of_America (read).
  • Confidence: Medium-High for the dates and counts on the page; who fired first and the officers' names are unknown.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): FBI Section 13 has an interest in a claimant who says he is the dead prophet reborn; the period's own category "Mohammedan" is exactly the sort the owner wants used. A "kidnapping" that turns out to be a rescue of a man who was being held by his own faction.

Mr. Electrico — the identity hunt (lead from R29)

  • Where: Waukegan, Illinois, Labor Day weekend 1932 (as Bradbury told it).
  • What happened: R29 already gives Bradbury's story. This adds what the search for the man turned up. Bradbury said a sideshow performer sat in an electric chair, was hit with somewhere between 50,000 and "10 billion" volts depending on the telling, and touched children with the charged sword and told them to "live forever." Bradbury named the show as the Dill Brothers Combined Shows, and insisted it was not Sam B. Dill's Circus; no record of a Dill Brothers Combined Shows has been found. Two Bradbury scholars (Sam Weller, who wrote The Bradbury Chronicles, and the Ray Bradbury Center's director, Jason Aukerman) believe it happened but cannot corroborate it. The dating is shaky: Bradbury tied it to Labor Day, but his uncle Lester Moberg, whose funeral came soon after, was shot on 17 October 1932 and died on 24 October 1932. Labor Day 1932 records show the Hagenbeck-Wallace and Downie Brothers circuses reaching Waukegan and an American Legion festival that weekend; there are no advertisements for "Mr. Electrico." Candidates raised: the Scottish "electric chair" magician Walford Bodie (died 1939; no evidence he was in Waukegan) and Ralph Redden, who worked the Downie Brothers sideshow in 1932. A search-result summary of a 2012 Chicago magazine piece describes him as a "Cairo native, defrocked minister, and veteran of the Ardennes" — UNVERIFIED, page not read (it redirected to the Tribune).
  • The odd detail: A man who tapped a child on both shoulders and the nose with an electrified sword and said "Live, forever!" — and has left no trace in any record.
  • People: Ray Bradbury — the boy · Mr. Electrico — carnival electric-man, unidentified · Walford Bodie and Ralph Redden — unproven candidates · Lester Moberg — Bradbury's uncle, shot 17 Oct 1932.
  • Sources: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-sideshow-magician-who-inspired-ray-bradburythen-vanished-180981764/ (read).
  • Confidence: High that the identity is unresolved; Low for any candidate.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar. If the "Ardennes veteran" detail is right, it BENDS: an American veteran of the Ardennes cannot exist in a setting where the U.S. never entered the Great War.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A carnival man nobody can find who touches children with an electrified sword and tells them they will live forever is an immortality-cult recruiter — or the setting's own "three layers of truth" in miniature: the period's reading (a sideshow), the folklore (a blessing), and the thing that is actually there.

D. The lakes: three losses

The barge Material Service — the crew list, the hatches and the inquiry, 29 July 1936 (lead from R07, R30)

  • Where: Lake Michigan, 2–4 miles off South Chicago near the 96th Street area, in Indiana waters, off the Calumet Harbor breakwall. R30 already gives the ship's build and lessee (Henry Crown's Material Service Corporation); this answers R30's open "human story" lead.
  • What happened: The motor barge left Lockport at 5:31 p.m. on 28 July 1936 with about 2,400 tons of sand (2,000 tons of sand and gravel in some accounts) and 22 men aboard. About 4 a.m. on 29 July, in a heavy storm, a wave washed the deck, she listed sharply and, in under a minute, was gone; men in their bunks could not get out. Seven survived and fifteen died. The hold hatches had forty-eight 8-inch holes cut to load sand, and the captain had not ordered tarpaulins fastened over them; for over an hour waves broke across the deck and water gained on the 3-inch and 6-inch pumps faster than they could clear it, and within five minutes the bilge water stood over two feet. A federal inspector said the fault "lies with Capt. Charles D. Brown for failure to order the hatches battened down." Brown, of Port Huron, Michigan, died with her; his body was recovered on 1 August. First Mate John M. Johnson (who was on deck beside Brown and grabbed his arm as the ship rolled) was found guilty of "negligence, unskillfulness, and inattention to his duties" on 9 October and pleaded guilty on 22 October before a federal marine board at the courthouse in Chicago. Divers recovered seven bodies by 31 July and four more on 1 August; the chief engineer was found on 5 October as the 14th; the last man, Marshall Oberlin, was still in the wreck on 6 October, and a body found on 2 September at Dunes State Park in Porter County may have been a crew member. The wreck went on the National Register of Historic Places on 25 March 2014.
  • The odd detail: One of the dead may have washed ashore at the Indiana Dunes — Diana's coast — five weeks later.
  • People: Capt. Charles D. Brown — master, Port Huron, d. · John M. Johnson — first mate, Chicago, survived, pleaded guilty · Melvin Tufts (34, second mate, Lockport), Odin Johnson (chief engineer, Lemont), Leif Olson (32), Elmer Bergsland (28), Michael McEvoy (27), Fred Samuelson (29), John Melby (47), Darwin Hansen (47), Carl Brown (Sault Ste. Marie), Harry Mauer, Marshall Oberlin — among the dead · Alfred Melby (deckhand, credited his life vest), Holgar Hanson (wheelman), Herbert Larson, Joseph Weber, Elton Washburne, Joseph Change — among the survivors.
  • Sources: https://www.usdeadlyevents.com/1936-july-29-barge-material-service-sinks-lake-michigan-storm-off-south-chicago-in-15/ (read; it compiles Hammond Times, Sheboygan Press, AP/UP and a 2013 NOAA risk assessment) · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Material_Service (read).
  • Confidence: Medium-High. The compiled page has small internal slips (it calls her a "160-foot boat," where R30 has 239.6 ft; one dead man is listed twice under two names), so treat individual ages and spellings as approximate.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A Chicago building trade's sand barge lost with a captain who "failed to batten down"; one body ashore on the Dunes and one never brought up. A short mystery for a Hunter Guild diver: the man still in the wreck.

The sandsucker Sand Merchant — 17 October 1936, Lake Erie (lead from R30)

  • Where: 14 miles northwest of Cleveland, off Avon Point, Lake Erie, in U.S. waters.
  • What happened: The steel steam sandsucker Sand Merchant (1,891 tons, 252 ft, launched 1927 at Collingwood, Ontario) loaded sand at Point Pelee, Ontario, bound for Cleveland, with 26 crew under Captain McClellan. Around 9 p.m. on 17 October, in a gale with 50-mph winds, she listed and capsized. Nineteen of the 26 crew died, plus a woman passenger — the first mate's wife, who had boarded at Windsor — so about 20 dead; seven were rescued at about 8 a.m. by the freighters Bessemer and Marquette No. 2 after some ten hours clinging to capsized lifeboats, drifting within about a mile and a half of shore and back out. An inquiry found that her heavy sandsucking gear on deck made her top-heavy. A web-search summary of a different page adds the detail that second engineer Martin White's 20-year-old son Harry, saying "Try to save yourself, dad," slipped off the overturned lifeboat, and that the mate and his wife clung together and went down — not read; UNVERIFIED, and that summary gives White's age as 39 where the page I read says 50.
  • The odd detail: The survivors drifted to within about a mile and a half of shore on the overturned lifeboats and were carried back out. (A search summary of another page says 18 men and a woman were later found in life preservers - not read; UNVERIFIED.)
  • People: Capt. The tanker J. Oswald Boyd and the Marold II — free gasoline off Beaver Island, winter 1936–37 (lead from R30)

  • Where: Simmons Reef, Lake Michigan, near the approach to the Straits of Mackinac and Beaver Island, Michigan.
  • What happened: The 255-foot steamer J. Oswald Boyd, carrying "almost a million gallons of gasoline," went aground on Simmons Reef in November 1936. The captain ordered the crew off after seeing a rainbow sheen of gasoline on the water and fearing an explosion, and the Coast Guard took the crew to Mackinaw City. Everett Cole, who owned the Beaver Island Transit Company, led a salvage of the fuel barrel by barrel, using the iron-hulled ferry Marold II to break through the forming ice. By late December the Marold II "exploded and burst into flames," and five men died, including Cole and his brother. Her wreckage scattered over the ice and partly onto the Boyd. After the fire, locals drove out over the ice in their cars to take the free gas — the article says this is on archival film. A web-search summary of other pages gives the date of the grounding as 8 November, the cargo as 20,000 drums, Captain M. W. Whitney and 19 crew, and a GenDisasters headline dating the explosion to January 1937 — none read; UNVERIFIED, and the two summaries disagree with the "late December" of the page I read.
  • The odd detail: After five men died salvaging the gas, the islanders drove cars across the lake ice to help themselves.
  • People: Everett Cole — owner of Beaver Island Transit Co., killed · his brother — killed (unnamed) · three others — killed (unnamed) · Capt. M. W. Whitney — master (summary only).
  • Sources: https://lostinmichigan.net/a-shipwreck-and-free-gas/ (read). The Beaver Island Historical Society's page on the wreck and the Great Lakes Vessel History entry both loaded with no text.
  • Confidence: Medium-Low (one blog page; dates and cargo conflict across summaries).
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.

E. Politics, Germany and the fair

General Leonard Wood commands the martial-law occupation of Gary — 6 October 1919 (lead from R07)

  • Where: Gary, Indiana (with East Chicago and Indiana Harbor by one account), during the 1919 steel strike.
  • What happened: After a scuffle on 4 October between strikers and strikebreakers, the Gary mayor called out Indiana militia; on 6 October 1919 federal regulars "under the command of General Leonard Wood" came from Fort Sheridan and "martial law was at once proclaimed." By the end of that day "the entire city of Gary was controlled by the US Army," and all public rallies and picketing were prohibited. The steel strike leader William Z. Foster, in his 1920 book, says soldiers arrested strike leaders and pickets and made them split wood and sweep the streets in front of City Hall while families watched from windows; that steel officials told strikers the "U.S." in U.S. Steel meant government ownership and threatened deportation or Fort Leavenworth; and that "spectacular midnight raids" broke a strike that had been 97 percent effective. Foster also wrote that the operation might have been "staged to further his [Wood's] political fortunes." The strike "effectively ended in Gary less than a month later" though it lasted nationally until January 1920. A web-search summary of a Northwest Indiana Times centennial story gives 4,000+ federal troops and Wood "in charge of Gary, East Chicago and Indiana Harbor" — not read; UNVERIFIED. Wikipedia's page on the strike says the Army took over on 6 October but does not name Wood.
  • The odd detail: In the setting the man who becomes President for 1921–27 ran a Great Lakes steel city under martial law in 1919, and a strike leader accused him of staging it for his career.
  • People: Maj. Gen. Leonard Wood — commanding federal troops (Foster, Discover Indiana) · William Z. Foster — strike leader and author · U.S. Steel officials — unnamed on the pages I read.
  • Sources: https://discoverindianahistory.org/items/show/603 (read) · https://www.marxists.org/archive/foster/1920/great-steel-strike-its-lessons/ch10.htm (read; a partisan source) · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_strike_of_1919 (read).
  • Confidence: Medium-High that Wood commanded the troops (two independent pages); Low for troop numbers.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — in the setting Wood is President 1921–27 and dies on an operating table in Boston in August 1927, and the president during the 1919 strike is Marshall (1916–21). The strike itself is a domestic event and can hold; the Red Scare backdrop and any "veterans of the war" among the troops do not (the U.S. never entered the Great War).
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The Gary occupation is the first act of the man the setting elects and buries. What did the general learn in Gary that he carried to Washington, and who in the Army (or in the aftermath of a certain 1916 séance) wanted a steel town frightened?

Hans Weidemann, the Goebbels aide, and the fair's "religious art" — May 1933 (lead from R04)

  • Where: The pier in New York where the S.S. Columbus docked, en route to the Century of Progress in Chicago.
  • What happened: The Jewish Telegraphic Agency (26 May 1933) reports Hans Weidemann, 29, described as the right-hand man of Dr. Joseph Goebbels, arriving with an assistant, Gotthold Schneider. On the dock the German vice-consul in New York, Otto Schlitter, and police officials told him that more than 500 hostile demonstrators were waiting at the pier. Weidemann said he was going to the Chicago fair "only as a technical art adviser to the German department of religious art," and in a fifteen-minute press interrogation "asserted his complete sympathy with the anti-Jewish measures in Germany." Wikipedia has a short page for a Hans Weidemann, born 1904, German artist, photographer and film director, in the Nazi party from 1927, who directed the 1937 propaganda film Festliches Nürnberg and produced the 1942 thriller Attack on Baku; it does not mention this trip, so the identification is likely but UNVERIFIED. A search summary of a scholarly article and a Wiley abstract says 50,000 people rallied two weeks before the fair to demand that Goebbels not be welcomed as Germany's representative — not read; UNVERIFIED.
  • The odd detail: A propaganda minister's right-hand man arrives to advise on religious art, and the dock is already full of protesters.
  • People: Hans Weidemann — Goebbels's aide, 29 · Gotthold Schneider — his assistant · Otto Schlitter — German vice-consul, New York · Joseph Goebbels — Reich propaganda minister in the real record's 1933.
  • Sources: https://www.jta.org/archive/demonstration-at-pier-disturbs-goebbelss-aide-arriving-as-nazi-envoy-to-chicago-fair (read) · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Weidemann (read).
  • Confidence: High for the JTA account of the dock; Medium for the identification with the film director.
  • Divergence check: BENDS hard — there is no Nazi government and no Reich propaganda ministry; Hitler is an occult figure with no office and there is no Goebbels aide. What survives is the pattern: an emissary of a powerful Berlin patron sent to a Chicago fair under a religious-art cover.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): In the setting a Berlin emissary of an occult patron gets a Hall of Religion cover story while the fair's Jewish exhibit draws crowds. IHS and the Kabbalahs both want to know what a "German department of religious art" wants to see or borrow.

Camp Hindenburg, Grafton, Wisconsin — the Bund's summer camp and the German-Americans who bought it out (lead from R10)

  • Where: Near Grafton, Wisconsin, north of Milwaukee (a search summary says on the Milwaukee River); the marchers in the radio account were heading back to "their camp on 12th Avenue."
  • What happened: A Military History of the Upper Great Lakes page describes the German-American Bund's Camp Hindenburg: in its first year it "served a total of 103 boys from Milwaukee and Chicago," with athletics, flag-raising, inspections and limited marching; the children wore Nazi uniforms; swastikas and American flags flew together at meetings; George Froboese was the Bund's Midwest leader. The Federation of German-American Societies, an established German-American body, "bought out the land" and forced the camp to move about a mile away, then opened its own Camp Carl Schurz on the original site. A radio segment on the Bund in Grafton recalls (from an 11-year-old in 1941, now 86) German flags marched through a village of about 1,100, a police force of one officer helped by about ten, and the marchers told to take down the German flags and carry an American one — which they dropped as soon as they reached their camp. The Bund's meetings were held at the Grafton Hotel. Search summaries give 1937 as the opening year and a 1939 speech by Fritz Kuhn — neither is on the pages I read; UNVERIFIED. No acreage found.
  • The odd detail: The people who ended it were Milwaukee's own German-American clubs, who bought the land and renamed the camp for Carl Schurz.
  • People: George Froboese — Bund Midwest leader · Fritz Kuhn — national Bundesführer (mentioned, no speech documented) · Carl Schurz — 1848 German-American statesman the replacement camp was named for.
  • Sources: https://ss.sites.mtu.edu/mhugl/2016/10/19/camp-hindenburg/ (read) · https://www.wuwm.com/podcast/lake-effect-segments/2017-03-13/the-day-the-german-american-bund-marched-in-grafton (read).
  • Confidence: Medium for the camp and its takeover; Low for dates.
  • Divergence check: BENDS hard — there is no Nazi Germany and no Nazi-uniformed youth; the setting has "Germany as the patron of other people's revolutions." The structure (a Berlin-linked summer camp for the children of Milwaukee and Chicago German families, and an established German-American federation buying it out) is what survives.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A river camp where German-American children from two cities are drilled by someone from Berlin, and an old-guard German society buys the land out from under it — a Golden Circle-adjacent front, and the reason the Secret Service's "old guard" would rather it were closed quietly.

The Zenge–Bauer mutilation murder — Chicago, 31 July 1935 (lead from R06)

  • Where: A filling station at 71st Place and Stony Island Avenue, South Side; Jackson Park Hospital; the Masterbilt Hotel, 4015 Lake Park Avenue; the Criminal Court (Judge Cornelius J. Harrington).
  • What happened: Early on Wednesday 31 July 1935 Dr. Walter J. Bauer, 38, a chemistry professor at the Kirksville (Missouri) College of Osteopathy and Surgery, was found mutilated with a pocketknife and dying at a South Side filling station, having been driven out from Ann Arbor, where he was at summer school. He died at Jackson Park Hospital after giving a deathbed statement that "Jones" had done it. Police arrested Mandeville W. Zenge, 26, a dairy farmer's son from Canton, Missouri, at a taxicab garage at 4642 West Madison Street at 3:30 a.m. on Thursday; he gave the names "Orville Smith" and "Al Johnson" until his father, J. Andy Zenge, arrived. The motive was a seven-year courtship: Zenge had bought rings for Louise Schaffer, a 23-year-old Kirksville nurse, who on 14 July told him she had married Dr. Bauer only hours before; friends quoted him saying he would "fix that doctor so he won't be able to treat another girl that way." The Tribune reports that Bauer had checked into the Masterbilt Hotel on 25 July as "E. L. Jones" — which of the two men used the alias "Jones" is UNVERIFIED on the OCR text I read. At trial (October 1935) Assistant State's Attorney Charles S. Dougherty's key witness was a taxi driver, William Leinnert, whom Zenge befriended in taverns and who told police; Leinnert said Zenge told him "he had cut Dr. Bauer... but had not intended to kill him." A 17-year-old newsboy, Herman Eichner (Pichner in an earlier issue) of 6133 Champlain, pointed Zenge out — but on cross-examination admitted police had shown him Zenge first. The defense, led by Joseph Roach, asked the jury to find insanity ("one of the strangest defenses ever heard in a murder trial," per the Tribune), and floated a jurisdiction argument that the fatal wound (a "brain clot due to the penknife operation") may have happened over the line in Indiana. Defense alienists were Dr. Harold S. Hulbert (of the Loeb–Leopold case) and Dr. Franz Alexander, a psychoanalyst formerly of Berlin; Dr. Harry Hoffman of the Criminal Court behavior clinic testified for the state. The Tribune of 19 October 1935 reports the verdict: guilty, life in the penitentiary, after 17 ballots and about 4½ hours (the first ballot had one vote for death and eight for life; foreman Edward Kieson); at least 20 years before parole; Zenge could be moved to a state security hospital if insanity developed. (A search summary says the Michigan Daily covered it from Ann Arbor and calls the injury "emasculation" — not read.)
  • The odd detail: The dying man named a "Jones" and the hotel register holds another "E. L. Jones"; and one of the defense's alienists was a psychoanalyst "formerly of Berlin."
  • People: Dr. Walter J. Bauer — victim, 38 · Mandeville W. Zenge — convicted, 26 · Louise Schaffer Bauer — the nurse, widow · J. Andy Zenge — father · William Leinnert — cab driver, prosecution witness · Herman Eichner — newsboy witness · Judge Cornelius J. Harrington · Charles S. The Marchand "witch murder" — the federal-versus-state fight over the defence (lead from R22; the verdicts are already confirmed in V_R22)

  • Where: Buffalo, New York; the Seneca and Cayuga homes searched by the district attorney; Washington.
  • What happened: R22 tells the crime and V_R22 confirms the verdicts (Nancy Bowen pleaded guilty to manslaughter; Lila Jimerson was acquitted at her second trial). This adds the politics, from Time (7 April 1930) and Wikipedia's page on Alice Lee Jemison. Time: Nancy Bowen, 66 (R22 says 60; Time calls her Cayuga, others Seneca), killed Mrs. Marchand with a hammer and chloroform-soaked paper after Mrs. Marchand, asked "Are you a witch?", jestingly answered yes; Lila "Red Lilac" Jimerson (39 in Time; R22 says 35) had told Bowen that Mrs. Marchand caused the death of Bowen's husband Charley. When the trial began, the U.S. District Attorney Richard Harkness Templeton tried to take over Jimerson's defence on the orders of Attorney General William DeWitt Mitchell, and the state prosecutor protested: "I deeply resent this eleventh-hour and unwarranted interference by Washington bureaucracy!" Wikipedia's Jemison page: District Attorney Guy Moore treated it as an "Indian" crime and ran warrantless searches of Seneca and Cayuga homes; the Seneca journalist and advocate Alice Lee Jemison, with Chief Clinton Rickard and Seneca President Ray Jimerson, appealed to Vice President Charles Curtis, and the Bureau of Indian Affairs arranged for a U.S. Attorney to represent the two women. Jemison's page says nothing about witchcraft; Time does not mention the ouija board that R22's press sources do.
  • The odd detail: The witch accusation ended in a jurisdiction fight — a Buffalo prosecutor calling the Department of Justice a "Washington bureaucracy" for defending a Seneca woman.
  • People: Nancy Bowen · Lila Jimerson · Clothilde Marchand — victim · Henri Marchand — the artist, at the Buffalo Natural History Museum (Time) · Richard Harkness Templeton — U.S. District Attorney · William DeWitt Mitchell — Attorney General · Guy Moore — district attorney at Buffalo · Alice Lee Jemison — Seneca advocate · Charles Curtis — Vice President.
  • Sources: https://time.com/archive/6778465/national-affairs-witch-murder/ (read) · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Lee_Jemison (read) · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Marchand_(sculptor) (read; thin).
  • Confidence: High for the Time quotations; Medium for the Jemison summary.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar. Attorney General Mitchell and Vice President Curtis are Hoover-era offices, and the setting has Hoover as President 1929–33; who its Vice President was is UNVERIFIED.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): In the setting a federal agency — the Secret Service's old guard, or the Bureau's Section 13 — has reason to send its own lawyer to defend a "witch" nobody else will. What did the U.S. Attorney's men learn about Jimerson's grave-dirt and ouija board that the state's trial never heard?

R24 — Almanac of Chicago and the Great Lakes, 1 October 1936 – 15 January 1937

  • Lane: R24 — a day-by-day almanac (weather, sky, papers, city) for the stretch the 2023 alpha was set in (December 1936), plus the autumn that led into it (Pacelli's tour, election week) and the first fortnight of 1937.
  • Date: 2026-09-26
  • Model: Claude Fable 5.1 (research agent; WebSearch exhausted — built from WebFetch on known pages, the Chronicling America JSON search, Wikipedia's API, timeanddate.com, NASA's eclipse catalogue and daily-climate tables)
  • How to use: find the day of your session. Weather is Chicago (the NOAA/NCEI co-operative record USC00111577, "Chicago Midway Airport 3 SW" — not the Weather Bureau's city station ⟨fixed: V_R24⟩) — high/low in °F, precipitation in inches; a value marked est. is interpolated or from a neighbouring day. Sky is moon phase and sunrise/sunset for Chicago, computed at UTC−6 (Central Standard Time). But Chicago's own clocks were on Eastern Standard Time (UTC−5) from 1 March to 15 November 1936 (IANA tz database, America/Chicago: "-5:00 - EST 1936 Nov 15 2:00" — https://raw.githubusercontent.com/eggert/tz/main/northamerica ), so for any time before 15 Nov add one hour to get what a Chicago clock read; from 15 Nov on (the whole of December, the solstice and the eclipse) the CST times stand as given ⟨fixed: V_R24⟩. In the papers quotes real headlines as printed, with paper, page and the URL read; Chicago and Great Lakes first, then national and world. In the city is what a Chicagoan could see, hear or buy that day. Divergence note flags where the setting's 1916 break bends a headline. Everything under "Umbra use" is a proposal, not a ruling.
  • Sources used (all read this session; per-day URLs inline): see the closing "Sources" list.
  • Divergence note for the whole file: the setting's timeline broke in 1916 (Wilson removed; America never entered the Great War; Germany won; no Nazi Germany, no Soviet Union; Roosevelt president from 1933). Ordinary domestic Chicago news holds. Anything touching Berlin, Moscow, the Spanish war's foreign backers, the British crown's standing, or the League bends, and each such day says so.

Overview

This almanac covers the 107 days from 1 October 1936 to 15 January 1937 — every day has a real Chicago temperature and rainfall (NOAA's Midway co-op record), the key days have USNO sunrise/sunset and moon data, and the world's news is keyed to Wikipedia's day-by-day month pages with real Indiana wire-service front pages quoted where they carried the Chicago story. The findings that matter most for the alpha: the solstice fell at 6:27 pm Chicago time on Monday 21 December (22 December only by Greenwich time — the owner's "22nd" is the catalogue date, and the 22nd itself was a clear, cold 38°/16° day [UNVERIFIED — V_R24: the NOAA record has temperature and precipitation only, no sky cover; "clear" is inferred from the dry day and wide spread] with a 64% moon high at dusk); the eclipse was entirely a Sunday-evening event in Chicago, 7:51–11:46 pm on 27 December, on a day of cold rain and a passing front, penumbral and so faint that no newspaper in the Indiana archive mentioned it; Christmas Eve was 55° and Christmas Day 57° with rain — no snow anywhere in the alpha's week; and Pacelli's Great Lakes day is now hour-by-hour — 2 pm at Bendix airport, the doctorate at Notre Dame on the feast of Christ the King, and, "despite threatening weather," the flight to Chicago to sleep as Mundelein's guest on the rainy night of Sunday 25 October (the Scholastic's account, read in full). Around those anchors: Landon (9 Oct, in a downpour) and Roosevelt (14 Oct) at the Chicago Stadium; the Speaker of the Illinois House dying with his name on the ballot and Richard J. Daley taking his seat on election day; the Granville Avenue "L" wreck of 24 November (nine or ten dead at rush hour); Ernest R. Graham's death; a 15-year-old's Bohème debut on a day that fell from 39° to 2°; Chicago's first All-American dead by illuminating gas; the sit-down winter from South Bend (Bendix, ~18 Nov) through Detroit and Cleveland to Flint (30 Dec) and the Running Bulls (11 Jan); the abdication as heard in Chicago; and the two most useful negatives — Armistice Day, which the setting cannot have, and the Anti-Comintern/Soviet/Nazi headlines that fall out of every week. Everything under "Umbra use" is a proposal.

The eclipse, in Chicago time (Sunday evening, 27 December 1936): NASA row 09505 — penumbral (type N), Saros 143, gamma −1.0970, penumbral magnitude 0.8451, umbral magnitude −0.1550 (the Moon missed the dark umbra by about a sixth of its diameter), penumbral duration 235.5 minutes. Greatest eclipse 03:49:09 TD on the 28th (Wikipedia gives 03:48:45 UT; the difference is ΔT). Contacts, converted: penumbra first touches the Moon 7:51 pm CST; greatest eclipse 9:49 pm; penumbra leaves 11:46 pm (Wikipedia's P1 01:50:57 / P4 05:46:28 UT). The Moon rose at 4:11 pm that day, was full at 10:00 pm and crossed the meridian at 11:56 pm — so at greatest eclipse it stood high in the east-southeast. What a Chicagoan would see: no "bite" at all; at most a dusky, smoky shading across one side of a very bright full moon for an hour either side of 9:49 pm. A magnitude-0.85 penumbral eclipse is at the edge of naked-eye visibility. The Indiana papers on Chronicling America's partner archive have no "eclipse" story for 26–29 Dec 1936 (Hoosier State Chronicles search, zero hits) — the press mostly ignored it. And it rained: 0.67 in. fell in Chicago on the 27th as a cold front took the temperature from 54° to 33° (see the day entry). Whether the sky had cleared by 8 pm is UNVERIFIED.

The solstice: see the entry for 21/22 December (USNO seasons table fetched for the exact Chicago hour; the owner's source gave the 22nd).

Daylight: the Sun rose at 5:56 am and set at 5:19 pm CST on 9 October (6:56 am / 6:19 pm on Chicago's EST clocks); by election day (3 Nov) 6:25 am / 4:42 pm CST (7:25 am / 5:42 pm EST clock time) ⟨fixed: V_R24⟩; by 1 December 6:59 am / 4:20 pm; the earliest sunsets of the year were about 4:20 pm (USNO gives 4:20 pm on both 1 and 11 Dec, so the earliest-sunset stretch runs through early December, not only 11–13 Dec ⟨fixed: V_R24⟩); at the solstice 7:15 am / 4:23 pm; on New Year's Day 7:18 am / 4:30 pm; by 15 January the sunset was creeping back toward 4:45 pm. Civil twilight ended about 32 minutes after sunset (4:55 pm at Christmas). In December the Loop's lights were on by half past four.

Weather source for every day below: NOAA/NCEI GHCN-Daily, station USC00111577 "Chicago Midway Airport 3 SW" (the co-operative record at the Municipal Airport, the closest continuous daily record for the city in 1936), fetched as JSON from https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=daily-summaries&stations=USC00111577&startDate=1936-10-01&endDate=1937-01-15&dataTypes=TMAX,TMIN,PRCP,SNOW,SNWD&format=json&units=standard . Format: high/low °F, precipitation in inches (rain + melted snow), snowfall. The record at this station has no rows for 1–4 October and 7 October 1936 (station gap). The Loop, warmed by the lake and the buildings, typically ran a degree or two milder than the airport on cold nights.

Chicago's papers in this window (general knowledge, not re-checked this session): the Chicago Daily Tribune (morning, Col. Robert R. McCormick, fiercely anti-Roosevelt), the Chicago Daily News (evening; its publisher Frank Knox was the Republican vice-presidential nominee this autumn), Hearst's Herald and Examiner (morning) and Chicago American (evening), the tabloid Chicago Daily Times (evening, pro-New Deal), the weekly Chicago Defender (the Black paper), plus the Abendpost (German), Dziennik Chicagoski and Dziennik Związkowy (Polish). None of these are in the free public-domain archives, so the real headlines quoted below come from the Washington Evening Star (Chronicling America) and Indiana dailies (Indianapolis Times, Greencastle Daily Banner, Decatur Daily Democrat) on Hoosier State Chronicles — wire-service papers that carried the same AP/UP Chicago stories the Chicago dailies ran.

October 1936

Thu 1 – Sun 4 October 1936

  • In the papers: the World Series (Yankees v.   • In the city: Sat 3 Oct — Northwestern 18, Iowa 7 at Dyche Stadium, Evanston (season opener); Notre Dame 21, Carnegie Tech 7 at South Bend. Top 15 days for a GM

  1. Sun 25 Oct 1936 — Pacelli's Great Lakes day. 2 pm at Bendix airport, South Bend; Notre Dame's doctorate on the feast of Christ the King; "despite threatening weather" flew on to Chicago as Mundelein's overnight guest. Rain, 53°, a 73% moon behind cloud. The only night the future Pope slept in Chicago.
  2. Sun 27 Dec 1936 — the eclipse night. Full moon 10:00 pm CST; penumbral eclipse 7:51–11:46 pm, greatest 9:49; 0.67 in. of rain and a cold front that day; no paper noticed. The alpha's "28th" is the Greenwich date.
  3. Mon 21 / Tue 22 Dec — the solstice. 6:27 pm CST on the 21st (= 22 Dec UT). The 22nd was clear and cold, 38°/16° [UNVERIFIED — V_R24: "clear" is inferred; the record has no sky cover], with a 64% moon high at dusk.
  4. Tue 24 Nov — the Granville Avenue "L" wreck. North Shore express at 40 mph into a stopped train at 6 pm; wooden car shattered; 9–10 dead, 58 hurt; motorman Grooms's story disbelieved.
  5. Tue 3 Nov — election day. 60°/31°; Roosevelt 57.7% in Illinois; Horner beats Brooks after beating the Kelly–Nash machine; Richard J. Daley elected on the dead Speaker's line.
  6. Sun 18 Oct — Speaker Shanahan dies with his name on the ballot, a secret wife and a million-dollar estate.
  7. Thu 24 – Fri 25 Dec — a 55°/57° Christmas with rain, a near-full moon over the Midnight Masses at Holy Name.
  8. Mon 14 Dec — Clarence Herschberger, the West's first All-American, dead by illuminating gas in a 64th Street basement.
  9. Wed 30 Dec — Flint sits down (and Cleveland on the 28th; Kelsey-Hayes in Detroit mid-month; Bendix in South Bend from ~18 Nov).
  10. Fri 9 Oct — Landon at the Chicago Stadium in 1.37 in. of rain; Wed 14 Oct — Roosevelt in the same hall, "nearly a million" on the route.
  11. Sun 22 Nov — Ernest R. Graham dies at 25 Banks Street: the Mart, the Opera House, the Post Office, Union Station, the Field Museum, all his.
  12. Sun 6 Dec — a 15-year-old's Bohème debut at the Civic Opera House on a day that fell from 39° to 2°; her MGM contract approved in probate court on the 9th.
  13. Thu 10 – Fri 11 Dec — the abdication, heard in Chicago in mid-afternoon; in a Britain that lost the war, a bigger story still.
  14. Sat 28 Nov — the International Live Stock Exposition opens at the Amphitheatre under a full moon; the Yards full of strangers for a week.
  15. Wed 11 Nov — Armistice Day: the holiday that does not exist in the setting. The most useful negative in the almanac.

Honourable mentions: the Halloween-eve full moon (29 Oct, 11:58 pm CST = 12:58 am 30 Oct on Chicago's EST clocks ⟨fixed: V_R24⟩) and Northwestern's 6–0 over Minnesota before 48,347 (31 Oct); Lights Out on WENR every Wednesday after 11 pm with Arch Oboler's "Catwife"; Coronet's first issue from Esquire's Chicago office (23 Oct); LIFE's first issue and the first snow (23 Nov); Mutual coast-to-coast from WGN (29 Dec); the Running Bulls at Flint on a moonless night (11 Jan); Bogart's Black Legion (late Jan).

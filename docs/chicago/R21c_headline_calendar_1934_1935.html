R21c — "What's in the paper", 1934 and 1935: headline calendar for the Dillinger year and its aftermath

Written 2026-09-30; months covered: January through December 1934 (all twelve months; March 1934 is also partly in R21); January through April 1935 in full, May 1935 only May 1-4 (the Indianapolis Times run ends there), June-October 1935 from a second title (the Decatur Daily Democrat), and November-December 1935 from the Times again. Only May 5-31, 1935 is left unread in these two years (see "Months still missing").

  • Lane: R21c — continues R21 (R21_headline_calendar.md) and R21b (R21b_headline_calendar_continued.md); same line format, same rules.
  • Date: 2026-09-30
  • Model: Claude Sonnet 5.5 (research agent; two Sonnet sub-passes wrote February-May 1935 from the same cached OCR under the same format)
  • Method: the Indianapolis Times (Scripps-Howard, United Press wire) front pages, read from Hoosier State Chronicles (the Indiana State Library's Veridian site). Each front page's OCR comes from https://newspapers.library.in.gov/?a=d&d=IPT<YYYYMMDD>.1.1&f=XML (the <PageTextHTML> element); the citable page URL is the same without &f=XML. The Indianapolis Times carried Chicago and Great Lakes wire stories (United Press datelines: Chicago, Detroit, Cleveland, Milwaukee, Toledo, Akron, Gary, South Bend, Michigan City, Minneapolis-St. Paul, Buffalo, Toronto) on page one, so a script (hsc.py, then scan.py, in the session scratchpad r21d\) fetched every issue's page one for 1934-01-01 to 1935-05-31 and 1935-11-01 to 1935-12-31 (about 585 pages, one request per page, polite pacing, no retries beyond the script's own 429 backoff). For June-October 1935, when the Times is not in the archive, the same script read another title on the same site with the same United Press wire, the Decatur Daily Democrat (Decatur, Ind.; ids DCDD<YYYYMMDD>.1.1, about 150 more pages) and pulled every paragraph whose opening named one of those places. Headlines are quoted as printed from the OCR; obvious OCR breakage silently repaired; doubtful readings marked [OCR]; a line where only the sub-headline ("deck") survived is marked [deck only]; a line where only a kicker or no headline is recoverable is marked [kicker only] or [paraphrase] and summarizes the lede, exactly as R21/R21b do. Every line carries the page URL that was read this session.
  • What did not work: Chronicling America (loc.gov) and the Illinois Digital Newspaper Collections both answered with a Cloudflare "verify you are human" page; no attempt was made to get past it. So the Chicago dailies remain unread; everything here is a United Press wire front page (the Indianapolis Times for 1934-May 1935 and Nov-Dec 1935, the Decatur Daily Democrat for June-October 1935).
  • Conventions: dates are the issue date (a UP wire dateline "CHICAGO, Jan. 9.—" etc.). "Umbra use" lines are proposals only, at most one per month. The Secret Service and FBI Section 13 are two separate, rival federal factions; J. Edgar Hoover directs the FBI (the Times calls its agents "Department of Justice agents" or "G-men"), not the Secret Service.
  • Divergence note (applies to this whole file): anything touching Germany, Russia, the Nazis/Fascists, the WWI settlement, the League of Nations, the empire, or the presidents before Hoover BENDS under the 1916 break; ordinary domestic Chicago/Great Lakes news holds. The Dillinger/Barker-Karpis/Baby Face Nelson/Touhy manhunts, the Insull trial, the Century of Progress, the drought and the Toledo/Minneapolis/Milwaukee strikes are domestic and hold. Items about Hitler, Mussolini, the Ethiopian war, the League and Britain's ministers that appeared on the same pages were skipped on purpose.

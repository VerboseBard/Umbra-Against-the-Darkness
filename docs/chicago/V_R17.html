The two headline checks

  1. Klutas = the "College Kidnappers": holds. Wikipedia (Sifakis; Newton) names the ten-man ring and its leader, the U. of I. link and the half-million; the 1933–34 wire copy (Edwardsville Intelligencer, Helena Independent, Evening Star) independently calls it the "collegiate" kidnap band and names Klutas as its head. Softened only on "exactly two years".
  2. "Posed … as a Government Secret Service agent": holds. Re-read in the Evening Star OCR (7 Jan 1934, p. 2, AP): "…the bungalow where Klutas roomed and where he 'posed to the owner as a Government Secret Service agent.'" It is in quotation marks in the original — the AP quoting officers — and the Helena Independent's separate wire copy says the same thing in other words ("living under the guise of a secret service operative"). What does not hold is the companion claim that he had altered his face and prints: that was a rumour Coghlan relayed in Nov 1933, and at death "fingerprints established the identities of Klutas and Detrick."

Divergence problems

  • Secret Service ≠ Section 13 (coordinator's correction, applied): Section 13 is K. Edgar Hoover's Bureau; the Secret Service is a separate, rival faction. R17 conflated them in five places — §3 note ("In this setting the Secret Service is Section 13"), §3 hooks (O'Hare as "Section 13's man"/"Section 13's asset"), §1 counterfeiting hook ("Section 13's cover story … the paper the real Bureau used" — also factually off: counterfeiting was Treasury's/the Secret Service's jurisdiction, not the Bureau's), §5 hook ("a Section 13 liaison … The Service's interest"), §7 hook ("Section 13 took it … why the Service wanted it"). All fixed.
  • J. Edgar → K. Edgar: §7 "The night of the body" and "The death masks" were labelled "holds — domestic calendar" though they turn on the Bureau and its director; relabelled BENDS (the director is K. Edgar Hoover, Section 13's head).
  • Soviet Union / Holodomor: §2's two entries are correctly labelled BENDS. But its divergence note used the real 1918 Brest-Litovsk settlement as if it held "in the setting's terms"; that treaty was signed with the Bolshevik government and a post-revolution Ukrainian republic, so in a world with no Soviet Union it cannot be assumed. Rewritten as the owner's call.
  • Presidents after Wilson: only Roosevelt appears (1937, "a plague on both your houses") — consistent with the setting (Roosevelt 1933–). No Harding/Coolidge references.
  • "Canon": the word appears only in the brief's own label "(proposal, not canon)"; no hook calls itself canon.
  • Worth the owner's notice (not an error): the §3 informant entry is a real-record Secret Service story (Wilson became its chief in 1937) — in the setting it belongs to the Secret Service faction, which fits the rulebook line that "the Service knew what Capone was and took him anyway". Also: Coroner Frank J. Walsh, whose jury cleared the Memorial Day police (§5), is the coroner whose investigators found O'Hare's threat letter (§3) — a second official who threads two seeds.

R15 — Immigrant and Ethnic Chicago in the 1930s

  • Lane: R15 — each community's institutions, leaders, press, feuds, folk beliefs, and what "the old country" meant to them. (Black Chicago = R14; places = R09.)
  • Date: 2026-09-26
  • Model: Claude Opus 5.5 (research agent)
  • Status: complete — 64 entries.

Overview

The richest source for this lane turned out to be the WPA Foreign Language Press Survey (Newberry Library): 1930s English translations of Chicago's own German, Polish, Czech, Slovak, Italian, Yiddish, Lithuanian, Ukrainian, Hungarian, Greek, Croatian, Serbian, Albanian, Chinese, Filipino and Mexican papers. They give the period's own voice — the first "layer of truth" — with names, addresses and numbers. Three findings matter most for the setting. First, the 1916 break rewrites almost every community's "old country" and several Chicago organizations become something new: the Ukrainian Sitch/Hetmanites (monarchists loyal to a Hetman whom Germany installed in 1918, drilling in the Illinois National Guard's 132nd Infantry and blessing their own airplanes in 1934) would be the overseas arm of a surviving German client state; Chicago's Czech independence war-chest, its Hungarian "Justice for Hungary" revisionists, its Croat–Serb feud over "the Jugoslav regime" and its Greek royalist quarrel all invert when Austria-Hungary survives and Germany wins; Polonia's great 1931 fear — Berlin getting the Corridor back — is simply the setting's world. Second, the German community in the real 1930s was already being courted by a consulate (the Hindenburg memorial of 1934 with the Stahlhelm in the chancel; the former consul's 1936 remark on the "rapid conversion" of Chicago Germans); remove the swastika and the pattern survives as "Germany, patron of other people's revolutions." Third, folk belief is richly documented by the communities' own hostile press: Polish witch-doctors' cures (bats buried at night in unglazed pots, matted hair under the threshold), All Souls' suppers left on graves, the 1913 Hull-House Devil Baby (Italian and Jewish versions, in Jane Addams's own words), an excommunicated polyglot priest said to head the Black Hand (1913), a 1933 Jewish pageant of 6,200 performers acting out Moloch before 125,000 people. Where a community's folklore is given (evil eye, banshee, strigoi, curanderismo, dybbuk, powwow), it is labelled FOLKLORE. The Encyclopedia of Chicago did not resolve this session; Irish, Syrian/Lebanese and Japanese Chicago are thinner than the rest (the FLPS has no Irish or Japanese section) — see Leads.

A. The whole map

The city by blood, as the German press counted it — 1 August 1930

  • Where: Chicago (city-wide)
  • What happened: On 1 Aug 1930 the German daily Abendpost printed its reading of the new census, counting people "of foreign descent regardless of birthplace." Its figures: German descent ~350,000 (vs 112,228 foreign-born in 1920); Poles up from 137,611 (1920) to ~400,000; the "Negro population" almost doubled to ~200,000; ~175,000 Irish; 75,000 Swedes; 30,000 Danes; 150,000 Italians; 150,000 Czechs; 60,000 Greeks; 2,500 Chinese; 300 Japanese. The WBEZ summary of the real census gives 1930 as the peak of immigrant Chicago: ~859,000 foreign-born plus 1.3 million children of immigrants, ~2.2 million people in a city of ~3.4 million.
  • The odd detail: the German paper was quietly conceding that the Poles had passed the Germans. The two biggest "tribes" of Chicago in 1930 are the two whose old countries the 1916 break changes most.
  • People: —
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/id/5418474_9_1637 ; https://www.wbez.org/curious-city/2014/02/10/native-numbers-how-many-chicagoans-were-born-in-the-city (search-result summary; not opened)
  • Confidence: High for what the Abendpost printed; Medium for the numbers themselves (a newspaper's estimate, mixing foreign-born and descent).
  • Divergence check: BENDS — in the setting America never fought Germany, so the German count carries none of the 1917–18 shame that made people stop saying "German" to the census-taker; the German figure may plausibly run higher and prouder.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): a ready-made table for character creation: roll or pick "descent" against these proportions so a Chicago party looks like 1930 Chicago.

B. German Chicago

The war that renamed things — Dec 1917 to 1921 (and why it never happens in Umbra)

  • Where: North Side and Loop — Bismarck Gardens, SW corner of Grace & Halsted (3760 N. Halsted); Bismarck Hotel, Randolph & Wells; Germania Club, 108 W. Germania Place.
  • What happened: When America entered the war (6 Apr 1917) German names came down across Chicago. The Eitel brothers' Bismarck Gardens beer garden (opened 31 May 1896, 4,500 diners by 1914) became the Marigold Gardens in December 1917 — the Eitels resisted until public pressure won. Their Bismarck Hotel was "temporarily renamed" the Randolph Hotel. The Germania Club (founded 1865 as the Germania Männerchor, which sang at Lincoln's Chicago funeral) became the Lincoln Club, and reverted in 1921. Lubeck, Frankfort and Hamburg Streets became Dickens, Charleston and Shakespeare. Downstate, German-born Robert Prager was dragged from the Collinsville jail and lynched as a "spy" (Apr 1918).
  • The odd detail: the Eitels named the hotel "with permission from Otto von Bismarck"; in the 1930s the rebuilt Bismarck Hotel (1926, Randolph/LaSalle/Wells, 600 beds, 2,500-seat Palace Theater) is where the German World's Fair committee meets (see below).
  • People: Emil (1865–1948), Karl (1871–1954), Robert and Max Eitel — Stuttgart-born hoteliers; Robert Prager — lynching victim, Collinsville, Illinois.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eitel_Brothers ; https://www.3750lsd.net/the-many-lives-of-the-marigold-gardens-steps-from-3750/ ; https://www.chicagohistory.org/germania-club/
  • Confidence: High (the 3750lsd page gives December 1917; some postcards say 1915 — trust 1917).
  • Divergence check: BENDS — no American war with Germany, so none of this happens: Bismarck Gardens keeps its name (until Prohibition closes it, 1923), Lubeck/Frankfort/Hamburg Streets still exist, the Germania Club was never the Lincoln Club, Prager lives. The setting's German Chicago never learned to hide.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): a period map of Umbra Chicago should keep the German street names — a quiet tell to players that this is not their history. An old Chicagoan in the real record remembers the renaming; nobody in Umbra does.

The Marigold Gardens in the 1930s — boxing, wrestling and Capone at Grace & Halsted — 1923–1930s

  • Where: 3760 N. Halsted / Grace St. & Bradley Pl., Lake View
  • What happened: Anheuser-Busch had bought the ground in 1900 ($51,000) and exercised its option in 1914 ($123,817). Prohibition closed the garden in 1923; in the Jazz Age it ran cabaret and revues (Joan Crawford, Sophie Tucker played; Rudolph Valentino and Al Capone were patrons). In the early 1930s it became a major boxing and wrestling arena; it was demolished in 1964 and the arena became the Tabernacle Church.
  • The odd detail: an old German beer garden with a 4,500-seat past, now a fight hall owned by a brewery that cannot legally brew.
  • People: Karl and Emil Eitel — founders.
  • Sources: https://www.3750lsd.net/the-many-lives-of-the-marigold-gardens-steps-from-3750/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eitel_Brothers
  • Confidence: Medium-High (celebrity patrons are the site's claim).
  • Divergence check: BENDS — in the setting it would still be the Bismarck Gardens (see above). Prohibition and its 1923 closing hold (domestic calendar).
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): "Bismarck Gardens" fight nights as a neutral ground where a German veterans' society, the Outfit and a werewolf promoter share a ring.

German Day at the Century of Progress, and the German exhibit that never happened — 27 Mar & 13 Aug 1933

  • Where: Bismarck Hotel (committee meeting); Soldier Field / the fairground (German Day)
  • What happened: On 27 Mar 1933, at the Bismarck Hotel, the German group of the World's Fair under Ernst Kruetgen voted, on the motion of Dr. Otto L. Schmidt, to abandon a German-American exhibit within the fair's "Epic of America": the Depression and the bank holidays had killed fund-raising trips to the East and St. Louis, and the Eastern German-American leaders "declined to work together" with Chicago — Schmidt said he "could find no explanation." They put everything into German Day, 13 Aug 1933. On the day, a mass chorus of 1,200 men from the Vereinigte Saenger and Vereinigte Maennerchoere (with Milwaukee singers) under conductor Reinhold Walter sang "Die Abendglocken rufen" and "Wie's daheim war," among nearly 2,000 German singers in all. On 27 Aug 1933 the Austrians, German-Hungarians and Volga Germans held a joint festival in the Court of Honor of the Hall of Science, with a speech vowing to "step into the lines of our other German brothers … until victory is ours."
  • The odd detail: the owner asked "what did Germany send to the World's Fair?" — the record's answer for German Chicago is: nothing; it could not raise the money. The "until victory is ours" speech comes from three groups "who once belonged to one and the same realm, the monarchy on the Danube."
  • People: Dr. Otto L. Schmidt — physician, historian, exhibit committee chair; Ernst Kruetgen — German group chair; Reinhold Walter — conductor; Reinhold Oeschler, Dr. W. Teichmann — fund-raisers.
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/id/5418474_7_1275 ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5418474_7_1222 ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5418474_7_1196
  • Confidence: High.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — in the setting Germany is the victor and paymaster of Europe; a German exhibit failing "for lack of money" is unlikely unless Berlin chose not to fund Chicago's Germans. The Austro-Hungarian festival is sharper still: in Umbra the Danube monarchy survives, so "who once belonged to one realm" becomes "who still do." (R04 has the fair itself.)
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Berlin's consulate refuses to fund the German-American exhibit because it wants its own pavilion, not a hyphenated one — the Eastern leaders' unexplained refusal was ordered from the consulate.

Hindenburg's memorial at St. Paul's, with the Stahlhelm in the chancel — 7 Aug 1934

  • Where: St. Paul's Evangelical Lutheran Church, corner of Fullerton Ave. & Orchard St., Lincoln Park
  • What happened: On the evening Paul von Hindenburg was entombed at the Tannenberg monument, Chicago's German Consul General held a memorial service at St. Paul's; the church and Sunday-school rooms overflowed. Behind the altar hung the Reich President's flag, the American flag and the swastika banner; the lights were draped in black. All of Chicago's consuls attended under their dean, Italian Consul General Commendatore Castruccio, with US Army and Navy officers. The color guards in the chancel included the Deutscher Krieger-Bund, the Society of German World War Veterans, the Marine-Bund, the Landwehr Society, the Austro-Hungarian Military Societies, the Stahlhelm and the Friends of the New Germany. Pastor Dr. George L. Scherger officiated; the Schiller Liedertafel sang Beethoven.
  • The odd detail: a Chicago Lutheran church with German war veterans' banners — including a Chicago chapter of the Stahlhelm — standing guard at the altar.
  • People: Dr. George L. Scherger — pastor; Charles Espenshade — organist; Commendatore Castruccio — dean of the consular corps.
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/id/5418474_11_0678
  • Confidence: High.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — in the setting there is no Nazi Germany, so no swastika banner and no "Friends of the New Germany"; Hindenburg's role after a German victory is not fixed by the setting (flag for the owner). The veterans' societies and the consul's ceremony transfer cleanly — they would be victors' veterans.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): replace the swastika with the Reich war flag and keep everything else: a consular memorial at St. Paul's is where the Secret Service photographs every German veteran in Chicago — and where one of the honour guard has not aged since Tannenberg.

The Friends of the New Germany, the Bund, and a former consul's warning — 1933–1939

  • Where: North Side halls; Lincoln Turner Hall (W. Diversey Pkwy.); Germania Club; Lake Shore Athletic Club; Merrimac Park (far NW side)
  • What happened: The Abendpost covered the Friends of the New Germany's Chicago district meetings (Aug and Oct 1934, "large crowds"; a tenth-anniversary district convention), a "Deutsch Voelkischer Evening at Lake Shore Athletic Club" (June 1935) and a Saar demonstration (Dec 1934). On 10 Dec 1936 WPA interviewer Dr. Harvey Wish recorded Dr. Hugo Ferdinand Simon, former German Consul General, at 443 Barry Ave.: he described "the rapid conversion recently of many Chicago Germans to supporters of the Hitler Government," called "the Jewish question" a purely German matter, and said German Chicago still kept few ties even to Milwaukee. Secondary sources (not opened this session) report a Bund meeting of ~1,000 at the Germania Club in Feb 1938, a counter-protest of 3,000+ at Lincoln Turner Hall in Oct 1938 against a Bund Sudetenland rally, and a Bund picnic of 4,000–8,000 in present-day Merrimac Park on 18 June 1939 [UNVERIFIED — V_R15].
  • The odd detail: the WPA interviewer's own aside, typed into the record: the effect of the new orientation "remains to be seen, and very shortly."
  • People: Dr. Hugo Ferdinand Simon — former German Consul General; Dr. Harvey Wish — WPA assistant supervisor, later a noted historian.
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/id/5418474_9_1626 ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5418474_10_0452 ; https://chicagoreader.com/news/in-1939-nazis-rallied-in-chicago-to-make-germany-great-again/ (listed; fetch refused — details via search summary only)
  • Confidence: High for the FLPS items; Low-Medium for the 1938–39 figures (unverified this session).
  • Divergence check: BENDS — hard. No Nazi Germany, so no Friends of the New Germany and no Bund. What survives is the pattern: a consulate courting the diaspora, a "rapid conversion" of Chicago Germans to Berlin's line, and anti-Bund protesters. In the setting it would be the Kaiser's victory — and the Jewish angle inverts (the setting's Germany is Jewish-integrated).
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): "Germany as the patron of other people's revolutions": a consular-funded Deutschtum league recruiting Chicago's German, Austro-Hungarian and Volga German societies — to back Irish, Polish or Ukrainian causes Berlin sponsors. The Golden Circle watches it as a rival.

Oskar von Miller comes to build a museum — Nov 1925 (living memory)

  • Where: the German Club, Chicago; guest of Samuel Insull and Rufus Dawes
  • What happened: The Abendpost (8 Nov 1925) reported Dr. Oskar von Miller, founder of Munich's Deutsches Museum, in Chicago as a guest of utility magnate Samuel Insull and Rufus Dawes, brought over by industrialists to advise on a science museum "after the pattern of the one built by him in Munich" — the institution that became the Museum of Science and Industry (Julius Rosenwald's museum, opened in stages from 1933). Introduced at the German Club by A. F. W. Siebel, he said: "During the war, when we all were starving … you helped us," and boasted that Munich's museum passages run nine miles.
  • The odd detail: Chicago's great science museum was modelled on a German one, and its German godfather was hosted by the brother of Charles Dawes — the setting's own president of 1927–29.
  • People: Oskar von Miller — Deutsches Museum founder; Samuel Insull — utility magnate; Rufus C. Dawes — Chicago businessman, later president of the Century of Progress; A. F. W. Siebel — German Club president.
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/id/5418474_10_0566
  • Confidence: High for the visit and speech; the MSI link is well known but not re-verified this session.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — "when we all were starving" (the Allied blockade) could hold even in a German victory, but the tone of a defeated nation begging respect does not; a victorious Germany's envoy would be patronising Chicago, not thanking it.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): the museum's German-built exhibits (the coal mine, the U-505 is later) as a Berlin cultural outpost: a German-designed "science" hall in Jackson Park whose basement plans were drawn in Munich.

Stock im Eisen — a Viennese devil-legend used as a Chicago sick-benefit society's name — 15 July 1934

  • Where: Elm Tree Grove (summer picnic grounds), Chicago area
  • What happened: The Austrian sick-benefit society Stock im Eisen held its summer fest at Elm Tree Grove: a dance hall filled to capacity, Franz Holz's orchestra and a Hungarian gypsy band in the beer garden, a 7 p.m. children's procession in which every child got three gifts, one a small American flag. The president of the German Day organization, Gustave A. Brand, attended. The society took its name from Vienna's Stock im Eisen, a medieval spruce trunk (grew c. 1400, felled c. 1440) into which hundreds of nails were hammered for luck — journeyman locksmiths did it from 1715 — and about which legend says the Devil himself bound the trunk in iron and an apprentice made an unopenable lock.
  • The odd detail: an Austrian mutual-aid club in Chicago named after a nail-studded devil's tree.
  • People: Franz Holz — bandleader; Gustave A. Brand — German Day president.
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/id/5418474_7_1183 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_im_Eisen
  • Confidence: High (event); folklore labelled as folklore.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar; BENDS lightly — Austria-Hungary survives in the setting, so "Austrian" here means a subject of a living empire.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): the society keeps a nail-studded log in its hall; each new member drives a nail and swears; the Devil's pact of the Vienna legend is the society's real founding charter.

"Reintroduce German in the schools" — 17 May 1929

  • Where: Hotel Atlantic, Chicago (German Day delegates' meeting)
  • What happened: Capt. George Weideling asked 200 German Day delegates to demand that German lessons return to Chicago high schools and to present the demand in person to Superintendent William J. Bogan. Speakers noted the school board had long been willing: the blame lay with German parents who would not enrol their children.
  • The odd detail: a decade after the war, German Chicago's problem was not hostility but its own children's indifference to the mother tongue.
  • People: Capt. George Weideling — German Day speaker; William J. Bogan — Chicago schools superintendent.
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/id/5418474_1_0304
  • Confidence: High.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — German was dropped from US schools under wartime pressure; with no war, German instruction plausibly never left Chicago high schools.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): in the setting German is a normal, even prestigious, high-school language — useful for a PC who reads German occult texts.

Dr. Louis E. Schmidt expelled for treating the poor cheaply — April 1929

  • Where: Chicago Medical Society meeting, 185 N. Wabash Ave. (behind locked doors)
  • What happened: The Chicago Medical Society moved to expel urologist Dr. Louis Ernst Schmidt, about 60, president of the Public Health Institute and the Illinois Hygiene League, for "unethical conduct": his institute advertised in newspapers and treated patients for a nominal fee or free. Several hundred of the Society's 4,500 members attended; reporters listened through the door. Health Commissioner Dr. Herman Bundesen stood with him ("Solidarity … between Doctor Bundesen and Doctor Schmidt," 11 Apr 1929).
  • The odd detail: the German press called him "world famous" and cast the expulsion as doctors protecting their fees; the institute was a venereal-disease clinic (the Public Health Institute) — the city's discreet door.
  • People: Dr. Louis E. Schmidt — urologist; Dr. Herman N. Bundesen — Chicago health commissioner.
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/id/5418474_9_0141 ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5418474_9_0137 (title only)
  • Confidence: High for the expulsion move; "VD clinic" characterisation is from general knowledge of the Public Health Institute — Medium.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): a cheap, advertised clinic that asks no questions is where a vampire's victims go for "anaemia" — Dr. Schmidt's records are an investigator's lead.

C. Polish Chicago (Polonia)

Polish Downtown, "Kostkaville" and the largest parish in the world — 1867–1930s

  • Where: Polonia Triangle, Division St. × Ashland Ave. × Milwaukee Ave. (West Town); historian Edward Kantowicz bounds Polish Downtown by Racine, Fullerton, Kedzie and Grand. St. Stanislaus Kostka church; Holy Trinity, 1118 N. Noble St.
  • What happened: St. Stanislaus Kostka (1867) was Chicago's first Polish parish; its pastor Rev. Vincent Barzynski founded 23 Polish parishes, schools, orphanages, newspapers and St. Mary of Nazareth Hospital. With Holy Trinity it formed "the largest parish in the world," over 60,000 members in the early 1900s; by 1898 eleven contiguous precincts were 86.3% Polish and one precinct had exactly one non-Pole among 2,500 people. Nearly every major Polish-American organization sat within a few blocks of the Triangle — the Polish National Alliance (1514–1520 W. Division), the Polish Roman Catholic Union and its Polish Museum, the daily Dziennik Związkowy. Holy Trinity (founded 1872) fought St. Stanislaus for twenty years over whether it could be a parish at all, until an Apostolic Delegate from the Vatican settled it and gave it to the Congregation of Holy Cross under Fr. Casimir Sztuczko; its present church (1905–06, $200,000) is a "Polish Cathedral."
  • The odd detail: a precinct with one outsider in 2,500; and a church war so bitter Rome had to send its own envoy to Chicago.
  • People: Rev. Vincent Michael Barzynski — Resurrectionist empire-builder of Polonia; Rev. Casimir Sztuczko — Holy Trinity pastor.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Downtown ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Trinity_Polish_Mission
  • Confidence: High.
  • Divergence check: pre-1916, happened (the structures); holds into the 1930s.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): IHS's Polish priests run out of the Resurrectionist houses around the Triangle; a parish so large no pastor knows every face is the perfect place for something to take a parishioner's place.

The Alliance versus the Union — Polonia's secular-clerical feud — 1873/1880 to the 1930s

  • Where: PNA headquarters, 1514–1520 W. Division; PRCU headquarters (Milwaukee Ave., Polish Downtown)
  • What happened: The Polish Roman Catholic Union (1873) was clergy-aligned and saw Polish-Americans as a parish people; the Polish National Alliance (founded 15 Feb 1880, Philadelphia) was lay-led and existed "to mobilize support … for the liberation of Poland." For most of the century they were "locked in battle." On 1 Jan 1931 the PNA's Weekly Zgoda claimed 300,000 members — "the largest Polish organization … in the entire world, Poland included" — with $19,750,706.85 paid in life insurance and $3,740,233.34 given to "educational, national and philanthropic purposes in America and in Poland" since 1880, and $18.6 million in reserve at 31 Dec 1929. The PNA ran Alliance College in Cambridge Springs, Pa. (1912–91) and a Polish Library in Chicago; Charles Rozmarek became its president in 1939.

I. Chinese Chicago

Chinatown moves south, and builds its "City Hall" — 1912 and 1926–28

  • Where: old Chinatown, Clark St. between Van Buren & Harrison (Loop); new Chinatown, 22nd St. (Cermak Rd.) & Wentworth Ave.; On Leong Merchants Association Building, 2216 S. Wentworth
  • What happened: The first Chinatown on Clark St. had 16 Chinese businesses by 1889. The move south in 1912 came from rising rents (higher for Chinese tenants), demolition for a new federal building (1911), the Hip Sing–On Leong tong conflict, and — the Chinatown article says — pressure from Italian residents on the South Side fearing a "yellow invasion." The On Leong built a $200,000 block on W. 22nd St. (15 stores, 30 apartments, a headquarters), leasing through the white-owned H. O. Stone Co. to get round discrimination. By the 1920s the new Chinatown held over a third of Chicago's ~2,000 Chinese; the Moy clan dominated (over 1,000 of 6,000 Chicago Chinese immigration files, 1898–1940, carry "Moy"), and Sam Moy was the first informal "mayor." The On Leong Building (1926–28, ~$1 million, pagoda towers, by Norse-Chicago architects Christian S. Michaelsen and Sigurd A. Rognstad) was "Chinatown's 'city hall'," with meeting halls and a shrine. (In 1988 the FBI found a casino hidden on its second floor and $350,000 in a vault.)
  • The odd detail: Chinatown's temple-and-city-hall was designed by two Norwegians; its lease had to be taken in a white company's name.
  • People: Sam Moy — first "mayor"; Christian S. Michaelsen, Sigurd A. Rognstad — architects.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Chicago ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Leong_Merchants_Association_Building
  • Confidence: High (the 1988 casino is outside the window but tells you what the second floor was likely always for — inference, labelled).
  • Divergence check: pre-1916 (move) / holds (building).
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): the shrine in the On Leong "city hall" is the one place in Chicago where the Golden Circle's writ does not run — the tong keeps its own gods and its own gambling, and pays nobody's Black Hand.

The murder at the register — Hoe Sai Gai's owner shot by a man who called him "uncle" — 25 Sept 1936

  • Where: Mr. Wu's West Side restaurant; his Loop restaurant Hoe Sai Gai
  • What happened: C. P. (or C. T.) Wu, born in Sunwui (Shin-Hwei), Kwangtung, over sixty, in Chicago more than thirty years, owner with brothers and cousins of several restaurants including Hoe Sai Gai, "the most famous of all Chinese restaurants in Chicago," was at the register of his West Side place at 1:30 a.m. when a Chinese man came in, addressed him as "uncle," exchanged a few words, and fired three shots before more than twenty customers. Wu died before reaching hospital. An hour later a stranger "who appeared much like a murderer" came to Hoe Sai Gai asking for the manager, Wu's cousin Wu Chien. Two Chinese suspects were held.
  • The odd detail: "uncle" — the killer used a family word; and the second visit to the Loop restaurant an hour later, asking for the cousin.
  • People: C. P. Wu — restaurateur; Wu Chien — cousin, Hoe Sai Gai manager.
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/id/5418479_0202
  • Confidence: High (as reported; the paper gives both "C. P." and "C. T.").
  • Divergence check: holds.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): a clan killing dressed as a robbery — or a tong collection. Ten days later the Hip Sing publicly told members in arrears to settle by 31 Dec 1936 "to avoid any necessary steps"; the investigators notice the dates.

Chinatown's war-fund pillory and the Hip Sing's deadline — Oct 1936 – Sept 1937

  • Where: Chinatown (Cermak & Wentworth)
  • What happened: On 5 Oct 1936 the Hip Sing Chinese Merchants Association gave members in arrears until 31 Dec 1936 to pay dues and pledges, warning of steps it was "reluctant but obliged to take in dealing with violators." In May 1936 the San Min Morning Paper refused a Japanese restaurant-owner's ad (he wanted to sell to a Chinese buyer "not known to other Japanese"). Relief chief Frank Moy died suddenly that month; the Chinese ambassador cabled condolences [UNVERIFIED — V_R15].

J. Mexican Chicago

Our Lady of Guadalupe and the shrine of hopeless causes — 1923–1936

  • Where: Our Lady of Guadalupe Church, 91st St. & Brandon Ave., South Chicago (by the Illinois Steel mills)
  • What happened: A mission chapel opened in 1923 for Mexican steelworkers brought to South Chicago by Illinois Steel; Cardinal Mundelein gave it to the Claretians in 1924. Fr. James Tort, a Barcelona-born Claretian, became pastor in 1927; with "about 90% of his parishioners … without jobs," he began devotions to St. Jude, patron of desperate causes — the first novena was held on 17 Feb 1929, eight months before the Crash — and the shrine drew thousands through the Depression. A WPA interviewer in Dec 1936 recorded 5,700 members (8,020 Mexicans counting children, by the parish's 1935 census), four Masses a day, six societies (the Holy Name society 420; the Archicofradía 575; Sagrado Corazón 620), English/Spanish classes for ~80 adults, and weekly plays: "All of the 8,020 Mexicans are catholics … others non-catholics do not interest us."
  • The odd detail: America's national shrine of hopeless causes began in a Mexican steelworkers' church, in February 1929 — before the Crash.
  • People: Fr. James Tort, C.M.F. — founder of the St. Jude devotion; Cardinal George Mundelein.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Shrine_of_Saint_Jude_(United_States) ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5423405_2_0401
  • Confidence: High.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — in the setting America took Mexico's mineral north and occupies Tampico/Veracruz; the South Chicago Mexicans are partly American subjects from the annexed north, partly foreigners from an occupied country — a sharper legal split than the real one.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): IHS's South Side desk: a Spanish-speaking Claretian house with a relic shrine drawing thousands of the desperate — the perfect cover for priests who hunt what feeds on desperation.

Repatriation — "for the outcasts," the Mexican press said — 1929–1936

  • Where: Chicago, South Chicago, Gary and East Chicago (Indiana Harbor), Detroit
  • What happened: Nationally ~82,000 Mexicans were formally deported 1929–39 and 300,000 to 2 million "repatriated," peaking in 1931; up to a third of US Mexicans had gone by 1934. The Midwest held ~3% of US Mexicans but supplied ~10% of repatriates. In Gary in 1931 people took reduced-rate train tickets home; by 1932 "repressive measures" forced "reluctant voyagers"; in Detroit a man told his consul police had "dragged" him to the train. In Chicago the paper México protested as early as 17 Nov 1929 that honest workers "are not exiles or outcasts" and "repatriation is for the outcasts" — "Down with the hypocrites and exploiters!" By Jan 1936 La Defensa reported that "since the depression the Cook County Social Agencies have paid the expenses of many Mexican families to the Mexican Border," and that Mexico had budgeted ~2 million pesos to take them onward.
  • The odd detail: Cook County relief bought one-way tickets to the border as a cheaper alternative to relief.
  • People: —
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_repatriation ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5423405_2_0509 ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5423405_2_0536
  • Confidence: High (national figures are ranges).
  • Divergence check: BENDS — hard. In the setting the US annexed Mexico's mineral north (by 1917). A man from Sonora or Chihuahua is now an American subject; "repatriating" him to the border means shipping him to American territory, and the "Mexico" beyond is under occupation. The Depression pressure holds; the legal logic scrambles.

"The picture of Our Lady of Guadalupe together with that of Benito Juárez" — Mexican Chicago's two faiths — 1933–1937 (and curanderismo, FOLKLORE)

  • Where: Mexican press of Chicago (El Liberal, El Ideal Mexicano, La Defensa)
  • What happened: A columnist in El Liberal (16 Sept 1933) described a childhood of "morbid mysticism" and "something like ecstasy," knowing the Latin Mass by heart, torn between a devout mother and liberal men — his father, uncle and godfather Archbishop Labastida "played a 'trio game'" — so that "at the head of my bed I kept the picture of Our Lady of Guadalupe together with that of Benito Juárez." By 1937 El Ideal Mexicano headed its New Year issue "Long Live Christ the King" — the Cristero cry. Alongside priest and anticlerical stood the folk healer: in mal de ojo the curandero sweeps a raw egg over the patient and sets it, broken in water, under the bed (see the evil-eye entry above).
  • The odd detail: the Virgin and the anticlerical president side by side over one boy's bed — Mexican Chicago's split in a single image.
  • People: Archbishop (Pelagio Antonio de) Labastida — the columnist's godfather (identity as named in the article).
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/id/5423405_2_0429 ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5423405_1_0195 (title) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil_eye
  • Confidence: High for the texts; curanderismo practice is folklore (labelled). No Chicago-specific curandero found this session.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — Mexico's Cristero war and anticlerical state belong to a Mexico that, in the setting, lost its north and lives under American occupation; the Church–state fight there would be partly against the US occupiers.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): three layers again: priest (IHS), liberal (a rationalist who denies it), curandero (who actually draws it into the egg) — and in South Chicago the investigators must ask all three.

K. Filipino Chicago

5,500 "marooned" Filipinos — the post office, 70 W. Oak St., and the Sunday hotel dances — 1931–1936

In Jan 1936 the Philippine Messenger called 70 W. Oak St., run by Mrs. Louise Berg, "a real home for the Filipinos": over forty Filipino roomers. The Christmas 1934 dinner for the unemployed at the Filipino chapel fed 100 [UNVERIFIED — V_R15].

  • People: Mrs. Louise Berg — landlady.
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/id/5427330_0169 ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5427330_0298 ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5427330_0282

L. Greek, Balkan, Scandinavian and "Mohammedan" Chicago

The Greek Delta — Hull-House's neighbours — 1890s–1930s

  • Where: the "Greek Delta," the triangle of Halsted, Harrison and Blue Island Ave. (Near West Side), beside Hull-House; Holy Trinity Greek Orthodox (1897); Socrates School (1908)
  • What happened: Greeks settled the Delta from the 1890s; by 1930 Chicago had "over 30,000" Greeks (the Abendpost guessed 60,000, and a 1938 Herald-Examiner headline hailed "60,000 Greeks in Chicagopolis"). Holy Trinity (1897) was the first Greek Orthodox church in the Midwest; Socrates School (1908) the first Greek parochial school of its kind in the nation. As early as 1895 the Tribune said "the Greeks have almost run the Italians out of the fruit business"; by the 1930s Greeks ran candy stores and restaurants across the city — and the Saloniki-Greek Press (2 Feb 1933) lamented that at the American Restaurant Association "there was not even one Greek member." Its editorial of 28 Sept 1933, "Twenty-Five Thousand Greeks That Have Accomplished Practically Nothing," said Depression-closed small-town parishes were a warning that Greeks would be "assimilated to the point of obliteration," and that Chicago's parishes "at times … seek to destroy each other because of jealousy or selfishness."
  • The odd detail: a community's own newspaper headlining that its 25,000 people "have accomplished practically nothing."
  • People: G. Halepas — Greek journalist.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greektown,_Chicago ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5422062_5_0076 ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5422062_2_1473
  • Confidence: High (population numbers vary by source).
  • Divergence check: holds (domestic); see the next entry for the homeland.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): the Delta's all-night Greek restaurants are where investigators eat at 3 a.m. — and where the counterman reads the olive oil in holy water for anyone who asks (see evil-eye entry).

Greek Day's "musical bedlam," and the king question — 10 Sept 1933 & 11 Sept 1935

  • Where: before the Hall of Science, Century of Progress (Greek Day); Greek press
  • What happened: On Greek Day, 10 Sept 1933, more than 10,000 Greeks filled the space before the Science Building expecting the Greek minister. Bishop George Kallistos and Fr. Ereneos Tsourounakis chanted a prayer; then the Byzantine hymn "Hypermacho Stratego" began "real musical bedlam," and Proodos called the day "a day of humiliation for the Greeks of Chicago" — saved only by "the impressive Cretans … with their music." It shared the day with the fair's Commodore Perry memorials [UNVERIFIED — V_R15]. In Sept 1935, as Greece moved to restore its king, Greek News asked "Are There Greek Royalists in America?" — "worshipers of King Constantine here and those who hate Venizelos" — and argued no one who believes in American democracy "can believe in a Monarchy for Greece."
  • The odd detail: a Byzantine hymn to the Virgin — "To thee, the Champion Leader" — sung so badly before 10,000 that the Greek press called it a national humiliation.
  • People: Bishop George Kallistos; Fr. Ereneos Tsourounakis; King Constantine I and Eleftherios Venizelos — the feud's poles.

M. Americanization and folk belief

"One hundred million individuals": Americanization and the settlement house — 1919–1937

  • Where: Hull-House, 800 S. Halsted (13 buildings by 1911); the Jewish People's Institute; Chicago Commons; parish schools
  • What happened: In 1919 US Commissioner of Education P. P. Claxton — who, the Abendpost noted, had defended German-language teaching during the war — wrote that American democracy "recognize[s] no groups": not "Englishmen, Scots, Irishmen, Germans … Bohemians, Poles, Greeks," not "Christians, Jews, and Mohammedans," but "one hundred million or more individuals." Hull-House (founded 18 Sept 1889 by Jane Addams and Ellen Gates Starr) served a neighbourhood of ~10,000 Italians plus Germans, Jews, Greeks, Irish, Poles and, from the 1920s, Mexicans; it built the city's first public playground (1893) and helped create the first juvenile court. Addams won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1931 and died on 21 May 1935; Adena Miller Rich was head resident 1935–37. The Forward (1927) remembered the "so-called 'Americanizers'" who helped "to a certain degree" but not always "with the best and most favorable methods."
  • The odd detail: the official Americanizer of 1919 listing "Mohammedans" among America's groups — the setting's own category, in a federal commissioner's mouth.
  • People: Jane Addams — Hull-House; Ellen Gates Starr — co-founder; Adena Miller Rich — successor; P. P. Claxton — US Commissioner of Education.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_House ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5418474_9_1657 ; https://flps.newberry.org/id/5423972_7_0165
  • Confidence: High.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — the real Americanization drive was supercharged by the 1917–18 war and the Red Scare; with no war against Germany and no Red Scare in the setting, "100% Americanism" is weaker and the hyphen survives: the setting's character categories (Irish, German, Polish, "no hyphen") are exactly what a world without the wartime campaign would keep. Addams's pacifism looks prophetic, not disloyal.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Hull-House as neutral ground for every faction — the one building where an IHS priest, a Kabbalah scholar and a Secret Service agent can meet without their own communities seeing; after Addams dies (May 1935) the truce is up for grabs.

The strigoi — Romanian vampire lore in a city of Romanian Jews and Banat Germans (FOLKLORE)

  • Where: Romanian-born Chicago: the Rumanian congregation on Douglas Blvd. (see Queen Marie entry); Romanian and Banat-Swabian societies (the German directory lists "German-Hungarian" choruses)
  • What happened: In Romanian folklore a strigoaică is a witch, a strigoi viu a living sorcerer, and the strigoi mort — the most dangerous — rises "from their graves in order to torment their families until their relatives die." Those at risk: the seventh child of the same sex, the sinful, those who die unmarried, suicides, perjurers executed, victims of a witch's curse. Signs: bald on top, avoids garlic and onions, a spine elongated "in the shape of a tail, covered with hair." To find one, a "pure" seven-year-old boy dressed in white rides a white horse through the graveyard at midday; the horse stops at the vampire's grave. Remedies: garlic, a stake through the chest, the heart cut out and burned, beheading and reburial face-down, pig meat blessed on St. Ignatius's day. The practice survived: in 2004 in Dolj County six relatives were charged after exhuming a man, removing and burning his heart and mixing the ashes in water.
  • The odd detail: the white horse and the white-clad boy at noon — a vampire test in broad daylight.
  • People: —

  • Confidence: Folklore (labelled). No Chicago strigoi case found this session.
  • Divergence check: holds (folklore); Romania's post-war state BENDS (it fought and lost against the Central Powers).
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): vampires conspiring in high society meet peasant lore: a Romanian-born cemetery sexton at a Chicago Jewish or Orthodox cemetery who still knows the white-horse test — and a society family that pays him to stay away.

The York hex murder — powwow, The Long Lost Friend, and a lock of hair — 27 Nov 1928 (living memory; Pennsylvania)

  • Where: Rehmeyer's Hollow, York County, Pennsylvania
  • What happened: John Blymire, convinced by the "witch" Nellie Noll (Emma Knopp) that he had been hexed by Nelson Rehmeyer, a practitioner of powwow (Pennsylvania-German Braucherei), went with John Curry and Wilbert Hess on 27 Nov 1928 to take Rehmeyer's copy of the powwow grimoire The Long Lost Friend and a lock of his hair — to burn the book and "bury the lock of hair eight feet underground."   • People: Nelson Rehmeyer — powwow doctor, victim; John Blymire, John Curry, Wilbert Hess — killers; Emma Knopp ("Nellie Noll") — the diviner.

Mother Cabrini's arm — an Italian saint divided for Chicago — d. 1917; exhumed 1933; beatified 13 Nov 1938

  • Where: Columbus Hospital, Lincoln Park (Lakeview Ave. area), Chicago — where she died; later the National Shrine of St. Frances Xavier Cabrini
  • What happened: Frances Xavier Cabrini, the Italian-born founder of the Missionary Sisters of the Sacred Heart and of Columbus Hospital in Chicago (1905), naturalized in 1909, died there suddenly of chronic endocarditis on 22 Dec 1917, aged 67; her body lay in state at the hospital before going to New York. Two miracles were credited for beatification: Peter Smith, blinded in 1921 at New York's Columbus Hospital by a nurse's "50% silver nitrate solution," whose eyes were normal 72 hours after contact with a Cabrini relic; and Sister Delphina Grazioli, dying after four operations in Seattle (1925–29), who saw Cabrini in a vision and recovered. In 1933 the sisters exhumed her body and divided it; her arm bone went to Chicago. Pius XI beatified her on 13 Nov 1938; she was canonized in 1946 (first US citizen saint), and 120,000 attended a thanksgiving Mass at Soldier Field.
  • The odd detail: during the 1930s a saint-in-waiting's body was being cut into relics, and one arm was coming to a Lincoln Park hospital.
  • People: St. Frances Xavier Cabrini — missionary; Peter Smith and Sister Delphina Grazioli — the miracles.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Xavier_Cabrini
  • Confidence: High.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic/Church calendar (Pius XI reigns in both).
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): IHS guards the arm. A relic that cured blindness is exactly what a vampire in Chicago society would want destroyed — or used; the 1933 division is the moment the relic chain is weakest.

Holy Trinity Orthodox Cathedral — Louis Sullivan's church, paid for in part by the Tsar — 1903 (and 1930s)

  • Where: 1121 N. Leavitt St., Ukrainian Village
  • What happened: Louis Sullivan designed this Russian-provincial church (octagonal dome, frontal bell tower, Art Nouveau ornament), completed in 1903 for "Rusyns, Ukrainians, Russians, Serbs and Greeks" from Carpathian Rus', Galicia and the Balkans.

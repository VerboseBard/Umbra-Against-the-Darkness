Part 1 — The Cardinal and the Catholic hierarchy of Chicago

Cardinal George Mundelein — the cardinal of Chicago — archbishop 1915–1939, cardinal from 24 March 1924

  • Where: Chicago (cathedral = Holy Name, 735 N. State St; his city residence on the Gold Coast — street address UNVERIFIED here); his seminary at Mundelein, Illinois (Lake County, ~40 miles NW)
  • What happened: Born 2 July 1872 on Manhattan's Lower East Side (German-American); ordained in Rome 8 June 1895 and said his first Mass at St. Peter's tomb. Auxiliary bishop of Brooklyn 1909; named Archbishop of Chicago 9 Dec 1915, installed 9 Feb 1916. Pius XI made him a cardinal-priest on 24 March 1924 (titular church Santa Maria del Popolo, Rome) — with his elevation Chicago became the first archdiocese west of the Allegheny Mountains to have a cardinal ⟨fixed: V_R05⟩. He centralised the archdiocese's schools, built Quigley Preparatory Seminary in the city and the enormous St. Mary of the Lake Seminary in the village of Area (renamed Mundelein in 1924/25 in his honour). A friend of FDR and backer of the New Deal and of trade unions. Died 2 Oct 1939 at the seminary at 67 ("heart disease"); entombed behind the main altar of the seminary chapel.
  • The odd detail: A whole town was renamed after him in his lifetime, and he is buried behind the altar of his own chapel.
  • People: George William Mundelein — Cardinal-Archbishop of Chicago; Pius XI — pope who created him cardinal; Franklin Roosevelt — his political friend.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mundelein ; https://usml.edu/history/

  • Confidence: High (dates).
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar (his elevation came from Rome in 1924: the Vatican still exists and functions; the Pope's relation to a German-won Europe is the owner's call).

The 28th International Eucharistic Congress — 20–24 June 1926 — LIVING MEMORY

  • Where: Holy Name Cathedral (opening Mass); Soldier Field (Children's Day, Women's Day, men's night procession); St. Mary of the Lake Seminary, Mundelein, IL (closing Mass and procession)
  • What happened: The first Eucharistic Congress held in the United States; theme "The Peace of Christ in the Reign of Christ." Papal legate Cardinal Giovanni Bonzano led a party of cardinals and bishops; the opening procession counted 10 cardinals, 60 archbishops and ~300 bishops, with 3,000 priests assigned just to hear confessions. On Children's Day (21 June) a choir of 62,000 children sang the Mass of the Angels at Soldier Field before 400,000+; the Soldier Field altar stood 30 ft high on a 214 × 224 ft platform. For the closing procession at the seminary, 820 trains (5,200 cars) ran Chicago–Mundelein, one every 40 seconds for eight hours; ~18,000 automobiles; estimates of 800,000 to a million people. Sisters baked 4.5 million hosts. A loudspeaker rig at Mundelein was called the largest public-address system ever built; Fox filmed a 96-minute documentary.
  • The odd detail: 12,000 people were already waiting at the seminary gates when they opened at 4 a.m.; the diocesan paper later claimed 8.3 million total attendance (double-counted).
  • People: Giovanni Bonzano — papal legate; George Mundelein — host; Mayor William Dever — civic host.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/28th_International_Eucharistic_Congress ; https://usml.edu/history/
  • Image: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mass_held_at_Soldier_Field_in_the_28th_Eucharistic_Congress_in_June_1926.jpg — the Soldier Field Mass, June 1926 (Wikipedia file page: public domain)
  • Confidence: High for the figures as reported; crowd figures are period estimates.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic event; the foreign cardinals came from a Europe that in the setting had been reshaped by German victory (their nations' situations bend, the Congress itself need not).
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A million people in one field for one consecrated host is the largest "ward" ever raised on American soil — and the reason Chicago's monsters went quiet for years afterwards. Something that crossed the ground in 1926 would remember.

The Cardinals' Train — 16–17 June 1926 — LIVING MEMORY

  • Where: Grand Central Terminal, New York → New York Central / 20th Century Limited route → detour onto the Illinois Central → Park Row Station, Chicago
  • What happened: The New York Central and the Pullman Company repainted seven Pullman cars in cardinal red and gold to carry the papal legate Cardinal Bonzano, Cardinal Hayes of New York, and seven visiting foreign cardinals and their entourages to the Congress. It left Grand Central at 10 a.m. on Wednesday 16 June 1926, made 8 stops so the faithful could greet the cardinals [UNVERIFIED — V_R05], and reached Chicago in almost exactly 24 hours, where Mayor Dever, the City Council, Cardinal Mundelein and thousands met it. Afterwards the cars were repainted and put back into ordinary service — which is why few remember it.

"The Flying Cardinal": Eugenio Pacelli tours America — 8 Oct–6 Nov 1936

  • Where: New York; Boston; Cleveland; South Bend (Notre Dame); Chicago; St. Paul; San Francisco; Los Angeles; St. Louis; Cincinnati; Hyde Park
  • What happened: Cardinal Secretary of State (and Camerlengo) Eugenio Pacelli — the future Pius XII — landed in New York on 8 Oct 1936 off the Italian liner Conte di Savoia, the highest-ranking Catholic official ever to visit the US. Fr. Francis Spellman organised most of the trip, including a chartered United Airlines plane from Roosevelt Field that took him coast to coast; the press called him "the Flying Cardinal." At Notre Dame he took an honorary doctorate; in Chicago he met Cardinal Mundelein; Cleveland was also on the air route. On 5 Nov, the day after FDR's re-election, he lunched with Roosevelt at Hyde Park for two hours. He sailed on 6 Nov and was back in Rome on 14 Nov.
  • The odd detail: The Vatican's number-two man spent the 1936 election campaign flying across the Midwest in a chartered airliner — he was in the air over the Great Lakes six weeks before the setting's alpha adventure (Dec 1936).
  • People: Eugenio Pacelli — Vatican Secretary of State, later Pius XII; Francis Spellman — organiser, later Archbishop of New York; John F. Noll — Bishop of Fort Wayne; John F. O'Hara — Notre Dame president.

The "paperhanger" speech — 18 May 1937

  • Where: Archbishop Quigley Preparatory Seminary, Chicago (Rush & Chestnut, Near North Side)
  • What happened: Addressing about 500 priests of the archdiocese, Mundelein asked how "a nation of 60 million intelligent people will submit in fear and servitude to an alien, an Austrian paper hanger, and a poor one at that, and a few associates like Goebbels and Göring." Germany protested: Ambassador Diego von Bergen handed the Vatican an unsigned, undated note on 24 May; chargé Fritz von Menshausen delivered a sharper one on 29 May demanding a public disavowal. Pius XI convened the cardinals of Extraordinary Ecclesiastical Affairs at Castel Gandolfo on 20 June 1937; Pacelli reported that the Holy See "cannot itself correct or deplore" the speech — disavowing him would only make the Nazi leaders more arrogant. The Vatican's reply: Mundelein had not spoken for the Holy See, had not spoken publicly, and was a free American citizen.
  • The odd detail: The Vatican's formal defence of an American cardinal rested on his constitutional rights as a U.S. citizen.
  • People: George Mundelein — the speaker; Diego von Bergen — German ambassador to the Holy See; Fritz von Menshausen — German chargé; Eugenio Pacelli — who defended him.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_hanger_(Mundelein's_speech) ; https://www.vaticannews.va/en/vatican-city/news/2026-05/cardinal-pacelli-pius-xii-mundelein-chicago-hitler-attack-histor.html
  • Confidence: High.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — no Nazi Germany, no Chancellor Hitler, no Goebbels or Göring in office. In the setting Hitler exists as an occult figure without office. A 1937 Mundelein speech could instead denounce an Austrian paperhanger who holds no office but holds a following — a speech about an occultist, which Berlin (the victor of the war) might still protest.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Keep the date, the room and the 500 priests; change the target: the cardinal names an Austrian occultist before his clergy, the German embassy protests, and IHS understands the speech as an order.

Part 2 — Orthodox, Eastern and ethnic Catholic Chicago

Holy Trinity Orthodox Cathedral — the Tsar's church (built 1899–1903; cathedral 1923)

  • Where: 1121 N. Leavitt St. at Haddon (Ukrainian Village; old address 560 N. Leavitt)
  • What happened: Founded 1892 by Carpatho-Rusyn and Galician immigrants (first called St. Nicholas, then St. Vladimir, worshipping at 20 N. Peoria). Louis Sullivan — one of only two churches he designed — built it in a Russian village style: an octagon-within-a-square, Louis Millet stencils, an iconostasis imported from Russia. Tsar Nicholas II gave $4,000 personally and $15,000 more was raised in Russia (total ~$30,000); Sullivan halved his fee, counting the rest as a gift, because of his friendship with Charles Crane and the Imperial consul Baron Schlippenbach. John Kochurov (1895–1907) went home and was shot in 1917, the first priest-martyr of the revolution. It became a cathedral in 1923 when its rector Theodore Pashkovsky was made bishop.
  • The odd detail: From 1949 to 2004 the rectory held the Tikhvin icon of the Virgin — said to be painted by St. Luke — brought out only for feasts. (Post-period; useful as a precedent.)
  • People: Louis Sullivan — architect; Nicholas II — donor; Tikhon (Bellavin) — consecrator, later Patriarch; John Kochurov — founding rector, martyred 1917; Theodore (Theophilus) Pashkovsky — rector, bishop 1923.
  • Sources: https://orthodoxwiki.org/Holy_Trinity_Orthodox_Cathedral_(Chicago,_Illinois) ; https://www.thedailybeast.com/tsar-nicholas-nazis-louis-sullivan-and-a-little-chicago-church-the-crazy-story-of-st-lukes-portrait-of-the-virgin-mary/

Part 3 — Radio priests, the Church and Hollywood

Father Charles Coughlin and the Shrine of the Little Flower — 1926–1939

  • Where: Royal Oak, Michigan (2100 W. Twelve Mile Rd at Woodward Ave), a Detroit suburb
  • What happened: Coughlin took a new parish of about 25 families in a Protestant suburb in 1926 and began broadcasting sermons on Detroit's WJR that year — citing a Ku Klux Klan cross burned in front of the church within days of its opening (the broadcasts also paid off the diocesan loan). When CBS dropped him in 1931 for politics, he and station owner George A. Richards built their own network: 25 stations by Aug 1932, 58 by 1938.

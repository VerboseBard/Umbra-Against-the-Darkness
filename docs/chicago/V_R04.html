  • Error rate (CORRECTED + WRONG): 17 / 80 = 21%. Counting UNSUPPORTED too: 29 / 80 = 36%.
  • Most corrections are small: a count, a word, a date's framing. The six that matter:
    1. The Graf Zeppelin story. Eckener did not fly on with the ship; he stayed 24 hours in Chicago. Luther being aboard is unsupported.
    2. The Foreign Participation list. It has 18 countries (Palestine was missing), and Germany is named in it, through the Koch Institute and the Dresden museum.
    3. Two Cermak date slips. He was shot 14 weeks before the opening, not 11 (Top 10 #8). And the odd detail's "twelve days before the Enabling Act" should be the Reichstag fire.
    4. The Arcturus hook. It calls 1896 "the year before the timeline's break"; the break is 1916.
    5. The Romance of a People. Both sources read give 150,000, with 75,000 more at a repeat, not 125,000.
    6. The German Day resolutions. They asked for an investigation of the war-guilt article; they did not "condemn Versailles".

Does the Germany answer hold up?

Mostly yes, with two repairs.

  • Holds: no official German pavilion; the German-American Building and the imperial-flag refusal (JTA, verbatim); Simon's recall; German Day with 25,000; Old Heidelberg (operated by Eitel, Inc., per the guide); the 1934 Black Forest Village (the guide says "from Germany"); the Transparent Man ("one of only two in the world", verbatim); the Koch Institute; the Gutenberg press; the Graf's clockwise circuit; and Luther's pro-Hitler speech at the Union League Club (Washington Times, 27 Oct 1933, verbatim).
  • Repair 1: the guide does not simply "omit Germany". Germany has no heading, but its scientific institutions are named in the Foreign Participation section.
  • Repair 2: Germany sent more than R04 lists. German firms and individuals exhibited: Bosch of Düsseldorf, Roemmert of Munich's Zeiss microvivarium, the Moscherosch-Schmidt figurines, and modern German church architecture. The German-American Building's own contents are also in the guide and R04 missed them: two restaurants including a Rathskeller, "a few exhibits from Germany showing the technical progress of that country", Alpine wood carvings, and a lakeside dance floor.
  • The Koch exhibit was a memorial to Koch, not current tuberculosis research. The Gutenberg press was a 1925 replica, brought at the Cuneo Press's expense.

Divergence-label problems (entries marked "holds" that touch the break)

  1. Opening day is marked "Holds" except for the fascist salutes. But it has Black Great War veterans marching. America never entered the Great War in this setting, so there are no AEF veterans. BENDS: replace them with Southern War veterans.
  2. Fort Dearborn rebuilt is marked "exhibit holds". It was assembled with the American Legion, which was founded in 1919 by AEF veterans. BENDS lightly: the Legion as known would not exist.
  3. Soldier Field itself is not flagged anywhere. It was named in 1925 as a memorial to American soldiers of the Great War. A dozen R04 entries are set there and marked "holds". The stadium's name and dedication need a ruling, for example Southern War dead.
  4. The Gutenberg press is marked "Holds", yet the entry itself says it "would come with an imperial seal". It is a loan from a German museum in 1933 Germany. Should be BENDS lightly.
  5. The Robert Koch Institute is marked "Holds", yet the entry itself says "under the imperial eagle". A German state institute. Should be BENDS lightly.
  6. The Black Forest Village is marked "Holds — no politics", but the 1934 guide bills it as "from Germany". Borderline; BENDS lightly, since a Kaiser's Germany might make it official.
  7. Sweden / Bernadotte is marked "Holds". But the odd detail and the People line rest on his WWII future: Himmler, the white buses, UN mediator murdered in Jerusalem in 1948. None of that exists in a world with no Nazi camps and an Ottoman Jerusalem. The biography BENDS; the 1933 pavilion holds.
  8. Cermak is marked "Holds — domestic calendar", but its odd detail used Hitler's Enabling Act, a Nazi event. That detail BENDS, and it was also wrong (now fixed to the Reichstag fire; that framing BENDS too).
  9. Old Heidelberg is marked "Holds". Borderline: the Eitels' 1917 renaming history, which R04's own "names that changed in 1917" entry flags, is part of the same family story.
  10. The Arcturus hook puts the break in 1896. That is wrong against the brief (1916), and is now fixed.
  11. Faction conflation (flagged by the coordinator mid-check): the Roosevelt-at-the-fair hook called the Secret Service "Section 13's own agency". Fixed. Section 13 is FBI (K. Edgar Hoover) and a rival of the Secret Service. Note for the owner: the Cermak hook's line that the Secret Service "knew what Capone was and took him anyway" was taken from the old, conflated brief text. Which faction it belongs to needs a ruling. It is not edited here.
  • Entries correctly marked BENDS: Italy/Balbo, Czechoslovakia, Ukraine, the Sokols, Belgium, the Mexican/Moroccan/Tunisian villages, Luxemburg, the Dominican Republic, the Congress Hotel 1939, New York 1939, the Dawes brothers, and the Graf Zeppelin. These are good. The Official Roster should add Palestine: BENDS, since Jerusalem is Ottoman.

6 German-American Building / Simon JTA 13 Aug 1933: the committee refused the swastika, "the committee is flying the imperial flag of Germany", and Simon was recalled CONFIRMED https://www.jta.org/archive/chicago-consuls-recall-traced-to-stand-against-flying-swastika-at-fair-exhibit All quotes match. 13 Graf Zeppelin The ship "set down at Glenview for only about twenty minutes… then flew over the fairgrounds again and on to Akron" (implying Eckener flew on) CORRECTED Washington Times 27 Oct 1933 p. 10 ("The Graf Zeppelin in its brief landing here left its commander behind"); chicagology 1933fair74 The twenty minutes aground and the flight back over the fair are confirmed. 14 Graf Zeppelin Washington Times, 27 Oct 1933, p. 10: Luther "outlined the ideals and aims of the Hitler government", "told of the Nazi position on disarmament", "vigorously denied charges of German imperialism", "criticized the Jewish American boycott" CONFIRMED https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn84026749/1933-10-27/ed-1/?sp=10 (OCR via the Chronicling America JSON) All four quotes are verbatim in the OCR. 15 Gutenberg press "lent a Gutenberg press… operated by the Cuneo Press"; "exact hall UNVERIFIED"; odd detail: "The one thing the German nation demonstrably sent… was a five-hundred-year-old machine" CORRECTED 1933 guide text ("From the Gutenberg museum in Mainz, Germany, has come the unique reproduction of the Gutenberg press…"; the Cuneo Press "taken it upon themselves to bring… at their expense the Gutenberg Press and type foundry") The loan is confirmed, and the hall is known: General Exhibits Group, Pavilion 2 (Graphic Arts). The machine was a reproduction (the museum's 1925 replica), not 500 years old. It came at the Cuneo Press's expense, not as something "the German nation" sent. It was also not "the one thing" Germany sent: the Koch and Dresden exhibits were sent by German institutions.

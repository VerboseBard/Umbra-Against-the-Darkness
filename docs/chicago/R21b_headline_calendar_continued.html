R21b — "What's in the paper", continued: headline calendar for the fair year and the alpha's autumn

Written 2026-09-27; months covered: April–July 1933 (the fair's opening months); September–December 1936 (the alpha's autumn/winter).

  • Lane: R21b — continues R21 (R21_headline_calendar.md); same line format, same rules.
  • Date: 2026-09-26/27
  • Model: Claude Opus 5.5 / Claude Sonnet 5 (research agents, continuing the same lane)
  • Method: the Indianapolis Times (Scripps-Howard, United Press wire — the same paper R21 leaned on for 1929–1934) is fully text-searchable and readable on Hoosier State Chronicles, the Indiana State Library's Veridian site, which was not behind the Library of Congress rate limit that stopped R21. Each front page's OCR comes back from https://newspapers.library.in.gov/?a=d&d=IPT<YYYYMMDD>.1.1&f=XML (the <PageTextHTML> element); the citable page URL is the same without &f=XML. A script (hsc.py, in the session scratchpad) walked every front page day by day and pulled every paragraph whose opening ~60 characters named a Chicago or Great Lakes-ring city, keeping the headline paragraph(s) immediately above it and the lede. Headlines are quoted as printed from the OCR; obvious OCR breakage silently repaired (letter swaps, stray punctuation); doubtful readings marked [OCR]; a line with no recoverable headline is marked [paraphrase] and summarizes the lede instead, exactly as R21 does. Every line carries the page URL that was actually read this session.
  • Coverage note: Hoosier State Chronicles' Indianapolis Times run covers 1930–1933 and 1937–1939 as well as the R21 years, so the rest of the missing months (see "Months still missing" below) can be filled the same way in a future pass. This pass covered April–July 1933 in full and stopped there for time; August–December 1933 were not reached.
  • Conventions: dates are the issue date (a UP/Scripps-Howard wire dateline "CHICAGO, April 6.—" etc.). "Umbra use" lines are proposals only, at most one per month. The Secret Service and FBI Section 13 are two separate, rival federal factions; K. Edgar Hoover directs the FBI, not the Secret Service.
  • Divergence note (applies to this whole file): anything touching Germany, Russia, the Nazis/Fascists, the WWI settlement, the League of Nations, the empire, or the presidents before Hoover BENDS under the 1916 break; ordinary domestic Chicago/Great Lakes news holds. 1933 sits right at the hinge (Hoover to Roosevelt in March; this file's April news is already Roosevelt's). 1936 is an election year in both timelines (Roosevelt re-elected in November); the Landon campaign train and the Republican convention news below hold as domestic events even though the world around them has diverged.

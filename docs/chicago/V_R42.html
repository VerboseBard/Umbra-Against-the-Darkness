Top-10 items first (the owner's list)

# Claim (R42 entry) Verdict Evidence / correction
1 Oscar Schrader died at Winnebago State Hospital, Oshkosh, in January 1934; legislative inquiry Feb–Jul 1934 spread to Mendota and Waupun; ~30 dismissals; a guard acquitted on two manslaughter counts but discharged; committee sought to compensate the widow CONFIRMED (one source) Wikipedia "Winnebago Mental Health Institute" states every one of these points in one paragraph. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnebago_Mental_Health_Institute — no second source turned up (searches return only mirrors of that paragraph), so the cause of death remains unknown, as R42 says.
1b "…emptied out the leadership of three state institutions by summer, and the jury said no one had committed the crime" (the odd-detail line) CORRECTED Overstated. The source says ~30 dismissals of staff and officials at Mendota, Winnebago and Waupun, and that the guard was acquitted by a jury on two manslaughter counts (then discharged). It does not say the leadership was emptied or that the jury found no crime. Line rewritten.
2 John Schrank: shot Roosevelt 14 Oct 1912, 8:10 p.m., outside the Gilpatrick Hotel; bullet through the 50-page speech; 50-minute speech before treatment CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Schrank ("When Roosevelt emerged … at 8:10 pm"; "He spoke for 50 minutes").
3 Schrank's 1901 dream: on 15 Sept 1901, the day after McKinley died, McKinley rose from the coffin, "Avenge my death!"; second apparition 14 Sept 1912 with "dying eyes" CONFIRMED Same page: "vivid dream in the early morning of September 15, 1901, a day after … McKinley died"; "early hours of September 14, 1912 … apparition … staring at him with 'dying eyes'".
4 Schrank died 15 September 1943, after 31 years in Wisconsin's asylums, no visitor or letter; the same calendar day as the dream CONFIRMED Same page: "died September 15, 1943, in the Central State Hospital for the Criminally Insane" (Waupun); "Time magazine reported that Schrank received no visitors and no mail during his 31 years." Cause of death, per the page: bronchial pneumonia.
4b Judge Backus refused the plea; sanity commission 22 Nov 1912; "dementia praecox, paranoid type" CONFIRMED Same page (commission report dated 22 Nov 1912; the 22 Nov order is quoted).
4c Which asylum came first (Wikipedia's Schrank page says "Waupun", the Winnebago page says Oshkosh) CORRECTED The commitment order quoted on the Schrank page itself sends him to "the Northern Hospital for the Insane, near Oshkosh, in the county of Winnebago"; the page's own "Northern Hospital … in Waupun" is its own slip. Winnebago's page agrees (committed Nov 1912 to Northern Hospital at Winnebago; later died at Central State in Waupun). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnebago_Mental_Health_Institute — note added in R42.
5 Charles E. Brown's Ghost Tales (1931), Sea Serpents (1942), Lost Treasure Tales (1945); 47 pamphlets, 444 pages, 1921–45 CONFIRMED https://legacy.wisconsinhistory.org/turningpoints/search.asp?id=1622 — "47 pamphlets totaling 444 pages … issued between 1921 and 1945"; lists Ghost Tales (1931), Sea Serpents (1942), Lost Treasure Tales (1945). (Ridgeway Ghost 1943 and Hermits 1945 not re-checked.)
5b WPA Federal Writers' Project folklore: Brown first, Dorothy Miller from Dec 1935; 1936–38; 600+ interviews; ~1,000 stories, 3,000 songs, 9,000 sayings CONFIRMED https://legacy.wisconsinhistory.org/Records/Article/CS324 (note the Turning Points page on the pamphlets says nothing about the WPA; the CS324 page does).
6 Sightings: Monona 12 June 1897 (Eugene Heath, Schott and two sons; ~20 ft; "bottom of a boat"; two shots); Mendota 1917 (fish-scale object at Picnic Point; snake head with blazing eyes) CONFIRMED https://edgeeffects.net/lake-monster/ quotes both, credits Brown's Sea Serpents (1942). (The "napping girl felt something tickle her foot" detail was not re-checked.)
6b Geneva Lake, 29 Sept 1902, Janesville Gazette, snake-like creature 65 ft long, 8–10 in thick UNSUPPORTED Appears only in search-result snippets ("Denizens of the deep", Journal Times/Janesville Gazette, Dec 2016 — "named 'Jenny'"); the Journal Times page redirects to a paywall and the Gazette PDF has no extractable text; edgeeffects.net mentions Geneva only in passing. Tagged [UNVERIFIED — V_R42] (entry and Top-10 line).
7 Mendota State Hospital thunderbird, 624-ft wingspan CONFIRMED Cronon's Forest Hill site: "The large thunder bird with a 624 ft wingspan … form the Mendota State Hospital group." http://foresthill.williamcronon.net/effigy-mounds/mounds-in-madison/ — it is a wingspan, not a length. Same page: Brown museum director 1908–1944, grave in Forest Hill section 30, Picnic Point restored 1939.
7b Brown's campaign produced Wisconsin's first historic-preservation law; state-funded survey mapped hundreds of mounds in two years CONFIRMED Archaeological Conservancy: "a law protecting archaeological sites. This was Wisconsin's first historic preservation law"; survey documented "hundreds of mounds" within the two-year funding. https://www.thearchcons.org/madison-effigy-mounds/ (a search snippet dates the law 1911; the page read gives no year, and R42 gives none).
7c Brown and volunteers excavated a Madison-area mound in 1931 just before development UNSUPPORTED Not on the Cronon page it is credited to. Tagged.
8 Summerwind: Lamont bought 1916; Tallmadge & Watson named in some accounts; Bober's 1979 The Carver Effect (pen name Wolfgang Von Bober); ≥2 earlier residents denied a haunting; 1980 Life essay; destroyed by fire after lightning 19 June 1988 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summerwind — every listed point is on the page. (Lamont as Hoover's Commerce Secretary is not on the page; from memory, not re-checked.)
8b Legend: "one evening in the mid-1930s the basement door flew open, a man's apparition appeared during dinner, and Lamont fired twice" UNSUPPORTED Wikipedia says only: "Lamont fired a pistol at a ghost one night and was so frightened that he and his family abandoned the house." No date, basement door, dinner or "twice" on any page read. Replaced by the sourced sentence; the extra details tagged [UNVERIFIED — V_R42].
8c Top-10 #6: Summerwind's ghost story "turns out to be a 1979 book" CORRECTED The record supports: the haunting reputation (Carver, the Hinshaws) spreads from Bober's 1979 book and the 1980 Life feature, and locals say the stories began then. It does not establish when the older Lamont pistol legend first appeared. Top-10 line reworded; the "forty-odd years after his supposed shot" line tagged.
9 Our Lady of Good Help, Champion: early Oct 1859 visions (Adele Brise, aged 28); Lambert's 10×12 ft chapel; larger 1861; brick chapel 1880 dedicated by Bishop Krautbauer; procession and survival in the 8 Oct 1871 firestorm; Rhode dedicated the present shrine July 1942; apparition approved 8 Dec 2010, first in the U.S. CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrine_of_Our_Lady_of_Good_Help (Wikipedia spells "Brice"; it also records the 2016 national-shrine designation, the 2023 rename to "Our Lady of Champion" and the Jan 2026 opening of Brise's canonization cause — new since R42).
10 Percy Julian and Appleton, 1936: sundown town ("No Negro should be a bed or boarded overnight in Appleton"); Institute of Paper Chemistry hire fell through; W. J. O'Brien (Glidden VP) offered the Chicago post; arrived 1936; German extraction plant; Oak Park house firebombed Thanksgiving 1950, dynamited 12 June 1951 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Lavon_Julian (Nova is cited there for "he was offered the job … because he was fluent in German, and Glidden had just purchased a modern … extraction plant from Germany"); Appleton's own page: "In 1936, the Institute of Paper Chemistry tried to hire … Percy Julian, but could not figure out how to do…" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appleton,_Wisconsin. The wording is Loewen's, via Wikipedia, as R42 says. (Wikipedia's Julian page has a "1956" typo for 1936 in the DePauw sentence — do not copy it.)

The Kohler strike, Progressives, and the other specific numbers

# Claim Verdict Evidence / correction
11 Kohler, 27 July 1934: Lee Wakefield (25) and Henry Engelmann (27) killed, ~9 p.m., north side; 40+ hurt (47 elsewhere); Sheriff Zehms; 105th Cavalry; funerals 30 and 31 July (Moose Park); September inquests "no one person could be blamed" CONFIRMED Sheboygan Press recap (Yahoo): https://www.yahoo.com/news/kohler-strike-claimed-lives-two-100801664.html ; Wikipedia "Kohler strikes" (47 wounded; 250 Guardsmen arrive 29 July; NLRB election went to the company union). The recap also confirms Zehms wired Gov. Schmedeman — R42 had that as "not opened". The recap says the strike began "mid-July" though the 14-point demand was 22 June and the plant shutdown 26 June — sources differ, as R42 notes.
12 Kohler village: incorporated 1912; Walter Kohler Sr. in Europe 1913, conferred with Ebenezer Howard; Olmsted Brothers from 1917; NYT 1931 "without a visible ash heap"; company keeps design authority CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohler,_Wisconsin (population 403 → 1,748 not re-checked).
13 Progressive Party founded 19 May 1934 at Fond du Lac, Evjue chairman CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Progressive_Party ("held on May 19, 1934, at Fond du Lac City … William T. Evjue served as chairmen"). Venue (Hotel Retlaw) remains unverified, as R42 says.
14 Philip La Follette 39.12% (1934), 46.38% (1936); Bob Jr. 47.78% (1934) CONFIRMED Results boxes of https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_Wisconsin_gubernatorial_election (373,093 votes, 39.12%), .../1936_Wisconsin_gubernatorial_election (46.38%), .../1934_United_States_Senate_election_in_Wisconsin (47.78%). Governor 1931–33 and 1935–39 also confirmed.
14b 1934: 11/33 Senate, 45/100 Assembly; 1936: "the party took the legislature (16 of 33, 46 of 100)" CORRECTED The numbers are right (party results table) but they are pluralities: the same table labels both 1936 rows "1st … Progressive minority". 16/33 and 46/100 are not majorities (the article's prose says "majority"; its own numbers do not). Reworded to "largest bloc in both houses". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Progressive_Party
14c "won two governorships and a legislature" CORRECTED Philip won the one office twice (1934, 1936); no legislative majority. Reworded.
15 Houdini: arrived 3 July 1878, SS Frisia; father rabbi of Zion Reform congregation, Appleton; lost the post and left for Milwaukee 1882; site on Appleton Street now Houdini Plaza; died 31 Oct 1926, peritonitis, Grace Hospital, Detroit; 24 Oct 1926 Garrick Theater with a 104 °F fever CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Houdini. Born 24 March 1874 (from R42's memory note) also confirmed.
16 Green Bay Packers: founded 11 Aug 1919; $500 from Indian Packing Co.; Agnes Aylward named them; $50 re-entry fee; rain "3/100th of an inch" short; "Hungry Five"; titles 1929–31 (12–0–1, 10–3–1, 12–2); Hubbard, Michalske; Hutson 1935; beat Boston Redskins 21–6 for 1936 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Green_Bay_Packers
17 August Derleth: born 24 Feb 1909; "Bat's Belfry" to Weird Tales 1926 at age 16; ~1,000 letters over 11 years, never met; Lovecraft d. 15 March 1937; Arkham House 1939 with Wandrei; The Outsider and Others first; Guggenheim 1938 CONFIRMED https://urbanmilwaukee.com/2013/10/30/the-horror-of-sauk-city-arkham-houses-weird-fiction-legacy/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Derleth. (Arithmetic aside: born 24 Feb 1909, he was 16 only if the sale preceded 24 Feb 1926; the sources both say 16.)
17b "Robert Bloch … phoned Derleth the news" of Lovecraft's death CORRECTED Urban Milwaukee: "On March 15, 1937, Bloch got a somber phone call from Derleth — Lovecraft was dead." The call ran the other way. https://urbanmilwaukee.com/2013/10/30/the-horror-of-sauk-city-arkham-houses-weird-fiction-legacy/
18 Bishop Paul Rhode: b. 18 Sept 1871 Neustadt, Prussia; ordained 17 June 1894; auxiliary bishop of Chicago (appointed 22 May, consecrated 29 July 1908); Green Bay 15 July 1915; d. 3 March 1945, aged 73; 10 parishes, 19 schools CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Peter_Rhode (came to Chicago at nine ✔).
19 Joseph McCarthy: b. 14 Nov 1908, Grand Chute, fifth of nine; Manawa; Marquette 1930–35; lost DA race 1936; 1939 judge over Werner, falsely calling him 73 (he was 66). Edna Ferber: Appleton at 12 (1897); Pulitzer 1925 for So Big; Show Boat 1926 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_McCarthy ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edna_Ferber (So Big was published 1924, Pulitzer awarded 1925 — R42's wording is right).
20 Steenbock: 1923 irradiation; $300 own money; $1 million Quaker Oats offer refused; nine alumni $100 each; WARF first tech-transfer office; 19 Feb 1927 Quaker Oats licence; Viosterol CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Steenbock (WARF "1925" and Harlow's 1930/1932 dates are not on the pages read; not re-checked).
21 Groves Act signed by Gov. Philip La Follette: "28 January and 28 February — UNVERIFIED which" CORRECTED 28 January 1932. Wisconsin DWD timeline: "On January 28, 1932, Governor Philip La Follette signs the nation's first unemployment compensation law"; first check issued 17 Aug 1936. https://dwd.wisconsin.gov/dwd/history/
22 Coon Valley: first national demonstration project; CCC dispatched 1934 after Leopold's lobbying; five-year cooperative plans; 43,000 acres ("more than half") managed when the CCC left in 1937; farm income +25% (1934–42); erosion −75%, sedimentation −98% after 40 years CONFIRMED https://www.aldoleopold.org/blogs/lessons-from-coon-valley-the-importance-of-collaboration-in-watershed-management (quotes each figure); https://www.nacdnet.org/2023/09/11/wisconsins-coon-creek-watershed-project-celebrates-90-year-anniversary/ ("first national demonstration project"; began 1933).
22b "first of twenty national watershed demonstration projects"; basin 92,589 acres UNSUPPORTED Neither page gives the count or the acreage. A search result says 10 sites by Jan 1934 and ~24 projects eventually. A 92,589-acre basin would also make 43,000 acres less than half. Both tagged.
23 Fromm brothers: 1901 silver fox pelt $1,200 in London; 1904 ginseng (Dietsch); 1913 first silver pup and the Rib Mountain inscription; 1915 $6,500 mortgage for three silver foxes; 13-row planter; Dr. Robert G. Green vaccines; "world's largest by 1929"; NRHP 2013 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fromm_Brothers_Fur_and_Ginseng_Farm
23b "1915 half an acre of ginseng brought $3,500"; "by 1919 ginseng sold $40,000"; 1935 farm auctions; 1937: $10 million, 180+ staff, 500 acres, 36,000 silver foxes UNSUPPORTED None on the Wikipedia page (it gives 3 acres of ginseng by 1915 and $45,000–$115,000 a year through the 1920s); the wi101 page it was drawn from would not load (DNS error). Tagged.
24 9XM/WHA: 9XM licensed June 1915; 1 Dec 1916 daily Morse weather announced; April 1917 all civilian transmitters shut, civilian receivers illegal; spoken weather 3 Jan 1921; WHA licence 13 Jan 1922; School of the Air 5 Oct 1931; Sterling Hall studios until 1934 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHA_(AM)
24b "The station returned in 1920" CORRECTED The university's radio work resumed in May 1917 under Navy supervision (secret war research; audio tested March 1919); the civil 9XM call was revived 11 Feb 1920. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHA_(AM) — sentence rewritten.
25 Waupun: site chosen 4 July 1851 by Gov. Dewey; South Cell Hall 1854; 853 inmates in 1918 (Warden Henry Town); 1,664 on 30 June 1934 (Warden Oscar Lee), ten camps, three logging CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waupun_Correctional_Institution ; the Winnebago page confirms the Central State Hospital for the Criminally Insane was at Waupun.
26 UW "Ku Klux Klan" honor society (Illinois, 1906), renamed Tu-Mas April 1923, thirteen chapters, defunct 1939, Fredric March; the real Klan's Kappa Beta Lambda (1923) at Wisconsin, only chapter CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ku_Klux_Klan_(honor_society) (the UW chapter's May 1919 founding and the five founders' names were not re-checked).
27 Menominee: 1905 windstorm → Forest Service took over; La Follette Sr. backed sustained yield; 1934 suit in the Court of Claims; settled 1952 for $8.5 million CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menominee ("In 1934, the Menominee filed suit … settled the claim finally in 1952, awarding the Menominee $8.5 million"). The "≈70% cut before 1926" figure is only implied (the page gives 30% afterwards).
28 Van Schaick: ~30,000 glass-plate negatives "between 1890 and 1900"; Historical Society acquired the collection 1970 (~8,000 salvaged); Lesy chose ~200 for the 1973 Pantheon book; 1999 Marsh film, Ian Holm CONFIRMED / UNSUPPORTED 1970, ~200 photographs, 1973 Pantheon and the 1999 film are on https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Death_Trip. The 30,000 figure: a search result gives ~30,000 negatives in all with ~8,000 salvaged; nothing read dates all 30,000 to 1890–1900 (that range dates the plates Lesy used). Tagged.

Not checked (left as R42 has them)

Fond du Lac lighthouse dates, Rib Mountain, Paine mansion, Ho-Chunk/Oneida/Stand Rock figures, Rock Island/Thordarson, Mineral Point/Pendarvis, Wisconsin Pearl Button, Sheboygan/Vollrath, New Glarus, Stoughton/Westby, Stevens Point/Pulaski, Wausau, Two Rivers sundae, Hotel Northland, Harlow's dates.

Bottom line

R42's Top-10 items mostly hold: Schrader (single-source but accurate on the record), Schrank (all dates right), the 624-ft Mendota bird, Brown's 1931/1942 pamphlets, Champion, Julian/Appleton and Kohler all check out. The real errors were: Derleth/Bloch phone call reversed, Progressives did not "take" the legislature (pluralities only), Groves Act signed 28 Jan 1932, the WHA "returned in 1920" line, the Schrader "emptied out the leadership / jury found no crime" overstatement, and Summerwind's Lamont legend given a date and details no page carries. The Geneva Lake 1902 serpent, the 1931 Brown excavation, the Fromm 1937 figures and "first of twenty" demonstration projects are tagged UNVERIFIED.

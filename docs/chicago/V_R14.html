The worst errors

  1. Jones kidnapping release (WRONG). Ed Jones was held about 13 days (11 to 24 May 1946), not "five days". "Loomis and 62nd Street" appears in neither cited source.
  2. Nixon locations (CORRECTED ×2). The hotel was the Devonshire, not the "Devon". The Johnson murder was in North Kenwood on the South Side, not "the North Side". This is the file's #1 find, so both matter.
  3. Policy survey date (CORRECTED). The 38 wheels / 400 stations / $600,000 figures come from an undated anti-vice brochure quoted by Chepesiuk, not "a survey in the early 1940s". The owner should not date them to the 1940s, or to the 1930s either, without the brochure.
  • Also notable: the Blackburn "same month as Baer" line (WRONG: it was the next month). The Regal's "fourteen months before the crash" (WRONG: about twenty). Julian Black as a "numbers man" (UNSUPPORTED: the cited pages call him a boxing promoter or a lawyer). And the Diana Gross third victim: Gebert's Daily Worker names Thomas Page, killed at the eviction, and has no Frank Armstrong. That undercuts the "body in the park" odd detail and hook.

Divergence-label problems

Most labels are good. R14 flags the 370th, Dawson, Dickerson, the eviction riot, Wright, Owens, Braddock/Schmeling, the aviators and Haiti (Dunham) correctly. Entries marked "holds" or "pre-1916" that actually touch the break:

Entry Label given Problem
O · Jack Johnson "pre-1916, happened" His 1913–1920 exile ran through wartime Europe and ended in Mexico (he came back across the border in 1920). In the setting that is a Europe where Germany won and a Mexico under American occupation after the Southern War. It should read BENDS (exile route).
A · Jones brothers "holds — domestic calendar" The Joneses' post-1946 flight to Mexico (and Big Jim Martin's in 1950) lands in an American-occupied country in the setting. It's outside the 1930s window, so minor, but it touches the empire.
A · Big Jim Martin "holds — domestic calendar" Same point: he fled to Mexico in 1950.
O · "Policy Sam" and Patsy King "pre-1916, happened" The entry also covers 1920s Harlem numbers (Holstein, St. Clair). Casper Holstein was a Virgin Islander, and the setting has the Danish islands in the empire. It's a light touch, but the label covers more than the 1890s.
G · Joe Louis (rise) "BENDS on the Schmeling fights" Correct, but the same entry's Schmeling 1936 fight text still says nothing about the Nazi frame being absent. That's handled in the Braddock entry. No change needed.

No entry labelled "holds" touches the presidents after Wilson in a way that breaks. De Priest (Hoover) and Mitchell (Roosevelt) are correctly held.

Canon and language checks

  • Canon: no hook calls itself canon. The word appears only in the brief's required label "(proposal, not canon)" and once as "Ghosts are canon in the setting", which describes the existing setting, not a proposal. Acceptable.
  • Respectful language: mostly good; period terms (Negro, race records, Black Belt) are used as period names. Two notes:
    • (1) The Nixon entry quotes the Tribune's "jungle beast" and "missing link" and uses "Brick Moron" as a heading. These are the period's racist framing of a Black teenager. They are quoted, not endorsed, but the entry never says so. I recommend the owner add "(the Tribune's racist framing)" where they're used. The hook's "a moron imitating the headlines" is presented as the period's reading but reads uncomfortably.

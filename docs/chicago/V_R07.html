V_R07 — Fact-check of R07 "The Great Lakes ring beyond Chicago, 1929–1939"

  • Checker: Claude Fable 5.1 (cross-model verifier)
  • Date: 2026-09-26
  • Chapter checked: R07_great_lakes_ring.md (73 entries; author: Claude Opus 5.5)
  • Method: WebFetch of the URLs the chapter cites (Wikipedia, Encyclopedia of Cleveland History, Encyclopedia of Milwaukee, MNopedia, airships.net, jcu.edu, the JCU Magazine PDF on the Vatican Observatory site) plus a few neighbouring pages (Karl Arnstein, University Heights, Indiana Klan, United Rubber Workers, Sit-down strike, Harry Sawyer, Keshena, Wikimedia Commons file pages). WebSearch was exhausted session-wide before this check started; no searches were used. Claims chosen for risk: every Top-10 item, every "odd detail" in a High-confidence entry, the specific numbers/dates/addresses/quotations, and everything that sounded too good to be true.
  • Verdict key: CONFIRMED = the cited page (or a page I name) says it · CORRECTED = wrong or imprecise; right fact + URL given · UNSUPPORTED = the cited page does not say it and I could not confirm it elsewhere · WRONG = false with no salvage.
  • Overall: The chapter is accurate to an unusual degree. Every quotation checked is verbatim or a fair truncation; every "odd detail" in the High-confidence entries stands, including the ones that sounded invented (the Airdock's indoor rain, the priest with the dog and canary in the tower, Curtis Williams's ashes dropped from an airplane, Bennett's dynamite-wired moat, the 150-year-old turtle, the fertility stones at Quintland, Sousa's march banned for 59 years). The errors are small: one wrong number (Buffalo Central Terminal's designed train count), one mis-framed statistic (Milwaukee's "30,000 children"), three date/precision slips, and a handful of details that are not on the pages cited.

Divergence-label problems

The chapter's labelling is careful and mostly consistent; the German-America, Philippines, Mexico, communism and Canada/Britain bends are all caught. Problems found:

  1. Wingfoot Air Express (1919) — "holds — domestic (… nothing here touches the war)." It does. Goodyear's blimp line was built on US Navy wartime contracts (Goodyear supplied Navy B- and C-class blimps from 1917); the Type FD was the commercial descendant. In a no-US-entry world Goodyear's 1919 airship business, and therefore this flight, is not guaranteed. Should be BENDS lightly (the Airdock entry already treats the German lineage as BENDS; this is the American-war side of the same company).
  2. Inconsistent treatment of communist-led strikes. Ford Hunger March = BENDS and Minneapolis Teamsters = BENDS (because Unemployed Councils / Trotskyists need reassigning), but Flint sit-down = holds and Auto-Lite = holds, each with a parenthesis admitting the same problem (communist organisers at Flint; Muste's Marxist AWP at Toledo). Either all four bend lightly or none do; recommend BENDS lightly for Flint and Auto-Lite for consistency.
  3. Harry Bennett "1915–1945 … holds — domestic." The 1930s content holds; the 1945 ending (Henry Ford II's wartime elevation after Edsel's 1943 death) is a WWII outcome and out of window. Suggest "holds for the 1930s; the 1945 ending is WWII-dependent."
  4. Eucharistic Congress "holds — domestic calendar." The entry itself concedes "Pope Pius XI by radio from Italy depends on the setting's Italy and Vatican" and the bishop's German birth; that is a BENDS-lightly caveat sitting under a "holds" label. Minor.
  5. Black Legion "holds." Fine as labelled (the setting already owns the Legion), but note the real Legion's platform was anti-communist as much as anti-Catholic/anti-Jewish; with no Red Scare its recruiting pitch changes. Worth a parenthesis, not a relabel.

No entry marked BENDS was found that should be "holds"; the BENDS-lightly calls on Mount Pleasant, Menominee, Honeymoon Bridge, Kamloops, Liggett and Red Ryan are all defensible.

The errors that matter

  1. The breathing dome (rows 93–94, WRONG). The roof did not rise and fall six feet, and not "twice a day". The guide says the circumference varied more than six feet with temperature, and the roof rose or sank up to eighteen inches with snow and air pressure. The dome was 310 ft across at the base (206 ft clear), not 200.
  2. Chicago's Czechs (row 67, WRONG). They were not "second only to New York". Chicago was first: Čapek's 1930 table gives 122,089, ahead of Cleveland (91,683) and New York (72,282).
  3. The Gutenberg quote (row 4). It was not fairgoers' chatter. It was the German Day keynote. George Seibel said: "When I look at Gutenberg's press exhibited at this fair, I feel like saying: 'My poor child, what have they done to you?'" That makes a better odd detail than R04's.
  4. The Belgian Village (row 70). It opened in 1933, not 1934, and was "famous" by the 1934 guide.
  5. The Torso mask (row 125). R04 put it "between the blimp rides and the Aquacade". The mask was shown in 1936; the Aquacade came in 1937.
  6. The Zephyr's nose (row 41). Cret did not design it; Albert Gardner Dean did. Cret and Harbeson did the side fluting.
  7. German Day choirs (row 1). Seven of the ten cities R04 lists sent delegations, not singing societies.
  8. Krupp's gun weight (row 16). R04 glued two sources that disagree: 240,000 lb (≈109 t) and 122.4 t (≈270,000 lb).
  9. Smaller fixes: the Sky Ride's 3,050 ft between counterbalances, not 3,200 (row 36); Ness about six months into the job, not a year (row 83); the Hall of Science's Transparent Man and Italy at the same end (row 96); the Maya temple was not the fair's only non-Christian temple (row 111); the balloon record had a few hundred spectators and a radio audience, not "nobody" (row 123); the Czech memorial book is a tribute, not an obituary (row 69); the Koch memorial was not "about the inside of the body" (row 131); "32-foot marble" Balbo column (row 23); Hungerford wrote "much of" the libretto (row 44).

New material for the owner, from the 1933 guide

  • "Did a cardinal show up?" (row 99). The Hall of Religion's tour text lists "Presbyterians and Catholics" among the exhibitors under one roof. A Catholic Extension Society chapel car stood near the Sky-Ride. The hall's star relic was the Chalice of Antioch, lent from its New York strongbox for only the second time since it reached America (the first was a loan to the Louvre). The hall was "a dream of George W. Dixon, Chicago business man". It had a tower carillon and a Chapel of Meditation. R04's text still says the exhibitors were "not confirmed". It is left unedited under the "change nothing else" rule, but this gap is now filled.
  • The Hall of Science carillon is confirmed (J. C. Deagan bells; a carillon tower at the southwest corner). R04's own "(UNVERIFIED in pages read)" flag on it is left in place for the same reason.
  • The Streets of Paris ocean-liner entrance is confirmed. Visitors crossed "the bridge connecting land with boat" past "the funnels of a steamer". Couney's incubators stood just before its entrance.
  • The National De Saible Memorial Society is named in the guide's exhibitor list, with "An exhibit of the life of De Saible — Special Building".
  • Ripley's Odditorium did run in 1934; it is listed in the 1934 guide.
  • The Czech census figures (48,814 / 73,275) are confirmed from the Czechoslovak memorial book. This closes an item V_R04 left open.

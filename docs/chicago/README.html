Chicago & the Great Lakes, 1925–1940 — Umbra research library (sweep of 2026-09-26/27)

Real history for UMBRA's Chicago book — crime, churchmen, the World's Fair, the Great Lakes ring, oddities, places, people — gathered at the owner's request on 2026-09-26: "find as many historical facts … locations, things of that nature … full spectrum research … around Chicago, not just in it … high-profile instances in the surrounding Great Lakes area."

Nothing here is canon. Nothing was merged into the rulebook or Docs/lore/ALTERNATE_HISTORY.md. Every "Umbra hook" is a proposal. Facts carry source URLs; anything unconfirmed is tagged [UNVERIFIED …]; anything a fact-checker corrected carries ⟨fixed: …⟩.

Start here (in this order)

  1. S01_UMBRA_THREADS.md — the synthesis (Fable): the 50 best finds, 10–15 threads that run across the library (Dawes; Germany's footprint; the Church's autumn of 1936; the 22nd of December; the Golden Circle's Chicago roots…), the divergence register (everything the 1916 break changes, with a proposed substitute), corrections to existing Umbra material, and ten questions only the owner can answer. Written before the later chapters (R19–R35) landed — it doesn't draw on them yet.
  2. S02_MISSION_SEEDS.md — 30 mission seeds built on real anchors (Opus). Seeds 1–7 are the owner's own seven design-doc seeds, his words quoted at the top of each. Audited against the corrected chapters (18 fixes applied); the audit was stopped before its report was written.
  3. S02b_MISSION_SEEDS_BATCH_2.md — 15 more seeds (31–45) from the later chapters: 1931, Toledo, Buffalo, Toronto, Detroit, Cleveland, Milwaukee.
  4. S03_OWNER_QUESTIONS_RESOLVED.md — every open question the research raised, answered from the rulebook/lore/audit evidence or settled as delegated — historical sense; only one is left for the owner.
  5. Rebuild in one command: REFERENCE_v2\scripts\rebuild_all.cmd. (The built files are not in git — see its .gitignore; a copy sits in OneDrive next to the 09-22 archive.)

What answered the owner's specific questions

He asked Where Short answer
The teachers' strike R03 (✔ checked) April 1933: thousands of unpaid teachers stormed the Loop banks; Charles Dawes — a president in the setting — was heckled at his own bank over his $90M RFC bailout and shouted back "To hell with trouble makers" (three 1933 wire texts). The exact chant "We want Dawes!" is only in a paywalled archive — unverified. Also the 09-22 archive's entry H05.
Did a cardinal show up? R05, R24 Yes. Cardinal Pacelli (the future Pius XII) toured the Great Lakes by plane in Oct 1936 and slept at Cardinal Mundelein's residence on Sunday 25 Oct 1936. Also the 1926 Eucharistic Congress legate, and Cleveland's 1935 Congress.
What did Germany send to the World's Fair? R04 (✔ checked twice) Officially, little: the 1933 guide lists 18 nations; Germany came as loans and firms (Dresden's Transparent Man, a Koch memorial exhibit, Bosch, a Zeiss microscope show, a replica Gutenberg press) plus the Graf Zeppelin's one day (Eckener flew clockwise to hide the swastika fin). Chicago's Germans built their own hall and flew the Kaiser's imperial flag to keep the swastika off it. 1893's Krupp gun pavilion is the model for what a victorious Germany would send (proposal).
The YouTuber R08 (✔) Best fit Adam Selzer (YouTube adamselzer, "Mysterious Chicago" / AdamChicago); runner-up Dilla Thomas, You Don't Know Chi (walks neighbourhoods on camera). Neither confirmed as the one.
His seven design-doc seeds R17 (✔), S02 All seven are real events. The "College Kidnappers" were Theodore "Handsome Jack" Klutas's ring (1931–Jan 1934) — he posed as a Secret Service agent (1934 wire story); the face/fingerprint alteration is only a rumour.

Things to know

  • Two federal factions, not one. An early version of BRIEF.md lumped the Secret Service and FBI Section 13 together under K. Edgar Hoover. Wrong — per rulebook §32 they are separate rivals, and Hoover runs the FBI. The error was corrected in the brief mid-run and fixed across the chapters (⟨fixed: factions⟩, log in V_FACTIONS.md). A few hooks still need an owner call (whose man Eliot Ness is; which faction owns the crime-lab "door") — listed in V_FACTIONS.md.
  • The Golden Circle's size. ALTERNATE_HISTORY.md §9.1 is right (~1,200 miles in radius, ~2,400 across). An assistant's memory note had it as "1,200 wide", and that error leaked into BRIEF.md and R10; both were caught.
  • For the alpha's December 1936 (R24): the solstice fell at 6:27 p.m. CST on Monday 21 Dec ("22 Dec" is the Greenwich date); the penumbral eclipse was a faint Sunday-evening event on 27 Dec (max 9:49 p.m. CST); no snow at Christmas. R24 is only partly checked — confirm before relying on it.
  • Search conditions: the session's web-search allowance ran out early; agents worked from direct page fetches, Chronicling America (until it rate-limited), archive.org full texts and the WPA Foreign Language Press Survey. The Encyclopedia of Chicago site was unreachable all session. Each chapter's "Leads not chased" lists what's left.

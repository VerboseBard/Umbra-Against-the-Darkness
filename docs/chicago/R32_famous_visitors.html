Overview

Chicago in 1925–1940 was a city people passed through as much as visited: the country's rail hub, where every coast-to-coast traveller changed trains, so many of the best "visits" here are an hour in a station — Einstein in a Pullman car at Union Station in 1931 with a peace delegation and the Frank Lloyd Wrights' small daughter; Meher Baba's two-hour taxi loop along Lake Shore Drive from LaSalle Street in May 1932. The set-piece visits cluster in 1926 (Queen Marie's three days at the Drake, Houdini's eight weeks at the Princess, Valentino's quarrel with the Tribune, the Swedish crown prince's cornerstone) and in 1929–33 (Churchill twice, Heisenberg in Hyde Park, Chesterton at Notre Dame, Einstein's 1933 birthday luncheon at the Standard Club, Stein's squad-car murder hunt, Maria Rasputin carried into the Chicago Stadium on a bed in 1935). Several famous names turn out never to have come in the decade, and the file says so: Ramsay MacDonald (1929 — New York, Washington, Niagara, not Chicago), Aleister Crowley (gone from America after 1919), Tagore (Chicago friend and correspondent, but no confirmed visit after 1916), and Yogananda (not confirmed in any page read). For the setting the richest vein is the visits that bend: Einstein declaring his homeland "as shaky as California was last week" in March 1933 — in a world with no Nazi Germany, the reason he won't go home has to be something else; Queen Marie's cold White House dinner, which in the setting is President Leonard Wood's; the Kaiser's grandson on a Ford line in Detroit, now a prince of the winning Reich; Tagore publicly accused in 1918 of plotting with Berlin; and Churchill guarded against an Indian society's threats on Astor Street — a defeated empire's ex-minister in a city where Germany's Indian clients once organised.

Part 1 — Statesmen and heads of government

Winston Churchill's three days in Chicago — 2–4 October 1929

  • Where: The Drake Hotel (140 E. Walton Place); the Union Stock Yards; the Coliseum (1513 S. Wabash); the Gary, Ind. steel mills; an Insull power plant; the Blackstone Hotel (636 S. Michigan); the Tribune Tower
  • What happened: Out of office since the May 1929 election, Churchill was crossing North America with his brother Jack, his son Randolph and his nephew Johnny, riding in a private rail car provided by the financier Bernard Baruch. In Chicago on 2–4 October he stayed at the Drake; P. D. Armour took him through the stockyards; he saw his first prize fight at the Coliseum, toured the Gary steel mills and one of Samuel Insull's power plants, and addressed the Commercial Club at the Blackstone before 300 people. Col. Robert R. McCormick was in New York on business, so he had the Tribune cartoonist John T. McCutcheon host a lunch with the editors and take the Churchills through the Tribune building. Baruch attended and went on with Churchill to New York — where, three weeks later, the market broke.
  • The odd detail: The future Prime Minister's first prize fight was in the Coliseum, the hall built out of the walls of Libby Prison (cross-ref R16) — and his host for the day was Insull, whose empire would be in ruins within three years.
  • People: Winston Churchill — ex-Chancellor of the Exchequer, touring; Bernard Baruch — financier, provided the rail car; P. D. Armour — packer; John T. McCutcheon — Tribune cartoonist, McCormick's stand-in; Randolph, Jack and Johnny Churchill — the party
  • Sources: https://winstonchurchill.org/publications/finest-hour/finest-hour-118/encounters-with-chicago/ ; https://winstonchurchill.org/publications/finest-hour/finest-hour-131/chicago-conference-prelude-winston-s-churchill-and-robert-r-mccormick/
  • Confidence: High (two Finest Hour articles agree on the hotel, hosts and venues).
  • Divergence check: BENDS — Britain lost the war and pays reparations to Berlin; Churchill (First Lord of the Admiralty 1911–15, the Dardanelles) is a statesman of a defeated, indebted empire touring a neutral country that got rich. The itinerary holds — a defeated Briton would want to see the stockyards, the steel and the power even more.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Stockyards, steel mills, power plant, Tribune — a tour of Chicago's engines, with a Secret Service man assigned to a foreign ex-minister and an Insull plant on the route. What Churchill was shown in the plant's basement is the hook; what Baruch's private rail car carried back east is the second one.

Churchill's lecture stop — Chicago, February 1932

  • Where: Dearborn Station (47 W. Polk); Col. McCormick's house, 1519 N. Astor Street; the Union League Club (65 W. Jackson); Orchestra Hall (220 S. Michigan)
  • What happened: Churchill was on a paid North American lecture tour, resumed after he had been knocked down by a car in New York in December 1931. In Chicago McCormick sent Capt. Maxwell Corpening to collect him at Dearborn Station and put him up at his Astor Street house, where Churchill "would park himself in front of a certain Cézanne." He spoke on "The World Economic Crisis" to more than 1,200 at the Union League Club and gave a second lecture at Orchestra Hall. McCormick gave at least one luncheon for him with University of Chicago president Robert Maynard Hutchins and Illinois Supreme Court Justice Frederic DeYoung; Mayor Cermak was sick and did not attend. Threats from "an Indian society" meant the Scotland Yard bodyguard Walter Thompson worked the visit; the secretary Phyllis Moir travelled with him. The Tribune praised his "candor" and called him "a forthright Briton… seasoned statesman."
  • The odd detail: An Indian nationalist society's threats turned a Loop lecture into a guarded event — and the guard was the same detective who shadowed Churchill through the next war.
  • People: Winston Churchill; Col. Robert R. McCormick — Tribune publisher and host; Capt. Maxwell Corpening — McCormick's man at the station; Walter Thompson — Scotland Yard bodyguard; Phyllis Moir — secretary; Robert M. Hutchins — University of Chicago president; Frederic DeYoung — Illinois Supreme Court justice
  • Sources: https://winstonchurchill.org/publications/finest-hour/finest-hour-131/chicago-conference-prelude-winston-s-churchill-and-robert-r-mccormick/ ; https://winstonchurchill.org/publications/finest-hour/finest-hour-118/encounters-with-chicago/
  • Confidence: High on venues, hosts and the threat; the exact day in February 1932 is UNVERIFIED (Wikipedia's tour article did not load).
  • Divergence check: BENDS — as above, plus the Indian threat: the real Hindu–German Conspiracy of 1914–17 was run partly from Chicago with German money; in a world where Germany won, Chicago's Indian revolutionaries are the clients of the winning side ("Germany as the patron of other people's revolutions"), and a British ex-minister on Astor Street is a target with no embassy to protect him.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The threat is real but its authors aren't Indian; the Hunter Guild sells "protection" to a foreign statesman while Section 13 watches the Guild. The Cézanne in McCormick's drawing room is a door.

Ramsay MacDonald's America — and why Chicago never saw him, 4–25 October 1929

  • Where: New York; Washington; Rapidan Camp, Va.; the Senate; Niagara Falls; Toronto; Ottawa; Quebec
  • What happened: The first sitting British Prime Minister to visit the United States landed in New York on 4 October 1929 (ticker-tape parade the same day), went by train to Washington for a 20-minute first meeting with Hoover, retreated with him to Rapidan Camp on 5 October for naval-disarmament talks, and on 7 October addressed the Senate for 20 minutes on peace and disarmament. He crossed into Canada at Niagara Falls on 15 October, reached Toronto, met Mackenzie King in Ottawa on 17 October and sailed from Quebec on 25 October — the day after Black Thursday. Chicago was not on the route.
  • The odd detail: The one week a British Prime Minister spent in North America straddled the Crash, and he left the continent from Quebec the day after the market broke.
  • People: Ramsay MacDonald — Prime Minister; Herbert Hoover — host; W. L. Mackenzie King — Canadian PM
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_1929 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ticker-tape_parades_in_New_York_City
  • Confidence: High on the dates read; the absence of a Chicago stop is as good as one day-by-day chronology.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — MacDonald opposed the war in 1914; in a Britain that lost he is the man who was right, leading a country that pays Germany. The visit's object inverts: not naval parity with Washington but loans, and the Senate speech becomes a plea. Hoover is president in both worlds.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A visit that stops at Niagara and Toronto but not Chicago is a GM's gift — whatever was meant to reach him in Chicago has to go to Buffalo instead, on the New York Central, past Cleveland (IHS) and through the Golden Circle's lake ports.

Cardinal Pacelli's Chicago day — between 23 October and 1 November 1936 (extends R05)

  • Where: Chicago (Cardinal Mundelein's residence / St. Mary of the Lake Seminary, Mundelein, Ill. — the exact Chicago venues are UNVERIFIED); South Bend (Notre Dame); St. Paul; Cleveland; Cincinnati; St. Louis
  • What happened: The Vatican Secretary of State sailed from Naples on 1 October 1936 on the Conte di Savoia with the Italian ambassador Fulvio Suvich, Prince Marcello Gentili del Drago and Bishop Hugh Lamb, landed in New York on 8 October (met by Cardinal Hayes and the Apostolic Delegate Cicognani) and was the guest of Genevieve Garvan Brady at Inisfada, Manhasset. Bishop Francis Spellman "had boarded the ocean liner even before Pacelli disembarked" and ran the trip. Boston 12–15 October (Coughlin was in Boston at the same time; they did not meet); Knights of Columbus headquarters, New Haven, 18 October; Washington 21–22 October (Catholic University, the Library of Congress, Mount Vernon, Georgetown, a broadcast speech at the National Press Club, a brief stop at Baltimore's cathedral). On 23 October he left Roosevelt Field, Long Island, in a chartered United Airlines plane for a coast-to-coast tour — "the Flying Cardinal" — that took in South Bend (honorary doctorate at Notre Dame; Bishop John Noll and president John F. O'Hara), Chicago (where he met Cardinal Mundelein), St. Paul, San Francisco, Los Angeles, then Cleveland, Cincinnati, Syracuse and St. Louis, ending in New York on 1 November with Mass at St. Ignatius Loyola and a Fordham doctorate. On 5 November, the day after the election, he lunched two hours with Roosevelt at Hyde Park; Spellman told the press they were forbidden to ask about Coughlin. He sailed on 6 November from Pier 59 after seeing Mayor La Guardia and Al Smith, and was in Rome on 14 November.
  • The odd detail: The one question the press was forbidden to ask the Vatican's foreign minister was about a Detroit parish priest.
  • People: Eugenio Pacelli — Cardinal Secretary of State; Francis Spellman — organiser; Genevieve Garvan Brady — hostess; Fulvio Suvich — Italian ambassador; Hugh Lamb — bishop, travelling companion; George Mundelein — Chicago host; John F. Noll, John F. O'Hara — Notre Dame
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_visit_by_Eugenio_Pacelli_to_the_United_States
  • Confidence: High on the national itinerary; the Chicago day, hotel and programme are UNVERIFIED (lead: the archdiocesan New World, the Tribune of 24–27 Oct 1936).
  • Divergence check: BENDS — Pacelli's career ran through Germany (nuncio in Munich and Berlin 1917–29); in a world where Germany won, the Kaiser's nuncio is a friend of the winning court, and his tour of a Catholic America is watched by Berlin as well as Rome. The Coughlin ban holds.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): As R05 says: an IHS inspection under cover of a tour. Add the detail that the plane was chartered — its crew list, its refuelling stops (Cheyenne? Salt Lake?) and what was loaded at Chicago Municipal are all a GM's to invent.

Part 2 — Royalty

The Crown Prince and Crown Princess of Sweden in Chicago — early June 1926

  • Where: New York (ticker-tape parade 27 May 1926); Chicago — the cornerstone of "the Morton Building" (2 June); the Swedish Old People's Home, Evanston; later Philadelphia and San Francisco (Bohemian Grove, July)
  • What happened: Crown Prince Gustaf Adolf (the archaeologist prince, king from 1950) and Crown Princess Louise (Mountbatten) toured the United States from late May 1926. New York gave them a ticker-tape parade on 27 May. The Swedish press of Chicago announced weeks ahead that "His Royal Highness, the Swedish Crown Prince" would lay a cornerstone on 2 June — Svenska Kuriren of 6 May 1926, reporting a Chicago contractor's award of "the Morton Building" contract — and that the royal couple would attend festivities at the Swedish Old People's Home in Evanston (Svenska Kuriren, 1 July 1926). In July the prince was at Bohemian Grove to see George Sterling's Grove Play Truth.
  • The odd detail: Chicago's Swedish papers printed the prince's schedule a month before he sailed — a GM's calendar for who knew where the royal party would be.
  • People: Crown Prince Gustaf Adolf — later Gustaf VI Adolf; Crown Princess Louise — born Mountbatten (Battenberg)
  • Sources: https://flps.newberry.org/?keyword=Crown+Prince&group=swedish (items: Svenska Kuriren 6 May 1926 and 1 July 1926) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ticker-tape_parades_in_New_York_City ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustaf_VI_Adolf
  • Confidence: Medium — the Chicago dates come from two press-survey snippets; which "Morton Building" (UNVERIFIED) and where the couple stayed are not established.
  • Divergence check: Holds in outline (Sweden was neutral), but a Sweden next to a victorious Germany is a German client, and the princess is a Battenberg whose family's British branch lost.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A cornerstone laid by a royal archaeologist — what he put in the box. The Evanston old people's home is the kind of place where a vampire keeps its oldest servants.

Queen Marie of Romania's three Chicago days — 13 to about 16 November 1926 (living memory; extends R15)

  • Where: Union Station (arrival, 13 Nov); The Drake Hotel, 140 E. Walton Place (where she stayed and dined); Lincoln Park; the Daily News Fresh Air Sanitarium for Babies; a radio studio (station UNVERIFIED); the closing banquet at the Blackstone Hotel, 636 S. Michigan; and on 14 Nov the Rumanian Shaarey Shomayim synagogue, Douglas Blvd. & Millard Ave. (R15)
  • What happened: Marie landed in New York on 18 October 1926 with Prince Nicholas and Princess Ileana. The tour was underwritten by Sam Hill, the West Coast millionaire who had met her in Paris during the peace talks, so that she could dedicate his "large and very eccentric mansion" above the Columbia River — Maryhill, which Hill conceived as "a monument to peace, to his wife Mary, and to Queen Marie" and which the dancer Loie Fuller, Marie's old friend, talked him into making a museum. She crossed the continent and back — about 10,000 miles — on a fifteen-car special train, The Royal Romanian, and about a million people saw her. Mayor William E. Dever met her at Union Station on 13 November; she stayed at the Drake, dined there that night with the city's important people, made a radio broadcast, visited the Daily News Fresh Air Sanitarium for Babies, and fell behind schedule in Lincoln Park because she stopped to talk with Romanian women. She asked the press photographers not to fire their flashes on staircases — the flash blinded her "and she was afraid of falling" — and they obliged; at the closing banquet at the Blackstone she raised her glass "To the Chicago newspaper photographers!" She sailed from New York on 24 November, taking home a bullet-proof armoured Willys-Knight town car, "a lawnmower and an ice-making machine."
  • The odd detail: A queen who asked Chicago's flash men to spare her on the stairs — and then drank their health.
  • People: Queen Marie of Romania; Prince Nicholas and Princess Ileana — her children; Samuel Hill — sponsor, builder of Maryhill; Loie Fuller — dancer, her friend; Mayor William E. Dever — host
  • Sources: https://www.wbez.org/2012/11/13/a-queen-in-chicago ; https://www.americanheritage.com/queen-marie (Gene Smith, Oct 1994) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_of_Romania ; https://www.rciusa.info/post/queen-marie-in-america-1926
  • Confidence: Medium-High — arrival date, Drake, Blackstone banquet, Lincoln Park and the photographers are from one WBEZ retrospective; the sanitarium visit from American Heritage; the day she left Chicago (about three days after arriving) and the radio station are UNVERIFIED.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — in the record the White House received her coldly under Coolidge ("thirteen minutes"); in the setting the president in November 1926 is Leonard Wood, so that awkward dinner is his. Romania fought on the Allied side and was crushed in 1916–18; in a German victory her crown exists at Berlin's pleasure (her husband Ferdinand was himself a Hohenzollern — general knowledge, not re-checked).
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Vampires conspiring in high society: the flash request was not about her eyes. Something in the royal party does not photograph cleanly on a staircase, and the one plate a Tribune man shot anyway is in a Section 13 drawer.

Crown Prince Olav and Crown Princess Märtha of Norway at Oak Park — 4 May 1939

  • Where: United Evangelical Lutheran Church, Ridgeland Ave. & Greenfield St., Oak Park (their Chicago programme otherwise UNVERIFIED)
  • What happened: On a long goodwill tour of the United States in spring 1939, the heir to the Norwegian throne and his Swedish-born wife arrived in Oak Park with a police motorcycle escort for what the local museum calls "their first public appearance of the tour." Some 4,000 Oak Parkers, many of Norwegian descent, packed the church and the blocks around it; the Hatch School across the street dismissed its pupils "to assist in the welcome of the royal couple." Pastor and Mrs. Edwin Hegge were hosts; Mrs. Hegge gave the princess roses. The brief service had the organist Arnold Schultz and the choir, prayers, a welcome talk and the presentation of an engraved Bible, and ended with the whole crowd singing the Norwegian national anthem. One onlooker: "They look like a couple of good looking farmers pretty well upset by all this fol-de-rol." (The Danish crown prince and princess, Frederik and Ingrid, made a similar tour the same spring that reportedly included Chicago — UNVERIFIED, search summary only.)
  • The odd detail: A school let out so its children could line a church door for a prince — and a spectator's verdict that royalty looked like farmers in their Sunday clothes.
  • People: Crown Prince Olav (later Olav V); Crown Princess Märtha; Pastor Edwin Hegge and Mrs. Hegge — hosts; Arnold Schultz — organist
  • Sources: https://oprfmuseum.org/this-month-in-history/royalty-visits-oak-park-1939
  • Confidence: High on the Oak Park service (one local-history page); the rest of their Chicago days UNVERIFIED.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — in the record the 1939 royal tours were Scandinavia courting Washington before a war with Germany; in the setting there is no such war coming, Germany already rules the continent's order, and a Norwegian heir touring America is a small kingdom looking for a friend outside Berlin's reach.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Cult versus population: 4,000 ordinary Norwegian Lutherans in the street, and somewhere in the choir loft the one family who asked the prince for something in private. The engraved Bible has a second inscription.

Prince Louis Ferdinand of Prussia, the Kaiser's grandson on the Ford line — Detroit, 1929–1933/34 (ring)

  • Where: Detroit — a Ford plant (which one is UNVERIFIED); in 1930 a Ford assembly line in Buenos Aires (search summary only — UNVERIFIED)
  • What happened: Louis Ferdinand, grandson of Kaiser Wilhelm II and third in the line behind his father Crown Prince Wilhelm and his elder brother, "settled for some time in Detroit," befriended Henry Ford, and "became acquainted with Franklin D. Roosevelt, among others," keeping "a great interest in engineering." A royalty-history site puts it as "settled in Detroit between 1929 and 1934, where he worked at a Ford automobile plant. Henry Ford, the automobile magnate, took him under his wing"; he "extolled the virtues of bourgeois life in the United States." He was recalled when his brother renounced his rights in 1933 to marry a commoner, and went into the German aviation industry. Newsreel footage of Ford and the prince talking outside a building exists (criticalpast, not opened). A Chicago visit was not found.
  • The odd detail: A Hohenzollern prince punching a clock in Detroit, and saying he'd have happily stayed.
  • People: Louis Ferdinand, Prince of Prussia; Henry Ford — patron; Wilhelm II — grandfather; Franklin D. Roosevelt — acquaintance
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Ferdinand,_Prince_of_Prussia ; https://www.histclo.com/royal/ger/royal-gerlf.htm
  • Confidence: Medium — the Detroit years and Ford friendship are stated in both pages; exact dates, the plant and what he did there are UNVERIFIED.
  • Divergence check: BENDS HARD — in the setting the Kaiser won; the Hohenzollerns presumably still reign (the owner's call). This is no exile's grandson but a prince of the victorious Reich working for America's greatest industrialist — a young man sent to learn how the neutral giant builds things.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Germany as the patron of other people's revolutions: the prince is the Reich's friendliest face in the Great Lakes, and Section 13 has a man on the same assembly line. What he carried home in 1933 was not only notes on engines. (Detroit: R26.)

Part 3 — Aviators and the machines that brought them

The Graf Zeppelin's first pass over Chicago — 28 August 1929, the world flight

  • Where: Over the city, on the Los Angeles → Lakehurst leg
  • What happened: On its round-the-world flight the LZ-127 crossed the Pacific non-stop, reached Los Angeles at 1:16 a.m. on 26 August 1929, left Mines Field about 23 hours later (12:14 a.m. on the 27th), made "a difficult summertime passage over the deserts of Arizona and Texas" and came up across the Midwest to the East Coast, landing at Lakehurst at 8:13 a.m. on 29 August — 21 days and about 12 days' flying time for the circuit. Chicago lay on the route: "the ship was greeted with wild enthusiasm by the people of Chicago." Sixty-one aboard, one woman — the Hearst reporter Lady Grace Drummond-Hay. Chicago's German press then ran an Eckener fund: Abendpost items of 1 and 17 October and 14 November 1929 list contributions totalling $12,387.98 by mid-October, continuing into November "despite some local protests." Eckener got his second New York ticker-tape parade on 30 August. (For the 1933 visit and the swastika trick, see R04.)
  • The odd detail: Chicago's Germans took up a collection for the airship's captain that ran past the Crash — the fund's November total is a measure of what the German North Side still had in its pockets three weeks after Black Tuesday.
  • People: Dr. Hugo Eckener — commander; Lady Grace Drummond-Hay — the only woman aboard
  • Sources: https://www.airships.net/lz127-graf-zeppelin/history/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_1929 ; https://flps.newberry.org/?keyword=Zeppelin&group=german (Abendpost 1 Oct, 17 Oct, 14 Nov 1929)
  • Confidence: Medium — the Chicago overflight is stated by airships.net and the Abendpost; the date (28 Aug) is inferred from the leg's start and end, and the hour is UNVERIFIED.
  • Divergence check: BENDS in tone only — an Imperial German airship, not a Weimar one, and the Eckener fund is money raised for the winning side's hero. The local protests (1929) need a new reason: in the setting they'd come from Chicago's Poles, French or Britons.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Something looked up at the ship on 28 August 1929 from a South Side rooftop and has been waiting for it to come back; it does, on 26 October 1933 (R04).

Francesco de Pinedo lands at Chicago — mid-May 1927 (living memory; the first Italian armada was one plane)

  • Where: Lake Michigan / the Chicago lakefront (landing site UNVERIFIED); earlier New Orleans; then Montreal
  • What happened: Italy's Marchese de Pinedo, with Carlo Del Prete and mechanic Vitale Zacchetti, left Cagliari on 13 February 1927 in the Savoia-Marchetti S.55 flying boat Santa Maria, crossed to Brazil (24 Feb), flew the Mato Grosso, and entered the United States at New Orleans on 29 March — "the first foreign airplane entering the U.S." — to the delight of Italian-Americans up the Mississippi. At a refuelling stop on Theodore Roosevelt Lake, Arizona, "a teenage volunteer helping to refuel the Santa Maria carelessly discarded a cigarette that ignited gasoline on the water's surface"; the plane burned and its engines sank 60 feet. An identical Santa Maria II reached New York by ship on 1 May, was christened on 8 May, and the crew resumed: up the Mississippi to Chicago, then an 11-hour flight from Chicago to Montreal on 17 May. They left Trepassey, Newfoundland on 22 May, ran short of fuel, were towed 200 miles to the Azores (30 May) and reached Ostia on 16 June — 29,180 miles in 124 days. The Chicago Italian community's Italo-American National Union later listed a $1,000 gift "for the air flight of General De Pinedo" in its ledger.
  • The odd detail: The first foreign aircraft to enter the United States was destroyed by a boy's cigarette on a desert lake — and its replacement sat on Lake Michigan six years before Balbo's twenty-four.
  • People: Francesco de Pinedo — aviator, later a Fascist air general; Carlo Del Prete — co-pilot; Vitale Zacchetti — mechanic
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_de_Pinedo ; https://flps.newberry.org/?keyword=Pinedo&group=italian (Bulletin Italo-American National Union, Dec 1933)
  • Confidence: Medium — the Chicago stop and the Montreal date are from Wikipedia; the Chicago landing day, site and reception are UNVERIFIED (lead: L'Italia, mid-May 1927).
  • Divergence check: BENDS lightly — Italy's politics after a German victory are the owner's call (see R04 on Balbo); the flight itself holds.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The Hunter Guild's Chicago chapter met its first Italian aviator in 1927 and remembered the name in 1933. The burned Santa Maria's engines are still at the bottom of an Arizona lake.

Part 4 — Scientists and thinkers

Werner Heisenberg lectures in Hyde Park — spring 1929

  • Where: The University of Chicago
  • What happened: In 1929 Heisenberg, 27, made a lecture tour of the United States, China, Japan and India; "in the spring of 1929, he was a visiting lecturer at the University of Chicago, where he lectured on quantum mechanics" — the lectures became his book The Physical Principles of the Quantum Theory (general knowledge; the book's Chicago origin was not confirmed from a page read). In June–July 1939 he was back in America at the University of Michigan with Samuel Goudsmit and refused an invitation to emigrate.
  • The odd detail: The man who would run Germany's wartime atomic project spent a spring in Hyde Park a decade before Fermi's pile went up under the same campus's stands.
  • People: Werner Heisenberg — physicist; Arthur H. Compton — Chicago physics (host — UNVERIFIED); Samuel Goudsmit — Ann Arbor host, 1939
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg

Rasputin's daughter waves from a bed in the Chicago Stadium — April 1935

  • Where: Hagenbeck-Wallace Circus winter quarters, Peru, Ind.; Dukes Memorial Hospital, Peru; the Chicago Stadium (1800 W. Madison — address general knowledge)
  • What happened: Maria Rasputin, daughter of Grigori Rasputin, landed in New York by steamer on 8 March 1935, speaking little English, to join the Hagenbeck-Wallace Circus — then second only to Ringling — as a lion tamer, billed as "the daughter of the famous mad monk whose feats in Russia astonished the world." At the Peru winter quarters, rehearsing with three bears, two lions, two tigers, two leopards and two pumas, she lost her footing and was mauled by a Himalayan black bear named Himmy (10 April per one account, 13 April per Billboard of 20 April 1935): twelve wounds, four of them two inches deep. She was taken to Dukes Memorial Hospital and released six days later in a wheelchair; the circus doctor S. D. Malouf gave her "a week to 10 days." The show was due in Chicago by 20 April. She followed it by ambulance, and when it was announced that she would appear in the Grand Entry, "the [Chicago] Stadium was packed to the roof with people who gave her an ovation as she waved to them from her bed upon a beautifully decorated wagon." She stayed with the circus one season.
  • The odd detail: Rasputin's daughter, like her father, would not stay down — mauled, stitched, and in the Stadium's Grand Entry in a bed on a float within about two weeks.
  • People: Maria Rasputin — "lion tamer"; Himmy — the bear; Dr. S. D. Malouf — circus physician
  • Sources: https://russianlife.com/the-russia-file/tiger-queen-the-incredible-american-odyssey-of-rasputins-daughter/ ; https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/lions-tigers-bears-rasputin-daughter-151600412.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Rasputin
  • Confidence: High on the mauling and the Stadium appearance (two accounts, one citing Billboard); the exact date of the attack and of the Chicago opening conflict slightly.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — no Bolshevik victory and no Soviet Union: Russia lost the war and pays Germany, but the Revolution's outcome is the owner's call. If the Romanovs or a successor court survive, Rasputin's daughter is not a refugee act in a Midwestern circus but a woman with a name that still opens doors in Petrograd — why is she in Peru, Indiana?
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The man who would not die had a daughter who heals too fast. The Hunter Guild buys a ringside seat at the Stadium; the bear was not the first thing in the Peru barns to try her.

Part 7 — The lakes ring: royal and political visitors who stopped short of Chicago

King George VI and Queen Elizabeth on the Ontario shore — 20–22 May and early June 1939 (ring)

  • Where: Ottawa (Supreme Court cornerstone, 20 May 1939); Toronto (22 May — the King's Plate at Woodbine, Coronation Park); Niagara Falls, Ont. (the Rainbow Bridge dedication and a monument); the United States 7–12 June (Washington, New York, Poughkeepsie/Hyde Park)
  • What happened: The first reigning British sovereign to visit Canada and the United States crossed the Dominion by Canadian Pacific royal train. On the Great Lakes leg the Queen laid the Supreme Court cornerstone in Ottawa on 20 May; the couple attended the King's Plate in Toronto on 22 May and dedicated Coronation Park; at Niagara Falls they dedicated the not-yet-finished Rainbow Bridge and unveiled a monument, then entered the United States for 7–12 June. Windsor, Sudbury and the Lakehead were not named in the page read (UNVERIFIED as stops).
  • The odd detail: The bridge the King dedicated at Niagara wasn't finished — he blessed a span with no deck.
  • People: George VI; Queen Elizabeth; W. L. Mackenzie King — host
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939_royal_tour_of_Canada
  • Confidence: High on the dates read; the Niagara date is not given in the article.
  • Divergence check: BENDS hard — a king of a Britain that lost the war and pays reparations; Ireland already free; the Empire selling colonies to Washington. The tour's purpose (rallying a Dominion for a coming war with Germany) does not exist. Whether such a king tours at all, and what Canada is in this world, is the owner's call.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A royal train on the CPR's lakeshore line, a bridge with no deck, and a crowd from Detroit and Buffalo across the water — the Golden Circle's map has a northern edge and this is it.

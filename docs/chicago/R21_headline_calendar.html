R21 — "What's in the paper": a month-by-month headline calendar, Chicago & the Great Lakes, 1929–1939

  • Lane: R21 — real newspaper headlines a GM can hand players for any given month
  • Date: 2026-09-26
  • Model: Claude Fable 5.1 (research agent, Chronicling America sweep)
  • Method: Library of Congress Chronicling America full-text search (JSON API), then reading each hit's OCR page text. Headlines are quoted as printed from the OCR; obvious OCR breakage silently repaired, doubtful readings marked [OCR], summaries of a headline (not verbatim) marked [paraphrase]. Every line carries the page URL that was actually read. Out-of-town papers (Washington Evening Star, Indianapolis Times, Detroit papers, etc.) reported Chicago constantly — that is where most of these come from, since the Chicago dailies themselves are not in the public-domain set.
  • How to use: find the month of your session, hand the table three lines. Dates are the paper's dateline/issue date. "Umbra use" lines are proposals only.
  • Divergence note (applies to whole file): anything touching Germany, Russia, the Nazis, the WWI settlement, or the presidents before Hoover BENDS under the 1916 break; ordinary domestic Chicago news holds.
  • Faction note (per the corrected brief): the Secret Service and the FBI's Section 13 are two separate, rival federal factions; K. Edgar Hoover directs the FBI (Section 13 is its supernatural division). "Umbra use" lines below name them accordingly.
  • Coverage note: the Library of Congress search endpoint rate-limited this session about 35 minutes in (Cloudflare challenge on www.loc.gov). Months after that point were recovered by walking known microfilm reels directly on tile.loc.gov, which is why coverage is uneven: some months have 15–20 lines, others 3–5, and some are missing. Every line was read from the page's OCR text; the URL given is the page.

Overview

This pass read the front pages of four out-of-town dailies that carried Chicago and Great Lakes wire copy every day — the Indianapolis Times (United Press), the Washington Evening Star (AP), the Washington Times (INS/Hearst) and the South Bend News-Times (UP/INS, with Calumet-region local news) — and pulled every story datelined Chicago or a Great Lakes city, headline verbatim. It covers ten months in depth (January–March 1929, September–October 1930, April 1931, January and March 1933, March 1934) with 3–25 real headlines each, about 130 lines in all, plus a stack of unused reels and titles in "Leads not chased" so the other 122 months can be filled the same way. The strange sits next to the famous on the same front page: the St. Valentine's Day wire copy shares a page with a Detroit domestic-court squib; Dillinger's wooden-gun escape shares a month with a "Stonewall Hotel" letter home from Cook County jail, a Milwaukee jail's nude New Year's dance and a circus band in a hospital ward. The best finds for the setting are the ones the papers themselves wrote in occult language: a Chicago widow tried for bombing a gas station so her dead husband could haunt it (Mar 1933); the "Kiss of Death" girl whose every lover is shot (Jan 1933); a financier's "suicide curse" (Feb 1929); thirteen men toasting a thirteen-day defiance of fate (Jan 1933); midnight séances in a Grand Rapids divorce (Jan 1929); a Flint poisoner inquiry exhuming a man dead since 1923 (Jan 1929). Session note: the loc.gov search endpoint rate-limited this agent after ~35 minutes; the rest was recovered by walking microfilm reels directly on tile.loc.gov (method in "Leads not chased"). No headline here was taken from memory or from another AI; every line has the page URL.

R06 — Murder, madness and disappearances: Chicago and the Great Lakes, 1925–1940

  • Lane: R06 — Murder, madness, serial killers, poisoners, lonely-hearts killers, unsolved deaths and disappearances (the uncanny, domestic and inexplicable side of crime; not the mob, not the bank-robber manhunts)
  • Date: 2026-09-26
  • Model: Claude Fable 5.1 (research agent, parallel sweep)
  • Status: COMPLETE for this sweep — 56 entries. Web search budget ran out a third of the way in; the rest was done by direct fetches of known pages, so some corners (North Shore society murders, Loop hotel deaths, the 1930s asylum press, Milwaukee 1935) are thin — see Leads.

Overview

Fifty-six entries. The heart of the file is the Cleveland Torso cluster (eight entries — victim-by-victim locations and dates, the Lady of the Lake, the Tattooed Man and the death masks shown to 100,000 people at the Great Lakes Exposition ⟨fixed: V2_R06 — the Cleveland Public Library page says "over 100,000 visitors"⟩, Rose Wallace, Ness burning the shantytowns, Dolezal's death in the sheriff's jail with six broken ribs [UNVERIFIED — V_R06], Dr. Sweeney's polygraphs and postcards, and the New Castle "Murder Swamp" boxcar bodies). Around it: the lonely-hearts and poisoner line that runs from Hoch (1905) and Gunness (1908) through Klimek (1923) to Powers (1931, victims from Park Ridge) and Hahn (Cincinnati, 1938); Chicago's own uncanny domestic killings (Wynekoop's cellar surgery, the "Torso Murderesses" and the "lake job," Marble Mildred in the Loop, the "Brick Slayer" and the Tribune's "jungle beast," the Ardens' relief-office spree, a diner cook shot over pie); Detroit's altar sacrifice on Dubois Street, the Black Legion's hunts, Buckley in the LaSalle lobby, Collingwood and Milaflores; the unsolved and the vanished (Trip 23 blown up over Chesterton — the first proven act of air sabotage in commercial aviation ⟨fixed: V_R06⟩; the Moroney kidnapping; the Blonde Tigress who walked out of Dwight; Captain Donner's locked cabin on Lake Michigan; Ambrose Small; Liggett; two unclaimed Wisconsin boys); the asylums (Dunning's 38,000 unmarked graves, Manteno's typhoid and its 45 runaways); the period's own monsters (the 1936 St. Coletta "beast," the Dogman, Resurrection Mary, the "Gorilla Man" who took three lake cities in six days); and a living-memory shelf (Holmes, the Shotgun Man of Death Corner, the Viana "resurrection," Wanderer's Ragged Stranger, Murderess Row, Stopa, Taliesin, Bath, the Marion lynching, Oberholtzer's train to Hammond, Loeb's death at Stateville). Almost all of it holds under the 1916 break; the flags are Sweeney's and Wanderer's war records, the federal Bureau on Trip 23, the Tribune's "Red" explanation for the Ardens, and Hahn's German Cincinnati.

A. The Cleveland Torso Murders (the owner already has a folder — this section goes deep and specific)

The Cleveland Torso Murders / "Mad Butcher of Kingsbury Run" — the series, Sept 1935 – Aug 1938

  • Where: Kingsbury Run, Cleveland, Ohio — a creek-bed ravine running from E. 90th St. and Kinsman Rd. southeast to the Cuyahoga River, carrying rail lines and hobo camps through the industrial East Side; also the Flats, the lakefront, and the West Side (Big Creek, Brooklyn).
  • What happened: Twelve official victims (thought to be 7 men and 5 women), all decapitated — the head usually taken away — most also dismembered, several emasculated, several never identified. The first two were found together on 23 Sept 1935 at the base of "Jackass Hill" where E. 49th St dead-ends into the Run: Edward Andrassy (29, decapitated, emasculated, rope burns on the wrists, dead 2–3 days) and an unidentified man whose body had been soaked in oil and burned, hair shaved or scorched off. Florence Polillo (44) was found 26 Jan 1936 behind a business at 2315 E. 20th St, dismembered, wrapped in newspaper and packed in half-bushel baskets; her head was never found. The series ran through the "Tattooed Man" (June 1936), the Big Creek vagrant (July 1936), a bisected torso floating in a stagnant pool in Kingsbury Run near E. 37th (Sept 1936) ⟨fixed: V2_R06⟩, the Euclid Beach torso (Feb 1937), Rose Wallace (June 1937), the Flats torso in a chicken-feed sack (July 1937), the morphine victim (Apr–May 1938), and the last pair, found on 16 Aug 1938 at the E. 9th St lakefront dump "in plain view of" Safety Director Eliot Ness's office windows at City Hall. Authorities questioned roughly 9,100 people. Nobody was ever tried; the case is officially open.
  • The odd detail: Of the last two bodies (16 Aug 1938), the woman's was the rare one found with its head and hands ("uncharacteristically," the cited page says) ⟨fixed: V2_R06⟩ — dumped in a rag-and-cardboard bundle at the lakefront dump where Ness could look down on them from his window. Coroner Samuel Gerber's protocols note a "surgical" disarticulation at the third intervertebral disc, the same joint each time [UNVERIFIED — V_R06].
  • People: Eliot Ness — Cleveland Safety Director 1935–42, the investigation's public face; Lt. Peter Merylo — lead detective, worked the case six years, went undercover as a hobo on the rails; Dr. Samuel Gerber — Cuyahoga County Coroner (elected 1936), autopsied most victims; Edward Andrassy — victim 1, ex-hospital orderly, "known to police"; Florence Polillo — victim 3, barmaid, identified by fingerprints.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Torso_Murderer ; https://case.edu/ech/articles/t/torso-murders ; https://www.clevelandpolicemuseum.org/collections/torso-murders/
  • Confidence: High on dates, counts and locations (Encyclopedia of Cleveland History + Wikipedia victim table). Medium on Gerber's exact anatomical phrasing (from Wikipedia's summary of the protocols, not the protocols themselves).
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar. (Flag: Ness's fame in this timeline came from a Prohibition Bureau that was Treasury, not Justice; in the setting the Secret Service "took Capone" — so who was Ness working for in 1931? Worth a one-line ruling.)
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The IHS seat at John Carroll University is a few miles up the hill from Kingsbury Run. Twelve bodies without heads over three years in the diocese where the order sits — either the order is hunting the Butcher, or it is quietly collecting the heads.

The "Lady of the Lake" — 5 Sept 1934

  • Where: Lake Erie shore at Euclid Beach / east of Bratenahl, Cleveland (the Wikipedia table puts it east of Bratenahl; the Police Museum page puts it at Euclid Beach).
  • What happened: A year before the official series began, the lower torso of a woman, thighs amputated at the knees, washed ashore. The head was never found. The skin had been treated with a chemical the coroner took for lime chloride (a preservative), which had reddened and toughened it. She was never identified and is usually listed as "Victim Zero." [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06] When the Euclid Beach torso of Feb 1937 came ashore at almost the same spot, the papers immediately tied the two [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06].
  • The odd detail: The chemical on the skin — the cited page's reading is that the killer meant to use a quicklime to speed decomposition and used a preserving lime by mistake ⟨fixed: V2_R06⟩.
  • People: Jane Doe ("Lady of the Lake") — never identified; Coroner A. J. Pearse — Cuyahoga coroner in 1934, before Gerber.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Torso_Murderer ; https://www.clevelandpolicemuseum.org/collections/torso-murders/
  • Confidence: Medium — the two sources differ on the exact shore location; the "lime chloride" detail is from Wikipedia's victim table. UNVERIFIED: whether Pearse personally handled it.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A preserved body dumped in the lake reads, in the setting, as an offering — or a failed attempt at a rite. Something in Lake Erie wanted her whole.

The Tattooed Man and the death masks at the Great Lakes Exposition — 5 June 1936 and the summer of 1936

  • Where: Body found between the New York Central and Nickel Plate tracks in Kingsbury Run, Cleveland; masks shown at the Great Lakes Exposition on the Cleveland lakefront (the Exposition ran summers 1936–37).
  • What happened: Victim 4, a man estimated at 20 to 23 ⟨fixed: V2_R06⟩, was found 5 June 1936, decapitated, the head found separately wrapped in his trousers [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06]. He carried six tattoos — including the names "Helen and Paul," a Cupid, a butterfly, and the initials "W.C.G." ⟨fixed: V_R06⟩ — so the police reasoned that somebody, somewhere, had to know him. Detective Lloyd Trunk made a plaster mould of the head [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06]; the death mask, with a chart of the tattoos, went on display in the police exhibit at the Great Lakes Exposition. More than 100,000 fairgoers filed past the face. ⟨fixed: V2_R06 — the Cleveland Public Library page says "over 100,000 visitors"⟩ Nobody claimed him. The mask, with three others from the case, is still displayed at the Cleveland Police Museum.
  • The odd detail: A hundred thousand people [UNVERIFIED — V_R06] paid to look [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06] at a murdered man's face at a world's-fair-style exposition, in a summer when the killer was still working a mile away — and the tattoos ("Helen and Paul") name someone who never came forward.
  • People: John Doe II ("The Tattooed Man") — never identified; Det. Lloyd Trunk — made the mask; Eliot Ness — approved the exhibit as Safety Director [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06].
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Torso_Murderer ; https://cpl.org/the-torso-killer-clevelands-lingering-mystery/ ; https://www.clevelandpolicemuseum.org/torso-murders/torso-murders-identifying-the-victims/ (search summary; page returned 403 on direct fetch)
  • Confidence: High on the mask and the exposition; Medium on the exact list of tattoos and the "W.C.B." initials (from search-result summaries of the Police Museum page, which I could not fetch directly).
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar. (The Great Lakes Exposition itself is an R04 matter.)
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A death mask is a classic vessel. If the Butcher takes heads, the one head the police did recover was copied in plaster and shown to a hundred thousand faces [UNVERIFIED — V_R06] — a spell cast on the whole city without anyone noticing. Hunter Guild or Kabbalah agents would recognise it instantly.

Rose Wallace — vanished 21 Aug 1936, found 6 June 1937

  • Where: Skeleton found under the Lorain–Carnegie Bridge, Cleveland, in a burlap bag with a newspaper from June 1936.
  • What happened: Wallace, about 40, a Black woman of the Scovill Ave neighbourhood [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06], disappeared on 21 Aug 1936. Ten months later boys found a skeletonised, decapitated body under the bridge, missing a rib, in a burlap sack with a year-old newspaper. Dental work led to a tentative identification as Wallace, which her son accepted and Coroner Gerber never fully endorsed [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06]. Charles Sadoti later told police she had partnered with a "one-armed Willie" — Willie Johnson — two weeks before she vanished; Johnson was executed in 1944 for a separate 1942 torso murder (Margaret Frances Wilson, 19, torso in a trunk) and was Gerber's favoured Torso suspect.
  • The odd detail: The June 1936 newspaper in the sack — either the body sat somewhere for ten months before the dump, or the killer wrapped it in old paper on purpose.
  • People: Rose Wallace — victim 8 (tentative); Willie Johnson — one-armed suspect, executed 10 Mar 1944; Charles Sadoti — witness.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Torso_Murderer
  • Confidence: Medium — the identification was never formally confirmed; the Johnson link is circumstantial.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The one victim in the series whose community (Scovill Ave, the Black East Side) had its own reading of the disappearance — a place to put the "folklore layer" in the owner's three-layer method.

Ness burns the shantytowns — 18 Aug 1938 (and the fire-inspection sweep, 22 Aug 1938)

  • Where: The hobo jungles of Kingsbury Run and the Flats, Cleveland.
  • What happened: Two days after the last two bodies turned up under his office windows, Ness led police into the shantytowns at night, arrested the inhabitants (sixty-odd men, by most accounts), fingerprinted them, and had the shacks burned to the ground. On 22 Aug he sent six two-man teams through every building from the river to E. 55th St and Prospect Ave, under the pretext of fire inspections, searching for the killing room. The Encyclopedia of Cleveland History's flat verdict: the burning "ended the torso murders." Ness was widely criticised in the press for burning the homes of the destitute in the middle of the Depression [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06].
  • The odd detail: The killer stopped the day the fires were lit — which either means the Butcher lived in the Run, or it means he understood the message.
  • People: Eliot Ness — Safety Director; the unnamed sixty-odd men fingerprinted that night.
  • Sources: https://case.edu/ech/articles/t/torso-murders ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Torso_Murderer
  • Confidence: High on the raid and date; Medium on the headcount arrested (numbers vary by source; not confirmed from a primary page this session).
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Fire as a ward. If a Section 13 officer whispered in Ness's ear that the thing in the Run could not cross burned ground, the raid reads differently.

Frank Dolezal — arrested 5 July 1939, dead in the county jail 24 Aug 1939

  • Where: Cuyahoga County Jail, Cleveland.
  • What happened: Dolezal, a 52-year-old Slavic bricklayer who had lived with Florence Polillo, was an acquaintance of Andrassy and Wallace, and frequented a bar where one of the victims hung out ⟨fixed: V2_R06⟩, was picked up by Sheriff Martin L. O'Donnell's men — not Ness's police — in a sheriff's-office attempt to steal the case. After days of questioning he confessed to killing Polillo "in self-defence," then retracted, saying the confession had been beaten out of him. On 24 Aug 1939 he was found hanged in his cell by a strip of cloth from a bunk [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06], from a hook lower than his own height [UNVERIFIED — V_R06]. The death was ruled suicide; the autopsy found six broken ribs, injuries friends said he did not have when arrested [UNVERIFIED — V_R06]. Modern researchers treat him as an innocent man killed in custody; he was declared exonerated in Aug 2010 after investigators found no case against him in his confessions (Find a Grave; Wikipedia says only "later posthumously exonerated"; no judge named) ⟨fixed: V_R06⟩.
  • The odd detail: He hanged himself from a hook lower than his own height [UNVERIFIED — V_R06] — and the sheriff who held him was running a rival investigation against the Safety Director.
  • People: Frank Dolezal — the scapegoat; Sheriff Martin L. O'Donnell — held him; Dr. Samuel Gerber — did the autopsy that recorded the ribs [UNVERIFIED — V_R06].
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Torso_Murderer ; https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/28202811/frank-dolezal
  • Confidence: High on arrest, confession, retraction, death and broken ribs; Medium on the exact arrest date (the Wikipedia summary gives both 5 July and 24 Aug in different places; other sources say six weeks in custody, which fits early July) and the low-hook detail (widely repeated, not confirmed from a primary page).
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A man who lived with a victim and could name the killer dies in a rival lawman's jail. "No one is clean": the sheriff's office as a Golden Circle rook.

Dr. Francis Edward Sweeney — the secret suspect, questioned 1938

  • Where: Interrogated in a suite at the Cleveland Hotel (by most accounts) [UNVERIFIED — V_R06]; his 1929 residency was at St. Alexis Hospital on the edge of Kingsbury Run, his later office on the same street where a man named Emil Fronek said a doctor had tried to drug him in 1934.
  • What happened: Sweeney (b. 5 May 1894), an army surgeon in a First World War amputation unit who came home gassed, alcoholic and, by 1934, separated from his wife, was Ness's private suspect. Ness had him held for days and had Leonarde Keeler of Chicago's Northwestern crime lab run two polygraph tests; Keeler told Ness Sweeney was his man. Sweeney was the first cousin of Congressman Martin L. Sweeney, a Democrat and Ness's loudest political enemy, which made prosecution on a lie-detector result impossible. Days after the questioning, on 25 Aug 1938, Sweeney committed himself voluntarily to a veterans' hospital [UNVERIFIED — V_R06]; the murders stopped. From institutions in Sandusky and Dayton [UNVERIFIED — V_R06] he mailed Ness taunting postcards into the 1950s. He died 9 July 1964 at the Dayton veterans' hospital.
  • The odd detail: The postcards — a suspect sending threatening cards to the detective and his family into the 1950s, stopping only after Ness's death in 1957 ⟨fixed: V_R06⟩, from inside an asylum he could leave whenever he liked [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06].
  • People: Dr. Francis E. Sweeney — suspect; Rep. Martin L. Sweeney — cousin, protector; Leonarde Keeler — Chicago polygraph man (R13 lane); Emil Fronek — the drugged sailor [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06] of 1934.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Torso_Murderer
  • Confidence: Medium-High. The Wikipedia biography is well cited but this is one page; the hotel-suite and voluntary commitment dates are from it. In the real record Sweeney's name only became public in the 1990s, so period press has nothing.
  • Divergence check: BENDS — Sweeney's war service and gassing are a US Army 1918 story; in this timeline America never entered the Great War. He would need a Southern War (Mexico) service record instead — an amputation surgeon from the Tampico or Veracruz occupation, gassed by whom?
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): A surgeon who learned the trade cutting men apart in an occupation hospital, protected by a congressman, writing letters from an asylum — the archetype of the demon-summoner who was never caught, with the Golden Circle holding his cousin's seat.

Murder Swamp, New Castle, Pennsylvania — headless bodies 1925–1941

  • Where: The marsh and rail yards around New Castle Junction and West Pittsburgh, Lawrence County, Pa. (on the Pennsylvania & Lake Erie line, which ran to Cleveland), and later Stowe Township / McKees Rocks near Pittsburgh.
  • What happened: Between 1921 and 1942 (the cited Wikipedia page's range) nine dismembered or decapitated bodies (eight never identified) turned up in the swamp or the train yards ⟨fixed: V_R06⟩. Three headless bodies were found in 1925 alone [UNVERIFIED — V_R06]. On 1 July 1936 two P&LE workers opened a boxcar at New Castle Junction and found a nude, decapitated man under burlap with three bloodied newspapers beside him. On 3 May 1940 railroad men found three rotting, naked, headless bodies in a string of derelict boxcars awaiting scrapping at Stowe Township. Lt. Merylo believed the Pennsylvania and Cleveland killings were one man riding the rails; the gap in the Pennsylvania killings (1934–39) matches the Cleveland series.
  • The odd detail: The killer's calendar fits the freight timetable — the same rail line, the same burlap, bodies in boxcars bound for the scrap yard.
  • People: Lt. Peter Merylo — rode the rails as a hobo chasing this theory; James Jessen Badal — modern historian (Hell's Wasteland, Kent State UP 2013).
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Torso_Murderer ; https://www.kentstateuniversitypress.com/2012/hells-wasteland/ ; https://www.sharonherald.com/news/the-murder-marsh-a-century-later-killers-shadow-still-looms/article_e6937980-fd72-11ec-9b8e-cf7f4153ed09.html (search summary)
  • Confidence: Medium — counts and dates from Wikipedia and search summaries; the Sharon Herald and Kent State pages were not read in full.
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Something that rides the freights between the steel towns. A Hunter Guild contract nobody wants, because the trail runs through a hundred miles of nothing.

B. Lonely hearts, poisoners and the "Bluebeard" press

Harry Powers, the "Bluebeard of Quiet Dell," and the Park Ridge family — June–Aug 1931

  • Where: Victims from Park Ridge, Illinois (Chicago's northwest suburb); the killing house at Quiet Dell, Harrison County, West Virginia, just south of Clarksburg.
  • What happened: Harm "Herman" Drenth (b. Beerta, Netherlands, 17 Nov 1892) ran a grocery with his wife Luella near Clarksburg and, as "Cornelius O. Pierson," worked the lonely-hearts columns of the American Friendship Society; postal records showed 10–20 replies a day. He wrote to Asta Buick Eicher, a Park Ridge widow, called on her on 23 June 1931, took her away, then came back for her children Greta (14), Harry (12) and Annabel (9) [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06]. He also took Dorothy Pressler Lemke of Northborough, Massachusetts. In August 1931 Park Ridge police, tracing the love letters, reached Quiet Dell [UNVERIFIED — V_R06] and found a basement garage fitted with four small rooms, and a freshly filled ditch containing all five bodies. A mob tried to storm the Clarksburg jail. The trial was moved to the Moore Opera House to hold the crowd; convicted 12 Dec 1931 for Lemke's murder; hanged at Moundsville 18 Mar 1932. Davis Grubb built The Night of the Hunter on the case.
  • The odd detail: The "garage" with four cells and a drain [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06] — and Powers' own account that he kept the children alive for days in it so their mother could hear them [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06].
  • People: Harry Powers / Herman Drenth / "Cornelius O. Pierson" — the killer; Asta Eicher — Park Ridge widow; Greta, Harry, Annabel Eicher — her children; Dorothy Lemke — the Massachusetts victim; Luella Strother Powers — the wife whose own lonely-hearts ad he had answered (m. 1927) ⟨fixed: V_R06⟩.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Powers ; https://www.wvencyclopedia.org/entries/1852 (search summary)
  • Confidence: High on names, dates and the garage; the children-kept-alive detail is UNVERIFIED this session (widely repeated from his confession, not read on a primary page).
  • Divergence check: holds — domestic calendar.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The Park Ridge end — a Chicago-suburb widow answering a mail-order suitor — is where a Section 13 investigator would start. The four-room cellar is the physical shape of a cult's holding pen; the lonely-hearts trade is how a vampire feeds in high society without leaving a mark.

Anna Marie Hahn, "Arsenic Anna," the Blonde Borgia of Cincinnati — 1933–37, executed 7 Dec 1938

  • Where: Cincinnati's German neighbourhoods; the final killing in Colorado Springs.
  • What happened: Born in Füssen, Bavaria (7 July 1906), sent to Cincinnati in 1929, married Philip Hahn in 1930. From 1933 she attached herself to elderly German men as a live-in "nurse" and poisoned them for their money to feed a gambling habit: Ernest Koch (73, d. 6 May 1933, left her a house), Albert Parker (72, a $1,000 IOU that vanished), Jacob Wagner (78, d. 3 June 1937, $17,000 to his "beloved niece"), George Gsellman (67, d. 6 July 1937, $17,000), and Georg Obendoerfer (67), whom she took by train to Colorado Springs and who died there 1 Aug 1937; the Colorado autopsy found arsenic and unravelled the rest. A jury of eleven women and one man [UNVERIFIED — V_R06] convicted her on 6 Nov 1937 after four weeks; she went to the Ohio Penitentiary chair on 7 Dec 1938, the first woman Ohio electrocuted. Her confession ran in the Cincinnati Enquirer after her death [UNVERIFIED — V_R06].
  • The odd detail: A variety of poisons (arsenic, croton oil) ⟨fixed: V2_R06⟩ and a jury of eleven women to convict a woman [UNVERIFIED — V_R06] — the press called her "Angel of Mercy" before it called her the "Blonde Borgia" [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06].
  • People: Anna Marie Hahn — poisoner; Philip Hahn — husband; Oskar Hahn — her son, who travelled with her and the victims; the five old men above.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Marie_Hahn ; https://www.executedtoday.com/2015/12/07/1938-anna-marie-hahn-serial-poisoner/ (search summary)
  • Confidence: High on victims, dates, verdict and execution; the croton-oil detail is UNVERIFIED this session.
  • Divergence check: BENDS lightly — a Bavarian immigrant of 1929 in a world where Germany won the war and America is under German sanctions; her German community would be prosperous, not humiliated, which changes why old German men in Cincinnati had $17,000 in cash to steal.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): The poisoner-nurse is the domestic face of the vampire: she drains men who trust her, at a table, over months. A Cincinnati Kabbalah could have caught the pattern from the estate records before the Colorado coroner did.

C. Chicago: the doctor, the washwomen, the tigress and the brick

G. Madness: the asylums and the mad

Dunning — Chicago State Hospital and its 38,000 unmarked dead

  • Where: 320 acres between Irving Park Rd and Montrose Ave, from Narragansett west to Oak Park Ave, on the Northwest Side; the Cook County poor farm, insane asylum and potter's field since 1854, state-run as Chicago State Hospital from 1912.
  • What happened: By the 1930s "Dunning" was Chicago's word for madness: parents threatened children with it, and the "crazy train" — a hospital rail car with guards — carried patients out from the Cook County Detention Hospital. Bones have since surfaced at Wright College and near the present Chicago-Read centre [UNVERIFIED — V2_R06].

The Marion lynching — 7 Aug 1930

  • Where: The Grant County courthouse square, Marion, Indiana.
  • What happened: Thomas Shipp and Abram Smith, young Black men accused of robbing and killing 24-year-old Claude Deeter and assaulting his fiancée Mary Ball, were dragged from the county jail by a crowd of about 5,000 — women and children among them — who broke in with sledgehammers, and hanged from a tree on the square; when Smith fought the noose they broke his arms. The Ohio Penitentiary fire — 21 Apr 1930

H. H. Holmes and the Englewood "Castle" — 1891–94 (living memory; the building stood until 1938)

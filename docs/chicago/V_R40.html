V_R40 — fact-check of R40 (the lower lakes: Toledo, Erie, Buffalo, Niagara, the Ontario shore)

Checked: 2026-09-30 by Sonnet 5.5 (fact-checker; no GPU, no git). Method: the owner's "Top 10" items first (Peace Bridge, Deskaheh, King and Wriedt, Auto-Lite, the Toledo bank crash, the Winter Garden, Baker and Father Justin, Tim Buck, the Oakville Klan, Ramesses I), the Council House takeover, then the numbers, dates, names and quotations of the other well-known entries. Each claim was compared with the page R40 cites (WebFetch); where that page was silent, a search-result summary, or a second page found by search, was used. Brief followed: BRIEF.md (Wood president 1921-27, dead in office Aug 1927; Dawes 1927-29; Hoover 1929-33; America never fought Germany; Britain lost).

The owner's three key items, answered precisely

  1. Peace Bridge, 7 August 1927 — both halves CONFIRMED. Wikipedia (Peace Bridge): dedicated 7 August 1927, about 20,000 present (the Buffalo History Gazette says 100,000), platform party = Prince of Wales, Prince George, Mackenzie King, Baldwin, Vice President Charles Dawes, Kellogg, Al Smith, Ferguson; billed as the first international coast-to-coast broadcast (up to 50 million listeners). The Buffalo History Gazette gives the start as 3 p.m. Daylight Saving Time. Wikipedia (Leonard Wood): "Wood died in Boston on August 7, 1927, during surgery on the brain tumor." Same day, confirmed. Not confirmed: the hour of Wood's death (no page gives it — R40 already said so), and that Cushing was the surgeon that day (the pages read say only that Cushing removed the 1910 tumour); tagged UNVERIFIED. The divergence label BENDS is correct and complete (Baldwin and the Prince of Wales are a defeated Britain's delegation; Kellogg belongs to Coolidge's cabinet).
  2. Mackenzie King and Etta Wriedt — partly CONFIRMED, partly wrong. Confirmed: the first séance was in 1932, at the Fulford home in Brockville (a senator's widow, per occult-world.com; "several séances in February 1932" per the Keyhole House piece), and he then went to Detroit repeatedly. Not confirmed: "over 60 séances with Wriedt" — on every page read the count is "several" (1932) or "dozens" (all mediums, all places); the figure appears only in a search-result summary. Wrong: King was not Prime Minister in 1932 — he was Leader of the Opposition 1930-35 (PM 1921-26, 1926-30, from October 1935; Wikipedia), so "the Prime Minister travelled to Detroit through the 1930s" is off by five years. Corrected in the entry, the overview and the Top 10.
  3. Deskaheh and the 7 Oct 1924 Council House takeover — CONFIRMED. Wikipedia (Deskaheh): b. 15 March 1873 Tuscarora Township, Royaner 1917, London 1921 with Decker on a Six Nations passport, the petition sent back to Canada, van Karnebeek forwarded it in 1922, sailed 14 July 1923, eighteen months, supporters Ireland/Panama/Persia/Japan/Estonia, left 3 Jan 1925, Rochester radio speech 10 March 1925, died 27 June 1925 at Rickard's, 2,000 mourners on 30 June; "On October 7 ... the RCMP dissolved the traditional government of the Six Nations, stealing important documents and wampums." The 17 Sept 1924 Order in Council (signed by Mackenzie King and Lord Byng, setting elections for 21 Oct) is confirmed via a search summary of the Wikipedia text; the elected-council article confirms 21 Oct, "Mounties Council", Dave Hill's "only 26 people voted, most or all of them twice", and 5.15 % turnout in 2013. One slip: R40 says the first election used six electoral districts; the six districts began with the 4th Elected Council in 1927 (corrected).

Divergence labels marked "holds" that touch the forbidden ground

R40's handling is careful; all the entries it marks BENDS (Peace Bridge, Deskaheh, Mounties' Council, Tim Buck, Orange Order, Casa d'Italia, Christie Pits, King, Hatch, Polonia, Oakes) match the brief. Soft flags on entries marked "holds":

  • Oshawa 1937, McQuesten, Mohawk Institute, Adams/Queenston-Chippawa — labelled "holds — domestic/Canadian". They assume a Canada and an Ontario government unchanged by Britain's defeat; R40's own framing note admits Canada's status "is not decided", so the label is conditional rather than certain. No text change.
  • Auto-Lite (1934) — "holds"; correct under the brief (FDR, Frances Perkins, Charles P. Taft II is a president's son).
  • Pierce-Arrow, Darwin Martin, Toledo bank crash — "holds"; Hoover is president in 1931 in both timelines; fine.
  • Father Baker — "holds ... BBC broadcast: Britain is beaten, not gone; plausible" — an assumption, flagged by R40 itself.
  • Roycroft / Lusitania — R40 says the ship's loss "did not bring America into the war in this timeline"; the same is true of the real timeline (America entered in 1917), so the remark is true but not a divergence.
  • Whirlpool Aero Car — opened 8 Aug 1916 in wartime; "Spain neutral" is right.
  • No "holds" label was found that names Germany, the presidents after Wilson or the Great War without flagging it.

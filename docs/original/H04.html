H04 An eviction defense at Diana Gross's home ends in killings

August 3, 1931 | documented | Housing, protest and policing

Neighbors and Unemployed Council activists resisted the eviction of 72-year-old Diana Gross by putting her furniture back inside. Police confrontation followed and Black protesters were killed. Communist organizer Bill Gebert's contemporary account names Abe Gray, John O'Neil and Thomas Page and gives the address as 5016 South Dearborn. Later scholarship describes a wider aftermath of demonstrations centered on Washington Park. The incident joins housing insecurity, racial inequality, political organizing and sharply opposed newspaper narratives in one neighborhood-scale event.

Uncertainties

Sources differ over crowd size, sequence and whether an additional death belongs to the same episode. Gebert is a participant with an explicit political agenda; his allegations about other organizations are not adopted as neutral findings.

Fictional Umbra possibility

Fictional campaign possibility: a fictional tenant's missing belongings lead to a housing-fraud mystery; real victims retain their historical identities and are not assigned occult motives.

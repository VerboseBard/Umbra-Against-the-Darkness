W04 Premature babies displayed in incubators at the fair

1933–34 Century of Progress | documented | Medicine and unsettling public spectacle

The Century of Progress included a public infant-incubator exhibit in which premature babies received care while visitors watched. Penn Nursing’s history situates the show within a difficult transition: specialized nursing, temperature control and feeding mattered, while many physicians had been skeptical of mechanical incubators and midway medicine. Chicago pediatrician Julius Hess had already developed hospital-based premature-infant care, so the fair did not introduce care into a complete medical vacuum. The episode joins crowded entertainment space to real family desperation and highly skilled labor. It is useful as documented social history, with claims about individual infants requiring additional records.

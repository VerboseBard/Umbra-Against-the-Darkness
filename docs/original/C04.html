C04 Billy Ranieri kidnapping and the intimidation of witnesses

September–December 1928; editorial aftermath January 1929 | documented | Unusual crime

Ten-year-old Billy Ranieri's kidnapping became a national story in 1928. A December newspaper reported the convictions of Angelo Petitti and Andrew Cappellano and sentences of twenty-five years, while Cappellano's young son was acquitted. A January 1929 Oregon editorial described a witness murdered, others beaten, and threats against the judge, prosecutors and witnesses. These are contemporary reports, not a modern reconstruction of every allegation. The case offers a valuable route beyond familiar bootlegging stories: family fear, ransom negotiations, an outlying farm, bilingual communities and the practical problem of persuading ordinary people to testify.

People

Billy Ranieri, Angelo Petitti, Andrew Cappellano, Judge Robert E. Gentzel

Uncertainties

Accessible accounts disagree on the exact September abduction day and ransom demand. Those details are intentionally unresolved here. Contemporary allegations about other captive children need independent confirmation.

Fictional possibility: a threatened witness asks the investigators to protect a farm child's drawing that seems irrelevant to the prosecution.

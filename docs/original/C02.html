C02 The Wynekoop operating-table murder

21 November 1933 death; 29 November indictment; 6 March 1934 verdict; 1934–1935 appeal | documented | Unusual crime

Rheta Wynekoop was found dead on the examination table in her mother-in-law's basement medical practice. The sixteen-room house and conflicting household accounts rapidly became a newspaper mystery. The Chicago Daily News described a telegram found in the surgery and an insurance policy obtained shortly before the death. A grand jury indicted Dr. Alice Wynekoop on 29 November 1933. A jury convicted her on 6 March 1934 and fixed a twenty-five-year term; the Illinois Supreme Court subsequently affirmed the judgment. The case supplies an unusually concrete investigation setting: delivery boys, household witnesses, a mortuary inquest, a physician's professional standing and competing stories about an apparently private home.

Uncertainties

Distinguish contemporary prosecution claims from the adjudicated outcome. The appellate opinion is dated 20 December 1934; rehearing was denied 12 February 1935, which vLex displays as its main date. The publicly visible opinion is partial.

Fictional Umbra possibility

Fictional possibility: a telegram messenger remembers a visitor whom no member of the household admits knowing.

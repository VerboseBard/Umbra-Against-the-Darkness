H02 Stockyards fire cuts local electricity, gas and telephones

May 19, 1934 | documented | Fire and utility disruption

The huge Stockyards fire crossed into nearby streets, destroyed businesses and housing, and disabled neighborhood services. The Chicago Tribune's next-day account, transcribed by local-history site Chicagology, reports telephone and electric lines and gas mains in the yards' vicinity out of commission. A train was abandoned when its power was cut. A New Zealand cable report also describes extraordinary firefighting water use curtailing water service elsewhere in Chicago. This provides a concrete dated utility interruption rather than a speculative Depression blackout.

Uncertainties

Exact outage footprint and restoration times are not established. The distant cable report's water volume and early damage estimates require comparison with fire and water-department records.

Fictional Umbra possibility

Fictional campaign possibility: during the real service collapse, an invented exchange operator hears calls from a building already burned away.

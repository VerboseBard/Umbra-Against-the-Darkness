H09 Unemployed factory workers grow food at West Pullman Works

July 1932 photograph | documented | Depression survival and industry

A July 1932 International Harvester photograph shows a man harvesting vegetables in the West Pullman Works community garden. Wisconsin Historical Society's catalog explains that company gardens were intended for unemployed and underemployed factory workers, with eligibility limited to employees of at least five years' service. The scene adds a quieter Depression landscape to the expected breadlines: factory-adjacent food plots, work histories determining access, and households supplementing scarce wages through cultivation.

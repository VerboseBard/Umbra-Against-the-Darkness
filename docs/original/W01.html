W01 Arcturus switches on the fair—and astronomers quarrel over the spectacle

27 May 1933 opening; dispute continued in June | documented | Science, spectacle and electricity

Light from Arcturus triggered the electrical opening ceremony of the Century of Progress exposition on 27 May 1933. The light produced a photoelectric signal, amplified and transmitted to Chicago; it did not supply the fair’s electrical load. Organizers liked the then-estimated forty-light-year distance because it linked the ceremony to the 1893 fair. University of Chicago correspondence reveals a less familiar conflict: Yerkes astronomers resented inadequate credit and resisted demands to repeat the stunt nightly. Four observatories supplied redundancy against cloud cover. A celebrated public miracle therefore had very human arguments, commercial pressures, and a fragile technical chain behind it.

Uncertainties

Forty light-years was the period estimate, not an exact present-day distance. Distinguish the signal from the source of electrical power.

Fictional Umbra possibility

Fictional campaign idea: a second signal reaches the control room after every participating observatory has disconnected; sponsors demand that tomorrow’s show proceed.

R04 St Valentines Day massacre and the competing first reports

February 14 1929; saved newspaper coverage dated February 15 | documented | Crime and public memory

Seven men associated with George Moran's bootlegging operation were killed in the garage at 2122 North Clark Street. The saved Evening Star front page of February 15 shows how unsettled the public account remained: one official suggested actual police involvement, while others disputed him or suspected a Detroit gang. Preserve these as contemporary claims, not proof of who ordered or carried out the killings. The Mob Museum's account establishes the site and victims and explains the later preservation of wall fragments.

People

George Bugs Moran, Al Capone, Fred D. Silloway

Places

  • Massacre garage — 2122 North Clark Street, Chicago. historical address; the original garage no longer survives Modern map locator

Uncertainties

The saved page is an early Washington newspaper report about Chicago. Its accusations are not findings. Do not make Capone's personal involvement or the identities of every gunman more certain than the evidence permits.

Fictional Umbra possibility

Fictional possibility: witnesses remember different uniforms and vehicles. Investigators must sort deliberate impersonation, frightened testimony and an invented supernatural deception.

Sources

  • St Valentines Day Massacre Wall — The Mob Museum. museum interpretation. Supports: February 14 date, seven victims, garage address and surviving wall history
  • Evening Star February 15 1929 front page rightmost column — Evening Star and Associated Press; Library of Congress scan. primary newspaper page inspected locally. Supports: Competing first reports and official disagreement; not proof of the reported theories

Next research step

Compare Chicago papers and the FBI Vault files with the first report. Distinguish statements made in 1929 from later confessions, memoirs and reconstructions.

W08 A glittering opera opening six days after Black Tuesday

4 November 1929 | documented | Society events and cultural patronage

Chicago’s new Civic Opera House opened with Aida on 4 November 1929, immediately after the stock-market crash. Period reporting gives the event unusually concrete social texture: a sold-out audience, extra traffic police, cars approaching the colonnade, and a jeweled audience in the boxes. Samuel Insull’s project joined an opera auditorium to a large office building whose rental income was intended to support performance. His published dedication emphasized citizens’ money and credit. The evening offers a dated gathering of cultural patrons and financial power at precisely the moment those assumptions were becoming precarious.

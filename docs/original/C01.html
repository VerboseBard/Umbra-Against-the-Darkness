C01 Jake Lingle killed in a crowded commuter passage

9 June 1930 | documented | Crime and corruption

Chicago Tribune reporter Jake Lingle was shot from behind while walking toward an Illinois Central train in the passage beneath Michigan Avenue at Randolph Street. He had been heading for the races. Contemporary coverage initially treated the killing as an attack on the press; subsequent revelations exposed Lingle's extensive underworld connections. Chicagology reproduces the changing newspaper narrative, while a Treasury investigator's later report says he had arranged to seek Lingle's confidential cooperation shortly before the murder. The combination of commuter crowds, newsstands, racing tips, corruption and conflicting public stories provides unusually concrete material for a city investigation.

People

Alfred 'Jake' Lingle, Al Capone, Leo Brothers

Places

  • Illinois Central Randolph Street station pedestrian passage — Michigan Avenue at Randolph Street. intersection; historical underground layout Modern map locator

Uncertainties

Do not equate contemporary police theories or newspaper accusations with proof of who ordered the killing. The Treasury report establishes the investigator's account, not a settled murder motive.

Fictional Umbra possibility

Fictional possibility: a commuter recovers a reporter's discarded racing sheet containing a second, unrelated investigation.

Sources

Supports: Wilson's account of initiating a confidential approach to Lingle on 1 June 1930; no interview date had been fixed before the murder.

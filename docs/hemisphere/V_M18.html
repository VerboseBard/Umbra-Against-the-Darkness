The errors worth knowing about

  1. Officer Szewczyk was killed on the night of Monday 27 January 1930, about 11:45 p.m. — not 30 January. 30 January is the date México printed the story ("yesterday"). The Chicago Police Memorial Foundation gives 27 January, 11:45 p.m., burial 1 February at Holy Cross, Calumet City. M18's title, table of contents, overview and Top 10 all carried the wrong date. (WRONG)
  2. The "off-duty officer in uniform" is backwards. The translator's note says the policeman was "not on duty nor wearing a uniform"; the news story says plain clothes; the police memorial says a plain-clothes patrol. Every source has him in civilian clothes; they differ only on on-duty/off-duty. (WRONG)
  3. The colony's defence money: $132.96 was the total collected by 6 March, of which $50 was to go to the attorney (Mr. O'Brien) — not "delivered to the attorney". By 6 May the total was $153.76, with $50 handed over and $103.76 on hand. (CORRECTED)
  4. The $15 "first emigrant train" of 14 May 1932 was an announcement of intent. The consul's letter is dated 10 May and says the consulate "wants to organize the first train for emigrants"; the price came "through the efforts of the Secretary of Foreign Relations". No departure is recorded. And the claim that it is "the same consul" who had the 1928 swindler arrested cannot be supported: the FLPS never names the 1928 consul, and Aveleyra first appears in it in January 1930. (CORRECTED / UNSUPPORTED)
  5. St. Francis of Assisi: "~1,000 worshippers" is the crowd at one special Mass, not the parish's size — Jones writes "at special Mass conducted for the Mexicans nearly a thousand worshippers often kneel". The "only a seventh of the colony went to church" inference falls; the Chicago History Museum says the parish drew up to 5,000 a Sunday at its peak. The Claretians began there in 1926 (not 1927), with a Spanish-language service dated 1925. (CORRECTED / UNSUPPORTED)
  6. The Halsted swindler was not "extradited". Later reports (31 Oct 1928) say Mexican authorities arrested "José Durand" in Ciudad Juárez at the Chicago consul's request, and now count "more than three hundred" victims (the first reports said "over two hundred"). (CORRECTED)

Facts found that M18 does not have (offered, not applied)

  • Lux in Umbra was "a division of the Rito Nacional Mexicano"; its sister lodge was "Hiram No. 6" of Chicago; the two joined for the solstice festival and a 22 June 1929 "Grand Honorable Session" (a Tenida Blanca); the South America restaurant belonged to Luis Flores, 324 Halsted.
  • Lillian Hall was at 8143 Buffalo Avenue (South Chicago) — the Villa play was a Southeast Side event, not a Halsted one.
  • The Halsted "Navy Yard" con was first billed at "over 200"; the Juárez reports say 300+.
  • Jones's pamphlet says "more than fifty" Mexican pool halls in Chicago (M18 has the five near Hull House).

S04 — Hemisphere synthesis: Mexico, Cuba and the Caribbean, 1898–1940 (sweep of 2026-09-30)

What this is. One reading of the seventeen chapters (M01–M17) and fifteen fact-checks (V_M01–V_M15) in this folder, set against the setting's own record: rulebook §31 (the presidents, the hemisphere row, "Which war") and Appendix 9–11; ALTERNATE_HISTORY.md §0, §3, §7.3–7.4, §8, §9, §28, §29, §30.8–30.9; PEOPLE_OF_THE_TIMELINE.md; and the hard audit's RESOLVED_ANSWERS.md (234 audit questions answered from the evidence, 25–26 Sep). Written by the synthesis agent, 2026-09-30. No GPU, no git; nothing outside this file was touched.

Status rules used here. Research is a lead, not canon. Text marked ⟨fixed: V_Mxx⟩ is the fact-checked version and is used as written; nothing tagged [UNVERIFIED …] is promoted, and such items say so. ✔ below means the fact-check for that chapter confirmed the item (or confirmed it after a correction). M16 and M17 have no fact-check yet. Every "recommendation" is a proposal. "Delegated — historical sense" marks a small lore call made under the owner's standing instruction ("just look at what would make the most historical sense", ALTERNATE_HISTORY.md:2022, :2308; RESOLVED_ANSWERS.md:1511 Audit 175); by that same instruction it never reverses one of the owner's own ideas. The whole setting is a WORKING DRAFT (owner, 2026-09-26, ALTERNATE_HISTORY.md:19).

1. Start here

  1. The real record fits the setting almost too well: nearly every place America "never leaves" in Umbra (Cuba, Haiti, the Dominican Republic, Nicaragua, Veracruz's customs house) was really occupied or run by Americans in these years, so most events hold and only the endings bend.
  2. The biggest structural finding is that the men who ruled Mexico from 1920 to 1934 (Obregón, Calles, de la Huerta) were all Sonorans, from the north the treaty gives away, so the first thing the Mexico book needs is a ruling on who governs what is left.
  3. Chicago runs through all of it: the city was the Mexican Church's desk in exile, a University of Chicago press and faculty that mapped Mexican folk religion, Chicago-owned oil in Aruba, and a 1926 Chicago wire story about Villa's stolen head on its way to Chicago criminologists.
  4. Germany's real footprint in the hemisphere was merchants, shipping, radio, airlines and money for other people's revolutions, not warships, which points to a victorious Germany that "looms and buys" rather than fights.
  5. The chapters raise about 110 questions, which merge into 66. Rulings and audit answers already on disk settle 16, historical sense settles 47 (with a recommendation for each), and only three need the owner himself: Mexico's rulers after the treaty (with Villa's fate), the 1937 Parsley Massacre, and which piece of the Circle's map is still missing (§5.1).

2. The 40 best finds

File and entry, then why it matters. ✔ = confirmed by that chapter's fact-check.

The Southern War and the ceded north

  1. Carrizal, 21 June 1916 — the day the record stops (Wilson refused Pershing's request to hit Chihuahua) and the setting's war begins; 12 killed, 24 captured. M01 "The Battle of Carrizal" ✔ (V_M01 #22).
  2. Villa's stolen head — killed at Parral 20 July 1923 (a pumpkinseed seller's "¡Viva Villa!" was the signal); head taken from his grave 6 Feb 1926; the Skull and Bones sale is labelled legend. Parral is ceded ground. M01 "Villa's death at Parral, and his stolen head" ✔ (V_M01 #1–4).
  3.   8. The Sonoran Chinese: 6,078 in 1919, 92 in 1940 — the leagues, not the Chinese, are the problem. M08 ✔ (V_M08: "strongest confirmations"); the 1931 expulsion as re-dated in M01 ✔ (V_M01 #50).

3. Threads across chapters

Twelve threads. Each names the chapters it runs through; the three strongest are marked ★.

T1 ★ The Chicago–Mexico Catholic thread (M07, M02, M12, M15, M16; Chicago sweep R05, R35). Chicago was the exiled Mexican Church's American desk from 1914, before Chicago had many Mexicans: Mgr. Kelley's Catholic Church Extension Society wrote to Wilson for the bishops (23 Feb 1915), paid for the Castroville seminary with Archbishop Quigley, ran chapel cars ✔. The Archbishop of Morelia, Ruiz y Flores — later co-signer of the 1929 peace — lived in Chicago in his first exile (one scholarly dictionary; tagged as thin). Pascual Díaz laid the cornerstone of Our Lady of Guadalupe, South Chicago, 14 Apr 1928 ✔ (R05 carries the church and the St. Jude shrine); a Cristero march by the blast furnaces followed (the 23 Sept date and the bishop's name are tagged). The University of Chicago Press published the period's Mexican folk-religion studies (Redfield's Tepoztlán 1930 and Chan Kom; Parsons; Spicer) and ran a Tarahumara expedition (1930–31) ✔; the scholar jailed over the 1932 Carvajal-diary theft had taught at Northwestern ✔. In the setting the key bend is that a priest fleeing Calles may only need to reach American Chihuahua or Monterrey, so the IHS's natural first northern base is inside the ceded Territory (M07 treaty note; M08 Q4). The relic-hiding spine is the Guadalupe tilma of 1926–29 (M16, M12). Chicago's IHS has a deposit in South Chicago's cornerstone.

T2 ★ Germany's footprint — "loom and buy" (M13, M06, M01, M03, M04, M05, M08, M10, M12). The real record gives a victorious Kaiserreich a ready-made presence that is almost never military: Hamburg coffee houses in the Soconusco and Alta Verapaz; 210 Germans holding ~80% of Haiti's commerce and financing its revolutions (Schmidt) ✔; HAPAG's Caribbean headquarters at St Thomas (US soil since 1917) and Albert Ballin, who in the setting never takes the pills of 9 Nov 1918; Sommerfeld arming Villa ✔; the Dresden with Canaris and Witzke ✔; Papen studying railway dynamite in Mexico (hearsay attribution, V_M13); a Carranza-government radio station built with German engineers (not "smashed by the British" — V_M13); the Gold Shirts' embassy money (1935) ✔; Davis's oil barter (1938) ✔; the Emden at Puerto San José, Guatemala (Jan 1936) ✔ corrected; Monterrey's brewers; Japanese and Okinawan coal miners in Coahuila in a world where Japan leans German (M08). Navy action was only ever debt-collection (Venezuela 1902–03), and that is what invented the Roosevelt Corollary — the parent of the setting's Veracruz receivership. Three places where "loom" could become "contest" without a war: a legal fight over the Veracruz ledger; Germany as patron of the rump Mexican state (advisers, radio, oil purchases — "the Zimmermann offer paid in kind" now needs rewording, see §6 C2); and the countries inside the Circle's 1,200-mile radius but outside America's holdings (Guatemala, Colombia's German airline SCADTA, Venezuela).

T3 ★ Villa and his head (M01, M06, M08, M14, M17). The raid-maker's whole arc sits on ground the treaty cedes: Columbus (9 Mar 1916) ✔, Parral (12 Apr 1916; the schoolteacher Elisa Griensen, contested legend) ✔, his death at Parral (20 Jul 1923) ✔, his bullet-riddled Dodge in Chihuahua's Quinta Luz museum (M08), and the head stolen on 6 Feb 1926 ✔. Then Chicago: the soldier-of-fortune Holmdahl (Patton's guide in 1916; accused, jailed, denied it to his death ✔) is tied by a named hearsay chain — L. M. Shadbolt's 1927 letter from an El Paso hotel to Haldeen Braddy — to a "$5,000 buyer in Chicago" (V_M14 says this is stronger than M14 thought), and the Indianapolis Times of 12 Feb 1926 ran the Chicago wire about the head going to "Chicago medical men and criminologists" (M17). The Gold Shirts of 1934 named themselves for his Dorados and were led by his old horseman (M01 ✔). Bierce's five rival deaths (M01, M06) are the same country's cold file. Whether Villa dies at Parral is the owner's call (§5, O2); the recommendation keeps the date, because the head needs the death.

T4 Leonard Wood and Havana (M03, M11; rulebook §31; PEOPLE). Wood governed Cuba 1899–1902 (the second US military governor, after Brooke — V_M11), cleaned Havana street by street (the fever gone by late 1901), licensed its first gambling hall over his own lawyer's objection, and wrote that Platt left Cuba "little or no independence" ✔. In the setting he is president 1921–27 — so the Crowder proconsulship, Zayas's presidency, the Isle of Pines ratification (13 Mar 1925) and the Maine monument's unveiling with his own bust (8 Mar 1925) all fall in his term. He is the only president who ran the city the Circle's map is drawn around. Hook shape: a president who knows Havana's streets, asking questions about his friend's death in 1926–27 (§18), while his own bronze face goes up on the Malecón.

T5 The zombie and Vodou thread — handled as three layers (M04, M17, M03, M10, M16). Layer 1, the period's frame (labelled as the source's, often racist): a Navy report packaged as news, "VOODOOISM IS FAITH OF HAITI" (29 Dec 1920, M17); Seabrook's The Magic Island (1929); White Zombie (1932); the "White King of La Gonâve" (1931). Layer 2, the real record: Haitian law treated zombification as poisoning (Code Pénal Art. 246) and still named Makandal in its sorcery law (M04 ✔); Felix-Mentor's case was studied by a Haitian psychiatrist (M04 ✔); Haiti's own 1935 decree-law against Vodou practice (M16) is the only state campaign against a religion in the islands M16 could attest. Layer 3, Umbra's overlay: the monster is the crime — a payroll of nameless workers on an American sugar estate (Hasco, 1918 ✔) — never the religion. The same rule reads Cuba's 1904 "niña Zoila" case and the 1919 "brujo" lynchings (M03): the investigator's job there is to stop the lynch mob. Voodoo Macbeth touring Chicago in 1936 (M04 ✔) is the thread's Chicago door.

T6 Tampico oil and Chicago capital (M09, M01, M05, M10, M13, M15; PEOPLE §6). Doheny's first oil at El Ébano (1901, San Luis Potosí — inland, on the enclave's unsettled edge); the Golden Lane; Cerro Azul No. 4 (Feb 1916); Potrero del Llano's wellhead sealed in concrete (1914); the Prieto killings (1918) ✔; Butler's "especially Tampico" ✔; Sandino on a Tampico refinery payroll ✔. Chicago money reaches the same system: Standard Oil of Indiana controlled Aruba's Lago refinery to 1932 ✔; International Harvester's secret 1902 henequen contract fixed Yucatán's price ✔; Wrigley's chicle; Dawes's Central Trust (PEOPLE). The hardest collision in the lane is the 18 March 1938 expropriation against an American-held Tampico (M01 Q8) — which resolves cleanly if Poza Rica (discovered 1930, south of the Golden Lane, El Águila's) is "the field Mexico kept" (see §4 and §5 D-items).

T7 The Golden Circle's Havana-centred map (M03, M11, M13, M15, M10, M05). The map has a literal centre point — kilometre zero under the Capitolio dome (1929) ✔ — and the record keeps drawing it: the 1940 Act of Havana, the treaty form for taking a European colony, was signed in the Circle's own city ✔; Yucatán's Casta Divina planters fled to Havana (M15 Q3); Mexican Carmelites were already writing to New Orleans from a Havana convent in 1915 (M16); M15 gives great-circle distances from Havana (Mérida ~490 miles). What sits inside the radius but outside America's holdings — Guatemala, Honduras, Colombia, Venezuela, the Isthmus of Tehuantepec, Yucatán — is the natural answer to §9.6's "is a piece still missing?" (§5, O3).

T8 Churches destroyed — three engines, and one correction to the owner's instinct (M16, M01, M07, M08, M12, M15). The revolutionary armies of 1914–17 (Guadalajara's bishops' tombs opened, July 1914, M16; Mérida's cathedral sacked 24 Sept 1915 to La Cucaracha, M15 ✔); the Calles-era governors of 1926–37 (the Calles Law, priests 4,500 → 334; Cubilete bombed or dynamited 30 Jan 1928 ✔; Tabasco's Garrido; Chiapas's Grajales, 20 Nov 1934 ✔; Sonora's thirty churches shut 1931–37 — ceded ground); and the people's answer (images hidden in caves and wardrobes; women retaking churches). In the islands the church-breakers were earthquakes and hurricanes, not Marines — M16 found no page putting US Marines quartered in a church in Haiti, the DR or Nicaragua, so a "Marines desecrate a church" scene has no record behind it. Villahermosa's cathedral (closed 1928, burned 1930, a "rationalist" school, razed 1934, its black Christ vanished) is the closest real match to §29.3's chapel built over something.

T9 The Southern War veteran on the street (M14, M01, M06; rulebook §31 "Which war", :931; Audits 141, 183). The first-era veteran is most likely a 1916 National Guardsman (Chicago's 1st Illinois was the first Guard regiment on the border ✔; the Irish 7th; the 8th Illinois ✔; the "1st Field Artillery" is unconfirmed and the 1st Illinois Cavalry is a documented substitute). What he remembers is concrete: Camp Wilson, paratyphoid, the Jennies that fell apart (1st Aero Squadron ✔), Ojos Azules' "last cavalry charge", Carrizal, and Pershing's 524–527 Chinese who came out with the column ✔. The rulebook already rules the hall is the VFW, never the Legion (rulebook:931); the naming irony is that the first "American Legion" was a Canadian battalion of Americans (the 97th CEF, 1915). Audit 141 re-derives the Bonus Army as Southern War veterans (1932); Audit 183 has the 1924 Indian Citizenship Act recognise service in Mexico — the Apache Scouts of Ojos Azules are its face.

T10 The ceded Territory's people, and the category problem (M08, M06, M01, M02; OPEN_DECISIONS 59; Audit 145). The treaty inherits finished societies: the Yaqui (never officially surrendered), Mayo, Rarámuri, Sonora's Chinese, Coahuila's ~11,000 Japanese and Okinawan contract miners, Utah Mormons in Chihuahua, and — if they still come — the Manitoba Mennonites of 1922. The Repatriation, the Juárez bath cordon (1917), the marijuana ordinance and Chinese Exclusion all turn on one legal question: what is a Mexican in "the Territory, which flies the flag"? Audit 145 has already answered the Repatriation card: nationals of the Mexico that is left are pushed across the new border; the Territory's own people are "sent home" into the Territory.

T11 "Held by the ledger" — the receivership verb (M09, M13, M04, M05, M14). The fourth verb beside bought, leased and taken (§29.4 item 4) has a complete real genealogy: Venezuela 1903 (the Washington Protocols pledged 30% of La Guaira's and Puerto Cabello's customs) → the Roosevelt Corollary → Santo Domingo 1905 → Veracruz 1914 (the sealed box) → Haiti 1915 (the Banque Nationale's gold shipped to National City Bank's vault seven months before the landing, Dec 1914) → Nicaragua. Smedley Butler personally ran the gendarmerie in Haiti and won his Medal of Honor at Veracruz — "the Marine who ran the ledger" (PEOPLE §3), and in 1935 the man who said so in print.

T12 No Soviet Union — who plays Moscow? (M05, M03, M12, M15, M17). Ponce, the Ciénaga strike, the Panama rent strike, Mella and Guiteras in Cuba, Sandino's Mérida contacts, Trotsky at Tampico (9 Jan 1937) and Eisenstein's Oaxaca footage all lean on a Moscow the setting does not have (rulebook:1633). The replacement vocabulary the record supports is home-grown — anarcho-syndicalist, nationalist, "bandit" (the papers' own word for Sandino; M17 #9 shows the period's dissent) — with German money where the record already shows it (the Gold Shirts, the 1938 oil).

4. The treaty-line gazetteer

The line as recorded. Rulebook §31 (hemisphere row, rulebook:1634) and Appendix 10 (rulebook:2138) cede "the mineral north — Sonora's copper, Chihuahua's silver, Coahuila's coal, Monterrey's steel" and "the Tampico oil coast as a separate enclave", and put an American receivership in Veracruz's customs house under a Mexican flag. Every chapter read this as four whole states — Sonora, Chihuahua, Coahuila, Nuevo León — and that is the reading used here (whole states follow the 1848 precedent of ceding named provinces). Audit 145 calls the ceded north "the Territory" (RESOLVED_ANSWERS.md:1265). Before March 1916 everything below happened as recorded.

5.3 DELEGATED — historical sense

Each: the recommendation, then the reason. None reverses an owner idea; D1 and D2 sit on items §0 still calls "genuinely open" (ALTERNATE_HISTORY.md:29), so they are offered for the owner to ratify with a word.

The empire's shape

  • D1. Bought, leased or taken — colony by colony (§8.6; Appendix 10; M10 summary; M05 Q7; M16:351). Adopt M10's table: bought — Trinidad & Tobago (c. 1920–22), British Guiana (c. 1928–29), British Honduras, the small British islands as one lot, the Bahamas after repeal (1933); leased — Antigua, St Lucia, Bermuda; still European with American leases — Jamaica, Martinique, Guadeloupe, Suriname (concessions); still European outright — Barbados, French Guiana, Saint-Pierre. The one ugly exception is Curaçao and Aruba, "held by a garrison" from Urbina's raid (June 1929) under the Dutch flag. Reason: it follows the real 1932 "war debts" list and the 1940 lease list. France cannot sell its citizens. The Dutch ruling (leases, not islands, ALTERNATE_HISTORY.md:2033–2035) holds.

  • D2. Does Germany contest the hemisphere? (§8.6; M13 Part E; M04 Q5; M05 Q6; M12 Q4; M01 Q10; M06 Q5). It looms and buys. It contests only by the ledger (a legal claim against the Veracruz receivership), by patronage of the rump state (advisers, radio, oil purchases), and by commerce inside the Circle's radius but outside America's holdings. There is no gunboat war. Haiti's Germans are protected by their consul (a Lüders affair in reverse). Sommerfeld is never interned and becomes a free Berlin man in New York. Reason: every real German instrument in the region was commercial or scholarly, and Berlin shelved its invasion plans in 1906 (M13 ✔). A Germany that took Britain's purse rather than its fleet (§30.1) would do more of the same.

  • D3. Who governs the ceded north (PEOPLE gap 4; M08 Q1; brief). A military governor under the Southern Department, with four military districts, one per state. Civil government is promised and never granted, because the second war never ends. Reason: every American precedent began this way: Cuba in 1899, the DR's naval military government in 1916, the 1846–50 Mexican Cession. Faces for the districts: Creel (d. 1931), Kosterlitzky (Bureau agent 1922–26), Hearst's managers (M08).

  • D4. The treaty's name (brief). The Treaty of Querétaro (1917). Reason: Carranza's government and the Constitutional Congress sat at Querétaro that winter (M01 Q3), and the 1848 treaty's ratifications were exchanged there (standard record; not re-checked this sweep). The second cession is signed where the first was sealed.

  • D5. What the Territory's people are in law (M08 Q2; M06 Q3; OPEN_DECISIONS 59). US nationals, not citizens — the Insular Cases' category the setting already uses for Filipinos (ALTERNATE_HISTORY.md:1801). Chinese Exclusion applies to Sonora's Chinese. The Yaqui are unrecognised until D10's reservation. Reason: the Territory is held, not incorporated.

  • D6. Land titles and exemptions (M08 Q3; M08:32 the Fundidora). Titles are recognised on paper and eroded in American courts. Babícora, Greene's, ASARCO's, the Maderos' and the Fundidora consortium's holdings survive; the military exemptions Mexican presidents granted lapse. Reason: this is the 1848 pattern of the treaty's property articles followed by the land-claims courts.

  • D7. The Mennonites (M08 Q3; M13 Part E 3). They do not settle in American Chihuahua, because no American president grants the 1921 privileges. They go to unceded Durango. Reason: they left Manitoba to escape exactly this kind of state control, and a Durango colony of 1924 is on the real record (not re-checked this sweep).

  • D8. The 1917 Constitution (M01 Q3; M16:52). It exists as the defeated rump's defiant charter, and Article 27 stands as a claim on the Territory's subsoil and the enclave's oil. Reason: it was written that same winter and keeps every date. The treaty does not erase a charter.

  • D9. The 1938 expropriation (M01 Q8; M13 #10; T6). It happens on 18 March 1938. It takes the fields south of the enclave line (Poza Rica, El Águila's), files an Article 27 claim against the enclave, and Germany buys the oil (Davis's barter). Reason: this keeps the date and gives the act a real target.

  • D10. Events that cannot happen as recorded in the ceded north (M01 Q6, :393; M08 Q4–5; M16 #8; M06:94, :444; M13:337).

    • Maldonado (1937) is killed by a nationalist band as a collaborator-priest.
    • Chihuahua's one-priest law and Sonora's 1931–37 closures do not happen. The churches Calles shut reopen under American protection in 1917, and that is where the IHS sets its first northern base (the IHS–Secret Service alliance, §23).
    • The Yaqui campaign of 1926–27 is America's war. The 1937 Yaqui land grant becomes a reservation under the 1934 Indian Reorganization Act, in the same year.
    • The 1931 Chinese expulsion is not a state decree; the leagues and the Exclusion law do the work instead.
    • The 1929 Escobar rising is a rising against the occupier (the Naco bombing kept, 2–6 April).
    • The Volstead Act applies in the Territory, so Juárez's cantinas close, Monterrey's brewery makes near-beer, and the drinking border moves to Tamaulipas and Baja.

    Reason: each keeps its date and meaning under American law. Prohibition applied in the territories.

  • D11. The Cristero frontier (M01 Q5; M07 treaty note, Top 10 #4). Washington does not arm the Cristeros officially; the real embargo and the Morrow/Burke mediation stand. Liga money and guns move through the Territory and San Antonio under the Bureau's eye. The Robert E. Lee Hotel plot stays in San Antonio, its real place. Reason: the rump that signed the treaty is Washington's counterparty.

  • D12. Mexico City's American presence (M12 Q1). An Embassy as before, plus a mixed claims commission for the indemnity. The ledger sits at Veracruz, not in the capital. Reason: the rump is sovereign.

  • D13. San Juan de Ulúa (M09:281). The Marines hold it — the receivership's harbour fortress, Veracruz's Guantánamo. Reason: a customs guard needs the harbour mouth, and the fort had closed as a prison in 1915.

  • D14. De la Huerta's 1923 rising at Veracruz (M09:361). The customs house stays an American island in a rebel city, and the rebels never get the revenue. Reason: that is what receivership guards did.

  • D15. El Águila and a bankrupt Britain (M09:106). As recorded: control passes to Royal Dutch Shell in 1919 (standard record; not re-checked). Reason: it keeps the record, and the Dutch half is neutral money (§29.4 item 6).

  • D16. The Huastec Adolescent (1917) (M09:177). It is found at Tamuín, outside the enclave line, and stays Mexican. Reason: §4.6's line.

  • D17. Durango, Baja, Zacatecas, Tampico's inland edge, Tehuantepec, the remaining border (M06 Q1–2; M07:386; M09:36 and :66; M15 Q2). See §4.6. In short: Durango, Baja (with Mexicali as a concession) and Zacatecas are not ceded; the enclave runs to the Tuxpan River and inland to El Ébano; the isthmus is Mexican with American transit rights.

Cuba

  • D18. Cuba's form and garrison (M03 Q1–2; M03:249, :569; M11 bends). A nominal Platt republic under a permanent garrison: every Cuban president keeps his name and date. Marines sit in the Oriente/Camagüey sugar zone, at Guantánamo and in La Cabaña. Crowder is ambassador-proconsul and answers to President Wood. Reason: Platt never ends (R2), and this keeps M03's Part C usable.
  • D19. What the Cuban garrison is "for" (M03 Q3). "Order and the harvest", and after 1917 the Southern War next door.
  • D20. The Isle of Pines (M03 Q5, :149). It is ratified to Cuba on 13 March 1925 as recorded — a Senate act in Wood's term. Reason: it keeps the date; under an occupied Cuba the island's flag is cosmetic, and the settlers' grievance becomes a hook.
  • D21. The Sixth Pan-American Conference, Jan 1928 (M03 Q6). President Dawes opens it; Hughes leads the delegation, as he did in the record.
  • D22. 1933 and Batista (M03 Q7, :311, :341). The occupation chooses Batista. The Porra was tolerated by the garrison until Machado fell. Reason: this was Welles's and Caffery's real reading of Batista, made into policy.
  • D23. The Dance of the Millions (M03 Q9, :239). A German decision on beet sugar crashes Havana in 1920, rehearsing the 1929 sanctions. Reason: the real crash came from Europe's beet returning, and in this world that market is Germany's (§16.3–16.4).
  • D24. Haitian deportations from Cuba, 1933–37 (M03 Q10, :609). Cuba orders them and the two occupation authorities carry them out — a transfer inside the empire, so no foreign government protests.

Haiti, the Dominican Republic, Nicaragua, Puerto Rico

  • D25. Haiti's form after 1930 (M04 Q1). Client presidents (Vincent) under a permanent High Commissioner. Reason: this is the real 1915–34 form, continued (R3).
  • D26. The Dominican Republic after 1924 (M04 Q2). The naval military government lasts into the late 1920s, then gives way to a client regime run through the Guardia (and on into O2).
  • D27. The Senate's Haiti inquiry (M04 Q6). Held as recorded, 1921–22, with Medill McCormick in the chair. Reason: it was a Senate act and needs no President Harding.
  • D28. Nicaragua never emptied (M05 Q1, :436). There is no 1933 departure and no peace. Sandino is killed on 21 Feb 1934 by the Guardia, under a garrison that cannot claim not to know. Reason: it keeps the date, and the retreat from empire does not happen (R2).
  • D29. Sandino's Tampico years (M05 Q2). The enclave produced him, on an American refinery payroll.
  • D30. Puerto Rico's draft (M05 Q3). Citizenship in March 1917 arrives alongside a Southern War draft. Reason: it keeps the real pairing of the Jones Act and the draft act; Crowder's draft is raised for Mexico (M03:249).
  • D31. The Virgin Islands handover, 1931 (M05 Q5). It happens (Navy to Interior), and Hoover's "poorhouse" line stands; the Navy keeps its watch on HAPAG's St Thomas station.
  • D32. Central America (M05 Q7). Not held — American companies in sovereign countries, as recorded. This feeds O3.
  • D33. Who plays Moscow (M05 Q4; M03 Q8; M15 Q6, Q8; M12 Q5). A home-grown vocabulary of anarchist, nationalist and "bandit", with German money only where the record shows German money. Trotsky's Coyoacán house stays a private house. Eisenstein's Sinclair-funded Mexican film is kept, its director a subject of a defeated Russian empire rather than a Soviet artist.
  • D34. Real officers' Great War careers (M05 Q8; M14:74). Re-derive each on Southern War service — the audit's standing method (Audits 141, 183). Butler is both: the empire's most decorated servant, and in 1935 the man who said "especially Tampico" in print.

The border, the veterans, the rump state

  • D35. A security law (M06 Q4). An Espionage Act still passes in June 1917, aimed at the Southern War and at German plots, so Flores Magón's sentence and Bisbee's "German agents" stand. Reason: the country is at war in 1917.
  • D36. Zyklon B (M06 Q6). Use it as a plain, labelled period fact where the record has it (the El Paso fumigations). In the world it casts no shadow, because there is no Nazi Germany (rulebook:1636).
  • D37. The Torreón claim and Mexico's debts in the ceded states (M01:181; M08 Q8). America assumes only its own citizens' claims, the 1848 pattern, so China's claim goes unpaid as in the record.
  • D38. Mein Kampf (M01 Q9, :455). It does not exist. It was dictated in prison after a putsch that a victorious Germany never produced. Calles's 1936 exile reading becomes some other book. Reason: the rulebook's Hitler holds no office and has only the occult (rulebook:1640).
  • D39. Rivera's National Palace stair (M12 Q3). The Southern War is on the wall. Rivera painted the history he believed, and a lost war would be central to it.
  • D40. The Casta Divina as "Havana men" (M15 Q3). Yes, as a hook: Molina really fled to Havana.
  • D41. The 1923 arms through British Honduras (M15 Q4). Belize is American (D1), so the arms door was American ground: a German or Circle hand, never proven.
  • D42. The archaeologist-spies (M15 Q5). ONI's March 1917 net exists as a peacetime watch on German Caribbean footholds (the fear that bought the Danish islands) and passes to the federal side (Secret Service).
  • D43. A Yucatán republic (M15 Q7). A secessionist current, but no republic; Yucatán stays Mexican.
  • D44. US recognition, Sept 1923, and Bucareli (M15 Q10; M01 lead). Moot, because relations run on the treaty. The 1923 date becomes a claims convention.
  • D45. Horse cavalry (M14:109). It lives on into the 1930s in the Territory's mountains; the real Army kept horse regiments to 1942.
  • D46. The Expatriation Act's wartime clause (M14:268). The law applies as written. If the war in Mexico counts, oaths sworn to King or Kaiser after 1916 cost no citizenship. Whether §31's row carries that is a rules-text choice for the owner; the history itself is settled.
  • D47. Trinidad's 1917 Shouters ban under an American governor (M10:193). Kept. The occupation also enforced Haiti's sorcery law (M04), and the period's racial fear outweighs First Amendment habits abroad.

V_M19 — Fact-check of M19_soldiers_of_fortune.md

  • Checked: 2026-09-30, Sonnet 5.5 fact-checker. No GPU, no git. Pages were fetched and read this session (Wikipedia via its API or WebFetch; the libcom article and Roosevelt's Through the Brazilian Wilderness downloaded and searched as text).
  • Verdicts: CONFIRMED / CORRECTED (+URL) / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG. "Rests on memoir" is flagged where Wikipedia's only source is the man himself.
  • Fixes applied to M19 are listed at the end; each is tagged ⟨fixed: V_M19⟩ (a change) or [UNVERIFIED — V_M19] (a claim left in place but not supported).
  • Headline: the Hornet plot stands, but four details were wrong (which ship Cooper commanded, the "failed first attempt", the Storyville "lost" wording, and whose claim the "State Department knew" line was). The River of Doubt roster was the biggest problem: Zahm, Fiala and Miller did not run the river — the "founding voyage" carried only Roosevelt, Kermit, Cherrie, Rondon and Brazilians down it.

A. The Top-10 items

1. The Hornet plot, Dec 1910 – Jan 1911 (Top 10 #1)

# Claim in M19 Verdict Evidence
1a Christmas, Molony, Bonilla and Dávadi went out in Storyville, visited the May Evans bordello on Basin Street, in December 1910, with Secret Service agents watching CONFIRMED libcom (Koeppel, Banana): "May Evans bordello on Basin Street"; agents "had been watching them for days"; the night is dated 23 December (the Hornet had left Algiers Point on the 22nd and waited outside the three-mile limit). https://libcom.org/article/ousting-president-honduras-1911
1b They "lost" the agents around 2 a.m. CORRECTED The agents did not lose them: "at two o'clock in the morning, they called it a night" and "walked away from their posts" (they reported "nothing but a drunken brawl in the District"). Same URL. M19 reworded.
1c Yacht at Bayou St. John → Ship Island → boarded the Hornet CONFIRMED (nuance) Same URL: yacht at Bayou St. John, across Lake Pontchartrain to Ship Island; rifles hidden there were ferried out to the Hornet, which was already at sea.
1d "A first, 1910 attempt had failed after Secret Service agents told the owners she could not sail without certification" WRONG Same URL: agents said she could not sail unless inspectors certified no weapons; inspectors found "only food, two hundred tons of coal, and twenty men… they could not detain her" and she sailed 22 December. There was no failed first attempt in this source.
1e Roatán taken on New Year's Eve; the Utila commander made to run round his cabin in his underwear shouting "Viva Bonilla!" CONFIRMED Same URL (verbatim).
1f La Ceiba, 25 January 1911; Molony's gun decisive; a Krupp piece captured; Gen. Francisco Guerrero killed CONFIRMED Same URL. (Weapon: libcom says Hotchkiss; Wikipedia's Lee Christmas page says surplus Colt Model 1895 machine guns — M19 already notes the split. The "potato diggers" nickname is not on that page.) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Christmas
1g Wikipedia calls La Ceiba "the first coordinated use of automatic weapons" CORRECTED (softened) Wikipedia: "is believed to be the first time automatic weapons were so used".
1h Gunboat captain George Cooper declared La Ceiba a "neutral zone" CONFIRMED libcom: he "sent a message to… General Francisco Guerrero, declaring La Ceiba a 'neutral zone'".
1i Cooper was the USS Tacoma's commander (People line) WRONG libcom: "Captain George Cooper, commander of the Marietta". Wikipedia's Tacoma page: Tacoma acted "pursuant to the orders of the senior naval officer present embarked in the gunboat Marietta". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Tacoma_(C-18)
1j Cooper "later reported that the State Department 'was well aware of all the plans of the revolutionists before they began'" CORRECTED The quote is Cooper reporting what Christmas told him on 17 January ("He informed me…"). libcom adds that Cooper, getting no contradiction from Washington, "logically came to accept it." It is Christmas's claim, relayed by Cooper.
1k Two gunboats, Tacoma and Marietta, offshore CONFIRMED libcom. Before La Ceiba, Cooper had put the Hornet under guard and ordered her seized for neutrality violations — the "friendly navy" is less clean than M19 implies.
1l Assembly voted 32–4 to reject the Morgan loan; Bonilla inaugurated February 1912; 75 U.S. Marines at Puerto Cortés; Zemurray got ~20,000 ha (10,000 + 10,000), duty-free imports, a $500,000 loan CONFIRMED libcom; Wikipedia's Zemurray page gives inauguration as 1 February 1912. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Zemurray
1m "the first time I've ever heard of anybody going from a whorehouse to a White House" CONFIRMED libcom (verbatim).
1n ~100 mercenaries recruited in New Orleans, Sam Dreben among them CONFIRMED Wikipedia (Zemurray; Lee Christmas).

Source note: the Storyville night and the Cooper dispatch rest on Koeppel's Banana via libcom; Wikipedia's Christmas and Zemurray pages do not mention Storyville, the Secret Service or Ship Island.

2. Lee Christmas and Guy Molony (Part A)

# Claim Verdict Evidence
2a Christmas born 2 Feb 1863, Livingston Parish; died 21 Jan 1924, New Orleans, of acute anemia after tropical sprue; train captured 14 April 1897 at the Laguna trestle CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Christmas
2b "1899 colonel and police chief of Tegucigalpa under Sierra" CORRECTED Wikipedia: defected to the government in 1899 and "was appointed a colonel and chief of police of Tegucigalpa by President Terencio Sierra, in May 1902."
2c Molony superintendent of police "1920 or 1921", resigned after four years (1925); died 13 Feb 1972 CORRECTED Encyclopedia.com (M19's own source): police chief 1921, resigned four years later; died 13 Feb 1972. "1920" not found in it. https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/molony-guy-1884-1972

3. Cannon and Groce, November 1909

# Claim Verdict Evidence
3a Mining the San Juan River to sink a troop transport; shot at El Castillo; Knox's "a blot upon the history of Nicaragua" CONFIRMED Executed Today: caught "mining the San Juan River in an admitted attempt to sink a Nicaraguan troop transport, and shot in El Castillo a few days later"; Knox quote verbatim. https://www.executedtoday.com/2009/11/17/1909-leonard-groce-and-lee-roy-cannon-american-mercenaries-nicaragua-zelaya/
3b Date "12, 16 or 17 November" CORRECTED Executed Today: 17th ("some sources cite the 16th"). The 12th is unsupported.
3c Holmdahl "fighting alongside the San Juan River" when they were caught UNSUPPORTED (M19 already flagged) Wikipedia's Holmdahl page: "Holmdahl probably took part in the fighting" — a guess, resting on his own account.

4. The River of Doubt party (Top 10 #4, #5; Part F)

Sources: Roosevelt, Through the Brazilian Wilderness (chapters 4–10, https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/theodore-roosevelt/through-the-brazilian-wilderness) and Wikipedia's expedition page (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roosevelt%E2%80%93Rondon_Scientific_Expedition).

# Claim Verdict Evidence
4a Fr. Zahm organised the trip / was on the founding voyage CORRECTED Zahm talked Roosevelt into a South American trip and organised the original Amazon-cruise plan (Wikipedia, Zahm and expedition pages; Roosevelt: Zahm raised it "in the White House" six years earlier). But Roosevelt's text: "From here [Utiarity Falls] Father Zahm returned to Tapirapoan, accompanied by Sigg" (chapter 6, late January 1914). He never went near the River of Doubt. (Wikipedia's expedition page wrongly has him following the Ji-Paraná.) He was a Holy Cross priest, C.S.C. — CONFIRMED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Augustine_Zahm
4b Fiala — quartermaster; 1903–05 Ziegler expedition; lost the ship America in Teplitz Bay; Champ's relief found the party at Cape Dillon, July 1905 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Fiala (also Brooklyn Daily Eagle 1894–99, born 1869, d. 1950). Roosevelt: Fiala "took charge of handling the outfit… with his usual efficiency."
4c Fiala was one of the party on the river / his choice "raised many eyebrows" UNSUPPORTED Roosevelt (chapter 7): on 3 February "Fiala and Lieutenant Lauriadó stayed at Utiarity to take canoes and go down the Papagaio… the Juruena and Tapajos"; Fiala's boat upset, his baggage lost, and he went home (chapter 10). The "eyebrows" line is not on Wikipedia's Fiala or expedition page nor in Roosevelt's text; it came from a search summary and could not be re-found.
4d Cherrie's Chicago Field Museum link CONFIRMED (nuance) Wikipedia: "assistant Curator of ornithology from 1894 to 1897" at the Field Museum, but also curator at Costa Rica's National Museum in the same 1889–97 span — that he lived in Chicago is not stated. Born 22 Aug 1865, d. 20 Jan 1948, Newfane VT; Dark Trails 1930; 1925 Simpson–Roosevelts expedition (sponsor not stated) — CONFIRMED. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Kruck_Cherrie
4e Cherrie, Kermit and Roosevelt were the three Americans in a party of 22 going downriver CONFIRMED Roosevelt: "food for twenty-two men"; seven dugouts; Wikipedia's page gives 19 men, 16 returned — sources differ on the count, agree on the three Americans.
4f Leo Miller "handled the mammalogy" on the River of Doubt WRONG Roosevelt (chapter 8), at the river's start: "Amilcar and Miller and all the others of the Gy-Paraná party were on the banks… to wave farewell." Miller went with Capt. Amilcar's Ji-Paraná party and was "collecting near Manaos" (chapter 10). He was on the overland march (mammals, with Cherrie on birds) only.
4g Rondon: born 5 May 1865; 4,000+ miles of telegraph; Indian Protection Service 1910; motto "die if need be, never kill"; Leticia mediation 1934–38; Marshal 1955; Nobel nomination 1957; d. 19 Jan 1958 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A2ndido_Rondon (the motto is the SPI's, created by him). Note the same page: the SPI was later disbanded in disgrace (1967) for abuses.
4h Cinta Larga shadowed the party and "could have wiped it out" but chose not to CONFIRMED as Wikipedia's claim; CORRECTED as history Wikipedia states it (uncited). Roosevelt's own book says only: "we had seen no human being, although we had twice heard Indians," and "fresh Indian sign"; he does not name the Cinta Larga or describe shadowing. The fuller story is a later retelling (Millard 2005; a search result cites Cinta Larga oral history that they followed the party and could not agree to attack). Treat "for weeks" and "a dozen days" as unsupported.
4i Simplício drowned 15 March 1914; Kermit, João (helmsman) and the dog Trigueiro; body never found CONFIRMED Roosevelt chapter 8: "the 15th of March"; "Poor Simplicio must have been pulled under at once… nor did we ever recover his body."
4j Julio de Lima killed Manoel Vicente da Paixão in early April 1914 for catching him stealing food; Julio abandoned CONFIRMED Wikipedia; Roosevelt chapter 9 (Roosevelt names the victim "Paixão, Paishon… a corporal and acting sergeant in the engineer corps," not "porters' supervisor"; Julio was "of pure European blood").
4k Party left New York 4 Oct 1913; reached the river 27 Feb 1914; reunited with relief 26 April CONFIRMED Wikipedia (expedition, Rondon pages).

5. Duquesne and Burnham (Top 10 #6, #7)

# Claim Verdict Evidence
5a Duquesne was Roosevelt's "personal shooting instructor" / in Roosevelt's circle UNSUPPORTED Wikipedia repeats it but adds "many of the claims Duquesne made about himself are in dispute." The only support seen is Duquesne's own 1909 Hampton's Magazine pieces ("Hunting Ahead of Roosevelt"). No independent source found. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Joubert_Duquesne
5b Hippo Bill, 1910, Broussard, Burnham; "narrowly failed" CONFIRMED Wikipedia: H.R. 23261, $250,000; "The bill fell just short of passing." Burnham's page: he and Duquesne met for the first time in 1910 lobbying separately; TR backed the plan.
5c Duquesne dates: b. 21 Sep 1877; arrested 11 Oct 1901; Bermuda escape 25 Jun 1902; arrested 17 Nov 1917; Bellevue escape 25 May 1919; recaptured 23 May 1932; spy ring arrested 28 Jun 1941; 18 years; released 1954; d. 24 May 1956 on Welfare Island CONFIRMED Wikipedia (all dates match).
5d Burnham a friend of Roosevelt CONFIRMED Wikipedia: "close friends… Theodore Roosevelt and Gifford Pinchot."
5e Burnham led 250 private security men (hired by Hammond) at the Taft–Díaz summit, 16 Oct 1909, and with Texas Ranger C. R. Moore seized an armed man CONFIRMED Wikipedia (Burnham): "Burnham and Private C.R. Moore… discovered a man holding a concealed palm pistol… captured and disarmed the assassin within only a few feet of Taft and Díaz."
5f Assassin named Julius Bergerson, 52, .32-calibre seven-shot "Protector Palm Pistol" disguised as a pencil UNSUPPORTED Name, age and caliber not found on any page read; a later search found "pencil pistol" in other retellings, and no "Bergerson". The Dallas Morning News page cited in M19 would not load.
5g Hammond bought ~900,000 acres; mines sold to Mexico 1930; Mexico barred foreign land sales 1917; one of 18 officers in Roosevelt's 1917 division; Wild Life Protective League 1914 CONFIRMED Wikipedia (Burnham). The Esperanza Stone "1909" year is not given there — unverified.

6. Tex O'Reilly and Pecos Bill (Top 10 #3)

# Claim Verdict Evidence
6a "The Saga of Pecos Bill", The Century Magazine, October 1923 CONFIRMED Wikipedia (Pecos Bill): "The first known short story about Pecos Bill… written by Tex O'Reilly and published in The Century Magazine in October 1923." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecos_Bill
6b Dobie and Dorson concluded the stories were O'Reilly's own inventions CONFIRMED (as Wikipedia reports it) Wikipedia (both pages); I did not read Dobie or Dorson directly.
6c Pecos Bill was "invented in 1923" CORRECTED (nuance) The name was already used for bandit characters in O'Reilly's 1921 films (West of the Rio Grande, On the High Card); 1923 is the first known story.
6d O'Reilly b. 15 Aug 1880, Denton; d. 8 Dec 1946, Tupper Lake; Roving and Fighting (1918); comic strip with Jack A. Warren 1936–37 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_S._O%27Reilly (the "ten wars" and Villa/Rif claims are flagged there as claims).
6e Born to Raise Hell with Lowell Thomas fits a "1930–1933" ghostwriting window UNSUPPORTED Wikipedia lists only the 2001 Long Riders' Guild edition; the original year was not confirmed here (from memory 1936 — unverified).

7. Holmdahl (Top 10 #2; Part B)

# Claim Verdict Evidence
7a Rosamorada: 112 "killers" picked from the prison book, shot at midnight by a six-officer firing squad; 700 prisoners; 500 freed with five pesos CONFIRMED as what the article says; rests wholly on memoir Wikipedia: "look at the prison book… take them out at midnight and shoot them… we'll use officers… 112 of the worst killers were selected… six officers… firing squad… 'This kept us busy the whole night,' Holmdahl wrote." The 500/five-peso lines carry [citation needed]. Extra detail M19 omits: the men were told they'd be pardoned if they marched to Acaponeta, then halted at a cemetery and shot. The "Mexican-priest kicked" and "stained-glass window" stories also carry [citation needed]. No independent source. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Lewis_Holmdahl
7b Rosario taken with dynamite from the Buena Noche mine (27 cases); 280 Cora at Tepic; 36 hours; two-thirds dead CONFIRMED (as the article says; same memoir source) Same page.
7c Born 26 Aug 1883, Fort Dodge; enlisted at 15 in the 51st Iowa; left San Francisco on SS Pennsylvania 3 Nov 1898; Calumpit 25 Apr 1899; discharged 31 Jan 1907; pardon July 1917; 6th Engineers; died 8 Apr 1963 loading prospecting tools; arrested at Parral 1926 (Villa's head), freed via Ben F. Williams and Mayor Antonio Martínez CONFIRMED Same page.

8. Funston's death (Top 10 #8)

# Claim Verdict Evidence
8a Died 19 Feb 1917, heart attack, lobby of the St. Anthony Hotel, San Antonio; orchestra; holding six-year-old Inez Harriett Silverberg CONFIRMED Wikipedia (Funston; The St. Anthony Hotel). Independent contemporary check: Laurens Advertiser (SC), 21 Feb 1917, p. 9 (OCR, garbled): died 19 Feb, "playing with little [Inez S…]" in the hotel lobby, last words "How beautiful it is" about the orchestra's waltz; the physician (Col. Ireland) gave the cause as sclerosis of the heart's arteries. https://historicnewspapers.sc.edu/lccn/sn93067760/1917-02-21/ed-1/seq-9/ocr (The specific waltz, the Blue Danube, is Wikipedia's alone.)
8b Lay in state at the Alamo and San Francisco City Hall (the first so honoured there); buried San Francisco National Cemetery CONFIRMED Wikipedia.
8c Wilson favoured him for AEF command; Pershing got it CONFIRMED Wikipedia.
8d Earlier career: Cuban insurgent 1896; 20th Kansas 13 May 1898; Medal of Honor 14 Feb 1900; Aguinaldo 23 Mar 1901; 1906 martial law CONFIRMED Wikipedia.

9. The Sheldon Hotel (Top 10 #9)

# Claim Verdict Evidence
9a Burned 9 April 1929 CONFIRMED Three sources: Wikipedia (Plaza Hotel); henrytrost.org ("destroyed by fire which originated in the upper part of the fifth wooden story"); plazahotelelpaso.com. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Hotel_(El_Paso,_Texas) · https://www.henrytrost.org/buildings/sheldon-hotel-remodeling/ · https://www.plazahotelelpaso.com/historic-hotel
9b "Unofficial headquarters" of Revolution participants from both sides CONFIRMED Wikipedia (Plaza Hotel). Independent support: Taylor's The Americas abstract names "the lobby and bar of the Hotel Sheldon or the Elks Club" as where recruits sought work. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/americas/article/abs/great-adventure-mercenaries-in-the-mexican-revolution-19101915/4D82D60DE3824A0104B76AF9A222EF16
9c Built 1884 by Brooklyn-born Dr. Lucius Sheldon; hotel from 1899 CONFIRMED (date wobble) plazahotelelpaso.com: 1884, office building, hotel 1899. henrytrost.org: "built in 1888"; 1899 remodel confirmed.
9d Taft stayed there in 1909 CONFIRMED, Medium Two Sheldon histories say so (henrytrost.org: "President Taft was a guest there when he came to El Paso to meet President Diaz"); no contemporary source seen.
9e "The Plaza Hotel was built on the site (later the Hilton)" CORRECTED Reversed: Conrad Hilton began building in fall 1929 and it opened 30 Nov 1930 as the Hilton; it became the Plaza after Hilton sold it in the 1960s (plazahotelelpaso.com; Wikipedia).
9f "The registers went with it" UNSUPPORTED No source says registers were lost.

10. The going rate (Top 10 #10)

# Claim Verdict Evidence
10a "Several hundred" mercenaries; Richardson and Dreben $500 gold a month; pilot Hector Worden $1,500 a month; Edwin Parsons $200 a month in gold CONFIRMED Taylor abstract (URL in 9b).
10b Masson's contract "$5,000 plus expenses and a monthly salary" CORRECTED Wikipedia (Masson): "$5,000 as an airplane purchase fee, an allotment of $750 for equipment costs, $300 per month salary, $50 per flight… and at least $250 for every bombing sortie." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Masson
10c Masson: 10 May 1913, Sonora, ≥5 gunboats, four pipe bombs (15 kg), Bauche observer; left 5 Aug; b. 23 Feb 1886; d. 2 Jun 1950 Mérida; Aero Club cert. no. 202 CONFIRMED Wikipedia. Its lead calls him "the second flier in history to bomb a surface warship" while its body says "first aerial bombing of a surface ship" — M19's "first in the Western Hemisphere" is safe; "first in the world" is not.

B. Other specific dates and names checked

Claim Verdict Evidence
Dreben b. 1 Jun 1878 Poltava; enlisted 14th Infantry 27 Jun 1899; married Helen Spence early 1917; DSC at St. Étienne (citation date 8 Oct 1918); Unknown Soldier pallbearer 1921; Alguin sting; d. 15 Mar 1925 LA of a nurse's wrong injection; Grand View Memorial Park CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Dreben (the wrong-injection cause is Wikipedia's only)
Juárez: ~2,500 rebels (1,500 + 500 + 500), 700 federals under Navarro, assault 8 May launched by Villa and Orozco without Madero, fighting parallel to the border, thousands of El Paso spectators CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ciudad_Ju%C3%A1rez_(1911) (Tom Mix is "news dispatches… mention"; Linderfeldt is named there)
Creighton (Wheelock) b. 10 Jan 1877; charged Jan 1910, $9,200; 250+ miles of track; $5,000 bounty; killed 15 Apr 1911 at Bauche; 2,000-peso widow's pension; Legion of Honor 15 Nov 1951; Danvers burial; El Paso fiancée Frances Hughes CONFIRMED http://www.wheelockgenealogy.com/pages/oscarbio.htm
Butler "Mr. Johnson" spy trip "1 March 1914" CORRECTED Wikipedia (Butler): 5–7 March 1914, Mexico City. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smedley_Butler
Butler: Philadelphia Director of Public Safety 7 Jan 1924 – 23 Dec 1925; 900+ speakeasies in 48 hours; "hunted the Cacos like pigs"; d. 21 Jun 1940 CONFIRMED Same page.
The McCormack–Dickstein report "called his account 'alarmingly true'" CORRECTED "Alarmingly true" is the New York Times's phrase for the committee's Nov 1934 preliminary announcement. The 15 Feb 1935 report says: "no question that these attempts were discussed, were planned, and might have been placed in execution when and if the financial backers deemed it expedient," and it "was able to verify all the pertinent statements made by General Butler, with the exception of the direct statement suggesting the creation of the organization." MacGuire "denied these allegations under oath" (d. 25 Mar 1935). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Plot
Schilt: 6–8 Jan 1928, ten flights, 18 casualties, replacement commander and supplies, town street burned and levelled, O2U Corsair with no brakes dragged to a stop by the wings CONFIRMED Medal of Honor citation as quoted in a search result (cmohs.org itself returned 403).
Davis's Soldiers of Fortune modelled on Christmas CONFIRMED Wikipedia (Lee Christmas): "believed to be the inspiration for… Captain Macklin and Soldiers of Fortune."

V_M20 — Fact-check of M20_haiti_dr_gazetteer.md

Checker: Sonnet 5.5 · Date: 2026-09-30 · Method: M20 relied on Wikipedia "fetch summaries" and on period books read in full text. I re-read the period books myself (archive.org _djvu.txt of Kuser, Franck, Seabrook, Schoenrich, Niles and the 1920 guide, grepped for M20's quoted words), and read the Wikipedia articles as raw text/API extracts instead of summaries. Second sources for the weak spots (Oloffson, Monte Cristi, Liborio, Bizoton). No GPU, no git. Verdict key: CONFIRMED · CORRECTED (+URL) · UNSUPPORTED · WRONG. Tally (45 claims checked): 39 CONFIRMED · 5 CORRECTED · 1 UNSUPPORTED · 0 WRONG. Every quotation from the period books that I grepped is verbatim. M20's own "UNVERIFIED" flags on the Memphis date, the Bizoton trial date, the Batraville date and the 1920 guide's author are now resolved.

The five things worth knowing

  1. Monte Cristi's $16,000 clock-tower bill is Franck's anecdote and nothing else. Local history says the tower stood in the plaza from 11 March 1895 and was inaugurated on 29 June 1895, its original value was about 15,000 pesos, and the town's ayuntamiento paid for it by a collection. (The same article says Máximo Gómez and José Martí attended and that Martí said this clock "will soon mark the hour of Cuba's redemption" — but Martí died on 19 May 1895, so that can only belong to the March installation; the article is internally inconsistent on this.) So the tower predates the Marines by 21 years and the "occupation billed the town" story is unsupported. (UNSUPPORTED)
  2. Papá Liborio was not, in the Dominican accounts, killed by Marines. The date (27 June 1922) is right; the camp at "El Hoyo del Infierno" was attacked at 6 a.m. by a detachment of the Dominican constabulary (the "P.N.D.") under a Capt. Williams and Lt. Luna; his body was taken to San Juan, photographed and shown to thousands. M20 took "the Marines killed him … in Parque Central" from M04. (CORRECTED)
  3. The Hotel Oloffson claim survives a second source. Wikipedia and Haiti's tourism site both say a US military hospital until the occupation ended in 1934, and a hotel from 1935 under Werner Gustav Oloffson. "From Germany" is Wikipedia only (Visit Haiti says just "Swedish sea captain"). Address: 60 Avenue Christophe — the same avenue Kuser rented on in 1919–20. (CONFIRMED)
  4. USS Memphis was wrecked on 29 August 1916 (M20's recollection was right; Franck's "September" and "battleship" are his mistakes — she was an armored cruiser, 43 dead or missing) and the wreck was scrapped in place, the last of it gone only in 1938. So "still on the rocks in early 1920" is safe. (CONFIRMED)
  5. Franck does not say only two buildings in Haiti have window-glass. He writes "only two buildings in Port au Prince — one might perhaps say in all Haiti". M20's overview turned the hedge into a country-wide fact. (CORRECTED)

Differences: Wikipedia says the Sams leased it to the Marines, Visit Haiti says it was seized; "from Germany" is Wikipedia only. Address 60 Avenue Christophe (Wikipedia infobox).

Wikipedia's Batraville article: "On January 15, 1920, Batraville was at the head of a band of cacos entering the Bel-Air neighborhood… At least three hundred were killed or captured" — the two figures differ, as M20 says. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beno%C3%AEt_Batraville

11 USS Memphis wrecked 29 Aug 1916 (M20: "by my recollection — UNVERIFIED"; Franck says September and calls her a battleship) CONFIRMED (date resolved) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Memphis_(ACR-10): at anchor off Santo Domingo on 29 August 1916, struck by giant waves, "43 men dead or missing… 204 badly injured"; armored cruiser; sold for scrap 1922; "the last of it did not disappear from the Santo Domingo shoreline until 1938". 13 Monte Cristi: "a near replica of the Eiffel Tower… a large four-faced clock some fifty feet above"; the bill "To one clock and tower, Dr $16,000 Please Remit" UNSUPPORTED (as history) Franck's text is verbatim ("with the sudden coming of the Americans, congress itself succumbed, and the two thousand or so good citizens of the hapless town found themselves face to face with a document… 'To one clock and tower, Dr $16,000 Please Remit'"). 15 La Romana: founded 1897 as an oil town; "one-company town" since the South Porto Rico Sugar Company built the Central Romana mill in 1917; the only sugar operation not taken over by Trujillo CONFIRMED (as Wikipedia states it) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Romana,_Dominican_Republic (raw text — "officially founded in 1897 as an oil town… since the South Puerto Rico Sugar Company built the mammoth Central Romana mill in 1917… the only sugar operation not taken over by Rafael Trujillo"). Facts found that M20 does not have (offered, not applied)

  • The Oloffson stands on Avenue Christophe: Kuser rented a house on Avenue Christophe in 1919–20, and the Oloffson is at 60 Avenue Christophe — the Marine hospital sat on the same avenue as an American visitor's rented house.
  • Monte Cristi's clock (in place 11 March 1895, inaugurated 29 June 1895 per the local article) is tied by that article to Máximo Gómez and José Martí, who were both in Monte Cristi in March 1895 (Martí's manifesto was signed there; he died 19 May 1895) — a Cuba-in-1895 hook for the Havana-centred Golden Circle. The tower stands on Parque Duarte.
  • The Memphis wreck was scrapped in place after 1922 and "did not disappear from the shoreline until 1938" — the same year as the concrete wall on the Ozama fortress.
  • Seabrook's Bellegarde hearing: "twenty-seven peasants testified that at various times between 1916 and 1918 they had been present at human sacrifices" — his hearsay, and the very kind of claim M20 correctly treats with caution.

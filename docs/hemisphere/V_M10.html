A. The ten "Top 10" items and the task's named claims

# Claim Verdict Evidence
1a 1932 US Naval Institute Proceedings ("Some Naval Aspects of the War Debt Question", May 1932, vol. 58/5/351) names Bermuda and Trinidad CONFIRMED (search-summary level; page 403) The article, per the USNI index, says the principal war debtors hold "territorial assets not indispensable to them but valuable to the U.S." — the British Bermuda and Trinidad in the Atlantic, and in the Pacific the French Marquesas and the British Solomon Islands. https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/1932/may/some-naval-aspects-war-debt-question . (M10 omits the Solomons; author not found; the "moderate oil" wording was not verifiable.) It is a naval-service journal, not "the Navy's" official view.
1b Lundeen's "Islands for War Debts" committee, 1938–40, German-funded; Lundeen killed 31 Aug 1940, Flight 19, Lovettsville VA, 25 dead CORRECTED — dates (edited) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Lundeen : he "founded and chaired" the Make Europe Pay War Debts Committee, later the Islands for War Debts Committee (aim: seizing the British West Indies); Viereck "provided substantial financial support", dictated speeches, staff distributed them in huge numbers; Flight 19 near Lovettsville on 31 Aug 1940, all 25 aboard died; aboard were an FBI special agent, a second FBI employee and a Justice Department prosecutor; Hoover denied surveillance. Dates: the committee is 1939–40, not 1938: Prescott Dennett was "paid by German agent George Sylvester Viereck [in 1939] to front (as Treasurer)" the Make Europe Pay committee "and later" the Islands for War Debts committee (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescott_Freese_Dennett); Star Tribune: Lundeen proposed seizing the British West Indies in October 1939 (https://www2.startribune.com/ernest-lundeen-george-sylvester-viereck-nazi-propaganda-world-war-2/600252419/). "1938" belongs to the Foreign Office side: Whitham, "Sore Thumbs and Beachcombers: Britain, the war debt, and the cession of the British West Indies, July 1938–May 1940", JICH 25:3 (1997) 466 — the title confirms M10's Whitham line. Also corrected: "a plane full of federal investigators" (3 of 25); "mailed out by the million" (one Lundeen speech went to 125,000 households — the "millions" is Viereck's whole franking scheme). German funding: supported (Wikipedia) but the money's exact source is not documented in pages read — Medium.
2a Destroyers-for-bases, 2 Sep 1940: 50 destroyers (3 Caldwell, 27 Wickes, 20 Clemson); 99-year rent-free leases; Bermuda and Newfoundland gifts; Churchill's 27 May refusal CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destroyers-for-bases_deal
2b Act of Havana, 21–30 July 1940: Pan-American trusteeship; any state could act alone; "No Transfer" CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havana_Conference_(1940) ("from July 21 to July 30, 1940"; "if a European nation should fall… any of the countries could take over the relevant territories").

C. What the owner should know before using M10

  • The fire-sale anchors hold: the 1932 Naval Institute piece naming Bermuda and Trinidad, Lundeen's committee with a German agent behind it, destroyers-for-bases and the Act of Havana are real. Only Lundeen's dates (1939–40) and the "plane full of investigators" phrasing needed fixing.
  • Bermuda's car ban: it was Wilson's petition (drafted on the island, printed 1 Feb 1908) with Twain beside him — better for the setting than M10's "among the signers".
  • Aruba's price and the Chicago link: Standard Oil of Indiana was the controlling owner from 1925 (majority) to 1932, with a ~$140M sale to Jersey (cash plus stock) — do not quote "$100M".
  • Do not repeat: Capone at Saint-Pierre as fact; "four million bottles"; the 1929 Pelée dome as higher than 1902's; the Obeah Law's £50 publishing penalty.

Top 10 for the owner (the chapter's list, in order)

# Claim Verdict Evidence
T1 Felix Sommerfeld's May 1915 proposal "to create an incident which would provoke a war between the U.S. and Mexico" CONFIRMED Wikipedia: "had proposed to the German government in May 1915 that he could create an incident which would provoke a war between the U.S. and Mexico". (The chapter's quoted wording, "creating an incident…", is a paraphrase.) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_A._Sommerfeld
T1b Sommerfeld's biography: b. 28 May 1879, Schneidemühl; middle-class Jewish family; Chihuahua 1908 as a mining engineer who was really a German informant; chief of Madero's secret service; ~$340,000 of arms to Villa 1915; worked for Boy-Ed; interned June 1918 at Fort Oglethorpe, released 1919; no proof he was behind Columbus CONFIRMED Wikipedia (above) plus search results for von Feilitzsch's In Plain Sight (Literal Magazine, Mexico News Daily).
T2a Bierce: last letter dated Chihuahua, 26 Dec 1913, to Blanche Partington ("As to me, I leave here tomorrow for an unknown destination"); age 71; "stood up against a Mexican stone wall and shot to rags"; "To be a Gringo in Mexico — ah, that is euthanasia" CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrose_Bierce ; https://lithub.com/no-one-knows-why-ambrose-bierce-disappeared-but-here-are-some-theories/ ; the "euthanasia" line via Lapham's Quarterly (search summary).
T2b Bierce theories: Sierra Mojada firing squad (Fr. James Lienert, gravestone 2004); "Tex" O'Reilly's cantina/"two old empty envelopes"; "Ambrosia" dying of pneumonia at Marfa 17 Jan 1914 (Jake Silverstein 2002) CONFIRMED LitHub and Wikipedia, as above.
T2c Bierce theories: "killed at Ojinaga"; Grand Canyon suicide as "Partington's own view"; the Marfa story as a "hitchhiker's tale" UNSUPPORTED Ojinaga is not on either page; Wikipedia credits the Grand Canyon theory to the skeptic Joe Nickell and LitHub to "some scholars"; LitHub says Silverstein found a 2002 letter. Tagged.
T3a McPherson: vanished 18 May 1926 (Venice/Ocean Park beach); reappeared 23 June 1926 at Agua Prieta; found by R. R. Gonzales and his wife; taken to Douglas, AZ; nurse picked cactus spines from her legs and treated a blistered toe; "Steve" and "Rose"; tin-can lid; Ormiston at Carmel as "George E. McIntyre" with "Miss X" (Elizabeth Tovey); DA Asa Keyes dropped the case 10 Jan 1927; Lorraine Wiseman-Sielaff unreliable; two died in the search CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disappearance_of_Aimee_Semple_McPherson ; https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-incredible-disappearing-evangelist-572829/
T3b McPherson's claimed walk "commonly given as ~13 miles" CORRECTED Wikipedia and Smithsonian both say 20 miles. Chapter fixed.
T4a Cantú: governed the northern district of Baja California 1915–1920, appointed by Villa, then nominally Carranza's; ignored federal orders; entertained secession; ousted by Gen. Abelardo L. Rodríguez in July 1920; built the Mexicali–Tijuana road; Rodríguez governor and later the land-owner under Agua Caliente CONFIRMED https://www.bajabound.com/bajaadventures/bajafever/two_leaders (read; thin) ; Agua Caliente page for Rodríguez's land.
T4b The "20 January 1915 telegram from Villa"; the Harry Chandler "conspiracy" UNSUPPORTED Neither is on a page read (the chapter already calls Chandler "a claim, not proven"; telegram tagged). Werne's monograph remains the source to read.
T5 Agua Caliente money-car robbery, 20 May 1929: Cadillac carrying Sunday revenue; guards Nemesio Monroy and José Borrego killed; ~$85,000–86,000 but all but $5,800 in checks, burned; Colson (27), Cochran (24), Kearney (28); Kearney's pocket-knife surgery; Thompson gun never fired, guards shot with .38s, gun dumped off San Pedro; life sentences 6 Aug 1929 (Judge Charles N. Andrews); Colson's moat swim, brother Emil's kegs of nails (July 1933), suicide 1934 in the warden's office CONFIRMED The San Diego Union-Tribune feature (13 Dec 2009), read in full as a PDF: http://www.sandiegoyesterday.com/wp-content/uploads/2010/05/Agua-Caliente-money-car-robbery.pdf. The 1929 police "first machine-gun crime" claim is in the feature and correctly labelled disputed in the chapter.
T6a 1917 bath riots: 28–30 Jan 1917; Carmelita Torres, 17; ~200 women growing to thousands; the 1916 El Paso jail fire in a gasoline bath (28 dead); Mayor Tom Lea; David Dorado Romo, Ringside Seat to a Revolution (2006); cyanide gas and Zyklon B CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1917_Bath_riots ; Zyklon B at the bridge in the 1920s–30s (Romo; Dr Gerhard Peters's 1937 article) via search summaries.
T6b "by the 1920s–30s DDT and Zyklon B were added" CORRECTED DDT's insecticidal properties were discovered in 1939 and it entered military use in 1942–43 (Wikipedia, DDT); at the bridge it belongs to the 1940s. Some web pages repeat the 1920s–30s claim; the chapter did too. Fixed.
T7 Ambos Nogales, 27 Aug 1918: carpenter Zeferino Gil Lamadrid; Customs Inspector Barber; Pvt. Klint's shot; Lt. Col. Herman, 10th Cavalry and 35th Infantry; Mayor Felix Peñaloza, 53, shot from the Arizona side; US dead Klint, Loftus, Lots, Whitworth (plus Capt. Hungerford); Mexican dead 15 official vs 129 graves; three-year-old Julia Medina; ceasefire ~7:45 p.m.; Calles and Cabell; first permanent border fence; the German-agent rumours unsubstantiated CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ambos_Nogales (Wikipedia says a white handkerchief, the chapter a flag on his cane — trivial).
T8 Agua Caliente resort: opened 22 June 1928; ~$10 million; Wirt Bowman and Baron Long (and Crofton); land owned by Abelardo L. Rodríguez; Wayne McAllister, 19; 500 rooms, minaret, Gold Room, airstrip; Cárdenas banned gambling 1935; later a school CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agua_Caliente_Casino_and_Hotel
T9a Niño Fidencio: healing from c. 1925; 4,000+ at Espinazo by 1927; Calles visited 8 Feb 1928 for nodular leprosy; pepper tree, sulphur mud pool; Nuevo León charged him with practising medicine illegally; 1898–1938 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ni%C3%B1o_Fidencio
T9b The Cubilete monument dynamited 30 Jan 1928 (nine days before Calles's visit) CONFIRMED Search summaries of Cristo Rey pages (the Wikipedia page fetch 404'd).
T10a Repatriation, general: 300,000–2 million (estimates vary); 40–60% of voluntary returnees US citizens; ~82,000 formally deported; Doak; Mexico paid for 90,000+ (July 1930–June 1931); California apology 2005; LA County 2012 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Repatriation
T10b "a common figure is 400,000+" CORRECTED Wikipedia: most scholarly estimates run 500,000 to 1 million. Chapter fixed.
T10c Gary/Indiana Harbor: 3,330 left Lake County (250 children; 1,800 Indiana Harbor, 1,500 Gary); fares $49, charity $15 adult / $7.50 child; Judge Maurice E. Crites; Chicago consulate; 1930 census 9,642 Mexicans in Indiana, 9,007 in Lake County CONFIRMED https://indianahistory.org/blog/by-the-train-loads-mexican-repatriation-movement-in-the-midwest-part-i/ (the source's own 1,800 + 1,500 = 3,300, not 3,330 — not the chapter's error).
T11a Plan of San Diego: dated 6 Jan 1915; "Liberating Army of Races and Peoples"; execution of white men over 16 (from 20 Feb 1915); German Americans exempt; signed in a Monterrey jail (Basilio Ramos, captured at Brownsville); Luis de la Rosa, Aniceto Pizaña; 30 raids, 21 Americans killed; Norias Ranch 8 Aug 1915 (five raiders killed); 300+ Mexican Americans killed by federal estimate; Wilson recognised Carranza 19 Oct 1915 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_de_San_Diego
T11b Canales hearing: 19 charges; 31 Jan–13 Feb 1919; force cut from well over 1,000 to 68 (the Loyalty Rangers, ~800 unpaid volunteers, abolished); 300–5,000 dead; Toribio Rodríguez 1912 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Ranger_Division (the Canales page says 1914–19 for the death range; the chapter uses 1910–19 in one entry and 1914–19 in another — both are on Wikipedia).

Corrections applied to M06_border_crime_north.md

  1. McPherson: the "~13 miles" walk → 20 miles (Wikipedia, Smithsonian); the nurse/cactus-spines detail marked as confirmed.
  2. Bath riots: DDT removed from the 1920s–30s (1940s); cyanide gas and Zyklon B stay.
  3. Torreón: the indemnity was never paid (not "never fully paid").
  4. Border Patrol: Mankin's 1924 death was an accidental rifle discharge; Clark (13 Dec 1924) was the first killed by hostile fire; Riley's crash was a broken tie rod, not a pursuit.
  5. Gregorio Cortez: 14 June → 12 June 1901; "roughly two weeks" → about ten days.
  6. Agua Prieta: the searchlights are what Villa believed were American; sources place them on the Mexican side.
  7. Ojinaga: the 8 generals are inside the 3,352 prisoners.
  8. Orozco: he had escaped house arrest in El Paso, not "out on bail".
  9. Bierce: the Ojinaga end, the Grand Canyon theory as "Partington's own view", and the Marfa "hitchhiker's tale" tagged UNVERIFIED (the Marfa date and Silverstein confirmed).
  10. Tayopa: the 1927 Ruggles/Dobie/"Custard" episode tagged UNVERIFIED.
  11. Cantú: the "20 January 1915 telegram from Villa" tagged UNVERIFIED.
  12. Juárez 1919: the "67 soldiers and 27 civilians" split tagged UNVERIFIED.
  13. Caesar salad: "recipe published by Rosa in 1986" tagged (Wikipedia: a 1987 interview).
  14. Repatriation: "a common figure is 400,000+" → most scholarly estimates 500,000 to 1 million.

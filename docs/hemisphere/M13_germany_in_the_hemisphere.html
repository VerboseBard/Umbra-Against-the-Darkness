M13 — Germany in Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean, 1900–1940

Lane: M13 — the real record of German presence, espionage and money in the hemisphere; the raw material for a victorious Kaiserreich contesting (or not) America's hemisphere (ALTERNATE_HISTORY §8.6 / §12.5 — still open; nothing here settles it). Sweep: 2026-09-30 Mexico & Caribbean · Date: 2026-09-30 · Model: Claude Opus 5.5 (research agent)

Overview (31 entries). The real record gives a victorious Kaiserreich a ready-made footprint in the hemisphere, and almost none of it is military. Before 1914 Germany's presence was merchant, shipping and scholarly: Hamburg houses shipped more than two-thirds of Guatemala's coffee (1913) and German planters held two-thirds of the Alta Verapaz and most of the Soconusco; about 210 Germans in Haiti — the figure M04 could not source is in Hans Schmidt's 1971 monograph, as a 1914 State Department estimate of 80% of commercial business — ran its wharf and utilities and financed its revolutions; HAPAG kept its Caribbean headquarters at St Thomas and carried dictators out (Díaz, 1911) and guns in (Huerta, 1914). The navy's record is short and sharp: Admiral Killick blowing up his own ship under Panther's guns (1902), the Venezuela blockade (1902–03) that begat the Roosevelt Corollary — the parent of the setting's Veracruz receivership — and staff plans to invade the United States with a base on Culebra. The 1914–18 secret war in Mexico is richer than M01/M06 had room for: HAPAG's secret supply fleet, Papen studying railway dynamite in Mexico, Rintelen and Huerta on a tapped line in a Fifth Avenue hotel, Witzke and Jahnke (both off or linked to SMS Dresden, the cruiser that took Huerta into exile with Lt. Canaris aboard), a 100 kW German transmitter at Chapultepec/Ixtapalapa ⟨fixed: V_M13 — really a Carranza-government station, 200 kW, built with German engineers and Mexican crews; see the Jahnke entry⟩, Dilger's anthrax — and a Chicago hook: the Witzke cipher was broken by two University of Chicago Chaucer scholars. The 1930s record (SCADTA, Kundt, Dietrich's print shop, Davis's oil barter, Viereck's franking scheme) is mostly Nazi-era and BENDS completely; the useful residue is its shape — airlines, advisers, schools, oil purchases and bought congressmen, not fleets. Part E sets out what that suggests for §8.6 (nothing settled).

Part A — The Kaiser's gunboats: the naval record, 1897–1914 (pre-1916, happened)

(M04 already has the 1897 Lüders affair at Port-au-Prince — SMS Charlotte and Stein, four hours, $20,000, the white flag over the palace. The entries below are the ones it doesn't have.)

The Admiralstab's plans to invade the United States — winter 1897/98 to 1906

  • Where: Berlin (the Admiralstab); on paper, Hampton Roads, Cape Cod, Sandy Hook, Boston, Manhattan — and Culebra, Puerto Rico.
  • What happened: From 1897 to 1903 German staff officers drafted war plans against the United States as staff exercises. Lt. Eberhard von Mantey (aged 28) drew the first in winter 1897–98: a fleet crosses the Atlantic, beats the US Atlantic Fleet in one battle and strikes Hampton Roads, "the most sensitive point". After America's 1898 gains the Kaiser ordered a redesign (March 1899): landings at Cape Cod and Sandy Hook with 100,000 soldiers, 60 warships and 40–60 transports, an advance on Boston and the bombardment of Manhattan to panic civilians. Schlieffen judged 100,000 men "would probably be enough" for Boston but far too few for New York. Wilhelm von Büchsel's Operationsplan III (November 1903) refined the landings (Manomet Point and Rockport, a pincer on Boston) and added a naval base on Culebra, Puerto Rico to threaten the future Panama Canal. Shelved 1906. Largely unknown until Holger Herwig found the full plans in the Freiburg military archive in 1970; the New York Times ran the story in April 1971. ⟨fixed: V_M13 — not wholly unknown before 1970: Alfred Vagts saw related 1900 reports in the 1930s (published 1940), Hubatsch mentioned one in 1958 and Grenville & Young described the 1899/1903 plans in 1966. The Manomet Point/Rockport pincer is Lt. Rebeur-Paschwitz's 1901 proposal, which Büchsel reworked⟩
  • The odd detail: the island the Kaiser's planners wanted — Culebra — is the one the US Navy then used for its own fleet exercises for most of the century.
  • People: Eberhard von Mantey — author of Plan I; Alfred von Schlieffen — the sceptic; Wilhelm von Büchsel — Operationsplan III; Holger H. Herwig — the historian who found it.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_German_plans_for_the_invasion_of_the_United_States
  • Confidence: High for the plans' existence, authors and dates; Medium on the exact troop/ship numbers (Wikipedia summary of Herwig).
  • Divergence check: pre-1916, happened. In the setting the plans are still in the file in Berlin — and Berlin won. Nobody outside Freiburg knows they exist until 1970 in our world; in the setting nobody has to find them.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): a copy of Operationsplan III, Culebra annotated in a later hand, surfaces in a San Juan pawnshop. The Secret Service thinks it is a forgery; FBI Section 13 thinks the annotation's date matters.

The blockade of Venezuela — 9 December 1902 – 19 February 1903

  • Where: La Guaira, Puerto Cabello, Guanta; Fort San Carlos de la Barra at the mouth of Lake Maracaibo.
  • What happened: Venezuela's dictator Cipriano Castro refused to pay foreign debts and damages. Britain and Germany issued ultimatums on 7 December 1902 (Italy's came on 11 December ⟨fixed: V_M13⟩) and began the blockade on 9 December (Germany's formal blockade of Puerto Cabello on 22 Dec, Maracaibo on 24 Dec). German ships: SMS Panther, Falke, Gazelle, Vineta. Gazelle took the Venezuelan gunboat Restaurador at Guanta (11 Dec); Panther took the customs vessel Zamorra at La Guaira; nearly the whole Venezuelan navy was captured in two days. On 17 January 1903 Panther and Falke chased a blockade-runner into the lake mouth; the fort's gunners — Manuel Quevedo and Carlos José Cárdenas, with an 80 mm Krupp gun — hit Panther and drove her off after half an hour. On 21 January ⟨V_M13: Wikipedia's Fort San Carlos article says Vineta arrived on 20 January; civilian dead given as 25 to 40⟩ SMS Vineta came back and shelled the fort for about eight hours, burning it and killing 25 civilians nearby. American opinion turned against Germany; the Washington Protocols (13 Feb 1903) pledged 30% of the customs of La Guaira and Puerto Cabello to the claimants, and the Hague arbitration (22 Feb 1904) gave the blockading powers preferential payment — which the United States opposed. The German claim rested largely on Krupp's Great Venezuela Railway (valued at 60 million marks).
  • The odd detail: the gun that hit the Kaiser's gunboat was made by Krupp — German money built the railway the blockade collected for, and German steel defended the fort against it.
  • People: Cipriano Castro — Venezuela's dictator; Manuel Quevedo, Carlos José Cárdenas — the fort's gunners; George Dewey — commanded the US fleet then manoeuvring at Culebra/Puerto Rico; Theodore Roosevelt — who later claimed he threatened Berlin (historian George C. Herring: "No evidence has ever been discovered of a presidential ultimatum").
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_crisis_of_1902%E2%80%931903 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardment_of_Fort_San_Carlos (via search) · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Panther_(1901)
  • Confidence: High.
  • Divergence check: pre-1916, happened. It is the direct parent of the Roosevelt Corollary (1904) — the doctrine of American customs receiverships that the setting extends to Veracruz's customs house (§29.4, "held by the ledger"). The setting's receivership verb was invented to keep the Kaiser's gunboats out.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Veracruz's American customs collectors are the Roosevelt Corollary made flesh; in a world Germany won, Berlin's lawyers cite the 1904 Hague award (blockaders get paid first) against every American ledger in the hemisphere. A paper war, a century old, that the Golden Circle reads as a map.

The Markomannia incident — Admiral Killick goes down with the Crête-à-Pierrot — 6 September 1902

  • Where: the harbour of Gonaïves, Haiti.
  • What happened: In Haiti's 1902 civil war the gunboat Crête-à-Pierrot, held by Admiral Hammerton Killick for Anténor Firmin's faction, stopped the HAPAG steamer Markomannia and seized the arms and ammunition she was carrying to Firmin's rival Pierre Nord Alexis. Berlin sent the gunboat SMS Panther (Kapitänleutnant Richard Eckermann). On 6 September she found the Crête-à-Pierrot at Gonaïves with most of her crew ashore; after a warning shot Killick ordered his men off, stayed aboard, and blew up his ship's magazine rather than surrender her; Panther finished the wreck with gunfire. Killick died with (per the search summary) four crewmen. The US State Department endorsed the German action, and Haiti's government later thanked Germany. Panther's own guns failed after a few rounds — the forward gun broke, the aft mounting tore the deck.
  • The odd detail: a Haitian admiral who chose to die on his own bridge rather than hand a ship to a German gunboat — thirteen years before the Marines — while Washington applauded the Germans.
  • People: Hammerton Killick — Haitian admiral, Firmin loyalist; Anténor Firmin — anthropologist-politician (De l'égalité des races humaines, 1885 — from memory, UNVERIFIED this session); Pierre Nord Alexis — president 1902–08; Richard Eckermann — Panther's captain.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Panther_(1901) · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_gunboat_Cr%C3%AAte-%C3%A0-Pierrot (via search) · https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d653 (via search; not opened)
  • Confidence: High on the date, ships and outcome (this resolves M04's "from memory — UNVERIFIED" lead: the ship Killick blew up was the Crête-à-Pierrot, the German ship was Panther, and the seized steamer was HAPAG's Markomannia); Medium on the number who died with him.
  • Divergence check: pre-1916, happened.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): Killick's wreck lies in Gonaïves harbour, and so does whatever the Markomannia's crates really held. In Vodou terms a man who went down with his ship is a very particular kind of dead (the waters, Agwe's domain) — let the Haitian reading, the Marines' reading and the truth diverge.

The Ypiranga — the HAPAG liner that took Díaz into exile and brought Huerta his guns — 1911 and 1914

  • Where: Veracruz; Havana; Le Havre; Puerto México (Coatzacoalcos).
  • What happened: The Ypiranga was built in 1908 by Krupp's Germaniawerft at Kiel for the Hamburg-America Line (HAPAG). On 31 May 1911 the fallen dictator Porfirio Díaz and his family boarded her at Veracruz; she called at Havana on 4 June for farewells and landed him at Le Havre (he died in Paris in 1915). In April 1914 she arrived at Veracruz carrying rifles, machine guns and ammunition for Huerta; the report of that cargo is what triggered Wilson's seizure of the port on 21 April (see M01). The United States tried to stop the delivery and backed down; she landed the arms at Puerto México instead (April–May 1914). ⟨fixed: V_M13 — the landing was on 28 May 1914 (SMS Dresden article); after the standoff she also carried 190 refugees, 178 of them German, to Mobile on 9 May (SS Ypiranga article); the cargo was US-made ammunition routed New York–Odesa–Hamburg plus German Mauser rifles and cartridges added at Havana (Ypiranga incident article)⟩ Laid up in Hamburg through the war, seized by Britain on 2 April 1919, renamed Assyria (1921) and Colonial (1929); sank off Scotland under tow to the breakers in September 1950.
  • The odd detail: one German ship sailed away with the old regime (1911) and sailed back three years later with the guns to save the next one — and the Americans let her land them at the next port down the coast.
  • People: Porfirio Díaz — dictator 1876–1911; Victoriano Huerta — the arms' buyer.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Ypiranga
  • Confidence: High on Díaz and the 1914 delivery; the page gave no cargo origin, quantities or landing date (UNVERIFIED — the "Russian rifles bought via an American arms dealer" story is widely told but not read this session). Correction to the lane brief: the Ypiranga did not carry Huerta into exile — the cruiser SMS Dresden did (next entry).
  • Divergence check: pre-1916, happened.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): HAPAG as the hemisphere's German bloodstream — a steamship line that carries dictators out and guns in. In the setting the Ypiranga is never seized in 1919; she is still on the Hamburg–Veracruz run in the 1930s, and the purser remembers both voyages.

SMS Dresden carries Huerta to Jamaica — and Lt. Canaris is aboard — 20–25 July 1914

  • Where: Puerto México (Coatzacoalcos) → Kingston, Jamaica; then Port-au-Prince; later the Pacific and Más a Tierra (Robinson Crusoe Island), Chile.
  • What happened: The light cruiser SMS Dresden (Fregattenkapitän Erich Köhler) arrived off Veracruz on 21 January 1914 and steamed to Tampico on 15 April — she was in Mexican waters through the Tampico Affair and the Veracruz landing. On 20 July 1914, after Huerta's fall, she took Victoriano Huerta, his vice-president Aureliano Blanquet and their families to Kingston, Jamaica, where Britain had granted them asylum; she arrived on the 25th. The next day at Port-au-Prince ⟨V_M13: 26 July per the Dresden article, 27 July per the Karlsruhe article⟩ Köhler swapped ships with Fritz Lüdecke, who had brought the new cruiser Karlsruhe. War came a week later: Dresden fought at Coronel (1 Nov 1914), was the only German warship to escape the Falklands (8 Dec 1914), and was cornered and scuttled at Más a Tierra on 14 March 1915. Her intelligence officer since December 1911 was Lt. Wilhelm Canaris, who negotiated with the British captain, was interned in Chile, escaped in August 1915 under the name "Reed Rosas" with the help of German merchants, and reached Germany in October 1915; in 1916 he ran shipping intelligence and U-boat supply from Madrid.
  • The odd detail: the future chief of German military intelligence was aboard the cruiser that carried a Mexican dictator into exile — and got home on fluent Spanish and a network of German merchants in Latin America.
  • People: Victoriano Huerta — deposed president; Aureliano Blanquet — his vice-president; Erich Köhler — Dresden's captain in Mexico; Fritz Lüdecke — her captain at the end; Wilhelm Canaris — intelligence officer.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Dresden_(1907) · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Canaris
  • Confidence: High.
  • Divergence check: pre-1916, happened (the 1914–15 cruiser war is before the break). BENDS after: Canaris's later career belongs to a Nazi Germany that does not exist in the setting; in a Germany that won, a naval-intelligence officer with Spanish, Latin American contacts and a Madrid U-boat network is exactly the man Berlin sends to the hemisphere in the 1920s.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): "Reed Rosas" as a working name that never retires — a Kaiserliche Marine intelligence officer the Secret Service has on file in Havana, Veracruz and Buenos Aires, always with that alias. Huerta's last voyage makes him the man who knows what the dictator carried.

SMS Karlsruhe — the raider that vanished off Barbados — July–November 1914

  • Where: St Thomas, Danish West Indies (arrived 1 July 1914); north of Watling Island, Bahamas; Puerto Rico; off Brazil; the Atlantic east of Barbados.
  • What happened: The new light cruiser Karlsruhe left Kiel on 14 June 1914 and reached St Thomas on 1 July. On 6 August, about 120 nautical miles north of Watling Island, she met the North German Lloyd liner Kronprinz Wilhelm to arm her as a raider — only two 8.8 cm guns, a machine gun and some sailors had crossed when HMS Suffolk appeared; she outran Suffolk and Bristol and reached Puerto Rico on 9 August almost out of coal. Off northeast Brazil she took or sank sixteen merchantmen (fifteen British, one Dutch; 72,805 GRT). On 4 November 1914, about 18:30, steaming for a raid on Barbados, she was torn apart by a spontaneous internal explosion; ~146 survivors reached her colliers Rio Negro and Indrani. Berlin hid the loss; the Royal Navy kept hunting her until March 1915.
  • The odd detail: for four months the British Caribbean was guarding against a ship that was already at the bottom of the sea.
  • People: Erich Köhler — her captain from the Dresden swap (said to have died in the explosion — from memory, UNVERIFIED).
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Karlsruhe_(1912)
  • Confidence: High.
  • Divergence check: pre-1916, happened. Her base at St Thomas is part of the documented reason America bought the Danish islands in 1917 (§8.3).
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): "the ghost raider" — a German cruiser that exploded for no known reason on the way to shell Barbados. Island folklore has room for a ship that keeps sailing after it sank; FBI Section 13 has room for a cause.

Part B — The secret war in Mexico, 1914–1918 (mostly pre-1916; what comes after BENDS)

(M01 has the Zimmermann Telegram and Rintelen's money in outline; M06 has Felix Sommerfeld, Sam Dreben and the El Paso gun trade. The entries below go under them: the money, the ships, the men and the radio.)

The Hamburg-America Line's secret supply fleet — the Buenz trial — contract 1913; ships August 1914; sentenced December 1915

  • Where: New York (HAPAG's office), Norfolk, New Orleans, San Francisco; at sea off South America and the Pacific islands.
  • What happened: Under a 1913 contract between HAPAG and the German Admiralty "for the provisioning, during war, of German ships at sea, using America as a base", Dr. Karl Buenz, head of HAPAG's New York branch, received Berlin's cable on 31 July 1914 and — with the naval attaché Capt. Karl Boy-Ed — chartered or bought a fleet of neutral-flagged colliers and supply ships and sent them out under false manifests, with supercargoes carrying sealed orders to take command outside the three-mile limit and meet German cruisers by wireless. The 1918 book lists twelve ships and their costs — Thor, Berwind, Lorenzo ($430,182.59), Heina, Nepos, Mowinckel, Unita, Somerstad, Fram, Graecia, Macedonia, Navarra — $1,419,394.49 in all, with the Deutsche Bank putting three payments of $500,000 and a further $750,000 at Boy-Ed's disposal. The Sacramento out of San Francisco did meet Scharnhorst off Más Afuera on 8 November 1914. But "out of twelve shiploads of supplies, only some $20,000 worth were ever transshipped." Buenz and three HAPAG men (Koetter, Hachmeister, Poeppinghaus) were convicted in December 1915; Boy-Ed had diplomatic immunity. They did not begin serving their terms (18 months in Atlanta; a year and a day) until 1918.
  • The odd detail: a million and a half dollars of coal and food for the Kaiser's cruisers — and all but $20,000 of it never reached a German ship.
  • People: Karl Buenz — HAPAG's New York director; Karl Boy-Ed — naval attaché, paymaster, immune; Koetter, Hachmeister, Poeppinghaus — HAPAG men convicted.
  • Sources: https://gutenberg.org/files/58652/58652-h/58652-h.htm (Jones & Hollister, The German Secret Service in America 1914–1918, 1918 — a wartime, anti-German book; its figures come from the trial) · https://www.americanheritage.com/thrifty-spy-sixth-avenue-el (via search)
  • Confidence: High for the scheme and conviction; Medium for the dollar figures (a 1918 propaganda-era book quoting trial exhibits).
  • Divergence check: the 1914 scheme is pre-1916, happened. The 1918 prison terms BEND — they began only because America entered the war; in the setting the HAPAG men's appeals may simply run out the clock.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): HAPAG as a naval auxiliary in merchant paint is exactly the thing the Secret Service would still watch in the 1930s. Every HAPAG purser in Havana or Veracruz is presumed to hold a sealed envelope.

Von Papen in Mexico: the military attaché who studied railway dynamite — July 1914 – December 1915

  • Where: Mexico City (the German colony), the Mexican railways; Washington and New York.
  • What happened: Captain Franz von Papen, military attaché accredited to both Washington and Mexico, was in Mexico in July 1914, "observing, among other matters, the effect of dynamite explosions on railways", according to the 1918 account; he "organized a military unit in the lukewarm German colony in Mexico City" ⟨fixed: V_M13 — the 1918 book gives this as hearsay ("According to Admiral von Hintze"); Wikipedia has Papen in Mexico in spring 1914 selling arms to Huerta, and the book has him there in July 1914, wiring Boy-Ed on 29 July⟩ and "used one or more of the warring factions in the southern republic to test the efficacy of various means of warfare." The same book gives the doctrine in one line: "Mexico must divert America's attention from Europe and remove to the border her available forces." Papen was declared persona non grata and left the United States in December 1915 (declared persona non grata on 3 December 1915 ⟨fixed: V_M13, Wikipedia "Franz von Papen"⟩).
  • The odd detail: the future Chancellor of Germany (in our 1932) learned his trade watching Mexican trains blow up.
  • People: Franz von Papen — military attaché to the US and Mexico; Karl Boy-Ed — his naval counterpart.
  • Sources: https://gutenberg.org/files/58652/58652-h/58652-h.htm
  • Confidence: Medium — a single wartime book; the "military unit in the German colony" is its claim, not independently confirmed.
  • Divergence check: pre-1916 for the Mexican visit, happened. BENDS after: Papen's later career (Chancellor 1932, Hitler's vice-chancellor) belongs to a Weimar and a Nazi state that never exist in the setting. In a victorious Kaiserreich a Westphalian Catholic cavalry officer with Mexican field experience is the obvious minister to Mexico City in the 1920s.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): the "military unit in the German colony" never disbanded. In the 1930s it is a veterans' shooting club in Mexico City with a rifle range, a HAPAG shipping agent as treasurer, and a map room.

Rintelen and Huerta: the restoration plot — April – 27 June 1915

  • Where: New York (the Manhattan Hotel, and "probably the Holland House", Fifth Avenue and 30th Street); Newman, New Mexico; Fort Bliss and El Paso.
  • What happened: Franz von Rintelen landed on 3 April 1915 on a false Swiss passport as "Emil V. Gasche", posed as "Frederick Hansen", and ran sabotage through front companies (the Bridgeport Projectile Company; the Transatlantic Trust Co. at 57 William Street), $500,000 through the "Labor's National Peace Council" to foment munitions strikes, and Dr. Walter Scheele's time-delay incendiary "cigars" — reportedly $10 million of material on 36 ships. Victoriano Huerta, exiled via Jamaica, Britain and Spain, reached New York in April 1915; he and Rintelen negotiated money for weapons and U-boat landings on the Mexican coast, Huerta offering to make war on the United States. US agents tapped and recorded the phone calls. On 27 June 1915 Huerta took the train toward Newman, New Mexico, to meet Pascual Orozco and armed men, and was arrested by an Army colonel for sedition; rearrested 3 July; jailed again 8 December; died 13 January 1916 (cirrhosis or cancer; "poisoning by the US was widely suspected"). Buried in El Paso (Wikipedia: Evergreen Cemetery). Rintelen was caught by the British in August 1915, interned at Donington Hall, extradited, and served time in Atlanta; he wrote The Dark Invader (1933), moved to Britain, opposed the Nazis and died in London 30 May 1949.
  • The odd detail: the ex-dictator's German money talks happened in a Fifth Avenue hotel on a tapped line — the Americans knew the plot in the words of its authors.
  • People: Franz von Rintelen — "the Dark Invader"; Victoriano Huerta — exiled dictator; Pascual Orozco — his general (killed in Texas, Aug 1915 — M06); Walter Scheele — chemist of the incendiary cigars; David Lamar — the "Wolf of Wall Street", the strike money's conduit.
  • Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_von_Rintelen · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoriano_Huerta
  • Confidence: High on the dates and arrest; Medium on the sabotage figures (Wikipedia citing later accounts). (Wikipedia's Rintelen page says Southampton; other accounts say Falmouth — UNVERIFIED.)
  • Divergence check: pre-1916, happened (Huerta dies thirteen days into 1916 — before the break in Wilson). The plot's purpose — "divert America's attention from Europe" — is what the setting's America does on its own; Rintelen's war happens without him.
  • Umbra hook (proposal, not canon): "poisoning by the US was widely suspected" — three layers exactly: the period says Washington poisoned Huerta; folklore says the drink killed him; the file says something else entirely sat at that deathbed in El Paso.

SMS Dresden's lieutenant: Lothar Witzke, alias "Pablo Waberski" — 1916 – 26 September 1923

  • Where: San Francisco; Mare Island Navy Yard; Mexico City; Nogales, Arizona; Fort Sam Houston; Leavenworth.
  • What happened: Lothar Witzke (b. 15 May 1895, Kreis Koschmin, Prussia) was a lieutenant on the cruiser SMS Dresden — the ship that carried Huerta to Jamaica (Part A). After her scuttling he escaped Chilean internment and reached San Francisco in May 1916 as a seaman on the SS Calusa, joining Kurt Jahnke under Consul-General Franz Bopp. US intelligence tied him to the Mare Island munitions explosion (July 1917 per his page); his part in Black Tom (30 July 1916) is disputed. The pair moved to Mexico City after April 1917. He was arrested at 10 a.m. on 1 February 1918 near Nogales, Arizona, travelling as "Pablo Waberski", with a cryptogram sewn into his jacket sleeve; cryptographers working with Herbert Yardley broke it; a military tribunal at Fort Sam Houston sentenced him to death — the setting's point of interest, since he is usually described as the only German spy sentenced to death in the US in the Great War (the page read did not say so — UNVERIFIED). Wilson commuted it to life on 27 May 1920; he was pardoned and released 26 September 1923 and deported to Berlin.
  • The odd detail: the 424-letter cipher sewn into his clothes was broken after three days of continuous work ⟨fixed: V_M13 — Wikipedia and the University of Chicago account; a letter shows Rickert saying she did all the work⟩ by two University of Chicago Chaucer scholars — Professor John Matthews Manly (51, joined MI-8 in October 1917) and his former student Edith Rickert — and it proved that Germany still wanted Mexico in the war. A Chicago hook for the core book.
  • People: Lothar Witzke — naval saboteur; Kurt Jahnke — his chief; Franz Bopp — German consul-general, San Francisco; Herbert Yardley — head of MI-8 (the later "Black Chamber"); John M. Manly and Edith Rickert — the Chicago philologists who broke the cipher (their Text of the Canterbury Tales, 8 vols., 1940).

Hans Kundt — the Prussian general who ran Bolivia's army — 1908 – 12 December 1933

Teoberto Maler — Maximilian's cadet who photographed the Maya cities — 1864 – 22 November 1917

  • Where: Palenque, Chichén Itzá, the Usumacinta sites and the Guatemalan Petén; Mérida, Yucatán.
  • What happened: Teobert (Teoberto) Maler (b. Part E — What the record suggests for §8.6: does Germany contest the hemisphere, or only loom? (for the owner; nothing settled)

  1. The record's own answer is "loom, and buy." Every real German instrument in the region before 1914 was commercial (coffee houses, HAPAG, hardware, breweries, utilities, loans to revolutionaries) or scholarly (Seler, Sapper, Maler, Dieseldorff); the navy acted only to collect debts or protect a ship (Charlotte/Stein 1897, Panther 1902, the Venezuela blockade 1902–03), and Berlin shelved its invasion plans in 1906. The 1930s pattern (SCADTA, Kundt, Davis, Viereck) is the same: airlines, advisers, oil purchases and bought legislators. A victorious Kaiserreich that took Britain's purse, not Britain's fleet (§30.1) would do more of this, not something different.
  2. Three places where "loom" could become "contest" without a war (proposals):
    • The Veracruz ledger. The Roosevelt Corollary was invented to keep German gunboats from collecting debts; the Hague award of 1904 gave blockaders priority. A Germany with claims on the rump Mexican state can dispute the American receivership in court and in the press — a legal war over the customs house.
    • Unceded Mexico's patron. Carranza really bought German radio (1917–19); the German legation really backed the Gold Shirts (M01); Germany really bought Mexico's nationalised oil (1938). In the setting all three are aimed at an American occupier: German advisers, German radio, German oil purchases for Mexico City — the Zimmermann offer paid in kind rather than in Texas.
    • Outside the American map, inside the Circle's. Guatemala's Alta Verapaz, Colombia's SCADTA and Venezuela sit inside the 1,200-mile Havana circle but outside the setting's American holdings. That is where a German presence can grow unchecked — and where the Golden Circle's map and the Kaiser's commerce collide.
  3. Where German people live inside American rule (so the question is local, not strategic): Haiti's ~210 Germans, never interned; HAPAG's St Thomas station under the US flag since 1917; the Germano-Haitian graduates of the 1912 Imperial school; the brewers of an American Monterrey; the Mennonites (if they come) of an American Chihuahua. These are the setting's likeliest scenario seeds — German subjects with Hamburg letters of credit in occupied towns, whom no American law can intern.
  4. What bends for the real people: no internments (Haiti 1918, Guatemala 1919/1941, Mexico 1942), no blacklists, no 9 November 1918 (Ballin lives, HAPAG keeps its fleet), Strasbourg stays German (Sapper stays), no Nazi careers for Papen, Canaris, Jahnke — each becomes a Kaiserreich professional available for the hemisphere.

Corrections applied to M05_puerto_rico_canal_nicaragua.md

All marked ⟨fixed: V_M05⟩ or [UNVERIFIED — V_M05] in the text; nothing else was changed.

  1. Emden — Puerto Barrios → Puerto San José in the overview, the Ubico entry (with the parade/German Club detail), its divergence line and Part F question 6.
  2. D. Hamilton Jackson — strike moved from January 1915 to 24 Jan–26 Feb 1916; the newspaper (1 Nov 1915) came first; heading, confidence and divergence lines adjusted; the unconfirmed "dozen estates/Bethlehem factory" detail tagged.
  3. Cane strike — the 75 cents → $1.50 doubling tagged UNVERIFIED (twice); $1.50 was the demand.
  4. Trincado — "Argentine" → Spanish-born (Cintruenigo), founded EMECU in Buenos Aires.
  5. Albizu Campos — the "guards wrapped his sores" origin of "Rey de las Toallas" replaced with what the page says.
  6. Fireburn statue — "largest statue of a Black woman" → Denmark's first public monument to a Black woman.
  7. St. Thomas synagogue — NRHP/NHL date 25 Sep 1997.
  8. Butler — quote attributed to the November 1935 Common Sense statement; the Tampico sentence added; the longer wording and the confidence line tagged.
  9. Tags: Sultan's "three times longer" route; the four-Senate-votes note updated as confirmed; the ONI/Ken/Ochi paragraph; the Cristo Negro 1658/1674/1776 dates; the Sandino "Blood Pact" meeting.

Notes for the owner

  • Sandino cluster is solid: the Tampico refinery job (a Standard Oil refinery, per Wikipedia), the Magnetic-Spiritualist school (EMECU), the Managua quake with Sultan's survey party on the ground, and the missing body all check out. Only the "Blood Pact" detail rests on a single Nicaraguan chronicle.
  • Ponce: the toll is exactly 19 (17 civilians + a policeman + a Guardsman), 200+ wounded; use those numbers and the 13 May/22 May Hays dates.
  • Butler's own words tie Tampico to the empire ("made Mexico and especially Tampico safe for American oil interests in 1914") — a line that sits naturally with the setting's Tampico enclave.

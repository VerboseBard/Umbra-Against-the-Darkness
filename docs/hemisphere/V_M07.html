T1 Chicago's Catholic Church Extension Society represented Mexico's exiled bishops from 1914; Kelley's letter to Wilson, 23 Feb 1915 CONFIRMED FRUS 1915 d1511: letter of 23 February 1915 from Francis C. Kelley, President of the Extension Society, "to the President"; the quotes are exact ("made antireligious and persecuting"; "liberty of conscience"; "Economic and agrarian questions … were only side issues"; Villa "now realizes the mistakes"; "nothing is to be expected of Carranza"; the exiled bishops "do not ask, nor do they want, American intervention"). https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1915/d1511 · Wikipedia (Kelley "represented the bishops of Mexico" at the Paris conference; Bishop of Oklahoma 1924; born Prince Edward Island; d. 1 Feb 1948; >3 million subscribers to Extension) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Kelley
T1b Extension Society "moved to Chicago from Lapeer in 1906" CORRECTED January 1907: "In January, 1907, the headquarters of the society were moved to Chicago" (Catholic Encyclopedia) and Wikipedia's Catholic Extension page. Founding 1905 with Quigley CONFIRMED. https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14078a.htm · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Extension. Fixed in the entry (two places).
T1c The Book of Red and Yellow (Chicago, 1915) is a record of outrages; Kelley's Blood-Drenched Altars is 1935 CONFIRMED Library of Congress / Internet Archive / Biblio: published by the Extension Society, Chicago, 1915, "an account of the outrages alleged … against Catholics in Mexico". Blood-Drenched Altars: Bruce, Milwaukee, 1935 (first edition). (Wikipedia's Kelley page says 1933 — that is Wikipedia's error, not the chapter's.)
T1d Castroville seminary: opened Jan 1915, funded largely by Kelley's Extension Society, sponsored by Shaw, Drossaerts and Quigley, 108 students, 59 ordained by May 1918, closed May 1918; revived Sept 1929 with 24 students, Missionaries of the Holy Spirit, $25 a month, San Luis Potosí / Mérida / Saltillo, closed July 1930 CONFIRMED Every figure matches the Handbook of Texas entry. https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/seminary-of-st-philip-for-mexican-students
T2 The Archbishop of Morelia's Chicago exile (single source) — Ruiz y Flores lived in Chicago after 1914 CONFIRMED (now two pages, not independent) DHIAL, verbatim: "en esa ciudad lo sorprendió la persecución iniciada por la revolución carrancista en 1914 y durante algún tiempo tuvo que irse a vivir a la ciudad de Chicago." Spanish Wikipedia repeats it ("for some time he had to go live in the city of Chicago") and lists exiles in 1914, 1926 and 1932–37 — it very probably derives from DHIAL. No English source and no address/dates found; no primary source. Treat as attested, not corroborated. https://dhial.org/diccionario/index.php/RUIZ_Y_FLORES,_Leopoldo · https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopoldo_Ruiz_y_Flores
T3 Bishop Pascual Díaz laid the cornerstone of Our Lady of Guadalupe, South Chicago, 14 April 1928 CONFIRMED Sun-Times page reproduces the caption "South Chicago church and Mexican center dedicated by bishop of Tabasco" and states that Rt. Rev. Pascual Díaz, Bishop of Tabasco, laid the stone on 14 April 1928. https://chicago.suntimes.com/2021/10/1/22698907/our-lady-of-guadalupe-chicago-history
T3b …"who was living in New York after his expulsion" UNSUPPORTED Neither the caption nor Wikipedia nor Encyclopedia.com gives his residence (Encyclopedia.com: "much of his exile was spent in the U.S."; exiled January 1927). Tagged.
T3c Díaz "a Jesuit of full Huichol descent, born in Zapopan" CORRECTED Born Zapopan (22 June 1876 per Wikipedia and catholic-hierarchy; Encyclopedia.com gives 1875), Jesuit, of "Huichol indigenous descent"; nothing says "full". "Fourteen months later, sign the arreglos" is loose — the bishops negotiated with Portes Gil on 12–13 June 1929 and the pact was announced 21 June (DHIAL); changed to "take part in". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascual_D%C3%ADaz_y_Barreto
T3d Díaz "Archbishop of Mexico (installed 22 June 1929)" CORRECTED catholic-hierarchy: appointed 25 June 1929, installed 28 June 1929 (22 June is his birthday). Wikipedia: 25 June. https://www.catholic-hierarchy.org/bishop/bdiazy.html. Died 19 May 1936 CONFIRMED.
T4 The Sept 1928 South Chicago march of 500 CONFIRMED (exists) / UNSUPPORTED (date, bishop) The passage — "some 500 parishioners of the Mexican Church of Our Lady of Guadalupe listened as a visiting bishop railed against the anticlerical leaders of their homeland, then marched, singing and waving religious placards, to their newly built, larger parish church" — is from Julia G. Young, "Cristero Diaspora…", Catholic Historical Review 98 (2012) 271–300 (search abstract; Gale and Project MUSE pages blocked). The two Visualizing-the-Cristero-War pages the chapter cites do not contain it, nor the date "23 September 1928"; the bishop is unnamed. Date tagged. Note the phrase "newly built" fits a church finished by 1928.
T4b Dedication by Cardinal Mundelein on 30 Sept 1928, ~8,000 served; mission July 1923; first Claretian Oct 1924; army-barracks chapel at 9024 S. Mackinaw; land bought 1926; Fr. Tort pastor from 1925 CONFIRMED (with a conflict) SE Chicago Historical Society: photograph "Our Lady of Guadalupe dedication ceremony", dated 30 September 1928 (CC BY-NC-SA, Cordero donors). Dominican University / parish histories (search summaries): "dedicated by Cardinal George William Mundelein on September 30, 1928"; land bought fall 1926. Shrine of St Jude page: mission July 1923, first Claretian October 1924, barracks "transported from Michigan", Tort pastor 1925 (Wikipedia's Shrine article says Tort became pastor in 1927). The Sun-Times page says 1930 for the dedication (and "8,000"), contradicting the dated photograph and Young's "newly built" — the chapter already flags this. https://www.sechicagohistory.org/archive/browse/our-lady-of-guadalupe-dedication-ceremony-2/ · https://shrineofstjude.org/the-shrine/history-of-our-lady-of-guadalupe-church/
T5 The 1927 San Antonio plot / Tenorio (Robert E. Lee Hotel; Bustos; Lainé; Manríquez; 6 Aug crossing; suitcase; worn-out nags; surrender 7 Aug; two shot; DOJ men; Torreón; vanished; Dec 1927 inquiry; "alarm or scandal") CONFIRMED Read in full in Young's Introduction (publisher's preview): Tenorio, 36, of Del Rio; neighbour Fr. T8 The apostolic delegate deported through Tampico — "17 October 1932" CORRECTED The TIME piece is dated 17 October 1932 (its issue date) and says the Chamber's vote was "the night before" he was seized; the Chamber voted on 3 Oct (Wikipedia) and DHIAL has him "arrested and expelled on 4 October 1932". Heading and content fixed. Everything else confirmed on TIME: "pernicious foreigner", pilot Floyd Clevenger, plane turned back over the mountains, overnight in Tampico, "special train (engine and one car) guarded by 30 soldiers", border "close to midnight", "a group of priests and U.S. officials" received him, third exile. https://time.com/archive/6750156/religion-third-exile/
T9 Mount Cristo Rey: Costa's vision "25 October 1933"; crosses 13 Feb / 25 Mar 1934; Soler 1937; 29-ft limestone; 29 Oct 1939; dedicated 1940 CORRECTED (one date) Historic El Paso (the cited page): Costa "envisioned the monument on October 28, 1933" and made the trek with sixteen others; wooden cross 13 Feb 1934, iron cross blessed 25 Mar 1934, Soler commissioned 1937 (arrived 4 Oct), Austin-quarry limestone, begun 24 May 1939, "ready in time for pilgrimage on October 29, 1939", dedicated 1940. Other accounts give 25 October — the chapter's date is not wrong, just contested; heading and text changed to show both. Cubilete Christ dynamited 30 Jan 1928 CONFIRMED (Mexican and Catholic sources). https://historicelpaso.com/historic_entry/mt-cristo-rey/
T10 Shrines: San Juan de los Lagos (1543 image, titzingueni, 1623 acrobat's child, church 1732/1769/1790); Chimayó (Abeyta c. 1810, 1813 petition, 1816, tons of dirt, 1929 sale) CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_San_Juan_de_los_Lagos · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Santuario_de_Chimayo (permission was granted in 1814; the chapter's "asked leave 15 Nov 1813" is consistent). Notes for the owner

  • Weakest remaining items (all tagged in the chapter): the 23 Sept 1928 date and the bishop's name for the South Chicago march; Díaz living in New York in April 1928; the four hours at Ulúa; the Chicago residence of Ruiz y Flores (real but only one scholarly dictionary and its echo). If you want any of these as a plot fact rather than colour, the source to pull is Young's Catholic Historical Review article (2012) and The New World (Chicago) for 23–30 Sept 1928.
  • The dedication year of the South Chicago church is contested between the Sun-Times page (1930) and the dated photograph plus Young's "newly built" (1928). The cornerstone (14 Apr 1928) is solid either way.
  • The chapter's date for the deportation of the apostolic delegate (17 Oct) was TIME's issue date; the event is 4 Oct 1932. Any GM handout built from the old heading would have been two weeks off.

M17 — What the American papers said: a dated headline calendar of Mexico, Cuba, Haiti, Nicaragua and the Caribbean as Midwestern readers saw them, 1914–1937 (+ the March 1938 oil expropriation)

Written 2026-09-30. 21 events, 110+ dated headlines, Apr 1914 – Mar 1938.

  • Lane: M17 (Mexico/Caribbean sweep of 2026-09-30); method follows R21/R21b of the Chicago sweep (..\2026-09-26_chicago_great_lakes\R21b_headline_calendar_continued.md).
  • Model: Claude Sonnet 5.5 (research agent). No GPU, no git.
  • Method: Hoosier State Chronicles (Indiana State Library's Veridian site, newspapers.library.in.gov) — the same site that worked for R21b — this time through its full-text search (date-windowed) plus the per-page OCR feed (?a=d&d=<DOCID>&f=XML, <PageTextHTML>). The search let me go straight to each event's days instead of walking every front page. Helper scripts (hsearch.py, f.py, h2.py, g.py, copied from the R21b hsc.py) live in the session scratchpad m17\ folder; polite ~1–2 s spacing, no 429s hit, no retries looped.
  • Which papers (all Midwestern; the reader is a small-city Hoosier, not a New Yorker):
    • IPT = Indianapolis Times (Scripps-Howard, United Press wire) — on the site 1920–1952, so it carries everything from Carranza (1920) on;
    • INN = Indianapolis News (1916–17 items); SBNT = South Bend News-Times (1914–21); DCDD = Decatur Daily Democrat (Decatur, Adams Co.; 1914–38, unusually clean OCR); RPD = Richmond Palladium; DT = Daily Tribune (Terre Haute); GH = Greencastle Herald; TDB = The Daily Banner (Greencastle); LPLTWO = Ligonier Banner; NAN = Nappanee Advance-News; WLKI = Walkerton Independent; JCDC = Jasper County Democrat and EVRP = Evening Republican (Rensselaer; article-level records — no page number given because the site indexes them as articles).
  • Line format: - <date> — "<headline verbatim>" — <newspaper, page> — <1-line gist> — <URL>. The URL is the page actually read this session (?a=d&d=<DOCID>; open it to see the page scan and OCR). Headlines are quoted as printed from the OCR; obvious OCR breakage silently repaired (letter swaps, stray punctuation); doubtful readings marked [OCR]; a line with no recoverable headline is marked [paraphrase]. Deck lines (sub-heads) are quoted inside the gist where they add something. Anything in "(context: …)" is mine, not the paper's.
  • Coverage honesty: the site is Indiana-only, so "Midwestern" = Indiana (with the Chicago dateline where a wire item touches Chicago). The indexed text is uneven: the Ponce massacre (21 Mar 1937) and Father Pro's execution (23 Nov 1927) barely register in it — that is itself reported below as a finding, but it may also be a search-index gap. Chronicling America (LOC) was not used (R21 hit its rate limit).
  • Divergence note (applies to the whole file): the setting's record — America never enters the Great War; troops cross into northern Mexico in 1916 and win by 1917; the treaty cedes the mineral north (Sonora copper, Chihuahua silver, Coahuila coal, Monterrey steel) and the Tampico oil-coast enclave and puts Americans in Veracruz's customs house; the Marines who landed in Cuba never leave; Haiti/DR/Nicaragua/Puerto Rico/the Canal Zone/Guantánamo are American; Wood governed Havana and is president 1921–27; the Golden Circle's map is centred on Havana — means everything before 1916 holds, and almost everything after BENDS. Each event below carries a one-line Divergence: tag. Still open in the setting (not settled here): the Caribbean's exact status; whether Germany contests the hemisphere; Villa's fate; who governs the ceded north; the treaty's name.
  • Respect: the papers' own framing of Mexicans, Haitians, Cubans, Nicaraguans and Vodou is often racist or propagandistic; it is quoted as the paper's, never as fact. Folklore is labelled as what the paper claimed; the living religions (Vodou, Catholic folk devotion, etc.) are not the villains — the headlines are the exhibit.
  • Umbra use lines are proposals only (one per event), not canon.

The setting's Marines never leave — a Wirkus figure is a natural Golden Circle contact.

Mexico closes its churches (Jul 1926), a priest is shot (Nov 1927), and the settlement (Jun 1929)

  6. "WATCH FOR VILLA'S HEAD" — Indianapolis Times, 12 Feb 1926 (p23) — Chicago criminologists, a millionaire's expedition, and federal agents — a real headline that sounds like a case file.
  7. "In the Long Run, We Must Decide on Hands Off in Haiti, or Absolute Possession" — Indianapolis Times, 9 Dec 1929 (p4) — the whole American Caribbean question in the paper's own voice.
  8. "'White Emperor' of Voodoo Isle Tells How He Ruled La Gonave" — Decatur Daily Democrat, 1 Apr 1931 (p4) — "Sixteen Years a Marine, Four Years a King" — the occupier as monarch, sold as adventure.
  9. "SANDINO" — "neither saint nor devil… the cut-throat bandit painted in lurid colors by American officers and diplomats forced to justify our imperialistic intervention" — Indianapolis Times, 24 Feb 1934 (p4) — the period's own dissent, printed by a Scripps-Howard paper.
  10. "1000 HAITIANS REPORTED SLAIN" → "Alleged 'Massacre' of 500" — Indianapolis Times, 6 Nov and 15 Nov 1937 — five weeks late, then halved and put in quotation marks: how a massacre was handled by the wire.

Runners-up: "Police Aid Wounded After Riot" (DCDD, 26 Mar 1937 — Ponce shown as police casualties); "Four Executed For Plot Against Obregon" (DCDD, 23 Nov 1927 — a priest's execution as filler); "U. S. MARINES IN NICARAGUA TEN YEARS FORECAST" (IPT, 3 Feb 1927); "U. S. Shakes Sugar Stick at Machado" (DCDD, 14 Aug 1933); "'White Zombie' Gets Under Way at the Palace" (IPT, 3 Aug 1932).

Leads not chased

  • The 1919–20 Haitian Cacos / Péralte (Oct–Nov 1919) — search returned nothing in the Indiana index for the date window; try "Charlemagne" or "Peralte" as OCR variants.
  • The Cuban Race War of 1912 and the 1917 "Chambelona" revolt with Marines — before/after the priority list; not searched.
  • Zapata's death (Apr 1919) — IPT starts 1920; other Indiana titles cover 1919 but were not searched.
  • Chicago's Mexican colony (Back of the Yards / South Chicago) and the Chicago Cuban and Puerto Rican press — the Indiana papers show Chicago mainly through wire datelines; a separate Chicago-sources lane would be needed.
  • Seabrook's The Magic Island reviews (1929) and the Wirkus book (1931) — only the feature/brief clippings were read.
  • Cristero-war incidents in the Chicago Catholic press (The New World) — not on Hoosier State Chronicles.
  • The Hoover / Roosevelt "Good Neighbor" front pages (1933–34) for Cuba, Haiti, Nicaragua together — one thematic sweep would complete the calendar.

The five errors worth knowing about

  1. Haiti's 1935 decree-law was NOT "repealed only in June 2012". It was repealed by Article 297 of the 1987 Constitution (which names "the decree law of September 5, 1935 on superstitious beliefs"). Ramsey's book frames the bans as 1835–1987. The 2012 amendment (19 June 2012) is reported to have removed Article 297 itself — a different act. "Kept in the statute for 77 years" becomes 52 years. (WRONG)
  2. Mérida's replacement Christ was blessed on 24 September 1919, the fourth anniversary of the sack — not 28 February 1919. (WRONG)
  3. Tilma: Excélsior's "30 July 1926 – 30 July 1927" is not a typo. It is the first year of custody in Murguía's house; the wardrobe was then moved (15 July 1927, per ACI Prensa/Infovaticana) to the Murguía family bookstore on 16 de Septiembre, and the tilma returned on 28 June 1929. (CORRECTED)
  4. Rodolfo Elías Calles governed Sonora 1931–1935, not 1931–34 (he took leave in 1935 to become Cárdenas's Secretary of Agriculture; Ramón Ramos succeeded). (CORRECTED)
  5. Tapachula's San Agustín was closed by the federal government in June 1932, but the petitions to make it a school were denied — there was no plan carried out. Ex Teresa was closed to worship in 1930, not "until" 1930. (CORRECTED)

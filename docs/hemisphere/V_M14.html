A. The Top-10 items and the task's named claims

# Claim Verdict Evidence
1a The 1st Illinois (organised Chicago 14 Sept 1874; Spanish War, Cuba; federalised 26 June 1916, San Antonio, mustered out 4 Oct 1916) and Illinois's boast that it was the first Guard unit to reach the border CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/131st_Infantry_Regiment_(United_States): "organized 14 September 1874 in Chicago … Mustered into Federal service at Springfield, 13 May 1898 as 1st Illinois Volunteer Infantry; served in Cuba … Mustered into Federal service 26 June 1916 for Mexican Border and stationed at San Antonio, Texas; mustered out 4 October 1916." The boast ("Illinois, which did not have a ready mobilization site, used its state fairgrounds … the first state to get a National Guard unit to the Mexican border") is from the Army's centennial article and the Iowa State study, as M14 says; it remains Illinois's own claim, not an independent record.
1b The 7th Illinois from the Hibernian Rifles, federalised 27 June 1916, out 26 Jan 1917 CONFIRMED (one lineage conflict flagged) The Army lineage on https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/122nd_Field_Artillery_Regiment: "Constituted 21 June 1893 in the Illinois National Guard as the 7th Infantry Regiment and organized at Chicago from the Hibernian Rifles, a former civilian-military organization … Mustered into Federal service 27 June 1916; mustered out of Federal service 26 January 1917." But it then converts the 7th into the 108th Ammunition Train and Supply Train (12 Oct 1917), while the 132nd Infantry article says that regiment drew on the 2nd and 7th — M14's "became part of the 132nd" is flagged as disputed.
1c The 1st Illinois Field Artillery "kept the same dates, 27 June 1916 – 26 January 1917" UNSUPPORTED (edited) Those dates are the 7th Infantry's on the page M14 cites. The same page's annex has the 1st Illinois Cavalry federalised 27 June 1916 and mustered out 17 Nov 1916 at Fort Sheridan, its Chicago elements converted into the 2nd Field Artillery (later 122nd) only in June 1917. There is no 1916 border entry for a "1st Illinois Field Artillery". So the "four Chicago units" are firmly three (1st Infantry, 7th, 8th) plus the 1st Cavalry.
2 The 8th Illinois: raised 1898 after Black Chicagoans were shut out; Gov. Tanner; Col. John R. Marshall; Cuba; armory; federalised 30 June 1916, out 27 Oct 1916; San Antonio and the Rio Grande; white troops refused to salute; "only regiment with a complete complement of Black commissioned officers, second lieutenant to colonel" CONFIRMED except the source date of the last clause (CORRECTED, edited) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/178th_Infantry_Regiment_(United_States): "federalized on 30 June 1916, in Springfield … discharged from service at Springfield on 27 October 1916". Audacy (Illinois Guard historian Adriana Schroeder): Tanner contacted Col. Marshall; armory "the first facility to be built in honor of an African American unit"; in 1916 the 8th "ended up patrolling the border south along the Rio Grande in Texas". Chicago Reader (https://chicagoreader.com/city-life/they-thought-he-was-an-agitator/): "The Eighth Regiment was mobilized to San Antonio in 1916. There, white soldiers refused to salute their superior officers." The "complete complement … only Lt. Col. James H. Johnson and Major William H. Roberts were white" sentence in the Western Front Association article (https://www.westernfrontassociation.com/world-war-i-articles/2008/may/the-370th-infantry-chicagos-8th-illinois-national-guard-in-ww1/) sits in its July 1917 call-up section (the Roberts named there is the surgeon; Thomas A. Roberts, a white colonel, replaced Denison in 1917) — M14 places it on the 1916 reporting day.
2b Col. Franklin A. Denison: born San Antonio 1862; Lincoln University; valedictorian, Union College of Law, 1890; Chicago's first Black assistant prosecutor; retired as brigadier general 1922; d. 1932; named by the Bureau of Investigation as "the chief individual agitator" of the 1919 riot CONFIRMED Chicago Reader (above): all of it, including "assistant prosecuting attorney in Chicago, the first person of color to hold that position" (a year after 1890), "At his retirement in 1922, he was elevated to brigadier general. After he died in 1932, 6,000 crowded the Eighth Regiment Armory".
3 Patton at San Miguelito, 14 May 1916: three cars, ten troopers, two civilian guides; Holmdahl; Cárdenas and two others killed; bodies on the hoods; notch; spurs now in the Museum of World War II CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_C%C3%A1rdenas: "three cars with ten additional American troopers and two civilian guides"; "an ex-Villista named E.L. Holmdahl" who "actually fired the last shot that killed the wounded Cárdenas"; "strapped Cárdenas to the hood of his car (along with two other dead villistas)"; "put a notch in his gun … spurs … now in the Museum of World War II". Cárdenas is described there as "head of his personal bodyguard"; the page does not say "Dorados" or give Holmdahl's rank (the Holmdahl page's infobox says "Colonel (Mexico)" — M14's "major" is unconfirmed).
4 Carrizal, 21 June 1916: Troops C and K, 10th Cavalry; Boyd, Morey, Adair; 23 enlisted men + one interpreter prisoners; returned at the El Paso bridge about ten days later; NARA "seven enlisted killed" vs 12 total; Morey's "hole" note CONFIRMED; note wording not re-verified https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Carrizal (Boyd and Adair killed; "12 killed 28 wounded 24 captured"); NARA Prologue (https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/1997/winter/mexican-punitive-expedition-2.html): "twenty-three enlisted men and one civilian interpreter" captured, "Seven enlisted men were killed"; search results quoting the regimental history: prisoners "returned to U.S. custody ten days later at El Paso". Morey's "hiding in a hole" text: USNI returned 403 and the searches did not surface the wording — stays as M14 has it (Medium).
5 Holmdahl and the $5,000 Chicago buyer: b. 1883 Iowa, d. 1963; pardoned July 1917; arrested at Parral 1926 for Villa's head; a 1955 letter; Skull and Bones version later retracted CONFIRMED (rumour label correct) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Lewis_Holmdahl (b. 26 Aug 1883 Fort Dodge, Iowa; d. 8 April 1963 Van Nuys; "In 1926 … federal police arrested Holmdahl and a companion and charged them with having vandalized Pancho Villa's grave and taken his head"; pardon "July 1917"; "the suspicion remains that he stole the head for an American customer"). The Chicago detail: https://mexicounexplained.com/bring-head-pancho-villa/ — "the 1955 letter from L. M. Shadbolt to author Haldeen Braddy": in an El Paso hotel in 1927 Holmdahl "told him he was being paid $5,000 for the skull by some buyer in Chicago". Braddy's 1960 Western Folklore essay adds that Holmdahl told friends "scientists in Chicago" paid $5,000. The Skull and Bones story is Ben F. Williams's 1984 memoir; Alexandra Robbins retracted similar claims in 2002. Still one hearsay letter.
6 Expatriation Act of 1907 (2 March 1907): §2 grounds (foreign naturalisation, foreign oath); §3 marriage; "citizenship could be lost only in peacetime"; §2 repealed by the Nationality Act of 1940 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriation_Act_of_1907: "provided that citizenship could be lost only in peacetime" (Secretary of State Hamilton Fish, 1870s: wartime loss would "afford a cover to desertion and treasonable aid to the public enemy"); §2 "repealed by Section 504 of the Nationality Act of 1940". The page has nothing on foreign military service, as M14 says.
7 The 1914 Red Cross "Mercy Ship": SS Red Cross (ex-Hamburg), sailed 12 Sept 1914, ~170 surgeons and nurses, Capt. Armistead Rust, units to Paignton, Pau, Kiev, Kosel and Gleiwitz (Germany), Vienna and Budapest; American-born staff only; special train to Berlin CONFIRMED Search results quoting the Red Cross history (https://www.redcross.org/content/dam/redcross/National/history-wwi.pdf) and GG Archives (https://www.ggarchives.com/OT/Ships/RedCross.html): "previously named Hamburg (1899-1914)", sailed Saturday 12 Sept 1914, "170 surgeons and nurses", "Paignton, England; Pau, France; Kiev, Russia; Kosel and Gleiwitz, Germany; and Budapest and Vienna", commanded by "Captain Armistead Rust, U.S.N. (Retired)". The special train and the American-born rule were not re-read.
7b Helen Scott Hay CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Scott_Hay: b. 6 Jan 1869 near Lanark, Illinois; Illinois Training School for Nurses 1895; nursing superintendent at Cook County Hospital 1906–12; "Matron of the American Red Cross hospital in Kiev" 1914–15; Bulgarian nursing school; Gold Cross of St. Anna; Florence Nightingale Medal; d. 25 Nov 1932 Savanna. (The page has nothing on the SS Red Cross.)
8 Edward Mandell Stone's Chicago birth; first American killed; wounded 15 Feb, died 27 Feb 1915 CORRECTED — born 5 January 1888 (not 1887), and Chicago is confirmed (edited) The Harvard memoir text, as reproduced at https://www.uswarmemorials.org/html/people_details.php?PeopleID=1944: "born January 5, 1888, at Chicago, Ill., the third son of Henry Baldwin Stone, of the Harvard Class of 1873"; the father went from a Waltham cotton mill to the Chicago, Burlington & Quincy at Aurora, became general manager of the Burlington system and later president of the Chicago Telephone Company. Service, wounding at Craonne 15 Feb 1915, death at Romilly on 27 Feb 1915, Buenos Aires secretary — all match (https://doughboy.org/first-american-to-die-in-the-great-war-remembering-edward-mandell-stone-french-foreign-legion/, which also has the classmate quotation, written by Rudolph Altrocchi '08 in the Harvard Crimson). M14's "Search summaries … 5 January 1887" was wrong on the year; the "Medium on the Chicago birth" flag is now High.
9 Parral, 12 April 1916 and Elisa Griensen CONFIRMED except "married in 1928" (CORRECTED, edited) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Parral: "eight officers and 120 men of Troops K and M, 13th Cavalry", 85 miles in 50 hours, Lozano, ~550 Carrancistas, two killed and six wounded "including Tompkins", 14 to 70 Mexican losses, 10th Cavalry "Just before 8:00 am", Santa Cruz de Villegas. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisa_Griensen: b. 2 Jan 1888 Parral; Alsatian father, Chihuahuan mother; the flag, the school and the Mauser; former pupils' stones-and-car version; "Chasing Villa makes no mention of Griensen"; d. 14 Nov 1972 Ciudad Juárez; "on vacation" from studies in the United States. But the page says she "married Óscar Martínez, a civil engineer who graduated from the Heroic Military Academy in 1928" — 1928 is his graduation, not her marriage.
10 Campbell of Kenilworth: killed 1 Oct 1917 in an engagement with three German aircraft north of Soissons near Pargny; parents on Kenilworth Avenue; a stunting pilot CONFIRMED Search results quoting the Wilmette Public Library local-history record (https://history.wilmettelibrary.info/1260753/data) and https://www.honorstates.org/profiles/366343/. Entry to French aviation July 1916 and the 30 May 1917 Escadrille date were not re-checked.

1st Aero Squadron: Foulois; eleven officers, 82 enlisted, one civilian; eight JN-3s; two survivors back at Columbus 20 April; four N-8s CONFIRMED (origin CORRECTED, edited) Search summary of the Army history: "eleven officers, eighty-two enlisted men, and one civilian technician … eight … JN-3 biplanes … remaining two JN-3s … arriving on April 20, 1916, where they found four new Curtiss N-8s". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Reconnaissance_Squadron: it "left Fort Sam Houston in San Antonio, Texas, by train on 13 March. Glenn Springs, 5–6 May 1916 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Springs_raid — every name, number and the Cadillac match, including the spring named after the first settler killed by Comanches.
Pershing's Chinese: first of >2,700 civilians 2 Feb 1917; 524–527 Chinese; army wards; Public Resolution 29 (1921); San Antonio CONFIRMED ("in defiance" CORRECTED, edited) Journal of Policy History summary (https://muse.jhu.edu/article/433260): "more than 2,700 civilians … 524 Chinese refugees … the number given was 527"; "Pershing had to request special permission"; "Public Resolution 29 in 1921". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancho_Villa_Expedition: Pershing "was permitted to bring into New Mexico 527 Chinese refugees … despite the ban".
The call-up: Guard from 18 June 1916; NARA "110,000"; National Defense Act 3 June; San Ygnacio 15 June CONFIRMED (headcount as M14 says) NARA Prologue: "In mid-June President Wilson called out 110,000 National Guard"; "bandits killed four American soldiers at San Ygnacio, Texas" (M14 lists it as a raid; it is). The 158,664 and 140,000 figures were not re-read.
Heat, flies, dust; VD rate; Jeffery trucks $450,000 CONFIRMED NARA Prologue: "dust storms and swarms of flies"; "one of the lowest venereal disease rates an army has ever known"; Baker "was somehow able to spend $450,000 of unappropriated funds to purchase new trucks". What changed in M14 (18 edits)

Stone's birth year, his father's occupation and the confidence notes; Griensen's marriage; the 1st Aero Squadron's origin; Ojos Azules' Scout count; the 8th Illinois source date; the "1st Illinois Field Artillery" dates and the overview's list of four units; the 132nd Infantry lineage conflict; "first American Legion"; Pershing's Chinese; Butler's Part reference; the VFW "same week" detail.

Two things the owner may want to decide: (1) the "four Chicago units" should read three plus the 1st Cavalry; the 1st Illinois Cavalry (federalised 27 June 1916, mustered out 17 Nov 1916) is a documented Chicago-area unit that could take the artillery's place. (2) The Holmdahl lead is stronger than M14 says: there is a named letter-writer (L. M. Shadbolt), a named recipient (Haldeen Braddy), a place (an El Paso hotel) and a year (1927) — still hearsay, but a real chain a GM can show as a handout.

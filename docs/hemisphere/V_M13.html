V_M13 — fact-check of M13_germany_in_the_hemisphere.md

Checker: Claude Sonnet 5.5, 2026-09-30. No GPU, no git. Every "Top 10" item first, then numbers, dates, names and quotes. Verdicts: CONFIRMED / CORRECTED (+URL) / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG. Fixes applied in M13 carry ⟨fixed: V_M13⟩ or [UNVERIFIED — V_M13]; nothing else in M13 was changed (22 edits).

Score (about 60 claim-rows): roughly 38 CONFIRMED · 12 CORRECTED (Schmidt page numbers, Italy's ultimatum date, cipher "weekend", Danish-treaty signatory, HAPAG's "$2 million", Mennonite train, SCADTA "first in the Americas", invasion-plan discovery, Papen's "military unit", Viereck's scheme date, transmitter power/role, Ypiranga landing date) · 6 UNSUPPORTED/flagged (British smashing the Mexican radio station, "177 steamers 1913", Dresden coaling at St Thomas 28 July, Argovia founders, Goltz's death year, "only German spy sentenced to death") · 1 WRONG outright (Erik Scavenius did not sign the Danish treaty) · about 6 rows not checked for time (Dietrich/TIME, the two breweries, Boy-Ed's immunity, Firmin's 1885 book, Eckardt's 19 January date, Argovia/Verapaz-railway "in service by 1896").

6 "A 100 kW spark German transmitter at Chapultepec/Ixtapalapa" set up by Telefunken engineers from Sayville, fixed by Haraden Pratt's direction-finders, and its Audions smashed by two British agents in 1918 at Ixtapalapa, "putting Kurt Jahnke out of business" CORRECTED / UNSUPPORTED (edited) M13 quotes Bart Lee, "Radio Spies" (2006) accurately (verified by extracting the PDF text: pp. 14–15, citing [76], [1], [8]). But it is the only source. Castro, Wireless: Radio, Revolution, and the Mexican State, 1897–1938 (Univ. of Oklahoma diss., 2013, from Mexican archives; https://shareok.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/e8bbd18b-392d-4b39-9783-58b3743966d9/content, pp. ~142–154): the Carranza government, "with substantial assistance from German specialists", finished the receiving station at Ixtapalapa (1917–18; Nauen's first messages arrived April 1917) and a 200-kilowatt transmitter at Chapultepec (three towers 165 m; completed July 1918; "Gustavo Reuthe, an engineer who had worked at the Sayville station"; also Eugene Dzinzilewsky); "the Germans never controlled the Mexico City stations. They were not 'German operated' as U.S. intelligence reports and subsequent scholars contended"; early transmissions failed to reach Nauen; in 1919 Mexico ran 23 stations and 304,089 messages. A Mexican telecom history (https://telecommxblog.wordpress.com/2016/12/04/la-historia-de-las-telecomunicaciones-en-mexico-parte-5/) agrees: Chapultepec (call sign XDA) 1918, three Telefunken transmitters, 200 kW, and a separate receiving station at Ixtapalapa. No page confirms any British sabotage, and the Jahnke Wikipedia page does not mention a Mexican transmitter. The station was real, German-built and important; the "German-run, smashed by the British" version is Lee's alone.

What changed in M13 (22 edits)

Overview and Top-10 #6 and the Jahnke entry (Mexican radio station); invasion-plan discovery; Italy's ultimatum date and Vineta's date; Ypiranga landing date, refugees and cargo; Dresden–Karlsruhe swap date; Papen (hearsay attribution, expulsion date); cipher duration; Mare Island date; Danish-treaty signatory; Caron's $2 million, "177 steamers" and Dresden coaling; Ballin's death; Mennonite train; SCADTA; Goltz's death year; Viereck's scheme date; Argovia founders; Schmidt page numbers.

For M-chapter authors: M13's "smashed by two British agents" hook and the "100 kW spark" wording should not be reused; the better version — a Carranza-government Telefunken station, German engineers, Mexican operators, a hidden receiver in Ixtapalapa "to hide it from American intelligence officers" — is in Castro and is at least as usable.

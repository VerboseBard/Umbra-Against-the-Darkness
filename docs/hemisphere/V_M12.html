V_M12 — fact-check of M12_mexico_city_gazetteer.md

Checker: Claude Sonnet 5.5, 2026-09-30. No GPU, no git. Every "Top 10" item first, then dates, names and quotations. The key period book — Luis González Obregón, Las calles de México: Leyendas y sucedidos (1922) — was downloaded in full text from the Internet Archive (https://archive.org/details/gonzalez-obregon-luis.-las-calles-de-mexico-epl-fs-2015 ; the digital edition's front matter gives the original title Leyendas y sucedidos. Vida y costumbres de otros tiempos, González Obregón, 1922) and each legend M12 cites was located and read against M12's summary; the Wikipedia pages M12 used were re-fetched. Verdicts: CONFIRMED / CORRECTED (+URL) / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG. Fixes applied in M12 carry ⟨fixed: V_M12⟩ or [UNVERIFIED — V_M12]; nothing else in M12 was changed.

Score (about 45 claim-rows): roughly 33 CONFIRMED (all six González Obregón legends match the 1922 text) · 7 CORRECTED (Villa's 1912 escape, Los Remedios' 1810 move and the soldier's first name, Cholula's Mural of the Drinkers, Coyoacán's 1928 borough count, Olmedo's blood-stain wording, Gamio's Teotihuacán dates) · 2 earlier UNVERIFIED items resolved (Emilio Cuevas 1933, Lindbergh's landing field) · 0 UNSUPPORTED · 1 of the CORRECTED rows is WRONG outright (Lecumberri as the place of Villa's escape, which M12 already flagged) · 1 named item not in M12 (Monte Albán).

A. The Top-10 items and the task's named claims

# Claim Verdict Evidence
1 Calle del Olmedo, 15 Sep 1791: the priest Juan Antonio Nuño Vázquez, chaplain of the Marqués de Guardiola, blindfolded, taken by coach, made to hear two confessions; guarda-faroles nos. 23 and 67; Viceroy Revilla Gigedo's search; the blood-fingerprints on the Bautisterio de Santa Catarina door; the priest kept the seal; the legend of the mad friar who confessed a dead woman; the proceso in the AGN CONFIRMED (one wording fixed) González Obregón (1922), chapter "La calle del Olmedo (ahora 6.ª del Correo Mayor)", pp. 138–143: every listed element is in the text, including the "proceso original" and "el proceso que existe en el Archivo General de la Nación" (which "no aclara el misterio"), the priest found at the corner of the Parque de la Moneda and taken to the Casa de Moneda then to his home in Calle de Vergara, and the folk version that "convirtió al buen clérigo en un fraile que había perdido el juicio por haber confesado a una muerta, e hizo de dos víctimas una sola". Fixed: the physician (D. Manuel Zivillas) called the stains very old and could not say whether human or animal — M12 said "too old to be human or animal". Also from the text: the house on that street was ruled out as too far from the scene.
2 Basilica of Guadalupe: copy by Rafael Aguirre hung from 1926 until June 1929; 14 Nov 1921 flower-bomb CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady_of_Guadalupe ("after the suspension of public worship in 1926, the original image was secretly removed … replaced by a copy painted by Rafael Aguirre. It was reinstalled in June 1929"); also the es.wikipedia entry and Catholic press accounts (Aciprensa, desdelafe.mx) — Aguirre was called to paint a replica so the original could be taken out the back while the government watched the front; the original was wrapped, sealed and hidden in the double bottom of an old Chinese wardrobe in the Basilica. The 1921 bomb: same Wikipedia page (14 November 1921, "concealed in a flower arrangement").
3 Templo Mayor: Gamio 1913–14 found the SW corner; 1933 Emilio Cuevas found a staircase and beam; 1978 disk CONFIRMED; surname resolved (edited) https://arqueologiamexicana.mx/mexico-antiguo/la-zona-arqueologica-del-templo-mayor-1913-1933 and the Gamio article: Gamio's dig at Seminario no. 2 / 2.ª Santa Teresa reached the temple 6–16 May 1914 (southwest corner); architect Emilio Cuevas in 1933, at Seminario and Guatemala, 18 test pits, an alfarda and steps of tezontle, the staircase preserved for visits. So "Cueto" is wrong and "Cuevas" is right. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Templo_Mayor for the rest (Wikipedia says only "first decades of the 20th century"; the 1913–14 date is from Arqueología Mexicana).
4 Palace of the Inquisition = National School of Medicine 1854–1956 CONFIRMED Search summaries of UNAM Facultad de Medicina / México Desconocido pages: the Palace was bought in 1854 with unpaid professors' salaries and housed the Escuela Nacional de Medicina until the move to Ciudad Universitaria in 1956; Pedro de Arrieta's 1732–36 building per M02's own source.
5 Treviño de Sobremonte: reconciled at the Santo Domingo auto of 15 June 1625; burned at the auto of 11 April 1649; "¡Echen leña, que mi dinero me cuesta!"; the mules that bucked; the Indian who punched him; the Quemadero between San Diego and the Alameda; the whipped Santo Niño; the Christ buried under a doorway CONFIRMED González Obregón (1922), "La casa del judío", pp. 61–67: every item is in the text, and the author says he found no historical foundation for the house tradition. One nuance M12 keeps right: the shop/Santo Niño stories are "se cuenta" (hearsay), not from the printed case abstract.
6 Monument to the Revolution: Díaz's 1910 first stone; Bénard; iron frame, Italian marble, Norwegian granite; Madero continued; abandoned 1913; 25 years; converted 1938 (Carlos Obregón Santacilia, Cárdenas) CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_to_the_Revolution_(Mexico_City) (all of it, including Villa/Carranza/Calles/Cárdenas as the mausoleum's occupants).
7 The US Embassy and the Pact of the Embassy, 19 Feb 1913; Madero and Pino Suárez killed three days later CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pact_of_the_Embassy ("February 19, 1913 … brokered by U.S. Ambassador Henry Lane Wilson … Díaz would recognize Huerta as provisional president"); Madero and Pino Suárez "murdered while en route to Lecumberri" (Lecumberri page).
8 Cholula: 300 × 315 m, 25 m; largest pyramid by volume; Church of Our Lady of Remedies 1594 on top; Marquina's tunnelling from 1931; ~8 km by 1954; 400+ burials CONFIRMED; one item CORRECTED (edited) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Cholula (all the numbers; "over 400 human burials"; tunnelling "started in 1931", ~8.0 km by 1954; and that most of the digging was in fact directed by the site guardian Marino Gómez). Corrected: the Mural of the Drinkers was found only in 1969 (by Ponciano Salazar Ortegón, exploring Edificio 3-1-A) in the later Proyecto Cholula, not by the 1931–54 tunnels.
9 Tepoztlán: Redfield 1926–27; Tepoztlan, a Mexican Village (Chicago, 1930); Tepozteco temple to Ometochtli, 2,310 m; Dominican convent 1555–80; 8 September CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tepoztl%C3%A1n (Redfields "lived in Tepoztlán 1926-27"; 2,310 m; 1555–1580; 8 September fiestas for Tepozteco and the Virgin of the Nativity; the 8 Sep 1532 baptism legend). The 1930 University of Chicago Press publication is well known and not re-fetched.
10 Casa de los Azulejos: Jockey Club 1881; Huerta banquet 1914; Carranza seized 1915; Zapatistas; Casa del Obrero Mundial; Sanborns remodel 1917–19; Orozco's Omnisciencia 1925; national monument 1931; the "Hijo, tú nunca harás casa de azulejos" legend; bought 2 Dec 1596 for 6,500 pesos CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_de_los_Azulejos (every dated item; Jockey Club "most exclusive social club between 1880 and 1914"; the Sanborns "two years to remodel"; national monument 1931). González Obregón (1922), "La casa de los Azulejos", p. 47–55: the legend text is verbatim, and "tomó posesión de la finca y plaza el 2 de diciembre de 1596" for "6,500 pesos" (Diego Suárez de Peredo). Bonus from the same chapter, not in M12: on 4 Dec 1828, during the Acordada/Parián riot, an officer, Manuel Palacios, stabbed to death the ex-Count Andrés Diego Suárez de Peredo on the house's staircase.

C. What the owner should know before using M12

  • González Obregón is solid: the six 1922 legends match the text line for line — including the Olmedo file's "the crime was never explained" and the Chavarría arm's "no manuscript or book says who allowed it".
  • Three real errors fixed: Villa did not escape from Lecumberri (Santiago Tlatelolco, Christmas Day 1912); the Virgin of Los Remedios went into the Cathedral in 1810, not out to Naucalpan; the Mural of the Drinkers is a 1969 find, not a 1931–54 one.
  • Two small ones: Coyoacán became one of 13 delegations in 1928 (not 16); Gamio's Teotihuacán work began in 1917. Emilio Cuevas (not "Cueto") is the 1933 finder.
  • A gift for the Azulejos scene: an ex-count was stabbed to death on the stairs of the Casa de los Azulejos in the riot of 4 December 1828 (González Obregón).
  • Monte Albán is not in this chapter — see M15 §31.

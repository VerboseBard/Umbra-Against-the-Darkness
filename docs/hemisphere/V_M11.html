V_M11 — fact-check of M11_havana_gazetteer.md

Checker: Claude Sonnet 5.5, 2026-09-30. No GPU, no git. Every "Top 10" item first, then dates, addresses, names and the guidebook quotations. Both period guidebooks were re-read locally from their Internet Archive full text (the 1922 Standard Guide, https://archive.org/details/standardguidetoh00reyn , and the 1941 Havana Mañana, https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.157137 ), so every quotation attributed to them was searched for and found. Verdicts: CONFIRMED / CORRECTED (+URL) / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG. Fixes applied in M11 carry ⟨fixed: V_M11⟩ or [UNVERIFIED — V_M11]; nothing else in M11 was changed.

Score (about 40 claim-rows): roughly 28 CONFIRMED · 7 CORRECTED (Wood as "first" governor, the Maine eagle's whereabouts, the Casa de Beneficencia's 1709 founding, the 1871 students' "oldest sixteen", Sloppy Joe's date, Bacardí's address, the Espíritu Santo confraternity upgraded) · 4 UNSUPPORTED/flagged (Riverside Club's features, the busts' 1925 installation date, Prado 83 in the 1930s, the "Palacio de Educación" name) · 0 WRONG outright; the folklore is labelled as legend or rumour throughout.

A. The Top-10 items and the task's named claims

# Claim Verdict Evidence
1a Maine monument unveiled 8 March 1925 with President Zayas and General Pershing; built 1924–25 by Félix Cabarrocas; two cannon and the anchor chain at the base CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_to_the_Victims_of_the_USS_Maine ; independently https://www.spanamwar.com/mainemonumenthavana.html ("inaugurated on March 8, 1925 … Zayas, General John Pershing, Admiral Dayton and the minister of Spain"; Miss Elvira Machado unveiled the plaque). The guns were given to Cuba for the memorial: https://www.spanamwar.com/mainsalv.htm . No 1925 newspaper page was found for Pershing (Chronicling America search returned nothing relevant), so Pershing rests on Wikipedia + spanamwar.
1b Busts of McKinley, Leonard Wood and Theodore Roosevelt; Rowan added 4 July 1943 CONFIRMED that the three existed; UNSUPPORTED that they date from the 1925 unveiling (flagged) Wikipedia (en, es) says there were "originally three busts" and the fourth (Rowan) came 4 July 1943; removed 18 Jan 1961. No page read dates the three to 8 March 1925; spanamwar's description of the 1925 monument mentions the eagle, columns and "Libertad" lintel but no busts. Corrected: Wikipedia (and M11) call Wood "first military governor" — John R. Brooke held the post first (Dec 1898/Jan 1899–Dec 1899), Wood succeeded him: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_R._Brooke . Edited to "second".
1c Eagle damaged by the October 1926 hurricane and replaced; "the first is now in the US Embassy" CORRECTED (edited) Wikipedia: the head is in the US Embassy, "the body and the wings are stored in the Havana City History Museum". spanamwar: hurricane 20 October 1926 destroyed the columns, eagle and lintel; repairs funded November 1926.
1d 1911–12 salvage: stern towed out 16 March 1912 by the tug Osceola, scuttled in "600 fathoms"; Arlington mast dedicated by Wilson 1915 CONFIRMED (fathoms vary) https://www.spanamwar.com/mainsalv.htm (Osceola, three-mile limit; it gives 620 fathoms; Wikipedia/others 600). Arlington mast dedication 30 May 1915 with Wilson present (search result of the Arlington memorial page).
2 Capitolio: built 1926–29 (Machado; Rayneri Piedra; Purdy & Henderson; ~$17M; 207×91 m; dome ~92 m); kilometre-zero diamond is a replica; the original stolen 25 Mar 1946, returned 2 Jun 1947 CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Capitolio (all figures, including 55/56 steps and the 49-vs-30-ton statue discrepancy that M11 already notes). Date: inaugurated 20 May 1929 (Independence Day) with the diamond already set — https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitolio_de_La_Habana and Cuban press summaries (cibercuba, cubanet, todocuba). The visitor's diamond was swapped for a replica in 1973 (Cuban press summaries) — so "a replica" is true of today, not of 1929. The Tsar's-diamond provenance stays legend (M11 says so).
3 Espíritu Santo: the only "immune" church (papal bull 1772, royal decree 1773); founded 1635/rebuilt 1648; catacombs; confraternity of free Blacks CONFIRMED; confraternity claim upgraded (edited) https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iglesia_del_Esp%C3%ADritu_Santo_(La_Habana) (1635, 1648, bull 1772, decree 1773 of Charles III, "única iglesia inmune", crypts found 1953) and the Oficina del Historiador page https://www.lahabana.gob.cu/post_detalles/es/10081/la-iglesia-del-espiritu-santo : "En 1636 una cofradía de negros libres solicitó autorización para construir … una ermita pequeña"; "la única a la cual se le otorgó el derecho de asilo por bula pontificia … 1772"; an 1855 inscription "Única iglesia inmune en esta ciudad"; the only church in Cuba with visitable catacombs. The 1773 decree is es.wikipedia only; founding is 1635 (es.wiki) vs a 1636 petition (Oficina) — flagged. What "immunity" meant in the 1930s is still unknown (as M11 says).
4 Prado 83: German Club and American Club in the 1922 guide CONFIRMED as print; 1930s status UNVERIFIED (flagged) The 1922 text says it twice: index "American Club.— No. 83 Prado" and the "Clubs" paragraph "German Club and American Club, Prado 83". The same paragraph calls the Centro Gallego palace "new" (built 1914–15), so the paragraph probably reflects pre-war editions; a German Club open in 1922 is not independently confirmed.
5 Riverside Club: Céspedes's estate with moat, drawbridge, caves (former zoo), India shrine Guidebook text CONFIRMED; the estate itself UNSUPPORTED (flagged) The 1941 guide (pp. 63–64) says exactly what M11 quotes. Carlos Miguel de Céspedes as Machado's Secretary of Public Works: confirmed (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Park_of_Havana — he invited Forestier). No other page found mentions a "Riverside Club", moat, caves or zoo; a different Céspedes house (a neo-historicist mansion with a Japanese garden and Andalusian patio, and his mother's Villa Miramar at the river mouth) is documented in Cuban sources. Treat the moat-and-zoo as a single tourist-guide description.
6 Casa de Beneficencia: Bishop Valdés 1709; children take his surname; foundling wheel CORRECTED — date and founder (edited) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Casa_de_Beneficencia_y_Maternidad_de_La_Habana : Casa Cuna founded by Bishop Diego Evelino de Compostela in 1687; "not until 1794 during the government of Luis de las Casas" was the Beneficencia in its final place at San Lázaro and Belascoaín; children without a surname "were given the surname Valdés" (Bishop Gerónimo de Nosti y Valdés); the torno on the Belascoaín side. Cubanet: Valdés completed the Casa Cuna, inaugurated 1711. So "founded by Valdés in 1709" (the 1941 guide's line) is wrong; the surname rule and the wheel are right.
7 Príncipe–La Punta tunnel rumour; El Morro's shark chutes CONFIRMED as period folklore Both are in the 1941 guide verbatim ("Rumor says that secret passages, tunneled underneath, connect it with La Punta"; cells "below sea level, with chutes cut through the solid rock from which prisoners were thrown to the sharks. Contrary to popular belief, however, this is no longer the accepted method"). Príncipe's red-brick tunnels, 1767–79, prison role and prisoners: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castillo_del_Pr%C3%ADncipe . Both labelled rumour/legend in M11.
8 Belén and Fr Viñes: charter 1854 (Isabella II), observatory 1857, Viñes director from 1870, "very likely" first hurricane warning 11 Sep 1875, 20 stations by 1888, Practical Hints 1885 (USN translation); move to Marianao 1925 CONFIRMED (one name flagged) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Vi%C3%B1es ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belen_Jesuit_Preparatory_School (nine radial pavilions, sixty acres, Morales & Cía, next to the Tropicana). Jesuits taught in the old convent until 1925–26. Flag: the "Palacio de Educación" name — Wikipedia's text is muddled and may attach it to the Marianao campus, not the old site.
9 Sans Souci: full-moon blackout; the pool "where infuriated suitors throw the girls who say no" CONFIRMED as a 1941 guide legend 1941 guide p. 146 verbatim ("Stay away from the garden's pool, though. That's where, so the tale goes, infuriated suitors throw the girls who say no! When the moon is full, Sans Souci turns off artificial light…"). Not independently attested; correctly labelled a tourist legend. (The English Wikipedia title I tried returned nothing.)
10a Camp Columbia: Batista's battered Chevrolet in the soldiers' club CONFIRMED (1941 guide only) 1941 guide, p. 102–103 verbatim.
10b Hotel Nacional: opened 30 Dec 1930; McKim, Mead & White; National City Bank; Purdy & Henderson; Taganana Hill; Villaverde's La Cueva de Taganana CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Nacional_de_Cuba (opening 30 Dec 1930; "constructed in fourteen months"; the Taganana legend and Villaverde named; segregated — turned away Joe Louis, Marian Anderson, Jackie Robinson, Josephine Baker; first Black guest Eartha Kitt 1956). Renamed 1939, Kirkeby July 1943.

C. What the owner should know before using M11

  • The "cast in bronze" hook is safe, with one label change: the Maine monument's unveiling (8 Mar 1925, Zayas, Pershing) is documented in two independent places; the three busts (McKinley, Wood, Roosevelt) are documented but not dated to that day; and Wood was the second US military governor of Cuba, not the first.
  • The Capitolio's diamond and 1929 date hold (inaugurated 20 May 1929); the visitor's diamond is a replica now (from 1973).
  • Espíritu Santo is on firmer ground than M11 said: a 1636 petition by free Black Havanans, a 1772 bull, an 1855 "única iglesia inmune" plaque — good for the setting, and for the respect note.
  • Do not repeat: the Beneficencia as "founded by Valdés in 1709" (1687 / c. 1711 / 1794); the students as "oldest sixteen"; the Riverside Club's moat-and-zoo as anything but one 1941 tourist guide's paragraph; a German Club at Prado 83 in the 1930s (a 1922 print of possibly older text).

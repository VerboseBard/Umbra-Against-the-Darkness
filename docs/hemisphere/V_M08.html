T1 Calles's 1915–16 Sonora: dry state; all priests expelled; "the model of the Calles Law" CONFIRMED (the acts) / UNSUPPORTED (dated inside 1915–16) Wikipedia (Calles): as governor from 1915 "he attempted to make Sonora a dry state" (not "banned alcohol"); Encyclopedia.com: as governor of Sonora "(1915–1916, 1917–1919)" he set out "a radical program to extend secularization (including the legalization of divorce and the expulsion of all priests)". Notes for the owner

  • Three things to tell your other files: (1) Babícora is not settled — Time's 1,625,000 acres conflicts with the 1954 grant (674,000 acres) and a 350,000-ha figure; M01/PEOPLE's "close to a million" may be closer. (2) Calles's Sonora priest expulsion is real but nobody I read dates it; "1915–16" is the governorship's first spell, not a dated decree. Don't put a date on it. (3) Nogales 1918: the chapter's American dead were Wikipedia's infobox; the article's text (and other sources) say four.
  • Left unchecked (no page reached): Casa Madero's 1597 date (Wikipedia URL 404), Hermosillo/Diéguez, the ASARCO/Parral entry, Tayopa/Flipper, the 1912 Dublán exodus details, Cloete/Lusitania.
  • The strongest confirmations for the Top 10: Stilwell (Pullman museum: 13 days, $1,000), the Chinese numbers (6,078/92 on Wikipedia with footnotes), Torreón (the museum's own memorial), Kosterlitzky (every date), Rosita Vieja.

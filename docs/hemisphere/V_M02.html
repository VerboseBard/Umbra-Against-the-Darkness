  • Checked: 2026-09-30, Claude Sonnet 5.5. No GPU, no git. Only M02_mexico_folklore_religion.md was edited (fixes marked ⟨fixed: V_M02⟩ / [UNVERIFIED — V_M02]); nothing else in the chapter was touched.
  • Method: the Top 10 first, then the first-attestation dates and specific numbers, names and quotes. Where the chapter rested on search summaries I re-read a real page (Wikipedia, TSHA, WRSP, Project Gutenberg text of Brinton, the archive.org full texts of Redfield and Parsons, the LARB and Mexico News Daily Carvajal pieces, Agostoni's peer-reviewed Fidencio article, the PMC Santa Muerte article, OU Press) or tagged the claim. # Claim Verdict Evidence
T1 La Pascualita first displayed 25 March 1930 at La Popular, Chihuahua City; owner Pascuala Esparza UNSUPPORTED (date), CONFIRMED (legend, owner, shop) The date is repeated by every popular page (Amusing Planet, History Defined, All That's Interesting …) but none cites a record; History Defined (fetched) says explicitly it gives no source for the date and that the owner "always refused to show proof" she was a mannequin. No contemporary newspaper or shop record found. Now tagged. https://www.historydefined.net/la-pascualita/
T2a Mummies of Guanajuato: Santa Paula cemetery opened 1861; exhumations from 1865; first mummy French physician Dr Remigio Leroy CONFIRMED https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mummies_of_Guanajuato
T2b Leroy exhumed 9 June 1865 CONFIRMED (month), day unconfirmed Mexico News Daily (fetched): "June 1865", no day. The "9 June" appears in a WebSearch summary only. The chapter already hedges ("popular sources date…"); left. https://mexiconewsdaily.com/mexico-living/guanajuatos-beautiful-and-terrifying-mummies/
T2c Paid viewing "by the late 1800s"; formal museum 1969 CORRECTED (sources differ) Wikipedia: "late nineteenth century", museum 1969. Mexico News Daily: "by the early 1900s… charging for a view", government museum 1968. Chapter now gives both.
T2d 111 mummies, 59 displayed (2007) CONFIRMED Wikipedia (above): "As of 2007, 59 of the collection's 111 mummies were on public display."
T2e Woman "buried alive", hands at her face CONFIRMED Named Ignacia Aguilar, a catalepsy case, "wounds on her forehead and her hands at her temples" (Mexico News Daily, above). Chapter now names her.
T3a Calles visited Niño Fidencio at Espinazo on 8 February 1928 CONFIRMED Wikipedia, WRSP, and Agostoni 2018 ("arribó… el tren presidencial Olivo" on 8 Feb 1928). https://wrldrels.org/2016/10/08/el-nino-fidencio/ · https://www.redalyc.org/journal/1271/127155842008/html/
T3b The visit lasted six hours CORRECTED → about three hours WRSP says "six hours". Claudia Agostoni (UNAM), Anuario Colombiano de Historia Social y de la Cultura 45(1), 2018, pp. 215–243, citing the press: Calles "sorprendió… durante las tres horas que permaneció en ese desolado lugar". Wikipedia gives no duration. Chapter now says three, notes WRSP's six.
T3c Fidencio came to Espinazo in 1925 CORRECTED Agostoni: "En 1921 o 1923… llegó a la hacienda de Espinazo"; his healing began 1925. Wikipedia says 1921. WRSP's 1925 is the year the healing began, not necessarily the arrival.
T3d Patron Teodoro von Wernich, German immigrant, hacienda owner, Spiritist CONFIRMED Agostoni: "empresario alemán y espiritista Teodoro von Wernich"; he promised to make Fidencio world-famous (photograph, 1927). WRSP's "may have introduced Fidencio to Spiritism" stays hedged.
T3e New York Times, "Cures by Healer Stir Mexico City", 22 Feb 1928 CONFIRMED Agostoni (title and date), and a WebSearch summary of the Wikipedia article.
T3f Born 17 Oct 1898 Yuriria, Guanajuato; died 19 Oct 1938 CONFIRMED WRSP (birth/place), Wikipedia (dates). (Agostoni places his childhood at "Yuriria, Michoacán" — a slip; Yuriria is in Guanajuato.)
T4a Carvajal: burned 8 Dec 1596; papers vanished from the Archivo General de la Nación 1932; suspect jailed three months, released for lack of evidence; Bloomsbury 2015; Swann 21 June 2016, lot 387; booklet about 4 × 3 in; Milberg returned it CONFIRMED Mexico News Daily ("three-month imprisonment… released", Bloomsbury 2015 at ~$1,500), Princeton library catalogue via search (Swann sale 2420, lot 387, 21 June 2016), Times of Israel ("four inches by three"). Wikipedia: burned 8 Dec 1596. https://mexiconewsdaily.com/mexico-living/story-of-first-jewish-writings-in-americas/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_de_Carvajal_the_Younger
T4b Nachbin (accused by Alfonso Toro) had taught at Northwestern, Evanston CONFIRMED (upgraded from UNVERIFIED) LARB (fetched): "a Yiddish-speaking shtetl orphan from Poland who taught at Northwestern University, in Evanston, Illinois, and at the University of New Mexico in Las Vegas"; Toro, an Archivo employee, called the authorities; alternative hypothesis that Toro was the thief. https://lareviewofbooks.org/article/the-thief-on-the-rediscovery-of-luis-de-carvajal-the-youngers-memoir/
T5a Teresistas hit Mexican customs houses: Nogales 12 Aug 1896, Ojinaga 17 Aug, Palomas CONFIRMED WRSP (Nogales 12 Aug; Ojinaga 17 Aug; Palomas early Sept), TSHA (Nogales, "sixty to seventy exiled Yaqui and Tomochi Indians"), EPCC guide. T5d Born 15 Oct 1873, Ocoroni, Sinaloa, to Cayetana Chávez (14) and Tomás Urrea; the trance "three months and eighteen days" CONFIRMED TSHA.

T7 Brinton read Nagualism to the American Philosophical Society 5 Jan 1894; "a cult … which united many and diverse tribes … into organized opposition against the government and the religion which had been introduced from Europe" CONFIRMED Project Gutenberg text (read): "Read before the American Philosophical Society, Jan'y 5, 1894"; the quotation is exact; also the Herrera line ("when such an animal dies, so does the Indian"). https://www.gutenberg.org/files/26426/26426-h/26426-h.htm
